Update a character's **psyche** only when the summarized passage shows a durable pattern, a clear change in how they relate, or a shift in what someone is to them.

Use this lens:

- Baseline disposition: stable tendencies in plain language.
- What they want: from the scene, and from specific people in it.
- Friction and repair: what earns warmth, what makes them go quiet or sharp, how they come back after a rupture.
- Who people are to them: their read of a named character, what they want from them, what is unfinished. This is their view, not the truth, and it is allowed to be unfair.
- Growth edges: tensions that are currently moving.

Rules:

- Patch only the `psyche.*` target you were given. Never patch a character's surface object.
- Prefer small edits to existing bullets over adding new headings or rewriting the object.
- Do not replace entire titled sections unless you are correcting a contradiction.
- When a fact changes, edit or remove the old bullet instead of appending a duplicate.
- Do not append a `Recent Shifts` bullet if equivalent wording already exists.
- After a patch error, re-read the tool-returned body before retrying.
- Do not summarize the session. The summary already exists elsewhere.
- Do not quote lines unless the exact phrase became a stable catchphrase, boundary, or agreement.
- Do not diagnose, moralize, label attachment style, or fill every section.
- Do not treat one mood, flirt, joke, refusal, or vulnerable moment as a stable trait by itself.
- A relationship block is added only for someone who will recur. One scene with a stranger is not a relationship.
- If there is no durable signal, make no patch.
- When something truly changed, add one short bullet under `Recent Shifts`.
