RUNNING A CLOCK

How to run a clock written into a pinned encounter or front: when to tick it, what to say out loud, and what happens when it fills. Applies whenever an object in context carries a `Clock: <name> [n]` line.

A clock is the scene's schedule made visible. It exists so that pressure arrives on stated terms instead of whenever you feel like it — which means the terms are not yours to adjust mid-scene.

TICKING

- Tick only on the events the object's `Ticks when` line names, in the amount it names. If something dramatic happens that the line does not cover, it does not tick. That is the point of writing it down.
- One event ticks a clock once, however many characters were involved. A patrol that spots the whole party is one tick.
- Never tick to make a scene feel tense, never tick because a beat felt like a failure, and never tick twice for the same event because it came up again in the narration.
- If the player asks whether something ticked, answer plainly. This is table talk, not a beat.

SAYING IT

Announce every change, in the fiction and then in the count: "the shout carries down the hall — **Alarm 3/4**." The count is the whole reason the artifact exists; a clock you tick silently is you keeping a secret timer.

Restate the current count when a scene resumes after a break, and when the party is deciding what to do next. Do not restate it every beat.

FILLING

When a clock fills, the `At full` line happens — as written, now, in the narration. Do not soften it, do not delay it to a more dramatic moment, and do not ask the player if they are ready. The clock filling *is* the dramatic moment.

Once it fills, it is spent. Do not keep ticking it. If the object defines a linked clock that starts on completion, start that one at zero and announce it.

A tug-of-war clock also empties: apply the object's stated conditions in the same way, and announce the count going down as plainly as going up.

WHAT YOU DO NOT DO

- Do not invent a clock mid-scene. Prepared clocks are prepared; if the scene wants one that is not there, run the pressure as ordinary fiction and let the player raise it in a design session.
- Do not add segments to a clock the party is about to beat, or remove segments to be kind.
- Do not explain how clocks work to the player. They can see the count.
