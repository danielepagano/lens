# SKIRMISH — fights, at the table

Layered on the base rules: dice, LASERS/FEELINGS, and success counting are
unchanged. This module only says how a fight is *structured* once one starts.

## The exchange

A fight runs in **exchanges**, not turns. One exchange is: the threat commits to
something, every character who wants in describes what they do about it, and
everything resolves together. Never ask "what do you do?" one character at a
time — put the threat's intent on the table and let the crew answer it at once.

Order inside an exchange:

1. **State intent.** Say what the threat is about to do and to whom. Never roll
   for the threat and never resolve its action before the crew answers.
2. **Take declarations.** Each character says what they are doing. Helping works
   as in the base rules — the helper rolls first.
3. **Roll once each.** One roll per character per exchange, no more. Add **+1d if
   the crew set the terms** (ambush, cover, height, a prepared trap) on top of
   the usual prepared/expert dice.
4. **Resolve together**, then re-state the new situation and stop. The exchange
   is a beat: end it and yield.

## Harm

Harm is a three-step ladder, not a number:

| Step | Effect |
|------|--------|
| **Grazed** | Cosmetic and loud. No mechanical effect; describe it. |
| **Hurt** | −1d on every roll until treated or the scene ends. |
| **Down** | Out of the fight. Not dead unless the player says so. |

A 0-success roll against a real threat moves the character one step down the
ladder. A 1-success roll succeeds *and* moves them one step. Nothing else does —
do not invent hit points, and never move a character down the ladder without a
roll they made themselves.

## Threat clocks

Give a dangerous fight a **clock**: 4 segments for a scuffle, 6 for a serious
fight, 8 for a set-piece. Tick one segment per exchange in which the crew makes
real progress, two on a critical success. When it fills, the fight is decided in
the crew's favour — announce that plainly instead of rolling the fight out to
exhaustion. Say the clock out loud when it changes: "**Ambush 3/6.**"

## Ending it

A fight ends when the clock fills, when the threat's goal becomes impossible, or
when the crew disengages. Losing means a cost — captured, robbed, separated, the
objective lost — never a dead character without the player's consent.
