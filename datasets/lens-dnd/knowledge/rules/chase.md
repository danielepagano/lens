D&D CHASE RULES

Use these rules whenever someone is trying to get away and someone else is trying not to let them. That includes chases with no violence in them at all — fleeing a creditor, tailing a courier, outrunning a collapse. Do NOT resolve a chase with ordinary movement rates: comparing Speeds makes the outcome arithmetic, and it is decided before the first round.

STRUCTURE

- Roll Initiative when the chase begins, as for combat.
- Track the DISTANCE between quarry and pursuers, not exact positions. Distance changes; the map does not need to.
- Each round every participant moves and acts as normal. The distance closes or opens by the difference in what they actually covered.

DASHING AND EXHAUSTION

A participant can Dash as usual. After two consecutive Dashes, a creature must succeed on a DC 10 Constitution check at the end of its turn or gain 1 Exhaustion level. The check repeats after each further consecutive Dash.

Exhaustion is what ends most chases. A creature reduced to Exhaustion level 6 dies, but long before that the −2 per level on D20 Tests and −5 feet per level of Speed decide the race.

COMPLICATIONS

Each round, introduce one concrete obstacle drawn from the terrain — a cart across the alley, a crowded market, a locked gate, a low branch, a drop to the next roof, a stretch of scree. State what it costs: extra movement, a check to pass at speed, or a choice between a slow safe route and a fast risky one. A complication that is only scenery is not a complication.

Complications should hit whoever reaches them, quarry and pursuer alike. A chase in which only the players face obstacles is a punishment, not a chase.

ENDING THE CHASE

The quarry ESCAPES when any of these is true:

- Distance exceeds the range at which the pursuers can keep track of them.
- The quarry is out of sight for 3 consecutive rounds. Reacquiring them requires 3 consecutive successful DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) or Wisdom (Survival) checks.
- They reach a space the pursuer cannot follow into — over a wall it cannot climb, through a door it cannot open, into water it cannot swim.

The pursuer CATCHES UP when distance closes to melee reach. At that point the chase is over and whatever happens next is a fight, a conversation, or a surrender.

A quarry that hides mid-chase uses the ordinary Hide rules: the Stealth check result becomes the DC to find them. Hiding while still in sight is not possible.

RUNNING IT WELL

- Say the distance out loud every round, in feet or in plain terms ("half a street", "closing"). The player is tracking a number they cannot see otherwise.
- Give the quarry a goal beyond "away" — a door, a rooftop, a crowd, a boat. A chase toward something is more interesting than a chase away from someone.
- Let smart choices shortcut the procedure. A rope across the alley, a spell that shapes ground, a bribe shouted at the crowd — resolve it and move on rather than making them roll out the remaining rounds.
