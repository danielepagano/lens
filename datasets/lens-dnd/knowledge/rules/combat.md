D&D COMBAT RULES

Running a fight: initiative and surprise, the action economy, opportunity attacks, movement and cover, mounts and underwater, and how damage and death land. Load the moment violence starts or initiative is about to be rolled — including when a conversation collapses into one, and before you narrate the first blow.

You are still not running the system — the player is. This module gives you the structure of a fight so you can direct the creatures you control, describe the situation accurately, and narrate results the player hands back. Everything here that involves a roll is theirs to make.

STARTING COMBAT

Combat starts when the fiction demands it. Call for initiative: the player rolls it and tracks it. You may need to clarify who the opponents are if that is not already obvious, and whether anyone rolls with Advantage or Disadvantage.

- A character who initiates hostilities (casting a spell, making an attack) has Advantage on the Initiative roll.
- SURPRISE: a creature that is caught unawares by combat starting has Disadvantage on its Initiative roll. Surprise is NOT a skipped turn, and it is not a free round. Do not give an ambusher a turn before initiative.

INITIATIVE

Roll 1d20 + Dexterity modifier. Higher result acts first. Identical creatures roll once as a group. The order stays the same from round to round. You cannot delay your turn — a character who wants to act later in response to something takes the Ready action. Ties: the DM decides between monsters, players decide between characters.

You only need the player to hand you control when it is the turn of a creature (or lair) you control and they are yielding narrative control. In a simple fight you can say "the zombies always attack the nearest enemy" and let the player run them for the whole encounter and report results — that is just as fun, and faster, for them. You are needed when the creatures you control have meaningful contextual choices to make.

ACTION ECONOMY

Each turn a creature moves up to its Speed and takes one Action, plus one Bonus Action if something specifically grants it. Movement can be split before and after the action. Talking, gesturing, and one free object interaction cost nothing.

One Action means one thing. A creature does not get a second attack because the fiction is exciting — extra attacks come only from a feature that grants them, and the player will tell you when they have one. Each creature also has one Reaction per round, which resets at the start of its turn.

ACTIONS

- ATTACK: Make one attack (or more if Extra Attack applies). A creature with "Multiattack" may only have multiattack on some of its attacks; the others take the full Action.
- DASH: Gain extra movement equal to Speed this turn.
- DISENGAGE: Movement doesn't provoke Opportunity Attacks for the rest of the turn.
- DODGE: Until the start of your next turn — attacks against you have Disadvantage; you have Advantage on Dex saves. Lost if Incapacitated or Speed = 0.
- HELP: Give an ally Advantage on their next ability check or attack roll (must be within 5 feet for attack help). Or use Medicine DC 10 to stabilize a dying creature.
- MAGIC: Cast a spell, activate a magic item, or use another magical ability. CONCENTRATION: only one Concentration spell active at a time; taking damage requires a Con save (DC = the higher of 10 or half the damage taken) to maintain; Incapacitated breaks Concentration automatically.
- READY: Declare a perceivable trigger and a response. When the trigger occurs, use your Reaction to execute it — immediately *after* the trigger resolves, so a response triggered by "it attacks" lands after that attack, not between it and a second one. Readied spells are cast now, slot expended, released on the trigger; if not released before your next turn, the slot is lost. Readying is not a commitment: the creature may spend that Reaction on something else instead, but it only ever has one.
- UTILIZE: Interact with a second object, or with one that takes real effort (a stuck door, a crank).

Bonus Actions exist only when a feature, spell, or rule explicitly grants one. They cannot substitute for an Action, nor an Action for them.

OPPORTUNITY ATTACKS

When a creature you can see leaves your reach using its action, Bonus Action, Reaction, or one of its speeds, you may take your Reaction to make one melee attack against it. The attack occurs right before it leaves your reach.

It does NOT trigger on teleportation, on movement the creature did not spend (an explosion hurling it, a fall), or when the mover took the Disengage action. A creature that has already spent its Reaction cannot make one. If a readied Reaction and an Opportunity Attack come up at the same moment, taking the Opportunity Attack consumes the Reaction and the readied action is lost.

- The trigger is leaving **that creature's** reach, so a Reach weapon changes it: next to a halberd wielder, a foe can step 5 feet away freely and only provokes when it leaves the 10-foot reach.
- A creature that cannot see the mover cannot make one. Blindsight is the exception — it counts as seeing within its radius.
- For a creature you control, an Opportunity Attack is **any single melee attack in its stat block, or an Unarmed Strike**. It is never Multiattack (that requires the Attack action) and never a breath weapon or anything else without a melee attack roll.
- The Unarmed Strike option means an Opportunity Attack can be a Grapple or a Shove instead of damage.

MOVEMENT AND POSITION

- Dropping Prone is free. Standing up costs half your Speed. A creature with Speed 0 cannot drop prone.
- You can move through the space of an ally, an Incapacitated creature, a Tiny creature, or a creature two or more sizes different from yours. Another creature's space is Difficult Terrain unless it is Tiny or your ally. You cannot willingly end a move in an occupied space. Knocking an enemy Prone does **not** open its space — Prone is not one of the exceptions.
- A flying creature without the Hover trait can stay aloft without moving; it does not have to travel each round to remain in the air.
- Hiding during a fight takes the Hide action. A creature that gave away its position by attacking is not hidden again for free.

MAKING AN ATTACK

Attacks are resolved against the target's Armor Class, and the player rolls them. What you own is the *situation* — say what is true about the shot and let them apply it.

- Choose a target within range; the DM determines Cover and any Advantage or Disadvantage; the attacker rolls and, on a hit, rolls damage.
- Ranged weapons list "normal/long range". Beyond normal range: Disadvantage. Beyond long range: impossible.
- Making a ranged attack while within 5 feet of an enemy who can see you and is not Incapacitated: Disadvantage.
- Melee reach is 5 feet unless the weapon or creature says otherwise.
- Attacking a target you cannot see: Disadvantage, and if the target is not where you aimed, you miss. When a creature cannot see you, you have Advantage against it. A hidden attacker gives away its location whether the attack hits or misses.
- Line of sight means the plain English thing: if the attacker cannot see it, it is not in line of sight. There is no special rules definition, so the call is yours — when it is genuinely unclear, trace a line from any corner of the attacker's space to any part of the target's, and if it passes through something that blocks vision, there is no shot. The same call decides what an area of effect reaches.
- Areas of effect: the player computes the shape; you rule on who is standing in it. One shape has a rule worth knowing, because it replaced the old "aura": an **Emanation** extends in straight lines from a creature or object in all directions, moves with that origin, and does **not** include the origin itself unless its creator says so.

UNARMED STRIKE: DAMAGE, GRAPPLE, OR SHOVE

An Unarmed Strike is a melee attack made with the body against a target within 5 feet. Choose one effect:

- Damage: an attack roll; on a hit, 1 + Strength modifier Bludgeoning damage.
- Grapple: the target must succeed on a **Strength or Dexterity saving throw — its choice** — or gain the Grappled condition. The DC for that save and for later escape attempts is 8 + the grappler's Strength modifier + Proficiency Bonus. Only possible if the target is at most one size larger and the grappler has a hand (or the body part its stat block names) free.
- Shove: the target must succeed on a **Strength or Dexterity saving throw — its choice** — or be pushed 5 feet away or knocked Prone, attacker's choice. Same DC. Only possible if the target is at most one size larger.

Grapple and Shove are **saving throws, not contested checks**. Do not call for "Athletics contested by Athletics or Acrobatics" — that is the older edition's rule, and it is wrong here. Escaping an existing grapple is the reverse: the grappled creature spends its action on a Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check against the escape DC.

Being Grappled limits **movement, not action**: a grappled creature can still attack, cast, and use its abilities. It is often paired with something that does restrain — many monsters' grabbing attacks also impose Restrained — but the grapple alone does not. A grappler who goes Prone does not drag the grappled creature down with it.

IMPROVISED WEAPONS

An object wielded as a makeshift weapon — a stool, a bottle, a table leg — deals 1d4 damage of whatever type suits it, and Proficiency Bonus does not apply. If it plainly resembles a real weapon, say so and let it use that weapon's rules instead. Thrown, it has 20/60 ft range.

COVER

- Half Cover: +2 bonus to AC and Dexterity saving throws.
- Three-Quarters Cover: +5 bonus to AC and Dexterity saving throws.
- Total Cover: cannot be targeted directly.

Only the most protective degree applies; degrees are not added together. A creature standing in the way grants the target Half Cover — it does NOT impose Disadvantage on the attacker. There is no penalty in this edition for shooting into a melee you are not in; if you find yourself reaching for one, apply cover instead.

MOUNTED COMBAT

Mounting or dismounting costs half your Speed, rounded down. A controlled mount (one trained to accept a rider) changes its Initiative to match the rider's, moves on the rider's turn, and has only three action options: Dash, Disengage, and Dodge. An independent mount keeps its own place in the Initiative order and acts as it likes.

If an effect is about to move the mount against its will while you are on it, you must succeed on a DC 10 Dexterity saving throw or fall off, landing Prone in an unoccupied space within 5 feet of the mount. The same save applies if you are knocked Prone or the mount is.

UNDERWATER COMBAT

A creature without a Swim Speed has Disadvantage on melee attack rolls underwater unless the weapon deals Piercing damage. A ranged attack roll underwater automatically misses a target beyond normal range, and has Disadvantage against a target within normal range. Anything underwater has Resistance to Fire damage. Without a Swim Speed, water is Difficult Terrain.

DAMAGE, IN THE PLAYER'S HANDS

The player rolls and tracks all of this. You need it to narrate accurately:

- A CRITICAL HIT (natural 20 on an attack roll) doubles the damage dice, not the total. Modifiers are added once. Anything on the attack that is not damage is not doubled — a rider that drains an ability score or imposes a condition happens once.
- RESISTANCE halves damage of a type; VULNERABILITY doubles it; IMMUNITY means none at all. Each applies once per instance no matter how many sources there are. When both apply, Vulnerability first, then Resistance — they do not cancel.
- Resistance is applied **after** everything else, including a successful save. A creature that saves against a dragon's breath and also resists the damage takes a quarter of it. Damage can be reduced to 0, but never below.
- TEMPORARY HIT POINTS are a separate pool that absorbs damage first. They do not stack — a new grant replaces a smaller one rather than adding — and healing does not restore them.
- BLOODIED means at or below half hit point maximum. Narrate it visibly; some creatures change behavior there.
- A creature reduced to 0 HP by a single hit, with damage to spare equal to its hit point maximum, dies outright.

Saving throw damage: full on a failure, half (round down) on a success, unless the effect says "no damage on a successful save".

RUNNING CREATURES WELL

This is the part that is actually yours:

- Monsters pursue goals, not the nearest target. The cornered captain takes a hostage; beaten minions break and run; something intelligent falls back to a chokepoint. A sapient enemy may try to talk if the fight turns against it.
- Share what becomes apparent. If a spell visibly does nothing to a creature, say so.
- Hasten a foregone conclusion. If victory is inevitable and the fight is dragging, have the last enemy drop.
- You do not control the numbers, so adjust through fiction: enemies flee when bloodied, reinforcements arrive, the terrain shifts, tactics change.

ENDING COMBAT

Combat ends when one side is killed, knocked out, has surrendered, or has fled — or when both sides agree to stop. A fight that becomes a pursuit is a chase, not a combat round with more running.
