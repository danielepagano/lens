USING A D&D STAT BLOCK

A `stat.*` object is the sheet for a creature YOU (the AI) controls. This is how to run one at the table.

WHAT IS YOURS AND WHAT IS NOT

Assume the player can see every stat block you can see. They have the whole KB and can look up any spell named in a block. So you never read numbers back to them, and you never work out their side of a resolution: no attack bonuses, no ACs, no save DCs for their characters to beat, no damage totals. You are likely to get those wrong, and it is their job.

What you do with a stat block is choose. Read it, pick what this creature does this turn, say who it is doing it to, and hand the resolution over.

ACT ONLY FROM THE BLOCK

A creature can do exactly what its block says it can do, plus move and take the ordinary actions any creature has. Do not invent an ability because the moment calls for one. If the block has no ranged attack, the creature closes or throws something improvised; it does not suddenly have a bow.

- **Multiattack** tells you what one Action buys. If a block has Multiattack, that is the creature's Action — it does not also get the individual attacks listed below it.
- **Recharge (N–M)** abilities are available at the start of the fight, then only after the recharge roll comes up. If the player is tracking recharges, ask; if nobody is, do not use the ability twice in a row.
- **N/Day** abilities are finite. Spend them deliberately — a creature that dumps its best option on round one is usually a worse scene than one that saves it.
- **Legendary Actions** happen between other creatures' turns; **Legendary Resistance** turns a failed save into a success and is spent when used. Both are resources the player is likely tracking.
- **At Will** spells cost nothing. Name the spell rather than describing a generic effect — the player can look it up and will want to.

NAMING AND NUMBERS

Say what the creature does in fiction and name the mechanic it maps to: "the aeromancer levels its staff and calls the wind down the corridor — that's Gust of Wind" is better than either bare prose or a bare stat line. Then stop and let the player resolve it.

If a block gives a number you need in order to describe the situation truthfully — reach, speed, a sense, an immunity — use it. If the number belongs to a roll, it is not yours.

MULTIPLE COPIES

Identical creatures share one Initiative roll and one set of tactics unless the fiction separates them. Give them a distinguishing detail when they start mattering individually (the one with the notched shield, the one that hangs back). Track them by fiction, not by number, unless a `tracker.*` object is doing that for you.
