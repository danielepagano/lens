# Chapter 15: Guide to Dark Fantasy

## Dark Fantasy Tone — What Makes Etharis Different

- **No reliable heroes.** Characters and NPCs alike struggle with impossible moral choices. Even good people commit terrible acts. A happy ending where everyone survives intact is unlikely. Villains don't hesitate to commit atrocities.

- **Magic comes at cost.** Infernal pacts consume souls. Arcane study takes a physical and mental toll. Miscalculation harms innocents. Magic is not a tool — it is a danger that must be managed.

- **Realism over idealism.** Etharis reflects the world as we fear it is: moral failings, corrupt institutions, and the cruel truth that those who fight the status quo suffer for it. War is not glorious — it is destructive, and those who profit from it rarely pay any price.

- **Horror is atmosphere, not shock.** Dread builds slowly through tension and atmosphere. Terror comes from catching characters unaware. Revulsion is cheap; the lasting horror is existential — the realization that something horrible is happening beyond anyone's control.

- **Hope is not absent — it is what makes darkness meaningful.** A world cruel for cruelty's sake is boring and illogical. Light exists in Etharis so that the darkness has contrast. Moments of levity, triumph, and kindness are not genre violations — they make the tragedies land harder.

## Conflict Scale

Stories in Etharis operate at three scales that work best when woven together:

- **Character conflicts** are personal — a character's backstory, goals, flaws, and bonds. The richest dark fantasy emerges when what a character wants collides with who they have to become to get it.

- **Party conflicts** are shared stakes — a threat or antagonist the whole group must confront together. The key is ensuring the conflict intersects with what the characters already care about, not just the world at large.

- **World conflicts** are backdrop — wars between nations, the Great Beast's shadow, the Weeping Pox, the fractured vampire nobility of Ostoya. These ground the party in a living world where things happen that are larger than them, and may eventually pull them in.

## Progression Philosophy

Milestone or goal-based progression fits dark fantasy better than XP-for-kills. Power should grow slowly enough that danger remains real — leveling too quickly undermines the sense of peril that gives the setting its weight.
