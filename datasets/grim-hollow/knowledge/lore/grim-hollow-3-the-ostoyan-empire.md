**Chapter 3: The Ostoyan Empire**

The sun never rises on the Ostoyan Empire. Gothic horror defines this realm: a sunless land of vampire aristocrats who drain their subjects dry — literally and economically — while the oppressed try to survive long enough to resist. The central question Ostoya poses is how much tyranny people will endure in exchange for the illusion of safety. The Crimson Court rules through blood, fear, and the exploitation of a people who once fled tyranny and built something new, only to watch it be devoured by something ancient.

## Geography

Ostoya lies east of the Grey Spine mountains, accessible from Bürach only through the Elian Pass — which is garrisoned and contested. Six provinces spread from the northern cliffs of Raevo to the southern coast of Soma. The land is shadowed and sunless, rivers cutting through forests and plains under a sky that never brightens. The Darkfall left fissures in the earth that still crack open to release undead from the necropolis beneath, particularly around the capital Nov Ostoya. Latent necromancy makes the dead restless everywhere; proper funerary rites include decapitation.

**The Stillborn Forest** (Bezcodru) — a suffocating canopy that swallows the sky entirely, inhabited by undead spirits, flesh-hungry ghouls, and packs of fzeg vampires permitted to hunt freely.

**The City Below** — the ancient necropolis beneath Nov Ostoya, revealed by the Darkfall. Still partially sealed, still leaking undead, and rumored to be where the oldest vampires sleep and where Klaus Vorgen (founder of the Crimson Court) may have retreated.

## History

**The Founding.** Bürach secessionists crossed the Grey Spine to build a nation free from imperial taxation and religious tyranny. They thrived briefly as a loose confederation of settlements, then fought off Bürach invasion at Kaer Vena — a fortress that predated them, built by an unknown civilization with a reverence for the sun.

**The Darkfall.** During the second Bürach-Ostoyan war, the earth cracked open, the sun dripped from the sky like cooling metal, and tides of undead flooded from fissures in the earth. Nov Ostoya's leadership sealed their own troops underground to contain the scourge — and unknowingly freed something ancient.

**Morgen Hund and the Sanguine Curse.** War hero Morgen Hund descended into the necropolis with a battalion and never returned alive. Weeks later, he rose from beneath the city as a vampire — starving, furious, and betrayed. He slaughtered Ostoya's leadership, drove the Bürach armies back across the Grey Spine, and gave the sovereignty of Ostoya to the vampires who rose with him. He reinvented himself as Klaus Vorgen the Betrayed. His current fate is unknown.

**The Crimson Court.** Seven vampires now rule Ostoya under Grand Duke Drago Koshevek. They are allied only by mutual self-interest. Each governs a province and answers to Koshevek, who has organized everything to ensure they cannot threaten him without first destroying each other.

## Vampiric Bloodlines

The sanguine curse fractures into distinct lineages: **Soman** (elegant, manipulative, play at being benevolent sovereigns); **Upyr** (ancient, wretched corpse-forms with no pretense left, only thirst); **Fzeg** (the unholy fusion of lycanthropy and vampirism — feral, unstoppable, barely able to stop feeding); **Strigoi** (pallid, spider-limbed stalkers who feed on flesh and bone, cunning but impatient with pretense); **Ecoukrylo** (bat-form shapeshifters with echolocation, quirky collectors, excellent at wearing mortal disguises).

## Major Conflicts

**The Raevo Rebellion.** Raevo province seceded and now fights a civil war to liberate Ostoya. Its governing magocracy in the fortress-city of Castalore is the primary organized resistance. Raevo armies besiege Fallowheart; their agents ferment rebellion in occupied provinces.

**The Blood Tax.** The Crimson Court's most intimate tyranny: all subjects owe blood. The wealthy can substitute servants or criminals; the poor give from their own veins. Even death isn't escape — the undead are put to work.

**Intra-Court Scheming.** The six lesser members of the Crimson Court are in permanent cold war with each other and with Koshevek. Alliances form and collapse. Failures are punished with death. The Grand Duke deliberately places weak or dangerous nobles in strategic positions to keep all others off-balance.

## Key NPCs

**Grand Duke Drago Koshevek** — the most ancient and powerful soman vampire in Ostoya, cold as marble and deliberately cruel. He existed before the first migrants crossed the Grey Spine. He made Morgen Hund into a vampire and pointed him at Ostoya's old rulers like a weapon. Every action Koshevek has taken since waking appears calculated across decades; his patience suggests a plan millennia in the making.

**Baron Mihael, "The Wolf"** — fzeg ruler of Bezcodru, the one member of the Court who comes closest to genuine loyalty and honor. Koshevek favors him, which breeds resentment in all the other nobles. Mihael builds roads, deploys troops efficiently, and hunts personally in the Stillborn Forest. His loyalty is to strength and the pack.

**Countess Augustine Vasinesca** — ecoukrylo ruler of Terinima, the "Blood Sommelier." Eccentric, chittering, obsessive about blood flavor. She maintains Terinima's farming operations to ensure the Court has livestock (humanoids) well-fed and available. Surprisingly charming despite compulsive behavior.

**Marchioness Iulia Cutov** — deliberately insulted with rulership of the worthless Black Mire province. Uses it as cover to run a secret double game: outwardly patrolling the coast for the Grand Duke, privately holding secret talks with Raevo and other nations, promising access to Ostoya's wealth in exchange for help destroying her fellow Court members. She wants sole rule of the Empire.

**Mina Taliesin, "Lady of Masks"** — First Minister of Raevo's Council of Mages for decades, always masked (officially from an assassination attempt, but theorized otherwise). Vast spy network, highly placed contacts even within Soma. Her goal is to destroy the Crimson Court without trading it for Bürach conquest — she moves cautiously, plays long games.

## Featured City: Fallowheart

A city caught in the jaws of conflict — squeezed between the Bürach Empire to the west, the Crimson Court to the south, and Raevo's armies to the north. Fallowheart is the emotional heart of Ostoya: the place where freedom was first defended, now occupied by a monster in noble clothing. The dark forests press against the city walls like fingers trying to claw their way in. The river crashes around the crag of Kaer Vena, the pre-civilization fortress that has always watched over the city, now housing the Viscount.

The architecture is gothic — steep roofs, arched windows, skeletal bas-reliefs on every surface — a Büracher immigrant aesthetic merged with the bone-and-gargoyle stylings of Kaer Vena. The city is tense, the streets watchful, the population split between survival and simmering rebellion.

### Notable Locations

**Kaer Vena** — the ancient black castle on a river crag, built by an unknown pre-migration civilization decorated with skeleton murals and sunburst motifs. The Viscount's seat, virtually impregnable. Inside: a cryptlike labyrinth of rotting doors, narrow passages, and centuries of forgotten heraldry collecting dust.

**Saint Dayla's Cathedral** (Cerest hill) — candlelit, singing, and guarded by Raevo soldiers who can do little beyond chase vandals. The cathedral is a beacon of resistance, a gathering point for the Arch Seraph faithful, and secretly the eyes and ears of Raevo's army — its congregation is ready to pour into the streets when the time comes.

**The Violet Rose** — an upmarket taphouse that functions as a pay-to-avoid-your-blood-tax operation. Wealthy families send servants or employees to be fed upon by the Viscount's inner circle, in exchange for being skipped over in the official collection.

**The Marla** (Grinfield) — the city's largest inn and taphouse, five stories, constantly full of locals and travelers. The top floor rooms are avoided by everyone who knows the city.

### Key Individuals

**Viscount Langkirk Warlow** — a strigoi with a needle-thin face, bulging yellowed eyes, and a crooked spine that bends double over his cane. The frailty is an illusion; he can crush flesh and splinter bone when he bothers to exercise his strength. Deeply insecure about his subordinate position in the Crimson Court, he compensates with manic cruelty and obsessive performance of civility. He playacts at dinner parties while blood drips from his fingers.

**Sister Abigail Florentine** — the young, unconsecrated acolyte who stepped up after her cathedral's priest was arrested for sedition. Publicly preaches only kindness and law; privately coordinates with Raevo soldiers and knows exactly what she is building toward.

**Edward Vilgé** — head of the Ebon Syndicate in Fallowheart, a career thief with no ideology beyond coin. Has ended up supporting Raevo not from principle but because the Volitant (Crimson Court enforcers) are hunting his people. Will deal with anyone who keeps him out of the Viscount's fangs.

## Featured City: The Free City of Malkovia

A mercantile city split by the Roiling River — Northside sympathetic to Raevo, Southside sympathetic to the Court — Malkovia has maintained independence through political canniness and violence. It is a city of slick streets, cramped alleys, constant commerce, and crushing poverty for the common folk while the powerful extract maximum profit. Both Raevo and the Crimson Court pressure it constantly. Its leadership holds the line by staying neutral, but that neutrality is eroding.

### Notable Locations

**The Ossuary Bridge** — one of seven river crossings, lined with the bones of criminals as a warning that Malkovia enforces its own law.

**The Clocktowers** — three towers that have recently stopped ringing; commoners have gone missing around them. The gnomish artificer Ciprian Johann, obsessive owner, has been quietly transforming, his body grafted with daemonic machinery.

**Hunters Guildhall** (Northside) — the institutional base for Malkovia's monster hunters, now openly killing vampires who cross the river and displaying the corpses as defiance.

### Key Individuals

**Matriarch Mariska Király** — a former street urchin turned monster hunter turned coup leader. Took Malkovia by militia force when the previous rulers lost the faith of the people, then dismantled trade regulations to flood the city with wealth. Northside loves her; Southside considers her a thug. Surrounded by subordinates who would rather hand the city to Soma or Raevo — or simply replace her.

**Father Elijah** — a fallen seraph turned vampire who now preaches that vampirism is a divine gift from a buried god. His crowds grow throughout both districts. He performs genuine miracles (remnant divine sparks blended with vampiric power), distributes food from Teriniman farms, and weaves people into transcendent communal song before promising them immortality. His sermons are preparing the city to be delivered to the Crimson Court — unless he decides to take it for himself.
