# Chapter 16: Dark Fantasy Rules

Creating campaigns, adventure, and encounters that promote a dark fantasy feel can be difficult with the core rules of fifth edition. Those rules present and encourage campaigns where the characters are powerful and capable compared to the threats that they face. Frequent rests and resource renewal, forgiving encounter design, and a predictable encounter-rest loop: these elements make for a very stable and predictable game that but push against the essence of what makes the dark fantasy genre special.

The following suggested rules can be used to bring the joyful terror of that genre into a campaign.

## Alternative Rest System

Rests are crucial for characters to regain their resources, including Hit Points, class features, and spell slots. For players seeking a grittier or more challenging campaign experience, this alternative rest system replaces the rest mechanics found in the core fifth edition rules. Any references to Short or Long Rests elsewhere in the core rules now refer to these rules.

### Quick Rest

A Quick Rest is a 1-hour period of relaxation, where a creature does nothing more strenuous than reading, talking, eating, or standing watch. A Quick Rest can occur anywhere. To start a Quick Rest, you must have at least 1 Hit Point.

#### Benefits of a Quick Rest

When you finish the Quick Rest, you gain the following benefits:

Spend Hit Point Dice. You can spend one or more of your Hit Point Dice to regain Hit Points. For each Hit Point Die spent in this way, roll the Hit Point Die and add your Constitution modifier to it. You regain Hit Points equal to the total (minimum of 1 Hit Point). You can decide to spend an additional Hit Point Die after each roll.

#### Interrupting a Quick Rest

A Quick Rest is stopped by the following interruptions:

- Rolling Initiative
- Casting a spell other than a cantrip
- Taking any damage

An interrupted Quick Rest confers no benefits and must be restarted to gain any benefits.

### Short Rest

A Short Rest is an extended period of rest—at least 8 hours—available to any creature. During a Short Rest, you sleep for at least 6 hours and perform no more than 2 hours of light activity, such as reading, talking, eating, or standing watch.

A Short Rest can occur in any location where you can sleep without interruption. This might take place in a dangerous location that has been temporarily secured and guarded. During sleep, you have the Unconscious condition. After you finish a Short Rest, you must wait at least 8 hours before starting another one or starting a Long Rest.

#### Benefits of a Short Rest.

To start a Short Rest, you must have at least 1 Hit Point. When you finish the rest, you gain the following benefits:

Regain Half Hit Point Dice. You regain a number of spent Hit Point Dice equal to half of your total number of Hit Point Dice (minimum of 1). For example, if you have eight Hit Point Dice, you regain up to four spent Hit Point Dice.

Spend Hit Point Dice. After you regain Hit Point Dice, you can spend one or more Hit Point Dice to regain Hit Points. For each Hit Point Die you spend in this way, roll the die and add your Constitution modifier to it. You regain Hit Points equal to the total (minimum of 1 Hit Point). You can decide to spend an additional Hit Point Die after each roll.

Exhaustion Reduced. If you have the Exhaustion condition, its level decreases by 1.

Special Feature. Some features are recharged by a Short Rest. If you have such a feature, it recharges in the way specified in its description.

#### Interrupting a Short Rest

A Short Rest is stopped by the following interruptions:

- Rolling Initiative
- Casting a spell other than a cantrip
- Taking any damage

An interrupted Short Rest confers no benefits. The interrupted Short Rest must be restarted from the beginning to gain its benefits. If the interrupted Short Rest goes for at least 1 hour, the party can treat it as a Quick Rest instead, but they cannot take another Quick Rest as part of this interrupted Short Rest.

### Long Rest

A Long Rest is a period of extended downtime—at least 32 hours—available to any creature. During a Long Rest, you sleep for at least two 6-hour periods separated by 16 hours of light activity, such as reading, talking, eating, crafting, trading, or performing downtime activities.

A Long Rest can only occur in safe locations that provide shelter, warmth, comfort, and resources like food and water. This could be a settlement, homestead, stronghold, inn, castle, or large city. A Long Rest cannot be completed in areas of discomfort or danger, such as a wilderness, dungeon, alley, or sewer. If the location is dangerous enough that characters feel the need to post watch, it cannot accommodate a Long Rest.

During sleep, you have the Unconscious condition. After you finish a Long Rest, you must wait at least 16 hours before starting another one or a Short Rest.

#### Benefits of a Long Rest

To start a Long Rest, you must have at least 1 Hit Point. When you finish the rest, you gain the following benefits:

Regain All HP. You regain all lost Hit Points and all spent Hit Point Dice. If your Hit Point maximum was reduced, it returns to normal.

Ability Scores Restored. If any of your ability scores were reduced, they return to normal.

Exhaustion Recovered. If you have the Exhaustion condition, its level decreases to 0.

Grievous Wounds Recovered. If you have one or more Grievous Wounds, you or another creature can attempt to treat them. See Grievous Wounds, below, for details.

Special Feature. Some features are recharged by a Long Rest. If you have such a feature, it recharges in the way specified in its description.

#### Interrupting the Long Rest

A Long Rest is stopped by the following interruptions:

- Rolling Initiative
- Casting a spell other than a cantrip
- Taking any damage
- Leaving the location of relative safety

If you rested at least 8 hours, including at least 6 hours of sleep, before the interruption, you gain the benefits of a Short Rest. You can only take 1 Short Rest during an attempt of a Long Rest.


## Grievous Wounds

A consistent theme throughout dark fantasy and related genres is the fragility of life and the consequences of actions. While the roleplaying ramifications of a party’s decision can be highlighted through story outcomes, combat ramifications can be a little harder to implement.

Grievous Wounds add grit and realism to combat encounters, bringing the consequences of a difficult fight to the forefront of storytelling.

### Implementing Grievous Wounds

Grievous Wounds come into play the first time a character is reduced to 0 Hit Points after finishing a Long or Short Rest. When a character is reduced to 0 Hit Points, they can choose to be at 1 HP instead. If they choose this, they roll 2d10 and add them together. Then they subtract from the result the number of existing Grievous Wounds they have. A GM can choose to offer this option to the character whenever they feel it is appropriate, after the first time. With that result, consult the Grievous Wound table. If a player rolls the same result as one they already have a wound for, they skip this result and select the next lowest result they do not have as a wound. Results with a * next to them can be taken more than once. Nozomi Matsouka

For example, if the modified result is a 6, and the character already has the Grievous Wound listed as result 6, they gain the Grievous Wound listed as result 5.

Upon completing a Long Rest, if the character or someone helping them succeeds at a Wisdom (Medicine) check, a player can remove all effects from Grievous Wounds, but not Permanent Wounds. The DC for the Ability Check equals 10 plus the number of Grievous Wounds the character has. A character with a Healer’s Kit automatically succeeds the check by expending one use of a Healer’s Kit.

## New Conditions

Confrontation

The following new conditions are used by certain effects in the Grim Hollow rules. They follow the same rules as other conditions in the 2024 fifth edition rules.

### Bleeding

If you have the Bleeding condition, you cannot regain Hit Points except by spending Hit Point Dice or completing a Long Rest. The condition ends when a creature finishes a Quick, Short, or Long Rest.


### Corruption

While you have the Corruption condition, you experience the following effects.

Heroic Corruption. You cannot have Heroic Inspiration or give it to another player character who lacks it.

Corruption Levels. This condition is cumulative. Each time you receive it, you gain 1 Corruption level. If your Corruption level is 6, you either die or transform into a creature of the GM's choosing, under their control.

Removing Corruption Levels. The Greater Restoration, Heal, or Wish spell removes all of your Corruption levels and ends the condition.

### Dazed

If you have the Dazed condition, you cannot maintain Concentration.

## Great Beast Mutations

The shadow of the Great Beast first rose within the Bürach Empire soon after the assassination of Emperor Leopold I. Stories of unspeakable horror began to spread throughout the realm. Although not every sighting of the Great Beast can be confirmed as real, and some consider it heresy to discuss the existence of something so dreadful, no one can deny the mark of the Great Beast’s presence.

In areas where the Beast is said to have wandered, nature itself shudders in disgust and revulsion. Plant and animal life mutates, turning the flora and fauna into twisted aberrations. Even the most benign plants and animals become poisonous, physically corrupted, and aggressively hostile.

This change also affects people as well. Those who hear the Great Beast’s trumpeting shriek, or explore places it has recently visited, risk changing completely and permanently into horrific creatures like gnolls or rougaugh. Such horrors further spread death and chaos. Even folk not entirely transformed suffer terrible mutations, temporarily altering their bodies and minds, if they survive an encounter with the Great Beast at all.

### Encountering the Great Beast

The Great Beast is not a mere monster with a stat block and a set of limited abilities. This is because the Beast is as much an idea as a creature. Evil. Corruption. The impulse towards violence and destruction. The inevitability of annihilation. Gods and mortals alike can defeat almost anything if they are lucky, skilled, and powerful enough. But they can’t outlast the sad truth that all things end. That’s what the Beast represents.

The presence of the Great Beast is a forbidding backdrop for a dark fantasy campaign where the world can never truly be saved. Yet by its very nature, it’s difficult to tell a satisfying story where the central characters confront the Great Beast directly. Doing so leads to almost certain death. Instead, an encounter with the Great Beast is a fight in the streets of a city where the citizens are clawing at each other whilst transmogrifying into gnolls. It’s fleeing through a forest with aberrant beasts snapping at your heels, trying to escape the Great Beast’s mutating aura.

A direct encounter with the Great Beast could be an epic climax to a grimdark campaign where the player characters were never expected to survive. Or it could be a prologue to a story set in the Bürach Empire, where the characters become monsters that are later encountered by the true heroes of the story. Deciding to feature an actual Great Beast encounter means you should ensure the players know the experience they’re in for. This is a foe from which they must escape or perish.

While encountering the Great Beast or some other mutative effect, each character must make frequent Mutation checks. The frequency of each Mutation check is up to the GM. The closer the characters come to the Great Beast (or it to them), the more frequently the Mutation checks should be required—even as often as the start of each combat round. The farther the characters manage to escape from the Great Beast, the less frequently a Mutation check is required, possibly only the start of each scene or encounter.

Once the characters have fled far enough from the Beast, they can stop making Mutation checks entirely. Remember that the Great Beast is huge and ethereal. The GM must decide the means by which the characters can escape the Beast’s presence, either by defeating groups of monsters obstructing them, accomplishing tasks like opening a city gate, or clever thinking such as the use of a Teleport spell or utilizing underground tunnels.

The deaths of characters in the Great Beast’s presence should be spectacular, epic, and dark. Allow players to have final words as their character is horrifically eviscerated or transmogrified. If a character transforms into another creature, either by reaching level 6 of the Corruption condition or otherwise, the GM should consider allowing the player to control the resulting monster, challenging their former allies in one final battle.

### Dangers of Magic

The Great Beast is not the only source of eldritch, mutative power in Etharis. Mages are hunted in Castinella because an untamed spell can yield similarly horrific consequences. The Coldfire Crisis in Valika sweeps through regions transforming the land. Fickle faerie magic in Charneault has caused its share of strife and pain. The cursed magical diseases of Liesech may manifest in something other than the Weeping Pox. While this table is titled Great Beast Mutations, any of those other powers and afflictions could result in similar consequences for a creature.

Some rules and effects specifically call for a Mutation check. In addition, the GM can require a Mutation check whenever a character is exposed to powerful magic, curses, creatures, or other effects that put them in contact with eldritch horrors or unknowable forces.

Here are some examples of when a Mutation check could be relevant and evocative:

- When a character fails a saving throw to avoid an effect created by a powerful creature who uses or was created by transformative magic.
- When a caster loses concentration on a spell.
- When a creature would receive a condition it already has.
- When a deal with a powerful immortal being is broken.
- When a creature from the Material Plane travels travels to another plane of existence.
- When a shocking or devastating event occurs outside of combat.
- When a character with two failed Death Saving Throws succeeds on a Death Saving Throw.

### Mutation Checks

When a Mutation check is required, the character must make a Constitution saving throw, with a DC equal to 8 plus the character's level. The more power flowing through a creature, the more likely they are to succumb to mutation.

On a failed save, the character gains 1 Corruption level and rolls 1d20 on the Great Beast Mutations table. If the result is a mutation that character already has, ignore the result and roll again.

A Mutation lasts until the character has no Corruption levels, or until they finish a Long Rest.

## Diseases and Afflictions

Diseases hold an odd position in fantasy worlds. They are meant to be as dangerous as real-world epidemics; however, magical cures and remedies are readily available to characters at lower levels, making them difficult to use as either an interesting game mechanic or a viable storytelling hook or plot device. In fact, 2024 5e rules barely mention diseases as a game element.

Grim Hollow, however, wants diseases to be a relevant—and even crucial—factor in adventures and campaigns set in Etharis. The Weeping Pox is an important piece of lore in Etharis’s history, so to eliminate them completely flies in the face of everything Grim Hollow is about.

As a way of making diseases, contagions, and afflictions a viable tool for GMs to tell stories, use the following rules.

### Natural Diseases

Bitten

Various naturally occurring viruses and bacteria regularly rampage through Etharis, causing both isolated cases of a disease as well as more dire epidemics. These diseases, however, can be combated with normal medicine, as well as eradicated by magic. Sometimes the symptoms of even the most contagious and debilitating disease can be managed with lower-level magic, or cured completely with spells such as Lesser Restoration.

#### Rules for Natural Diseases

For a standard, nonmagical disease or affliction, an exposed creature can make a DC 10 Constitution saving throw. For harsher diseases, the DC could increase as necessary to enforce the deadly nature of the disease.

On a successful save, the character might show very mild symptoms but suffer no other game-mechanically relevant effects. On a failed save, the character gains 1 Exhaustion level.

After two days have passed, the character can repeat the saving throw. On a failed save, they gain another Exhaustion level. If they did not complete a Long Rest over that 2-day stretch, they have Disadvantage on the check. On a success, they have resisted the disease.

This process continues until the character has either resisted the disease by succeeding on a saving throw, been cured via medicine or magic, or die from gaining 6 Exhaustion levels.

A DM can determine the ways in which Medicine checks or other healing efforts might succeed. It’s important to let characters with proficiency in skills or tools use those avenues for healing.

### Magical Diseases and Contagions

The Core Rules for 2024 fifth edition have rules and examples of magical contagions, but the rules here could supplement them to show the particularly deadly place these contagions hold in Etharis.

Magical diseases and contagions have a similar effect and method of resolution to natural diseases. However, there may be affects in addition to Exhaustion levels. The DC for resisting the magical diseases is 15 instead of 10, and typical magic or rest cannot remove Exhaustion levels.

The other rules for suffering from and resting the magical diseases and contagions should follow the pattern set in the previous section.

### Curse Diseases and Afflictions

Even more virulent, pervasive, and deadly are curse diseases and afflictions. These are not just magical in nature, they are harder to shake and have more potentially dire consequences if not healed in a reasonable time. The Weeping Pox is an example of a curse disease that is highly unlikely to be cured easily, even with magic.

Unlike the lesser varieties of diseases and magical contagions, curse diseases and afflictions cannot be cured by Lesser Restoration or other lower-level spells or powers. Magic on the level of Wish is needed to even have a chance of curing such afflictions, and even then it is not a sure thing.

#### The Weeping Pox

An example of a curse disease in Etharis is the Weeping Pox. A person subjected to the disease must succeed on a DC 20 Constitution saving throw once each day they are exposed. If they succeed, they are safe for 24 hours. On a failed save, they have the affliction and begin to show symptoms within 24 hours.

At first a sick person will be subject to fever causing sharpened senses, overexcitement, and a sense of wellbeing. For the first three days, creatures suffering from the Weeping Pox must succeed on a DC 20 Constitution saving throw each day. If any of those saving throws fail, the creature reaches critical stages at the end of those 72 hours.

After 72 hours, these original symptoms transition to delirium, burning fever, insatiable thirst, and weltering lesions that have given the illness its name. Beyond this, nearly all who contract the disease die withing the next 72 hours. Most people succumb to the pox within the week, while the most resilient may hold on for almost two weeks.

## Creating Legendary Foes

When creating encounters, it is often situationally appropriate to pit the party against a singular enemy. However, what is meant to be a dangerous and climactic boss fight sometimes ends as a one-way romp, not presenting a challenge for the players. This is largely due to the single creature taking far fewer actions per round of combat than the party. Legendary templates are designed to assist in this process and help make non-legendary creatures present a greater threat.


### Legendary Templates

To implement a legendary template, select a monster that does not already have Legendary Actions. This monster should be CR 3 or higher, and it should be one that you want to be a difficult challenge for the characters to overcome.

Next, select a template from the options below. Add the Legendary Actions listed to the monster’s stats. You should select a template that fits the existing abilities of the monster, such as the Spellcaster template for a Mage or a Commander for a Hobgoblin Captain.

## Heroism and Corruption

Though the gods of Etharis are gone, the weave of Fate still governs the lives of its inhabitants. Without oversight, however, that once-indomitable force has become unstable and susceptible to outside influence. The interplay between heroism and corruption plays an important role in the lives of the people and creatures of Etharis. This is handled by Resolve Dice and Beast Dice.

While playing in Grim Hollow, the players and the GM each have a pool of six-sided dice to draw upon in order to shift Fate in their favor. The players’ dice are known as Resolve Dice, while the GM’s pool consists of Beast Dice. The dice in each pool can be spent to activate a number of different abilities, representing the ways in which Fate has been altered for good or for ill.

At the start of a campaign, each pool contains a single d6, but events during the game add or remove dice from the pools. Also, at the end of each session, if either pool has at least 2 fewer dice than the other, the GM should add one d6 to that pool. This represents Fate attempting to correct the imbalance.

The size of each dice pool is known to the GM and players at all times. This allows players to consider the dice pools when plotting their next course of action.

The dice pools are also great tools for storytelling and roleplaying. For example, as dice are added to the Beast Pool, NPCs likely grow wearier or more scared by current events. News may reach the party of a supernatural phenomenon that has locals on edge, or an informant might warn of increased monster attacks in the area. Omens and portents foretell disaster. Monsters can appear more twisted, and even the environment itself might become more disturbing, all to represent the growing threat.

By contrast, a growing Resolve Pool can spur a more positive attitude in the characters. Confidence and good humor should increase while the Resolve Pool outweighs the Beast Pool, as characters grasp a rare and fleeting moment of optimism.

### Resolve Pool

The characters in a Grim Hollow campaign face a world of overwhelming odds. In order to triumph, they must draw upon deep personal reservoirs of courage and willpower. This is represented by the Resolve Pool, a special pool of six-sided dice that the players can use to thwart Fate and seize victory from the jaws of defeat.

The Resolve Pool starts with a single d6. Events happening within the game can add dice to that pool, up to a maximum of six d6. The GM award Resolve Dice to the party based on their actions and at specific plot moments within the game. If a player accurately and faithfully represents their character’s heroic attributes in difficult circumstances, it’s appropriate for the GM to awards a Resolve Die based on their roleplaying.

Furthermore, events or actions that dramatically bolster the party's determination can also add a Resolve Die to the pool. These rare instances of self-sacrifice, kindness, and charity provide a light in the darkness, helping the party to forge ahead and achieve success.

Finally, if the characters manage to heroically overcome a horrific event that had added a die to the Beast Pool (see below), add a die to the Resolve Pool. Overcoming dire challenges is the surest way to gain courage.

#### Resolve Abilities

Once there are dice in the Resolve Pool, the party can draw upon them to activate special abilities throughout the game. Resolve Abilities can only be activated by spending dice from the Resolve Pool, and each ability has a certain cost as detailed in the description. Feel free to come up with your own list of abilities for the players to use their Resolve Dice on, but certain examples may include the following:

Hasty Recovery. The character spends 1 Resolve Die during a Short Rest to remove 1 Exhaustion level. Once during that rest, the character may roll the Resolve Die and add it to a saving throw they make.

Inner Strength. When a character would expend a Hit Point Die, they may instead spend 1 Resolve Die, rolling and using its result if necessary.

Final Push. After a character rolls a Death Saving Throw, they may spend 2 Resolve Dice. Roll both dice and add the result to the triggering Death Save.

I’ve Just the Thing. During their turn, a character may spend any number of Resolve Dice. They immediately gain a non-magical item whose GP cost does not exceed 10 times the number of Resolve Dice spent.

Blaze of Glory. During their turn, a character can spend 6 Resolve Dice to instill heroic might into up to 6 creatures within 120 feet. Until the end of that character's next turn, each target has Advantage on D20 Tests and has Resistance to all types of damage. In addition, damage rolls by each affected character are doubled.

### Beast Pool

Transformation

The Great Beast is a powerful supernatural entity that stalks the lands of Etharis, and the Beast Pool represents how its influence twists the weave of Fate to oppose the characters' efforts. The Beast Pool begins with a single d6, but certain callous, evil, or horrific actions and events add more dice to represent the growing threat. Like the Resolve Pool, the Beast Pool can hold a maximum of six d6.

Ironically, the characters themselves may be the most significant cause for a growing Beast Pool. Whenever a character takes an action that darkens the tone of the game, the GM may add another d6 to the Beast Pool. Such actions include killing an innocent creature, choosing selfishness or callousness at the expense of others, working to undermine the common good, and so forth.

Horrific events can also add dice to the Beast Pool. When the party encounters a particularly strong evil creature, or when they witness something extremely disturbing, the GM can add a d6 to the Beast Pool. Reserve this for rare moments that truly shake the resolve or threaten the psyches of the characters. Simple fear effects and the Frightened condition aren't enough to increase the Beast Pool.

#### Spending Beast Dice

The GM can spend Beast Dice to hinder characters or bolster enemies, altering the balance of encounters at critical moments. This option is best used to build tension and raise the stakes of an important encounter, rather than as a blunt tool to simply defeat the party.

Here are some sample effects the GM can use or adapt:

Bitter Disposition. The GM may spend one Beast Die in order to decrease the starting disposition of an NPC from Friendly to Indifferent, or from Indifferent to Hostile, in order to make the scene more memorable or challenging.

Death Rattle. When a character is reduced to 0 Hit Points, the GM may spend one Beast Die and roll it. On a 5 or 6, that character immediately suffers a Death Saving Throw failure.

Outnumbered. The GM may spend any number of Beast Dice at the start of an encounter to increase the number of creatures facing the party. For each die spent, the GM may add an additional creature with Challenge Rating no greater than half the party’s average level, rounded down.

Affliction. Before or during a session, the GM can spend 4 Beast Dice to inflict a serious ailment upon a character or significant NPC important to the party. The target gains a Shadowsteel curse, a cursed affliction, or a Transformation of the GM's choice. Before applying a Transformation to a character, check with the player to ensure this is acceptable.

The Beast. Before or during a session, the GM may spend 6 Beast Dice to have the Great Beast manifest near the characters' location. The effects of its presence are soon felt throughout the region, perhaps as an onslaught of vicious gnolls or the transformation of a forest into a writhing nest of corrupted plants. Alternatively, this option may be used to represent a different major and lasting threat to the local populace, such as an outbreak of coldfire in the Valikan Clans or the arrival of a Crimson Court member in Ostoya.

### Etharis Abilities

Apart from Resolve Abilities and Beast Abilities, there are a set of abilities that can be activated with dice drawn from either pool. Both the GM and the party have access to Etharis Abilities, which represent more general methods of shaping the whims of Fate in your favor.

Minor Fate. Roll one die and add the total to a single D20 Test after the check has been made but before the result is known.

Bolster. Roll two dice and increase the Hit Point maximum of a creature by the total for eight hours.

Brutal Strike. Whenever a creature is hit by an attack roll, the attacker may spend two dice and inflict a condition on the target for one round. This condition may be Bleeding, Dazed, or Stunned.

Stroke of Luck. By spending two dice, the GM or a player may cause a minor alteration to the environment, such as adding a small item or feature, moving an existing object five feet, or slightly altering weather conditions or lighting. Examples would be a dropped weapon rolling out of reach or suggesting that phosphorescent fungi might bring a cave’s lighting up to dim light rather than total darkness.
