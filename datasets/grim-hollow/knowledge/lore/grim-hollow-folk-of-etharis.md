# Folk of Etharis

Humans dominate Etharis. The elder peoples -- dragonborn, dwarves, elves, gnomes, and halflings -- share human nations after the conquests of the Era of Expansion. Rarer and more unsettling heritages also exist, though their presence often provokes fear, suspicion, and violence from common folk. Mutative magic prevalent throughout Etharis means physiological traits can differ greatly even within species.

### Dragonborn

Dragonborn bear draconic features -- jagged scales, talons, fearsome jaws, and dragon's breath. Their lineage may predate all others in Etharis. When humans first reached Castinella during the Era of Expansion, they found not a thriving civilization but the hollow remnants of one -- the granite metropolis of Ember Cairn already fallen to ruin. The dragonborn themselves could not recall what calamity had befallen them. Their gods were silent.

As humans settled Castinella, the dragonborn abandoned their territories to wander Etharis seeking divine answers. Those who remained were receptive to human missionaries who spoke of the Aetheric War and the Divine Seraphs. A terrible revelation took hold: their gods had not abandoned them but had been obliterated by the Aether Kindred. This epiphany transformed the dragonborn into the most fervent believers in the Eternal Dogma.

Castinella granted them dominion over a fragment of their ancestral lands. They reconstructed Ember Cairn atop its ruins. Many dragonborn became clerics, missionaries, and inquisitors, spreading their adopted faith with zealotry matched only by the searing flames from their throats.

### Dwarves

Dwarves emerged in Etharis before recorded history, establishing realms within the Rock Teeth and Grey Spine mountains. They are most common throughout the Burach Empire and Valika.

During the Era of Expansion, dwarven clans retreated behind their greatest strongholds: the terraced citadel of Stehlenwald and the mountain fortress of Grabenstein. Grabenstein's walls eventually crumbled, its mines seized by human warlords who would later found the Burach Empire. Stehlenwald survived by delving deeper -- miners uncovered adamantine, and clad in impenetrable armor, dwarven battalions drove back the invaders. Victory came at terrible cost in lives.

When the dwarves emerged from seclusion, the world had transformed. The dwarf king negotiated a pact with the Burach Empire: relinquish territories and join the empire, but retain independent governance. Dwarves have since risen to prosperity throughout Etharis, their outward success masking generational scars. A common saying holds that one might sooner move a mountain than change a dwarf's mind.

### Elves

Elves were among the first to walk Etharis's primeval forests. Tall and ethereal, they claim descent from nature spirits. The dense woodlands of Caer Neiada -- now part of the Charneault Kingdom -- are their ancestral territories. They are still most commonly found in Charneault.

Centuries of unchallenged supremacy cultivated fatal arrogance. They dismissed humans as crude and ephemeral, and failed to perceive the rising threat until too late. The Era of Expansion shattered their kingdoms and reduced ancient forests to ruin. Forced into the deepest reaches of their woodlands, they clung to hope that the Spirits of Nature would shield them. When they eventually returned, they formed an uneasy pact with humanity that gave birth to the Charneault Kingdom -- compromise born of necessity rather than desire.

Elven grief now manifests in haunting art and soul-rending melodies of a lost golden age. They call themselves ulufey -- "mortal fey" and descendants of the fey sidhe. Many harbor the silent hope that the faerie courts might yet aid in restoring their lands.

### Gnomes

Restless of mind and slight of frame, gnomes share distant ancestry with dwarves. Their ceaselessly churning intellects have produced Etharis's most terrifying mechanical marvels -- cannons, clockwork automatons, flintlocks, and explosives. For gnomes, the fevered process of creation often eclipses consequence. A common saying warns that to locate a gnome, follow the trail of destruction to its smoking origin.

During the Era of Expansion, gnomes sheltered within Stehlenwald. While dwarven miners unearthed adamantine, gnomish alchemists perfected explosives. Victory came at grievous cost to both peoples. The gnomes followed their dwarven allies into centuries of isolation, and later into the Burach Empire.

Gnomes maintain an uncanny connection to the natural world. Those who did not seek mountain sanctuary felt drawn to ancient forests -- a peculiar affinity suggesting shared lineage with elves, though neither acknowledges such kinship. While common throughout the Burach Empire, they often seek solace within Charneault.

### Halflings

While other peoples earned human wrath through resistance during the Era of Expansion, halflings survived through calculated accommodation. They place little worth in material treasures, surrendering such trinkets as tribute. Their modest settlements became provisions for human empires. Halflings infiltrated human society as merchants, advisors, educators, and scholars -- roles deemed valuable, performed while privately abhorring humanity's brutality.

Halflings remain tenacious yet outwardly peaceable, seeking tranquil corners far from conflict. They nurture fascination for ancient lore, forbidden knowledge, and tales whispered in shadows. This apparent docility masks unexpected resilience and ferocity when cornered.

For generations, halflings have compiled fragments of wisdom into extensive private libraries -- centuries of accumulated knowledge concerning domestic crafts concealing secret purposes, whispers of nature, and histories unaltered by conquerors' blades.

### Humans

Humans now cast their shadow across all of Etharis, from Valika's tundra to Castinella's plains. Historical records first note their emergence from temperate woodlands in what would become the Burach Empire. Left unchecked, they multiplied with disquieting speed.

The Era of Expansion encompasses countless conflicts erupting in the wake of human migration. Humans sent settlers who felled ancient trees and claimed inhabited territories. By the time other kingdoms recognized the danger, containment was impossible. The slaughter forever altered Etharis.

Humanity's success stems from adaptability. Their brief lifespans cause customs to wither quickly, preventing entrapment by tradition. Every human culture bears marks of other heritages in claimed territories. In the present age, humans control vast swathes of land and sea. Among all their vices, ambition proves the most pernicious. The Era of Descent has witnessed the slow decay of each human domain -- with no more realms to subjugate, humanity turned its violence inward.

### Dreamers

Long before the Era of Expansion, another civilization rose to impossible heights before plummeting into oblivion. Beneath present-day Stehlenwald once stood the heart of a nameless empire thought to have eclipsed any that followed. Before catastrophe consumed their civilization, a secretive order of mystics known as dreamers used oneiromancy to sever themselves from time, suspending in perpetual slumber within the dream realm.

Their plan succeeded but at terrible cost. Millennia in the dream realm wrought irreversible changes -- upon waking, they could no longer distinguish dreams from memories. When dwarven miners broke through their sealed vaults, these dreamers stirred from ancient slumber.

Now predominantly found in Rock-Teeth Mountain settlements, they maintain uneasy coexistence with dwarves. Their prolonged slumber infused them with preternatural energy, allowing endurance beyond even dwarves. Sleep remains where dreamers feel most authentic -- they can slip into sudden slumber whenever no task demands attention. They may experience prophetic nightmares or perceive threats others cannot.

### Grudgels

Grudgels are descendants of the orcs, whose heritage is rooted in Etharis's frozen North. Ancient legends recount how the orcs abandoned their territories in a mass exodus, heeding some mysterious ancestral summons to sail westward across the sea. Those who carry orcish blood in present-day Etharis name themselves grudgels.

Grudgels remain imposing figures with undiminished physical prowess, but demonstrate no greater inclination toward violence than any other people. Among their ranks are artisans, wanderers, arcane wielders, and stargazers. They alone preserve the craft of forging stryllum -- a substance formed when starlight congeals into glass through jealously guarded processes.

Beyond the North, grudgels are uncommon enough that most encounter rumors of their kind before meeting one. All grudgels share profound reluctance to discuss their orc ancestors -- whether from desire to distance themselves from that legacy, or from secret knowledge regarding the orcs' disappearance.

### Laneshi

Far beneath Etharis's eastern shores lies the Llana'Shi Empire, home to laneshi -- pallid humanoids with writhing tendrils of kelp-like hair who commune with flora and fauna of their lightless domain. Their culture operates through stark absolutes and rigid caste hierarchy.

The mystic caste encompasses all laneshi born as twins. The first-born is inducted; the second is sacrificed through necromantic ceremony, their soul forcibly bound within the surviving sibling. Each mystic therefore houses two souls -- one living, one eternally trapped -- granting perception into the spirit realm and enhanced necromantic power. Mystics preside over ceremonies, crafting, and construction, aided by reanimated corpses.

The warrior caste handles martial, diplomatic, agricultural, and child-rearing duties. Rulers of the Llana'Shi Empire have begun directing their gaze toward the surface for purposes yet unclear -- perhaps fleeing some ancient deep-sea horror, or seeking territories to subjugate in service to an unspeakable master.

### Ogresh

Ogresh are imposing, reclusive beings who serve as wellsprings of knowledge for neighboring communities. Young ogresh develop slowly, spending decades in compulsive wandering before selecting permanent habitation near abundant resources and established settlements. In the second phase of life, they develop voracious, almost unnatural appetites -- a single ogresh could consume an entire village's surplus provisions.

Given this dependence on others for sustenance, many develop uncanny mastery of social manipulation. Some who have gazed into their deep-set eyes maintain that their capacity to extract insights possesses supernatural quality, gleaning secrets others would never willingly divulge.

### Accursed

Each accursed exists as a bloodline unto themselves -- forged through aberrant magical convergence, planar rifts, or as the final vestiges of a lineage presumed erased. The name reflects how the populace perceives them: walking omens, living aberrations whose existence violates the natural order. Unlike other heritages, the accursed rarely represent a collective people, instead standing as solitary anomalies.

### Arisen

Arisen are singular constructs born of arcane manipulation, obscure science, or the marriage of both. Incredibly rare, they are most often found in Ostoya and Stehlenwald where dark experimentation is commonplace. No arisen enters the world through natural birth -- each is meticulously assembled and granted consciousness. They are not undead, nor divinely restored. They are thinking beings with distinct personalities, yet the question haunts their existence: do they harbor souls? Their bodies consist predominantly of organic material with mechanical components to varying degrees.

### Dhampir

A dhampir bears the weight of vampiric taint -- a mortal half-damned, existing between life and undeath. They carry sinister aspects of vampiric progenitors yet retain enough mortality to avoid a vampire's most crippling weaknesses. Dhampir hunger for blood but are not wholly enslaved by this craving if their resolve holds.

Common folk view them as abominations; pure-blooded vampires despise them as diluted shadows of true power. Their life becomes an endless search for belonging. Some cling to humanity and suffer relentless guilt; others surrender to their darker nature and contend for position within Ostoya's vampire nobility. Vampires who exploit this relationship gain servants capable of walking in daylight and integrating with mortal communities.

### Disembodied

When the mages of Ulmyr's Gate attempted to establish a permanent threshold to the Ethereal Plane, their spellwork rent reality and the entire city was cast into the void between worlds. Years later, spectral figures began appearing where Ulmyr's Gate once stood -- survivors trapped in liminal space between dimensions, desperately maintaining tenuous connection to the Material Plane.

Those who have returned bear semblance to their former selves but are simultaneously more and less than human. Their eyes reflect glimpses of the void, and in moments of distress their forms may flicker or partially fade. They seldom speak of their city's annihilation.

### Downcast

At the culmination of the Gods' End, thousands of divine souls fell from the sky like burning stars. These celestial beings, condemned to mortal life and bereft of their powers, are called the downcast. Some still serve the Arch Seraphs seeking to recover lost divine order. Others have accepted mortal existence. Still others follow the Arch Daemons, embittered by their fall.

Downcast retain traces of angelic form -- faintly glowing eyes, faded Celestial runes on their skin. Those who embraced daemonic corruption might present cracked skin, horns, or a faint scent of sulfur. Other mortals who recognize what they were react with pity, hope, or fear.

### Wechselkind

A wechselkind is a construct fashioned from wood, clay, and porcelain in the semblance of a child, animated through faerie enchantments and shrouded in illusion to replace a stolen mortal infant. When the glamour dissolves, they are typically banished or destroyed. Those shown mercy discover that while their minds develop naturally, they remain eternally imprisoned in unchanging childlike form.

With the spread of the Weeping Pox, many wechselkind emerged from exile -- their immunity to disease enabled them to assist healers, earning grudging respect. Yet they remain haunted by certainty that once their utility expires, abandonment follows -- eternal children with ancient eyes, belonging fully to neither the mortal world nor the faerie realm.

### Wulven

Wulven are natural offspring or descendants of those cursed with lycanthropy, inheriting not the full curse but its feral essence. They cannot assume animal forms and suffer neither loss of control nor bloodthirst under the full moon. The untamed nature within drives most toward wilderness isolation.

Where they traverse forest and scrubland, their wild countenance leads them to be mistaken for fey spirits, druids, or true lycanthropes. Many forge existences in druidic circles or as silent guardians of sequestered communities. Most exist as perpetual outsiders, wary that their subtly bestial features -- elongated canines, light-catching eyes, occasional low growls -- will be misread as signs of the curse they have been spared.
