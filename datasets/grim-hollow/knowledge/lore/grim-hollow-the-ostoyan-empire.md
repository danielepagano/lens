# The Ostoyan Empire

The sun never rises on the Ostoyan Empire. Ruled by an aristocracy of vampires and their servants, the Crimson Court governs the nation with their greedy claws, sharp eyes, and sharper fangs.

Located to the east of the Grey Spine, Ostoya was founded centuries ago by Bürach secessionists—migrants who fled the Bürach Empire’s authoritarian rule and the edicts of the Hearthkeepers. Ostoya was a new land anyone could call their own. Though in recent times, it has become a place as fractured and overwhelmed with monsters as their Bürach cousins across the western mountains.

Of its six provinces, Raevo has split from the Ostoyan Empire. It currently engages in a civil war to uphold that spirit of freedom which founded Ostoya. Raevo aims to push beyond its current borders to liberate other provinces from the blood-drenched grip of the Crimson Court.

Even within the provinces loyal to the Crimson Court, power is elusive. The members of the Court must remain vigilant to quell any further rebellion within their own regions of sovereignty. More importantly, they must keep an eye on each other. The machinations of the nobility run deep and seem to be as sustaining as the blood they drink.

For average citizens, life in Ostoya is dark and often terrifying. Peasants work the land, which is loaned to them by deathless masters, and pay crushing tithes on any modicum of wealth they might collect. But what the nobility ask for in return for social order and protection is far costlier than any coins. All who are ruled by the Crimson Court fear the blood tax.

There’s a particularly morbid Ostoyan peasant saying: “Only in death will your toils end.” But even that’s not true. After all, many of the dead in Ostoya are forced to toil further upon the bloody strings of their vampiric masters.

## Geography

The land of Ostoya was once beautiful and bountiful. Anything a burgeoning empire could need was within reach: navigable rivers, resource-rich mountains, mighty forests, and vast plains. Newly built roads connected the various towns that settled across the region, making trade and travel common despite the dark forests and dangerous swamps.

The Darkfall changed all that. Once eternal dusk struck the land, crops became hard to maintain. The perils of the swamps and forests spread. The dangerous natural beasts gave way to outright monsters. And people became less likely to travel and trade. What was a haven for many became a realm of nightmares.

### Forests

The dense forests of Ostoya could provide timber and game for its people, but those entering the woods are likely to become prey themselves. To the south, the Stillborn Forest grows thick and winding. Its trees, mostly yew and oak, grow wide-reaching branches and coiling roots, making travel slow and perilous. The Stillborn’s weaving canopy blots out the moon and stars, blinding the forest to any light.

The creatures that call this forest home prowl the dense terrain. Fools who traverse the forest with bright lanterns and rustling steps inadvertently advertise themselves as dinner. Even the most experienced hunters and woodsfolk run afoul of the horrors who call the Stillborn Forest home, many of which emerged during the Darkfall but are far too monstrous to playact as nobles.

North of Fallowheart, along the Grey-Spine Mountains, is the Sunstretch Forest. The redwoods and firs stand as massive grey sentinels that had once been witness to the sun’s warmth. Deep within the folds of those trees is the Solar Grove, tended by a circle of druids who renew hope that light may return to northern Ostoya in the coming days.

Separating central Ostoya from the Black Mire is a forest known by the locals as the Dark Wood. This region of thinning black trees bridges secessionist Raevo with the rest of the Empire, its sullen landscape scarred by crossed roads and host to an abundance of carnivorous plants.

### Waterways

The rivers that flow through Ostoya are essential to its trade and transportation. These deep veins of water, which coil across the entire nation, feed its settlements with fresh supplies and enable the rapid movement of troops and information that have been so vital during wartimes.

The river that runs from beneath Mt. Vengeance through Malkovia to the Raevan Bay is known as the Roiling River. It starts as scalding rapids heated by volcanic activity, cooling in temperature and speed as it travels eastward to the sea.

South of Fallowheart is Lake Iorta, which feeds into both the Tithewater River to the south and the Great Arteriyan River to the east. The Tithewater winds through the Stillborn Forest, connecting to the large Glasswater Lake and its portside city of Lunsk. The Great Arteriyan River bisects Ostoya, passing through Crowsbend before splitting near the Black Mire into smaller twisting currents.

Nov Ostoya, the empire’s capital city, was founded on the coast of what they would come to call the Black Bay, where a tremendous amount of naval trade and whaling is conducted. The bay is a critical feature of Ostoya; it is a gateway to, and a guardian against, the other nations of Etharis.

### Mountains

The Grey Spine Mountains act as a defensive border, a source of valuable resources, and a funnel for travel between the Ostoyan and Bürach Empires. There are several paths through the mountains from one nation to the other, though all are difficult and guarded. Immigrants, merchants, and wanderers who traverse the Grey Spine do so at great risk and expense to themselves.

The original Ostoyan migrants used the pass between Grabenstein and what would become Fallowheart, now called the Elian Pass. Still used today, it’s heavily guarded by both empires. Travelers through the pass in either direction are invasively inspected for contraband, often interrogated as potential spies, and—if permitted to pass—must pay a heavy tax.

Another passage to the far north is used less often. It’s simply known as the Valley of Mists, filled with a cold fog that never escapes from between the mountains.

This narrow valley slices through the Grey Spine north of Mt. Vengeance. It’s often completely covered by the towering cliffs above and in places it becomes a labyrinth of beckoning caves. The bones of lost travelers are frequently found within. Folk who manage to escape the valley report being racked with despair, believing they too would never find their way out from the shadow of the Grey Spine. Travelers who make the successful journey rarely decide to take their return trip the same way.

### Weather

Ignoring the weather in Ostoya can be deadly. Howling winds tear through the trees like screaming of banshees. Rain is a near daily occurrence, turning the black earth into a thick mire that sticks to boots and weighs down cloaks. Fog is common as well, ranging from wispy blankets that cling to the ground, to thick curtains that conceal the shapes of lurking predators. The unprepared often emerge from Ostoya’s wilderness drenched, muddy, slashed by branches, and exhausted from pelting rain.

Regardless of the weather, Ostoya never sees the sun. There are few still alive who remember its warmth and radiance. Most Ostoyans today have never been outside their home villages, and therefore have never seen the direct rays of the sun. The youngest generations make up stories of what they imagine it looks like. As a result, Ostoya’s fields are black and difficult to tend. Travelers being a saying across the Grey Spine; that warmth in Ostoya is like honesty in Altenheim.

Folks who travel from distant lands witness their days getting shorter as they approach Ostoya. By journeys end, the sun barely breaks the horizon before one day it simply never rises again. The exact cause of this darkness is a mystery. Yet one thing is for certain; the vampiric nobility that rule Ostoya revel in its presence.


## History

Ostoyan history has been written by the hand of vengeance, penned in blood.

Since the first migrants to flee the Bürach Empire dared to settle this land as their own, they’ve paid in lives to keep it. The Ostoyan Empire was founded as a haven; from war, crippling taxation, persecution, religious authority, and the oppression of magic. Those looking for a better life crossed the Grey Spine to build something new.

They enjoyed calm for a time, founding several settlements in loose connection around self-started industries of farming, lumber, and mining. They spread their settlements to the shores, founding Nov Ostoya on the natural harbor of Black Bay, which would become Ostoya’s capital and an international hub of seafaring trade.

Noting the bounties of this new land, the Bürach Empire decided to force Ostoyans into making a very simple choice: pay the imperial taxes or die. Ostoya refused to yield to such tyranny. Thus began the first war between the two empires.

Ostoya scrambled to muster forces as a young nation. The realm was not yet united beneath their own imperial identity, and they were ill-prepared for conflict. Yet the Bürach Empire could only assault Ostoya through the Elian Pass. And Ostoya’s provinces were rich with resources to feed their growing military.

The war was incredibly demanding for both sides. The victory of Ostoya is still sung today, having survived the mages of Erlefurt and black powder of Stehlenwald. It was at Fallowheart the Bürach Empire finally broke, the castle there withstanding sieges and battles whilst the rest of Ostoya rallied.

But Kaer Vena, the fortress of Fallowheart, was not built by Ostoyan hands. It existed long before any migrants had journeyed through the Grey Spine. These lands had once belonged to an earlier civilization.

Something ancient slept beneath the soil.

### The Darkfall

The second Bürach-Ostoyan war came during a time when the Bürach Empire was fracturing from within. In an attempt to stabilize its imperial identity, the Bürach made a common enemy of Ostoya for its people to hate.

Years of bloody back and forth unfolded, until a world-shaking event occurred. The Darkfall.

The entire realm shook. Ravines spread across Ostoya like opening veins. The sun turned orange, and like heated metal it began to drip from the sky like sparks of golden liquid falling beneath the horizon. Darkness crept into the land. From beneath the earth, like blood swelling to the surface of a wound, tides of undead monstrosities charged from the fissures to devour the living.

Nov Ostoya was the epicenter of this catastrophe. Beneath the city, a vast necropolis was revealed by the earthquakes. It teemed with undead like maggots through flesh. It pulsed with dark energy like a heartbeat beginning to awaken.

Acting decisively, the aristocracy of Nov Ostoya sent troops to investigate the necropolis. Panic led the leaders to order the rifts collapsed using magic, sealing their own troops beneath the earth. The only thing that this act sealed would be the fate of Ostoya.

### The Tragedy of Morgen Hund

Siraj Fakhri Morgen Hund Morgen Hund was a true champion of Ostoya. Born to traveling merchants, Hund was raised on the road. He traveled Ostoya with his parents, visiting every province and learning about its people. As he grew older, he learned to fight defending his family’s caravan from thieves and monsters. When war broke out with the Bürach Empire, Morgen enlisted as a loyal patriot. As the war dragged on, he proved his mettle and skill, slaying Büracher knights and mages while fellow soldiers died around him. Thrust into a position of leadership, Hund’s valor and hatred for the invaders made him popular among the Ostoyan troops. After Fallowheart was captured by the Bürach Empire, Hund was called back to Nov Ostoya as a military envoy to answer to the Grand Duke about the state of the war. Estspine had been lost and Büracher soldiers were advancing into Terinima and north to Raevo. The Ostoyan Empire was slipping away from its people.

During Hund’s stay in the capital, the earth shook and the sun disappeared. And Morgen Hund was chosen to lead soldiers down into the necropolis that had been revealed from the darkness of the fissures. His orders were to slay the undead. He was to find the source of the scourge and end it. With a battalion at his back, Hund descended beneath Ostoya. Neither he, nor his soldiers, ever returned alive.

After days of waiting and deathless ghouls continuing to emerge from the fissures, the Grand Duke made the decision to collapse the rifts. Arcane explosions buried the necropolis once more.

### The Sanguine Curse

Morgen Hund found something in the darkness beneath Ostoya. Something ancient. Something that had slept for untold centuries before, awoken by the Darkfall. It changed Hund, transforming him into an undying and fearless monster. A monster that hungered.

Thought dead for weeks, miraculously Hund returned to Nov Ostoya, starving and furious. Betrayed by the Grand Duke, Hund began his slaughter in the capital by decapitating the leadership of the Empire. He then seized command of the military and turned his ire to the invading Bürach armies, driving them back across the Grey Spine over the course of several bloody years.

But Morgen Hund was not alone in clawing himself out of the darkness. A horde of vampires rose with him, blood starved after centuries of being buried beneath Etharis. Freed at last, these undead horrors aided Hund in his crusade for vengeance, and in return he gave them that which he had valued most.

The sovereignty of Ostoya.

### The Crimson Court

The Ostoyan Empire is under the tyrannical rule of a cabal of vampires, extremely powerful in ability and personality, known as the Crimson Court.

Initially they were led by Hund, who reinvented himself as Klaus Vorgen the Betrayed. In the years since, nobody is certain what happened to Vorgen. Some say he was killed by another member of the Court; others claim he’s buried himself in the City Below. A few believe he’s still alive, somewhere in Ostoya, grieving over what he has wrought and hoping for a chance at redemption.

The Crimson Court is composed of seven ruling nobles who, through a tenuous alliance, rule over every aspect of Ostoyan life. While they are allies, no true loyalty exists among them. Each noble vies to sit at the head of the table, employing schemes that can take decades to bear fruit. Yet they’re all aware their bloody grip on Ostoya only remains strong through shared strength.

Currently, the Court is ruled by Grand Duke Drago Koshevek. The remaining six members each rule over a province of the Ostoyan Empire, assigned to them by the Grand Duke.

### Vampiric Bloodlines

The sanguine curse is ancient. Through the uncounted centuries its manifestations have divided into vampiric bloodlines, which vary greatly in appearance, abilities, and attitudes. The bloodline a vampire belongs to may be determined by their mortal heritage, as described in the Grim Hollow Monster Grimoire, or it may reflect the nature of their sire. The following are a few of the bloodlines established in Etharis:

#### Soman

Soman vampires claim to be divine beings destined to rule over, and be served by, the mortal humanoids of Etharis. They believe the sanguine curse began from the blood of a from a death god, making them its immortal seraphs. Whether they truly are the descendants of such a being is a question for philosophers and monster hunters.

Soman vampires tend to dress lavishly and drink blood from wine chalices. They bend mortals to their whims through manipulation while playacting at benevolent sovereigns. Their countenance is a balance of grace and power that is often lacking in other bloodlines, enabling them to mask themselves as humanoids more easily than most of their undead kindred.

#### Upyr

Upyr believe they are the first and most ancient bloodline, having long abandoned their humanity along with any pretense of appearing mortal. Their crooked frames are the very image of a living corpse. Their limbs are stretched and emaciated. Their eyes are pale and bloodless. Upyr rest in coffins filled with the dirt of their homeland, feeling a deep connection to the shadowy earth of Ostoya. In truth, few have ventured outside the City Below.

For upyr there are no machinations. No intrigues. No masquerade of nobility. There is only the thirst. They shepherd mortals like a flock but do not care if they’re worshipped or feared. Death is the only god left, and upyr are its seraphs.

#### Fzeg

An unholy biological alliance, the curse of lycanthropy and the gift of vampirism combine to become what is known as a fzeg. The ferocious strength of werewolves pairs horrifically with the ceaseless bloodlust of vampires. Easily the most physically deadly of the bloodlines due to their cleaving claws and bloodsucking bite, fzeg have no trouble hunting; their difficulties lie in stopping.

#### Strigoi

Strigoi are pallid, gangly, and utterly wretched creatures. Their eyes bulge from narrow features above a grin of pointed fangs. They’re unrivalled as nighttime predators, stalking their prey from above by crawling upside down in cavern ceilings or beneath bridges. Strigoi feed on blood, flesh, and bone, making mutilated corpses of their victims.

Despite their hideous appearance, strigoi maintain their mortal intelligence. They will bargain with and manipulate mortals with the same cunning as other bloodlines, yet strigoi have far less patience for pretending to be courtly. Many of their progeny hunt freely through the cities of Ostoya to slake their thirst.

#### Ecoukrylo

More batlike than humanoid, ecoukrylo resemble chiropteran monsters more than nobles. Their sensitive ears and leaf shaped noses enable them to hunt prey in pitch blackness and over great distances, swooping down on leather wings in the midnight of Ostoya. Many who witness this horror swiftly forget that an ecoukrylo was once a mortal.

Vampires of this bloodline tend to adopt strange behaviors such as hording trinkets, shrilling as they feed, or spending a disturbing amount of time grooming themselves. Although quirky, ecoukrylo are devious shapeshifters. Many disguise their terrifying true form within a noble masquerade.

## Provinces

Ostoyan War Table

Ostoya is divided into six provinces. Each of the six lesser members of the Crimson Court, as overseen by the Grand Duke, is given autonomy over one of the regions. The rulership of a province is seen as both an honor and a burden, and each member of the Court treats their leadership differently. One thing is true regardless: failure to keep a firm grip on a province in a sign of weakness, and any show of weakness is an invitation to be removed from the Crimson Court—and possibly from Etharis entirely.

### Estspine

Home to the first Ostoyans, Estspine was the province that gave birth to an empire. Its soil is stained with the blood of sacrifice and vengeance. From here the migrants spread to escape from Büracher oppression, and here they have rallied each time to defend their independence.

Estspine is under immense political and military pressure. The province is trapped between the spears of the Bürach Empire to its west, the blood-hungering aristocrats of the Crimson Court to its south, and the agitators of the Raevo Rebellion to its north. The simple folk of Estspine are in disagreement over who should rightfully lead them.

The province is a gateway to the Ostoyan Empire from the west, and therefore a powerful symbol to the Ostoyan people of resistance, independence, and pride. Above the river that flows from the Grey Spine looms the fortified city of Fallowheart, which has been used as bastion to hold the Bürach Empire at bay for generations. By ruling over Fallowheart, the Crimson Court legitimizes their reign of Ostoya.

Raevo aims to liberate Fallowheart from the vampires’ murderous grip. Armies from both sides of the conflict camp at the city walls like two wolves snarling over a carcass. Yet both Raevo and Soma recognize that to weaken themselves in a struggle over Fallowheart would create opportunity for a Bürach invasion to seize the city. Therefore, a tense standoff endures.

The wider Estspine economy is fed by trade. The region exports lumber, produce, and minerals across Ostoya via the Empire’s arterial rivers. Estspine also benefits from neighboring the Bürach Empire, from where notably daring merchants brave the Elian Pass to bring foreign wares. Stehlenwald black powder is of particular interest, in exchange for Soman wines and Raevan talismans.

#### Notable Individuals

Viscount Langkirk Warlow. By far the most obsessive and pitiable member of the Crimson Court, Viscount Warlow didn’t earn his position. He was raised to it by the Grand Duke Koshevek. Whispers in Nov Ostoya say the Grand Duke deliberately chose a weak ruler for Fallowheart. One Koshevek could easily intimidate and manipulate. Other rumors claim the viscount is expected to fail at his task of overseeing Fallowheart, which the Grand Duke can use as a pretense to destroy Warlow for some past insult.

Viscount Warlow is an ugly and insecure little man. The strigoi plays the part of noble badly, pretending to hold courtly dinners while horrifically devouring dismembered limbs in front of his subjects. Yet anyone who would dare comment on Warlow’s bad decorum or the rumors about his weak position within the Court are likely to end up on his dinner plate.

Castellan Gavril Bosković. A stoic knight from Castalore, Lord Bosković marched his army south from Raevo with a mandate to liberate Fallowheart from the Crimson Court. However, the civilian lives within the city have stayed his hand from ordering an attack.

Instead, Lord Bosković has camped his army on the northern bank of the Arteriyan River while his agents quietly ferment rebellion within Fallowheart. If a civilian uprising were to occur, the Castellan is ready with soldiers to support them.

### Raevo

Some say Raevo was destined for rebellion. The province was founded when a young diviner, Lissandra, dreamed of a shadow stretching from Nov Ostoya. She witnessed in her dreams the growing darkness slowly consuming the realm, until she followed her visions to the high cliffs of the north. Here, she saw a light. The place she stopped became the fortified city of Castalore.

The people of this province, Raevans, exhibit the same spirit of resistance and independence that began Ostoya. The commoners loath the undead with righteous passion. They consider any of their countryfolk who serve or aid the Crimson Court as traitors and cowards.

Since its secession from the Ostoyan Empire, Raevo has thrived under their governing magocracy. They have become the epicenter of unrestrained magical study, spell craft, and artifice. Magics thought too dangerous to explore in the Bürach Empire or Castinella are the very strength Raevo uses to resist the preternatural power of the Crimson Court.

Raevo has its fair share of difficulties, however. Aside from the frequent snowfall in winter and punishing summer rains, Raevo harvests are no better than the rest of Ostoya for the lack of sunlight. The latent necromancy of the realm makes the dead restless, leading to funerary rights that include decapitation and other desecrations of the dearly deceased. From the north, Valikan raids are seasonal and devastating, taking what little food and resources the smaller settlements have managed to scrounge together. Undead nightmares come hunting from the south, the Crimson Court exacting its blood tax one way or another.

Castalore is the largest settlement in Raevo and home to the governing magocracy. Designed as a fortress first and a settlement second, the impregnable city rests on a mighty cliff with massive stone walls and layered districts that give it the shape of a coiling python. Higher up the spiral are the more affluent neighborhoods, further from the mud and soldiers of the outer battlements. At the very top sits the Nightingale Tower, the magocracy’s building of governance. The city has the distinction of never being occupied by either the Bürach Empire or the Crimson Court.

Despite its unified purpose, Castalore is not without its divisions or intrigues. Commoners will never fully trust the secrets of mages, and therefore the loyalty of the people is shared between the magocracy, the Order of Dawn, and even the druids of the Solar Grove. While outwardly a coalition, the ideals and spirituality of the Order of Dawn and Solar Grove can be a hindrance to the magocracy’s pragmatic rulership.

#### Notable Individuals

Viscountess Nebüma Zeilgor. Brilliant and ever inquisitive, Viscountess Zeilgor replaced Virgilia Petinscu as the overseer of Raevo. Her predecessor was destroyed by Drago Koshevek after Zeilgor informed the Grand Duke of Petinscu’s failures to bring Raevo to heel.

The new viscountess is aware that her appointment, and her life, will be brief unless she can show progress in bringing Raevo back into the fold. As such, she and her strigoi progeny slink north of the Roiling River to sow discord and weaken Raevo from the inside. A shadow sorceress and a spy, Zeilgor is unafraid to bloody her own taloned hands.

When in Nov Ostoya, the viscountess advises the Grand Duke on matters of religion, history, and all things arcane. It’s rumored that her ultimate desire is access to the Ravencourt Sanctuary, making her quest to capture Raevo a personal ambition.

Mina Taliesin, the Lady of Masks. The Raevo Republic is governed by the Council of Mages, who are self-elected from among their peers. Lady Mina Taliesin has been First Minister of the Council for decades, successfully defending her rule against regional strife, political coups, and assassination attempts. Her expansive spy network keeps her informed of all potential dangers to her government. Some suspect that she even has a highly placed contact within Soma itself, allowing her to monitor and sabotage the efforts of the Crimson Court.

Yet for all her years in public office, Lady Taliesin remains a mystery to her own people. At all times she conceals her face behind a decorative mask. This has earned her the moniker as the Lady of Masks, of which she seemingly has many to wear on different occasions. Most believe she does so to hide the scars of an assassination attempt. Yet her enigmatic history, and close ties to the Ravencourt Sanctuary, have led to wilder conspiracy theories.

What isn’t in doubt is that Lady Taliesin seeks to destroy the vampire aristocracy and its control over Ostoya. But she schemes cautiously. Lady Taliesin does not wish to trade the bloodthirst of the Crimson Court for the conquest by the Bürach Empire.

General Artemios Dulka. Leader to the Order of Dawn, Artemios Dulka has been fighting against the vampires of Ostoya since he was a boy. Still as zealous in his cause as when he was a young knight, years of battle have brought age and injury, but also insight into his enemy. General Dulka fights less in battles but trains new recruits personally, drilling into them the same passion that burns within himself.

General Dulka was born a Soman noble before The Darkfall. His father was killed in Klaus Vorgen’s cleansing of the nobility, which forced Dulka to abdicate his birthright and flee north to escape the vampires’ jaws. Though that dream dwindles with age, General Dulka’s ambition has always been to liberate the land that had once been his.

### Terinima

Terinima is the largest and central province of the Ostoyan Empire, fed by the Great Arteriyan River which nourished its boundless horizons into arable farmland. Terinima is extremely valuable to the Crimson Court. Thanks to a large population of farmers, ranchers, hunters, and fishermen, the province is the breadbasket for the rest of Ostoya. It provides all the feed necessary for the vampires to maintain their own livestock—humanoids.

Terinimans are people of the soil. They have few ambitions besides keeping their families safe and fed. The lack of direct sunlight makes tending to sun-seeking crops difficult, but the residents of the province work with what they have. They’re honest, blunt, and sturdy, just looking to get by one day at a time. They tend to keep to their own small communities, rarely travel, happy to sell their goods to the same merchant for the same pittance every month.

Those simple needs make Terinimans simple to rule. Their lack of ambition makes them unlikely to resist, and they’re more inclined to report dissenters to the Crimson Court if it means their own families are spared any cruelty. Distrustful of outsiders but loyal to each other, Terinimans band together to deal with community hardships such as banditry or prowling beasts.

Settlements in Terinima are small, but properties are large. The exception is Crowsbend, which is an ever-growing town of craftsmen, merchants and smugglers. The one place most locals are fond of traveling to, Crowsbend is a hub of interprovincial trade, gossip, and entertainment. It’s a muddy town of ever-expanding streets. At its center is the riverside market, which is always clamoring with local farmers, whining livestock, tinkering artisans, miserly traders, and traveling bards who sing of distant sunlit realms.

#### Notable Individuals

Countess Augustine Vasinesca. Surprisingly for an ecoukrylo, Countess Vasinesca is among the most charming of the Court. That doesn’t mean she isn’t eccentric. Known by noble and commoner alike as the “Blood Sommelier,” Vasinesca’s keen smell makes her excellent at sniffing out vessels with the most peculiar or decadent tasting blood. Her Teriniman subjects fear to be brought before her. Once Vasinesca detects a new scent, she insists on adding them to her stills.

As the overseer of Terinima, Countess Vasinesca advises the Grand Duke on issues of food production and supply in Ostoya. But her true value to the court is finding the most delightful blends of blood to please even the most demanding vampiric palette. This talent has made her popular among her fellow sovereigns, even if they find her compulsive and chittering behavior uncouth.


### Bezcodru

The borders of Bezcodru start and end at the tree line of The Stillborn Forest. The province is known for primarily providing two things: lumber and ore. The woods are ample and the mines run deep. The Tithewater River flows through the province and into the depths of the Glasswater Lake, both of which are critical for transporting the resources of Bezcodru elsewhere in the Empire. The waters, the mountains, and the woods all make Bezcodru an enviable fiefdom. But they are all also home to fearsome beasts and deathless monsters.

Bezcodrans are coarse. Blunt. Always on guard. The sunless sky of Ostoya often remains out of sight underneath the suffocating boughs of the Stillborn Forest, and anyone, or anything, could be lurking among the rustling bush. Bezcodrans work, eat, sleep, and rarely leave the safety of their homes.

Lunsk is the most noteworthy settlement of Bezcodru, built into the southern bank of the Glasswater Lake where it meets the Lower Tithewater. Lunsk is a port town. But it has none of the mercantile bustle that usually crowds such settlements. At its edges are large bonfires patrolled quietly by guards, each with their backs to the flame and their eyes trained on the woods. The villagers dim their lights using red-tinted glass in their lanterns and windows, trying go as unnoticed by the denizens of the Stillborn Forest as possible.

Mercenaries and monster hunters can make a tidy living in the Stillborn Forest, escorting folk for whom travel is an unfortunately necessary part of life. However, the profession is a perilous one. Undead spirits drift between the trees, trying to drag others to join them in their graves. Flesh hungry ghouls follow the scent of warm blood. Deadliest of all are the hunting packs of fzeg, the Baron’s own fledglings, who are permitted to hunt freely in his realm.

#### Notable Individuals

Baron Mihael, The Wolf. Loyalty and honor are foreign concepts to the members of the Crimson Court. But Baron Mihael—a fzeg feared by his enemies as the “The Wolf”—comes closest to capturing those virtues. Prone towards the habits of lycanthropy, the Baron rules through violence more than any other Court member. But he believes in the strength of the pack and rewards his loyal servants richly.

Baron Mihael is a fearsome and cunning warlord. The Grand Duke favors him greatly, much to the jealousy and discomfort of the other vampire nobles. The Wolf has ended the lives of many would-be usurpers to the Grand Duke’s title. He’s razed villages sheltering rebel sympathizers and slaughtered vampire hunters who’ve dared travel south of Raevo. All of Ostoya fears the Baron greatly.

For his loyalty, and to keep the great wolf’s appetite and ambition sated, Mihael has been given the rich barony of Bezcodru. He advises the Grand Duke on issues of resource production and infrastructure development. He’s built roads across the realm, ensuring the swift deployment of troops to respond to Raevan rebels or Büracher soldiers. While Mihael does not lair within a castle himself, he ensures his fellow nobles have the materials needed to maintain the defenses of their own.

When not required elsewhere, Baron Mihael disappears into the Stillborn Forest. His fzeglaich retinue travels unseen between the trees, falling upon caravans or villages whenever The Wolf decides to collect his share of the blood tax.

### Umbroi

Mud, muck, and misery. While Umbroi is the official name of the province, most call it the Black Mire. It’s a dark, dank hellhole that swallows anything that wanders within. The ground is wet and thick. The air hangs heavy, reeking of decay. The trees are stooped into twisted shapes, and the ceaseless droning of insects whittles at the sanity of outsiders who choose to visit.

Umbroi is thick with insidious plants that creep from beneath the bog to drag victims below, while toxic fungi breathe their spores into the air. The thick peat mummifies humanoid remains. Even before the Darkfall, the Black Mire was renowned for its tales of hungry corpses clawing their way from the mud. Glowing spirits try luring wayfarers deeper into the brackish waters. Folk claim to have watched hag covens sing terrible songs over bubbling cauldrons filled with poisonous ingredients.

Only fools enter the Black Mire. Few can imagine why folk make their homes here.

Umbroians are quiet, suspicious, and prefer mumbling to themselves rather than holding conversations. They survive by remaining hidden, using secret water channels to move quietly between tiny settlements. Outsiders find it difficult to navigate the bog and are destined to meet a dismal end without aid from the locals. But the folk of the Black Mire don’t give out their secrets, easily. The marshland conceals them even from the power of the Crimson Court, and when escorting travelers, they may intentionally take longer routes so the quickest and safest roads remain concealed only to them. Coin holds little value in the marsh. Umbroians trade only in food, tools, or favors.

The folk of the Black Mire share a morbid sense of humor. They snigger at outsiders’ fear of the bog. Sometimes they laugh heartily when a victim is pulled screaming into the water by some fell beast or bog monster—even when it’s one of their own. Rumors circulate that a cult of Beleth, the Arch Daemon of fear, is growing among the Umbroians. If so, choosing an appropriate guide through the area may be perilous. Such folk revel in the suffering of others.

The largest settlement in Umbroi is Riven, on the southern edge of the Black Mire. The town’s size and shape is always shifting, as its stilted structures constantly sink into the earth and new ones are built to replace them. These waves of reconstruction cause the town to slide across the map, ever so slightly. People who travel to and from Riven infrequently must usually navigate a completely different layout each visit.

### Soma

Soma is the seat of power for the Ostoyan Empire, holding the majority of its wealth. The region enjoys open plains of farmland, scattered woods, and a strong coastal border. Soma’s defining landmark is Nov Ostoya, the City of Gargoyles. Built above the Black Bay, wealth flows through Nov Ostoya from the rest of Etharis in exchange for Ostoya’s resources. The city is like a gaping maw draining the blood from its own provinces. Its streets are divided into two districts. The dangerous and dismal Low Streets are infested with gang members and bliss addicts, while the Crimson Heights are the decadent neighborhoods of the vampire elite.

Nov Ostoya is still scarred by the fissures that opened during the Darkfall. Beneath lies the City Below, also known as the Necropolis. Although authorities try to keep the entrances to the underground ruins sealed, minor earthquakes and curious explorers unseal them frequently.

These breaks in the surface of Ostoya extend to other regions of Soma, as well. Ghouls and cursed creatures continue to crawl out to this day. Many of these chasms have been gaping ajar for years, but on occasion new ones’ crumble open to reveal fresh horrors. Soma is patrolled by the armies of the Crimson Court like hounds protecting a flock. But folk are often as afeared of their leaders as they are of the monsters beneath the earth.

Somans range from the most affluent of aristocrats to the desperate and destitute. They tend to be cutthroat, both politically and literally. Gangs form within the lower circles of society while the powerful trade lives with the nobility to keep fangs out of their own throats. Trust is difficult to earn and easy to lose. In Soma, you watch your back and cover your neck. Friends, with or without fangs, can be just as dangerous as the monsters outside the walls.

#### The Ravencourt Sanctuary

Miles from the Raevan shore the horizon is obscured by a blanket of fog called the Mistwall. Those who, by accident or design, sail into the fog end up hopelessly lost. The Mistwall conceals an island that is marked on very few maps but is well known to the sages of Castalore. It is home to one of the most secretive and protected places in Etharis; the Ravencourt Sanctuary.

In Ostoya’s distant past, the seer Lissandra came to the shores of Raevo and discovered a derelict boat. Aboard she found a wax-sealed envelope and inside were written ten names, including her own. Assembling her crew of nine others, Lissandra took the boat and sailed into the Mistwall, believed to have perished until they returned seven years later. Except Lissandra was no longer with the group. And each of the others had changed.

Power is what the Ravencourt Sanctuary offers. Every seven years the boat returns with nine mages of various disciplines, and ten new pupils depart with their names written in the wax-sealed envelope delivered by their forebears. To be chosen is a great honor. Apprentices vie for favor with the magocracy in Castalore hoping for their name to be written within the letter. Some even begin preliminary studies in preparation.

Nevertheless, when the boat returns to harbor on the Raevo coastline and the names are read, all are inexorably in attendance among the crowd. Yet never are all ten names expecting to be called.

Regardless of expectation, all ten depart for the Mistwall, but only nine of them return every seven years. The cycle has been unbroken since Lissandra. What isn’t known is what happens to that tenth student. Are they sacrificed to the mysterious Keeper, who ministers the Ravencourt Sanctuary? Do they remain on the island as servants or teachers? Are they fed to the seas to maintain the Mistwall? None but the Keeper know, and they hold their secrets close.

Of the sanctuary itself, the wizards who’ve returned describe a crumbling castle built above shoreline cliffs. A ceaseless storm envelops the castle. Flashes of lightning are a constant warning before the crack of thunder. Debris from the castle drifts through the air, headless of the wind and rain, including entire towers which orbit the central keep like an orrery.

Past graduates describe the interior of the Ravencourt Sanctuary in a way that sounds dreamlike. The doors don’t always open into rooms that make physical sense. The end of a small hallway may open into a resplendent library. A repository of arcane knowledge unseen anywhere else in Etharis. The same door the next day leads into a scorch-marked laboratory, the monstrosities from a previous experiment still lurking in the dark corners.

Lodgings are damp, and a chill wind howls through the corridors. The only fire to be had is conjured with magic—a first lesson for new apprentices. The students must survive the Ravencourt Sanctuary before they can learn from it.

Despite the perilous education, the Sanctuary is the only place in Etharis where magic is taught entirely without restraint. Students read ancient tomes forbidden in Erlefurt or Tol Leyemil. They commune with spirits and conjure daemons for interrogation, permitted to make pacts if the terms are agreeable to the Keeper. Spells deemed offensive to the Arch Seraphs are practiced openly. The secrets of the cosmos are laid bare before the apprentices. Those who cannot endure such trials return to Raevo scarred by their experiences, rather than empowered by them.

All of this occurs beneath the watchful eyes of the Keeper. Although this mysterious entity can make themself appear human, the Keeper is certainly not. At least not anymore.

The Keeper acts as a guide for students within the Ravencourt Sanctuary more than a teacher. They whisper to their students from the quiet darkness, encouraging each pupil to follow their deepest ambitions. Knowledge is what the Keeper provides. How it’s wielded is in the hands of each apprentice.

The Keeper is omnipresent within the grounds of the Sanctuary, often appearing in more than one place at a time. Sometimes the Keeper is content to take the shape of a raven with tattered feathers, patiently watching their pupils practice spells. Other times the Keeper will adopt the form of a loved one or rival from a student’s past, or a shape emblematic of a student’s greatest fear. Regardless, the eyes of the Keeper are always the same—rapturous and deep, their pupils a subtly swirling cosmic void.

Nobody is certain of the Keeper’s intent. Conspiracists claim the Keeper is the head of the Thaumaturge, a secretive cabal of mages most people don’t believe exists. Others ponder if the Keeper is the Ravencourt Sanctuary itself—a place where magic coalesces and calls out to those able to grasp it.

#### The Solar Grove

Deep within the forests of western Raevo, the Solar Grove has remained hidden from the prying eyes of the vampire nobility. Though the sun may be absent from the Ostoyan sky, the druids of the Solar Grove can still feel its warmth on their skin. The folk of the grove are few. But their hope has never dwindled, despite the enduring darkness.

At the center of the grove grows the Solarwood, a magnificent tree with silver bark and burnished gold leaves. Radiant light showers down beneath its boughs. The grass and flowers that grow among its roots burst with life and color. The druids seed and harvest plants within the tree’s glow, grateful that such a beacon shines for them in Ostoya’s eternal night.

The Solarwood was cultivated by Sylina Florian, an archdruid old enough to remember when the true sun shone through the trees of her forest. Well read in her youngest years, Sylina heeded the ancient writings of Lissandra and anticipated the coming darkness. She performed repeated rituals to draw upon the power of the sun and imbued it within a sapling. By the time of The Darkfall, the Solarwood had already towered above its neighboring trees.

The Solar Grove is hallowed ground. Undead cannot come within the light of the tree and vampires are burned by its radiance. But what has kept the grove safe is secrecy. If the Crimson Court learned of the Solarwood’s existence, they would no doubt stop at nothing to destroy it. This has made Sylina and her druidic circle reluctant to join Raevo in its rebellion.

Both Mina Taliesin and General Dulka are aware of the grove. The General envisions another purpose for the Solarwood than letting it sit idle deep in the forest. Its bark is exceptionally durable, able to deflect an axe blow and only malleable to druidic woodcarvers. What’s more, the wood is warm to the touch while it emits its radiant glow. Surely it’s deadly to a vampire. Would that it could be felled for timber and turned into a thousand stakes or spears to equip an army.

Sylina Florian is horrified by such a suggestion. She refuses to sacrifice the soul hope of her druidic circle. Nevertheless, she’s provided a handful of smaller branches to be crafted for Dulka’s purpose. Solarwood weapons are therefore extremely rare, usually carried by veteran knights within the Order of Dawn.

#### The City Below

Untold centuries before the Ostoyan Empire, eastern Etharis was home to a civilization with a different name. What this realm was called has been lost to history. Yet its bones have now been unearthed by the Darkfall.

Folk brave or foolish enough to descend into the fissures of Soma find themselves in a labyrinthine ruin that runs throughout the land like veins beneath the surface of the skin. Known as the City Below, this necropolis is concentrated beneath Nov Ostoya but it reaches as far as Fallowheart or Malkovia. The necropolis is filled with broken streets and ruined buildings. Temples, statues, and houses have all swallowed by the earth. The walls of the City Below are decorated with magnificent murals. A forgotten language is scribed into the stonework, describing ancient myths and stories.

What remains a mystery is how deep the necropolis descends into Etharis. It’s an underworld infested with zombies, ghouls, skeletons, and wraiths, making it dangerous to explore the city’s secrets. These revenants are believed to have once been the citizens of the old civilization.

The Crimson Court are content spreading the lie that they are the most ancient and dangerous creatures to awaken from the City Below. But archeological evidence suggests there is something even greater buried in the dark, which the vampires themselves dread and revere.

## The City of Fallowheart

Fallowheart is a city caught in the jaws of conflict. It lies on the western border of Ostoya, the first bastion against invasion from the Bürach Empire. Throughout its long history, Fallowheart has repelled countless assaults and sieges; as the gateway into the Ostoyan Empire, any invasion is doomed to fail if it cannot first capture Fallowheart.

At the same time, Fallowheart is caught directly between the grip of the Crimson Court in the south and the rebellious influence of Raevo in the north. The battle for Ostoya’s soul is concentrated on Fallowheart.

### Geography

Kaer Vena casts a dark shadow over Fallowheart. The ancient fortress is built on a rocky crag that rises from the Arteriyan River, causing the water to crash and break around it. Kaer Vena is virtually impregnable and the seat of power in Estspine, usually held by the province’s ruler. In times past, such rulers were descendants of the emigrants who traveled through the Grey Spine to found the city of Fallowheart. Presently, Kaer Vena is the blood fort of Viscount Langkirk Warlow of the Crimson Court.

Fallowheart is built on the Arteriyan River’s southern bank, its great walls enclosing the city’s inner boroughs. The settlement is besieged by dark forest on all sides; twisted tree branches scrape at the city’s walls like fingers trying to claw their way inside.

Fallowheart’s southern road leads toward the Stillborn Forest and eventually to Nov Ostoya. That road also forks toward the Grey Spine and the mountain pass that connects Ostoya to the Bürach Empire. Currently, the pass is held by the western nation, who have a small fort garrisoned at the eastern base of the mountains. Büracher spies frequently skulk into Fallowheart, while Ostoyan scouts watch the mountain pass closely; neither nation moves without the other noticing. Anxiety grows in Grabenstein to the west about the news of armies mustering outside Fallowheart’s gates.

The bridge that crosses the Arteriyan at Fallowheart leads north to Malkovia and Castalore. Those roads are traveled by soldiers loyal to Raevo, where the Crimson Court’s influence thins. Though fewer lives are claimed by monsters in that direction, skirmishes are fought between Raevo militias trying to gather strength at Fallowheart and Soma loyalists determined to undermine such efforts.

By far the quickest and safest way to travel through Fallowheart is by boat. Merchant ships, freight barges, and passenger boats arrive and depart from the docks in Brevic daily. This form of transport heavily favors travel and trade with the southern provinces of Ostoya, where the nation’s rivers spread like arteries.

Fishing in the Arteriyan is a regular source of food for the city. In addition to the docks of Brevic, a small fishing village called Malach crouches on the north bank of the river. Malach has become a rest stop for travelers entering or leaving Fallowheart. It’s also a way in and out of the city without being seen—if one is willing to pay a smuggler’s toll.

The village of Blud similarly clings to Fallowheart’s southern gate. The settlement grew organically around what had once been a noble’s estate before The Darkfall. Locals now use the old mansion as a tavern. New homes frequently appear along the road into town, built by commoners hoping it’s safer to live in sight of Fallowheart’s gates.

### History

When the first emigrants to leave the Bürach Empire journeyed through the Grey Spine in search of a new home, they soon discovered Kaer Vena. While the settlers were initially hesitant to build in the shadow of this inexplicable castle, Kaer Vena seemed to await their arrival. Its halls were empty and silent; those who erected it had long since disappeared. In need of refuge, the pilgrims claimed it.

The architecture of Kaer Vena was quite unlike the strongholds of either dwarves or humans in the Bürach Empire. Its stonework was arching and eerily ornate, murals made from skeletons lined the corridors, and sculpted bats spread their wings above doorways. The macabre imagery inside Kaer Vena might have frightened away the pilgrims had it not been for another sigil carved into the castle’s great doors—a radiant sun.

Those who built Kaer Vena seemed to revere the sun: its likeness was emblazoned above mantels and painted across the ceiling of the main chamber. To the migrants, it was a symbol of the goddess Aurelia, and its appearance convinced them more than anything that this eastern land had been promised to them.

#### Lady of Vengeance

The separation of Ostoya from the Bürach Empire was not sudden. The realm that would one day call itself Ostoya began as a scattering of settlements that spread eastward from the Grey Spine, nestled along the twisting rivers and through the untamed pastures of this new land. Some folk still called themselves Büracher for a generation; then a new identity began to form. When the Bürach Empire came by force to reassert its laws and taxes, it was at Kaer Vena the eastern folk rallied to defend their newfound freedoms.

The Ostoyan armies gathered beneath the banners of the Arch Seraph Solyma and her chosen prophet, Saint Dayla.

Dayla was humbly raised in the farmland of Estspine, a generation removed from the migration across the Grey Spine. But she remembered the stories her parents told of their struggles in the Bürach Empire—wealth stolen by nobles, mages imprisoned and separated from their families, loyalty to a distant Emperor enforced with iron. That such oppressors would come to reimpose their laws on the eastern free lands filled Dayla with rage and vengeance.

Saint Dayla’s words, in the name of the Lady of Silence, changed the soul of Ostoya: from meek farmers who fled their homeland into warriors of defiant fury—the first war of secession forged Ostoya into a nation.

#### The Darkfall

At the time of the Darkfall, Fallowheart was embroiled in a second war against the Bürach Empire. The power of the west dwindled in the years following Emperor Leopold I’s assassination. While the Hearthkeepers clutched to keep their fracturing empire from falling to pieces, seizing land seemed a shrewd strategy for bolstering Büracher national pride.

No hero rose this time to rally defenders at Kaer Vena. The Ostoyans fought for their homes but fell beneath the Bürach war machine. For a time, Unter and Raulish banners hung from Kaer Vena. Then the sun melted from the sky.

When Klaus Vorgen returned from Nov Ostoya to Fallowheart, he brought death to the invaders. No longer concerned with preserving life, Vorgen ordered the Ostoyans into battles they couldn’t win simply to exhaust the Bürach battle lines. When the Ostoyans lay dead in the field, Vorgen raised them repeatedly, until their corpses were too broken to be of service. Suzanne Helmigh

The Büracher retreated in terror. Strigoi and fzeg hunted them down. There was no mercy—for what mercy can death provide? When his vengeance was satisfied, Vorgen gave Fallowheart to his Crimson Court to decide who among themselves should rule it.

#### Grinfield

Grinfield is Fallowheart’s central and largest borough. Its pointed architecture, with steep tiled roofs and gothic archways, is typical of an Ostoyan city. Not a single stone of masonry is without some embellishment: every surface is an opportunity to engrave a tangle of thorny roses, a skeletal bas relief, or the glaring countenance of an owl. This grim ornamentation typifies how the architecture of ancient structures like Kaer Vena influenced the building styles brought from the west by the Büracher emigrants.

Most public businesses in Fallowheart are found in Grinfield, including merchants, trade posts, and reputable healers. This makes it a desirable borough for urban living. Homes are usually small multistory town houses with gothic facades lining cobblestone streets.

The folk of Grinfield are quiet and withdrawn, choosing not to acknowledge the grim realities of living in Ostoya. They are workers, tradesfolk, business owners, or inheritors with a modicum of wealth. Though they may have comparatively little to lose, they value what they do have when weighing the cost of rebellion against the oppressive Crimson Court.

The Marla. Facing into a main city square with multiple intersecting streets, the Marla is a dominating five-story building at the heart of Grinfield. The Marla is Fallowheart’s largest inn and taphouse. Its banquet hall is frequented by travelers and locals alike, making it an excellent venue to gather news about the city and abroad.

The Marla is recommended as the most comfortable lodgings in Fallowheart, though folk warn against renting any room on the top floor.

Cemetery. The cemetery is a dark void among the lights of Grinfield. The earth is choked with corpses buried one upon the other, making the ground uneven and the tombstones lopsided. Fetid limbs and faces poke from the soil, picked at by ravens who crowd the branches of dead trees.

Near the center of the graveyard towers a mausoleum; this gothic building is the only other structure left by those who built Kaer Vena, decorated with similar ghastly engravings of skeletons and gargoyles. Rumors about the mausoleum suggest it’s an entrance to the City Below—yet nobody has ever seen its interior for fear of the Gravekeeper.

Augustine Trading Post. The Augustine Trading Company operates several warehouses in Brevic, but their public-facing business is in Grinfield. They offer a large selection of resources and equipment, and occasionally sell magic items to select clients. The Trading Post also provides services such as transport or message delivery.

The Augustine Trading Company is officially neutral in the Ostoyan civil war, though it’s known to have contracts on both sides. The specifics of such dealings are private, but the Trading Company prizes its access to vampire blood for their research in Morencia.

#### Brevic

Brevic’s skyline is unique for its large storehouses, the masts of merchant ships in the harbor, and the Belltower near Fallowheart’s western gate. The city’s northern corner runs up against the muddy banks of the Arteriyan River. Its narrow, poorly lit streets are wet with filth, fish offal, and the water used to wash it all away.

The presence of the Belltower does little to make the citizens of Fallowheart feel safer, least of all because the soldiers housed inside are under the command of the Viscount. The Fish Market is a shanty town of ramshackle stalls crowded into a muddy square. The Fish Market is always cacophonously busy. There are many eyes watching what happens here, but also many places to get lost in the crowd. Despite its folksy charm, the Fish Market is rumored to be a hidden battleground between the smugglers of the Ebon Syndicate and agents of the Volitant.

#### Cerest

Cerest is the hill atop which Saint Dayla’s Cathedral is built. The great dome and steeples of the cathedral crown the hill and watch over the surrounding city like a shining beacon of hope in Ostoya’s eternal night.

A band of soldiers guard the cathedral steps. Their allegiance is to Raevo, and their mandate is to protect the sacred hill from the Crimson Court. Truthfully, they’re able to do little more than chase away vandals and Soma loyalists; their numbers are too few in the event the Viscount decides to bleed the cathedral entirely.

Saint Dayla’s Cathedral. The high arched windows of Saint Dayla’s Cathedral are always lit by countless flickering candles. The singing of the congregation spills out into the surrounding city; their voices are an invitation for honest hearts to take shelter in the cathedral’s light and join in the prayers to the Arch Seraphs.

Saint Dayla’s Cathedral was built to honor the prophet of Solyma. Her bones are buried beside it. In Dayla’s name, the cathedral is a symbol of resistance against the oppression and autocracy the Crimson Court represents. Yet the congregants are not soldiers who can fight for their freedom against the Viscount’s monsters. Furthermore, hope has not yet spread to the whole city; some consider the congregation to be mere agitators and Bürach sympathizers.

Nevertheless, those who hold faith with the Arch Seraphs or are foes of the Crimson Court can find friends in Saint Dayla’s Cathedral.

#### Est Vale

The eastern corridor of Fallowheart is built between the low hills near Kaer Vena and the rise along which the city walls run. The borough is most notable for its larger dwellings, which belong to the city’s wealthiest, and its barracks, which are home to Fallowheart’s standing garrison. Though technically loyal to the city itself, the garrison is under Viscount Warlow’s command.

Violet Rose. The Violet Rose is outwardly an upmarket taphouse. It’s a venue for the city’s wealthy and connected to liaise with the Viscount’s inner circle, and therefore petition the Court outside official channels.

The more sinister purpose of the Violet Rose is for the privileged to shirk their obligations to the blood tax. Wealthy families or organizations who owe blood to the Court can instead send a relative or commoner in their employ to the Violet Rose. Here, the Viscount and his progeny feed on these sacrificial lambs at their leisure. Records are kept ensuring all taxes are paid.

Grisham’s Tower. The crumbling tower in Est Vale is a mystery. It’s stood for more generations than anyone can remember, yet it certainly wasn’t there when the first migrants journeyed through the Grey Spine. Fallowheart’s mighty walls awkwardly jut around the tower as if to accommodate its presence. There’s no public record of the tower’s construction, though rumors claim it belonged to a powerful wizard from whom it got its name.

The languishing citadel sits at the top of a low rise. Its ornate, iron-bound doors are barred to any visitors, and there is no light in any of the windows. Approaching the tower causes one’s hair to stand on end, which superstitious locals take as an indisputable sign the tower is haunted. Yet those educated in the arcane can deduce the presence of a powerful eldritch aura emanating from the tower.

Bridge Forts. The bridge that connects Fallowheart to the Arteriyan’s northern bank is protected at each end by two fortresses and in its middle by twin watchtowers. The design is such that an invading army would need to fight for every foot gained along the bridge. There are also water gates that can be dropped between the bridge’s supports to block river travel.

#### Blud & Malach

Blud runs along the main road from Fallowheart’s southern gate, as if the city walls simply couldn’t fit every building inside. Near the center of the borough is the Baron’s House, a boisterous tavern operating out of what used to be a noble’s mansion. The bar staff wear the gaudy outfits found in the mansion’s dusty wardrobes as uniforms. Travelers stop by the Baron’s House to hear news or rumors before entering Fallowheart or leaving the safety of its lights for the wilderness.

Malach grew organically as a small fishing village. It soon became its own rest stop for folk traveling northward, a place to find cheaper food and board than inside the city. Most recently, Malach has gained a reputation as a den for smugglers moving contraband through Fallowheart via the Brevic docks. Raevo soldiers protect Malach from investigation by the Viscount’s authorities.

#### Kaer Vena

The great battlements of Kaer Vena watch over all of Fallowheart. The black castle is at once a symbol of Ostoya’s resistance against the Bürach Empire and its oppression beneath the Crimson Court. Currently occupied by the Viscount Langkirk Warlow, the blood in the streets of Fallowheart flow from Kaer Vena.

The castle is heavily defensible. Only a single stone bridge connects Kaer Vena to the southern shore, and a fortified gatehouse protects the south end of the bridge from assault. The Arteriyan River flows treacherously around the crags beneath Kaer Vena; landing boats on the island is impossible, even if one were able to then scale the sheer cliffs beneath the castle walls. The impregnability of Kaer Vena is what made it a bastion against Bürach’s invasion.

Inside, the castle is cryptlike. High stone walls enclose small chambers and narrow passageways; arched iron windows invite in only the darkness of Ostoya, and some rooms are without windows entirely. Endless rotting doors, secret corridors, and gothic ornaments make Kaer Vena a sepulchral labyrinth to explore.

Warlow’s throne room is unimpressive. Flickering embers from the fireplace cast an orange glow on the cold stone. Heraldic shields, rusted weapons, and faded tapestries collect centuries of dust on the walls. The names of Fallowheart’s rulers who hung them have long been forgotten.

The Viscount sits on a simple chair in a corner of the room. A cluster of ash-faced courtiers attend him with their backs to the fire, their shadows shielding the Viscount from the light. Warlow listens to petitioners from the darkness; a hunger glints in his eyes.

### Quest Hooks

The following quest hooks can be used to flesh out adventures set in the city of Fallowheart.

#### Defeat the Gravekeeper

Viscount Warlow has decreed it illegal to attack the Gravekeeper. Its undead visage gives credence to the Crimson Court’s authority and keeps the populace afraid. However, Sister Abigail wants it destroyed. She’s secretly offering a reward to anyone who can kill the Gravekeeper and prove it will never return. Doing so would require the player characters to descend deep into the mausoleum’s undead-infested catacombs and discover what curse binds the Gravekeeper to its eternal servitude.

#### Curse Breakers

A common folktale shared in Fallowheart is about Thaddeus Grisham, the eccentric wizard who once lived in the tower in Est Vale. The story goes that he was an advisor to the dukes of Kaer Vena before The Darkfall. The tower held the realm’s criminals given to Grisham by the Grand Duke, and the mage would conduct hideous experiments upon them at his own whim. When the moon hides its face behind the clouds and Fallowheart is darkest, some say the screams of Grisham’s prisoners can still be heard from within.

The truth is that Thaddeus Grisham was corrupted by Shadowsteel. He practiced weaving curses and crafting objects using the profane metal. His prisoners were indeed the victims of such magic; they now infest the tower as cursed monsters, escaped from the cages that once contained them.

Grisham himself still haunts the tower’s highest level as a Shadowsteel Ghast. He’s protected by the same arcane traps and conjured elementals that guard his treasures. Grisham’s faculties are no longer entirely his own, but he is covetous of his Shadowsteel and his arcane library. He is hostile to anyone entering his tower unbidden.

From the outside, Grisham’s Tower doesn’t appear to pose any great danger to Fallowheart. Yet both Viscount Warlow and Sister Abigail know its rumored treasures and secrets could be powerful in the hands of their enemy.

#### Church and State

Two armies camp outside Fallowheart’s walls like rival wolves snarling over a carcass. On the northern bank of the Arteriyan River fly the banners of the Raevo rebellion, while outside the southern gates are soldiers loyal to Soma and the Court. Neither is eager for open bloodshed. The ties of kinship run through the city on both sides. But amongst this tension, even the slightest provocation could explode into open war.

Within this maelstrom, Sister Abigail quietly searches for brave confidants she can entrust with an inglorious quest—to assassinate the Viscount. She believes the only way to break the stalemate in Raevo’s favor is to kill Warlow and prove the Crimson Court isn’t invincible. Sister Abigail can’t risk open defiance or treachery herself; such provocation would give the Viscount pretext to arrest her as a traitor and demolish the cathedral, snuffing the last stronghold for Raevo within the city.

For these reasons, Sister Abigail only offers this quest to folk she trusts, such as those who might have destroyed the Gravekeeper or defended the cathedral from Volitant spies.

Entering Kaer Vena will be difficult. The player characters could arrive under false pretenses to petition the Viscount. They might alternatively discover the secret passages in the cliffs beneath the castle walls, which lead into Kaer Vena’s tombs. Regardless, those who do gain entry would face many loyal soldiers, vampiric spawn, and other undead horrors beside the Viscount in Kaer Vena’s darkened halls.

#### Snuff the Candle of Hope

Few events in Fallowheart occur without the Viscount’s knowledge. Those who perform notable deeds are frequently summoned to Kaer Vena if the Viscount believes they may be of service to him. Such feats could include routing smugglers from the Ebon Syndicate or breaking the curse on Grisham’s Tower. Currently, the Viscount seeks individuals of a particular skillset for an unscrupulous task—to kill every soul in Saint Dayla’s Cathedral and burn it to the ground.

Unwilling to make martyrs of Sister Abigail and her congregation, the Viscount refuses to send soldiers to perform public slayings. Furthermore, Saint Dayla’s Cathedral is consecrated ground; neither Warlow nor his vampiric spawn can enter it themselves. Therefore, this quest will be entrusted to those the Viscount can deny connection with.

Sister Abigail and her congregation are not formidable warriors. They’re defended by a small militia of Raevo soldiers, though their true protector is Solyma’s Champion, the Pactbreaker. Defeating the celestial construct and slaying Sister Abigail will close the Crimson Court’s bloody grip on Fallowheart and earn high rewards from Viscount Warlow.

## The Free City of Malkovia

The “Free City” of Malkovia is conflicted in its identity and allegiance. Bisected by the Roiling River and split between warring provinces, Malkovia was founded as an international market that accepted business from everywhere in Etharis. The city has had to fight fiercely to maintain their open trade policies. After the Darkfall, Malkovia became torn, as both Raevo and Soma clutched for control of the city’s location and commerce, making it vulnerable to swindlers and opportunists. Anything goes in Malkovian markets, so long as you pay the proper taxes.

While dozens of bridges cross the Roiling River along its length, delivering merchants and wayfarers from one side to the other, Malkovia sits upon the arterial road that connects Raevo to the rest of Ostoya. No less than seven bridges provide access from one side of the river to the other. The city itself is a network of slick streets and cramped alleys, crowded with folk moving from one market or warehouse to the other. Something is being sold on every street and at every corner, either from shops, stalls, or sleeves. The affluent and empowered reap excessive profits from their vast ventures, while the common citizens on the street fight the crushing weight of poverty while fending off each other.

The Raevan rebellion and Crimson Court both endeavor to pressure the Free City into joining their cause. Although Malkovian leadership has cannily resisted these advances, the commoners of Malkovia have been keen to listen. The Northside favors the rebellion, denouncing the vampiric nobility of Ostoya as godless monsters. Conversely, the Southside favors the Court, preferring the security of a united empire and the imposing strength of supernatural leadership. Mariska Király, the Matriarch of Malkovia, remains staunch in her position that the city remains independent. But her subordinates may have other ideas.

### Geography

Malkovia’s architecture is as pointed, intricate, and arching as any city in Ostoya. Countless steeples point toward the night sky. Statues of weeping seraphs, hooded knights, and hackled werewolves brood beneath curved alcoves. While dark deeds go unnoticed in darkened alleys, the winding, cobblestoned streets are witnessed by the glowing eyes of each houses’ lantern-lit windows.

The two halves of Malkovia are known simply as Northside and Southside of the Roiling River. This arbitrary divide that has nonetheless resulted in diverging communities.

Multistoried terraces of ornate stonework are far more common on the Northside. Here, Malkovia’s wealthy and influential make their homes. The center of Northside is dominated by the wharf district, made of warehouses and dock workers, but wide roads make it easy for black carriages to ferry the nobility through the less-desirable parts of town.

By contrast, the infrastructure in Southside has fallen into disrepair. The oldest parts of the city were flooded during the Darkfall and never properly restored, making each home a breeding ground for mold, assuming it still has all four walls. Though Malkovia’s vast markets operate on the Southside, the wealth they generate does not remain there.

#### The Roiling River

The Roiling River carves across the entire width of northern Ostoya. It starts beneath the fires Mt. Vengeance in the Grey Spine, boiling its way through the realm and cooling in temperature and temperament before it reaches Malkovia.

The river is the lifeblood of the city. It’s essential for Malkovia’s renowned mercantile industry, but also for daily use as a source of food, transport, and a place to dump refuse. The water is pierced with a network of drains and pipes. Downriver is littered with filth and sewage that obscure the murky depths below. Impoverished “River Rats” throw nets into the waste to catch discarded valuables or swim along the riverbed, raking up the muck and sediment in hopes of discovering small treasures.

The Roiling River averages nine hundred feet across as it passes through Malkovia. Tiny uninhabited islands dot the surface. Rushing waters cascade over muddy shoals and between bridge supports, requiring skilled captains to navigate their boats along the city’s length. The banks are thick with mud and marshland reeds, home to several species of birds, fish, eels, and very often, monsters.

#### Fissure Lake

During The Darkfall, a destructive fissure opened on the Southside of Malkovia and split the Roiling River. As undead poured out of the earth, water torrented in, draining the river and dragging ships, buildings, and people into the darkness of the unknown caverns below. In the aftermath of the disaster, a dam was constructed to seal off the newly formed lake and correct the river’s flow. Today, Fissure Lake remains as a haunting reminder of the countless souls consumed by the Darkfall, and the fragility of life within the Ostoyan Empire.

Fissure Lake is filled with ghost stories. Many fear that openings to the City Below are still present under the water. Indeed, sodden corpses shamble onto the banks from time to time, and spirits are said to drift upon the lake’s surface. Nobody dares fish or sail upon those waters.

Perhaps the most frightening rumor is that the lake is slowly draining. That year after year, the surface is lowering. It is feared that one day soon it will be a wide-open door to the underworld once more.

### History

#### Founding the Free City

Spreading north from the Elian Pass, many of the first Ostoyan migrants settled along the Roiling River. Others followed it eastward. They searched for the perfect place to call their home, trekking through the Dark Wood, eventually arrived at a looming structure built from rock and bone: an immense and imposing bridge. This solitary crossing empowered the settlers to utilize both sides of the river and send boats westward to trade with others. And so here, they started to build their city.

Malkovia quickly became a booming trade hub, attracting merchants from across Etharis wishing to access the riches of a burgeoning empire. Inevitably, as often occurs in mercantile cities, circles of illicit activity arose as Malkovia became an attractive base of operations for smugglers and thieves.

#### The Fight for Freedom

When the Bürach Empire marched to claim sovereignty over Ostoya, Malkovia became critical for funding the war effort and recruiting foreign mercenaries. Rarely had the warfront threatened the city directly. That was, until the Darkfall.

When the sun vanished, Malkovia was plunged into darkness and terror. Undead clambered from a gaping fissure that opened on the river’s southern bank, unleashing death and destruction on an unprecedented scale. Unlike Nov Ostoya, there were no heroes or armies to protect Malkovia. Instead, there was simply the river. The crag filled with water, flooding away the undead who would have otherwise consumed the city, but dragging many commoners into the cold darkness with it. By the time the last glint of sunlight was gone, the fissure had become full. The black waters brimmed with wreckage and corpses.

The Büracher retreated, the Crimson Court seized power, and the Raevans rebelled. Malkovia suddenly found itself trapped on the front of a new war—one that expected the city to pick a side. Both the Crimson Court and the Council of Mages have made their case for Malkovia’s loyalty, wielding promises and threats in equal measure. As with Fallowheart, the Court prefers not to incite rebellion through military action, which could unite the citizens of Malkovia with soldiers from Castalore. Meanwhile, Malkovia gives few concessions and pays no tax to Raevo, ensuring continued trade partnerships with southern Ostoya.

Unfortunately for both sides, Malkovia’s preferred form of independence is absolute. The city’s markets are open to all. Mortals and monsters alike.

#### A Tenuous Balance

Today, Malkovia remains a crowded city. Folk arrive every day seeking the safety of a larger settlement, a reprieve from the blood tax, or the opportunity for work. But as the population rises, so do tensions. The sprawling city is more divided than ever.

The prosperity of Northside is looked upon jealously by the commoners of the south. The free flow of resources from Raevo is on full display, alongside the untaxed wealth imported through trade with other nations.

Residents of Northside do not give their blood to the Ostoyan nobility. Oleg the Butcher, overboss of the northern districts, pays the hunting guilds personally to punish vampires who take blood from north of the river. Support for the Raevan rebellion is not uncommon, although outwardly campaigning for the cause is swiftly silenced, both because it diminishes the power of Matriarch Király and invites retribution from the Crimson Court.

Meanwhile, the influence of the Court is strongly felt in Southside. Their progeny hunt openly to exact the blood tax, while many residents give their blood willingly to win favor from agents of the vampires.

Malkovia has not received a direct visit from a member of the Crimson Court in some time. Many residents welcome it, however. A strange religion revering the vampires as seraphs has emerged in the Southside of Malkovia, spreading quite strongly, even bleeding into Northside.

### Notable Locations

Besides the Northside and Southside, Malkovia is further divided into six smaller districts. This proves to be essential knowledge to any visitor who doesn’t wish to become lost in Malkovia’s dark and narrow alleyways.

#### Old Malkovia

The eastmost district north of the river, “Old” Malkovia is the place where the first structures of the city were built. It’s often the first district incoming ships see when they glimpse Malkovia’s spires, and its historic architecture from the early days of the city are among the most ornate and finely crafted. The proud bloodlines of old Malkovia date back to the city’s founding. The district is wealthy, but it is mostly comprised of old nobility of lesser houses.

#### The Ossuary Bridge

There are seven major bridges within Malkovia that cross the Roiling River. None is more recognizable or infamous as the Ossuary Bridge.

Constructed from the skulls, bones, and skeletons of countless people, the Ossuary Bridge was discovered by the migrant Ostoyans who would found Malkovia. Masterfully crafted with pillars of vertebrae, ribcage columns, and a wide street of teeth and bones laid like cobblestones, the bridge was a lone edifice surrounded by swamp. Unsettled by its appearance but in need of its utility, the first Malkovians settled the north bank.

The name “Malkovia” comes from this very bridge. Carved into the stone along its length, the phrase “KREKI STOMALK OVIAVINDI” is prominently carved. However, when the bridge was discovered, mud and rubble obscured most of the phrase, displaying only “MALK OVIA.” After the discovery of the City Below, the phrase has since been translated to mean “all are equal in death.”

The Ossuary Bridge remains to this day, unshaken and undamaged by even The Darkfall. As the eastmost bridge of Malkovia, every ship must enter and leave beneath it. An architectural wonder, its origin had remained a mystery until discovering the City Below, where familiar mosaics of tooth and bone can also be found.

#### Ralakia

Across the water from Old Malkovia is the Southside district named Ralakia, now derisively called Lake Town. Flooded during the Darkfall and the formation of Fissure Lake, most of the water never drained away, leaving behind permanent marshland.

The most destitute of the districts, Lake Town is a labyrinth of packed slums and tenements. The folk here are often overlooked and ignored. They survive through their own grit, scrounging for food in the waters, and begging for coins.

#### The Clocktowers

The three watch towers that overlook Malkovia from an eastern bend in the river were originally built to protect the city from Valikan longboats. Northern raiders were a frequent threat in Malkovia’s earlier history. Since the Darkfall has enveloped Ostoya, only especially ambitious or brash Valikans would dare attack such an established settlement.

The towers have since been purchased by a gnomish engineer named Ciprian Johann, who modified each with brass plates and internal mechanisms. Every tower has its own clockface. Their collective chimes create an hourly symphony of thunderous bells that hauntingly echo over Malkovia.

The moonlit towers have become a landmark of the city. Their tolling begins and ends the day. Shipping crews keep their schedules synchronized to the hands of the clocks. However, what occurs inside the towers is ever a mystery, as Ciprian Johann permits only himself and his engineering apprentices inside.

#### Chimera Wharf

Chimera Wharf has grown to dominate Northside as the largest district, encompassing docks, workshops, warehouses, and union halls. Most residents are dockhands, sailors, merchants, or craftsmen. The workers here don’t consider themselves wealthy, forced to pay union dues and city taxes, so when coin goes missing, most folk don’t mind provided the amounts aren’t undercutting profits.

The district draws its name from the monster hunters who helped found Malkovia, killing local threats to keep the infant settlement safe. A brass statue in their honor collects rust in a modest town square. It depicts a chimera slain beneath the blade of one of their forebears.

#### Hunters Guildhall

Today, the monster hunters’ guildhall doesn’t much reflect their warrior traditions. Unless a servant of the Crimson Court crosses over the river, there are fewer monsters to hunt in the heart of Malkovia compared to tax avoiders and petty thieves.

Only the older guild members have even been trained to hunt monsters properly. Newer recruits are little more than thuggish enforcers for the Matriarch, and their de facto guild leader, Oleg the Butcher.

#### Óvrosz

Óvrosz began as a separate settlement upriver from Malkovia, before urban expansion connected them. Home to the city’s wealthiest, the nobles of Óvrosz enjoy the clearest waters of the Roiling River before it passes through the filth of Malkovia. More space means larger estates. The south end of the district opens entirely onto the Jade Marsh.

The marshland isn’t suitable for building structures. Therefore, it remains a large midnight garden, groomed and cultivated on the coin of the nobility living in Óvrosz. Although open to the public, the nobility don’t take kindly to the unwashed commoners who may visit.

#### Mooreland Manor

In the middle of Malkovia, the Roiling River splits around a low, crescent-shaped island known as the Mooreland. Once nothing but grass, reeds, and mud, the island now contains the large estate of Malkovia’s ruler. For many generations that was the noble household, von Dülvet, until the family was massacred by the Matriarch of Malkovia.

Mooreland Manor is a lavish and multi-story mansion, surrounded by well-kept gardens and sculpted hedges. The manor and its grounds are used extensively for special occasions of both business and pleasure. An invitation from the Matriarch to attend her at her home could be a lucrative opportunity, but one that should be considered with caution. Business conducted on the Mooreland isn’t always clean.

#### Miskavir

The most crowded district of Southside is Miskavir, home to the Etharin Market. Countless shops, stalls, and services shout for attention among the throng of visitors to Malkovia. The market is a gateway to the rest of the world. Charnelaise silks, Castinellan wines, Valikan tapestries, and even Büracher automatons find their way into Ostoya through Malkovia’s markets, which isn’t closely watched by either the Crimson Court or the Council of Mages.

However, Miskavir is a perilous place. Deals are cut as often as throats. Such an unregulated marketplace attracts thieves and smugglers, some of which even sell body parts and viscera that can be offered as a blood tax on behalf of the Southside. Where these body parts are sourced would be a dangerous investigation.

#### The Drain

Somewhere deep underground, beneath the city streets of Miskavir, is a notorious fight pit known as the Drain. Combatants fight to the death within a grated cage, suspended above a funnel. The funnel catches all blood and gore lost from the warriors within, which is then offered to the Crimson Court to further cover Malkovia’s blood tax. Fighters are encouraged to gut and bleed out their opponents, maximizing the blood collected and therefore the prize paid out.

The Drain is a common place for scum of all sorts to gather. Betting on fights is a grim avenue to escaping poverty. Voluntary fighters are rarer than involuntary ones, who are usually prisoners of the city, unfortunate travelers who upset a local, or political rivals to Ylana Svarog. The Drain is commonly a stage for executions and other tragic ends.

#### Lupul

To the far west in Southside is Lupul, casually called the banking district, where Malkovia stores much of its wealth. Most of the Southside nobility reside here. So too does a garrison of soldiers who protect the coin repositories and noble estates, though they seem more of a disheveled gang than a trained military.

Malkovia’s money is reposited south of the Roiling River to further avoid taxation from Raevo, in the event Castalore ever attempted to exert such authority over the city. Nevertheless, the banks are close to Óvrosz, so nobles never need travel far to count their coins.

In the darkness of Ostoya, the Ebon Syndicate uses the Malkovian banks to siphon funds for themselves. This arrangement is not entirely unknown, and the Syndicate is a quiet supporter of Matriarch Király for as long as she sanctions their activities. In exchange for the coin they steal from Malkovia, the Ebon Syndicate feeds information to the Matriarch from across Ostoya, acquires particularly difficult to obtain objects from distant places, and makes the Matriarch’s most dangerous rivals disappear when the situation calls for subtlety.


### Important Individuals

#### Matriarch Mariska Király

Mariska’s story echoes many of the lost and nameless children of Malkovia. Born a street urchin and surviving only through cunning and skills she had beaten into her, Mariska’s short life was almost ended when the waters broke the riverbanks during the Darkfall. A monster slayer named Grivr rescued her. A matter of right place, right time, as he often reminded Mariska.

Siraj Fakhri Mariska Király Strict training and painful mutations preceded the young elf into adulthood. She was tempered into a deadly devourer -trained monster hunter whose legend quickly grew, and Grivr and Mariska hunted frequently south of Malkovia, where undead now roamed freely. This dangerous trade in a realm newly ruled by vampires cost Grivr his life. Mariska’s middle years were spent as a mercenary in the city, demanding a high price for her particular skills. While dissatisfaction mounted with Malkovia’s rulership in the aftermath of the Darkfall, the monster hunter amassed wealth and connections with unions, syndicates, and merchants. When the ruling household had lost the faith of its people, Mariska landed her own militia upon the Mooreland and took Malkovia for herself. Shortly after, she kicked down just about every wall of regulation that limited trade, flooding the city with wealth. She forged alliances among her connections to strengthen her militia. In Northside, the self-titled Matriarch is a champion of the people who keeps the armies of both Soma and Raevo at bay. In Southside, Mariska is considered a thug, wielding threats and violence to enforce her regime. Despite her success maintaining Malkovia’s independence, Mariska is surrounded by vipers. Those who would rather give the city to Soma or Raevo, and those who would rather sit in the Matriarch’s seat, wait for her downfall.

#### Oleg the Butcher

Oleg is the overboss of Malkovia’s Northside, governing the districts and enforcing the city’s laws on behalf of the Matriarch. Though order ultimately reigns in Malkovia, there’s much Oleg is willing to let slide, provided city dues are still being paid.

Better known to his friends and enemies as the Butcher due to his skills learned as a monster hunter, Oleg’s specialty is killing vampires who dare to cross the river. The grudgel’s belt is laden with body parts harvested from his prey. When dealing with mortal criminals, Oleg doesn’t hesitate to devour portions that inflict terrifying mutations, making himself stronger.

Having been trained by the Matriarch herself, Oleg’s loyalty to Mariska has never been questioned. But he hates the vampires more than she. Becoming a devourer has changed his biology into a dhampir, a fact that Oleg mourns and loathes.

He meets frequently in secret with Raevan rebels and hopes to provide an edge to expanding their influence within the city. Should conflict erupt in Malkovia, the Butcher will gladly wet his axes with the blood of the damned.

#### Ylana Svarog

Once an exceptional student of the Ravencourt Sanctuary, Ylana Svarog is constantly underestimated in Malkovia due to her gnomish stature. Her enemies that consider Ylana easy prey are quickly reduced to windswept ashes.

Despite her arcane talents, Ylana never felt at ease among the rigid bureaucracy of Castalore or the Council of Mages, preferring the free-spirited culture of Malkovia. For a time, she was the court mage to the von Dülvet nobility. She won her position as overboss of Southside by helping facilitate Mariska’s coup and has overseen the function of the city’s markets and banks ever since.

Despite coming into power alongside the Matriarch, rumors persist that Ylana has become at odds with Mariska and Oleg. The gnomish wizard has found her own source of wealth and resources by dealing with delegates from the Crimson Court, and she permits the spread of a dogma that worships the vampires as seraphs. Allowing this religiosity to grow in Malkovia erodes Mariska’s, and therefore Ylana’s, authority within the city. It seems unlikely the wizard would permit this if she didn’t have certain assurances from Soma.

#### Marienne Marilyn

Marienne was born into nobility but couldn’t help herself from wanting more. Affluence bored her, and a dangerous thirst for excitement and eyes for opportunity led to her exploring a passion for thievery. Small thefts from other nobles weren’t enough. Her indiscretions escalated into embezzlement, until she was caught and forced to flee from her former life in disgrace.

Starting over on the shadowy streets of Malkovia, Lady Marilyn already had contacts with the Ebon Syndicate and Matriarch Király. A confidant and mediator between the two, Marienne is now considered the head of the Ebon Syndicate in Malkovia and its most powerful agent in Ostoya. The moonlit city is the Syndicate’s safest gateway into the empire. Its independence shields their activities from the talons of the Court or the eyes of the Council of Mages.

Despite her opulent upbringing, Lady Marilyn is not easily bullied or manipulated. What she lacks in grit she makes up for in charm and cunning. Her true occupation is a guarded secret. While Marienne and Mariska Király pretend at having a close friendship, the Matriarch is ever aware of the knife held against her throat, should her decisions inconvenience the Syndicate’s operations in Malkovia.

#### Father Elijah

A tragic martyr. A divine prophet. A bloodthirsty deceiver. Father Elijah is all of these and more, depending on who is asked in Malkovia. Downcast from the heavens during the Gods’ End, Elijah wandered Etharis without a cause. Faithless. Spent. That was, until he became a vampire. Father Elijah’s path is now strikingly clear. He is once more divine, a gift returned to him when the sanguine curse ran down his throat in rivulets. In his own words, this revealed to Elijah an ancient truth. Vampires are seraphs, born of a god buried in the furthest depths of the City Below, with a mandate to kill the faithless and elevate the worthy to conquer death entirely. Elijah has proselytized across Ostoya, emboldening its dour citizens to hope, to yearn, to crave the promise of immortality. Most recently, the Father has taken up residence in southern Malkovia, intent to bring the Free City into the fold of the Crimson Court. His speeches draw crowds who he enraptures with miracles made possible through both the power of vampirism and the dwindling sparks of his divinity. Father Elijah weaves songs, to which the congregation dance in celebration of life and weep at the finality of death, before he showers them with food and gifts brought from the broad farmlands of Terinima. Even Malkovia’s nobility find the Father fascinating. His promises of power through the divine providence of vampirism is intoxicating. He has masses of followers from every district of the city, threatening Malkovian leadership with a new banner for its people to unite beneath. Elijah has plans to do just that, and perhaps instead of indoctrinating the city into the influence of the Crimson Court, he’ll take it for himself. After all, what’s greater than a Grand Duke? ### Quest Hooks

#### Drowned Underworld

Grimhilde is a strange and sordid Ralakian local. She often wanders into the Etharin Market, looking for daring individuals who might like to help her extract treasures from the depths of Fissure Lake. Having discovered gold coins and strange trinkets on the lake’s muddy banks, Grimhilde has convinced herself that further ancient riches must be hidden just below the dark water’s surface. The urchin offers what she’s found so far to help entice prospective divers.

The water beneath Fissure Lake is murky and filled with floating corpses. They drift in and out of view like fleeting nightmares, some thrashing about weightlessly in the silent gloom whenever light draws near.

Crushed buildings that fell into the lake during The Darkfall are scattered along the bottom, the haunting visage of a drowned city greeting divers who make it this far. Tunnels into the City Below proper can also be found. Bone-laced passageways and ancient chambers that have not been seen by mortal eyes in unknowable centuries. Some are still submerged in muddy water while others have been drained. The necropolis spreads out from beneath Fissure Lake to other parts of Malkovia, and possibly to other parts of Ostoya, if a journey far enough is made. The types of lost treasures and arcane artifacts that could be unearthed here are outnumbered only by the number of undead horrors lurking within this drowned underworld.

Fissure Lake can be explored across multiple expeditions, encountering different monsters and treasures each time. Grimhilde expects to share in any recovered wealth. They can, of course, be ignored but may seek restitution from the Southside overboss if they believe they’ve been cheated.

#### Noble Prey

Lord Rathulf is a wealthy Óvrosz noble obsessed with hunting. While not a warrior himself, he’s a patron of the local monster-hunting guildhall, receiving certain benefits including guild appointed bodyguards and favors from Oleg the Butcher. He’s traveled across Etharis killing monstrosities and building a fortune selling rare salvage in the Etharin Market.

Now with his coin and connections, Lord Rathulf enjoys playing a most dangerous game.

Rathulf has posted a bounty on a beast that’s stalking the commonfolk of Malkovia from within the Jade Marsh. He believes it to be a rogue strigoi or ecoukrylo vampire. Too old to hunt it himself, and no longer trusting the local guildhall’s aptitude against an actual monster, he contracts independent mercenaries, offering a considerable reward to whomever brings him a hunting trophy as proof of the deed.

The monster in question is a powerful werewolf ravager—a fact that becomes clear after some investigation. What isn’t obvious is that the lycanthrope is Rathulf himself. He intentionally deceives mercenaries into pursuing him before turning them into his prey.

Oleg the Butcher is aware of Lord Rathulf’s scheming pastime. If Rathulf is defeated and Oleg is informed, the overboss feigns ignorance but resolves to pay the bounty on the Lord’s behalf.

#### Gangs of Malkovia

Tensions on Malkovia's streets threaten a violent gang war. Monster hunters from the Northside guildhall have slain vampires in the city’s south, openly displaying the carcasses in defiance of the Crimson Court. Ylana Svarog has petitioned the Matriarch to punish Oleg and his thugs for these crimes. Guild hunters who cross the bridges into Southside are now attacked on sight by Ylana’s own enforcers.

Meanwhile, the teachings of Father Elijah have spread into Northside and increase sympathy for the Crimson Court. Oleg appears powerless to stop this religious infiltration. He’s outlawed the faith on his side of the river, enraging his own citizens and southside believers.

Matriarch Király hires independent investigators to act as her secret and personal agents. She suspects a greater plot to destabilize Malkovia is afoot and intends to learn if the threat is coming from Raevo or Soma. Her agents are asked to spy on both Oleg and Ylana in hopes of finding ways to ease tensions. The investigation involves discovering who has been inciting the skirmishes occurring in the streets.

The Matriarch’s spies may eventually discover a young and talented monster hunter named Darius, who is partially responsible for the vampire killings. He was raised and trained by Oleg himself. However, Darius also secretly spreads Father Elijah’s religious propaganda. The boy’s full name is Darius von Dülvet. He is the last heir of Malkovia who survived the Matriarch’s massacre of his family. Now old enough to fight, he intends to take his revenge on Mariska. The first step is destabilizing her authority.

The Matriarch’s spies can choose to either capture Darius and bring him before Mariska or help with his vengeful crusade. Either choice may change the fate of Malkovia.

#### For Whom the Bells Toll

Malkovia’s clock towers have stopped ringing. Additionally, commoners have recently gone missing near the three towers. Surely, this isn’t a coincidence. This has prompted the Matriarch to send investigators to look into the matter.

Exploring the clocktowers discovers arcane libraries hoarded with blasphemous books. Eldritch laboratories are filled with nightmarish constructs. These half-living humanoids have had their bodies painfully grafted with fiery machinery and gruesome weapons. Also found throughout the towers are the manic scribblings of Ciprian Johann. The gnomish eccentric owns the three buildings, and until now has permitted very few to see inside.

Johann’s writings exalt his own brilliance. He believes the three clocktowers are his master work, unrivalled even in Stehlenwald. Johann obsessively turns away tourists who have come to marvel at the towers’ magnificence, thinking the common rabble would desecrate his three holy edifices.

The gnomish artificer has been captured by his own pride. In the highest levels of the clocktowers, the mechanisms have become twisted and daemonic. The cogs are powered by fiery arcane objects. Malikirian imps flock like bats inside the belfry.

Ciprian himself is transforming into a fiend, having grafted his own body with hellish prosthetics. Unless he is stopped, he begins capturing commoners to subject them to the same horrific procedures.
