# The Ostoyan Empire

The sun never rises on the Ostoyan Empire. An aristocracy of vampires — the Crimson Court — rules from the shadows, governing through fear, blood tithes, and centuries of accumulated power.

Located east of the Grey Spine, Ostoya was founded centuries ago by Bürach secessionists — migrants who fled the Bürach Empire's authoritarian rule and the edicts of the Hearthkeepers. Ostoya was a new land anyone could claim. In recent times, it has become as fractured and monster-ridden as their Bürach cousins across the western mountains.

Of its six provinces, Raevo has split from the Empire and wages civil war to liberate the others from the Crimson Court's grip. Even within the loyal provinces, power is elusive. Court members must quell rebellion in their own territories while watching each other — the machinations of the nobility run as deep as their thirst.

For common citizens, life is dark and terrifying. Peasants work land loaned by deathless masters and pay crushing tithes. But what the nobility demand beyond coin is far costlier. All who are ruled by the Crimson Court fear the blood tax. As the peasant saying goes: "Only in death will your toils end." But even that is a lie — the dead in Ostoya are often forced to toil further upon the bloody strings of their vampiric masters.

## Geography

The land of Ostoya was once beautiful and bountiful — navigable rivers, resource-rich mountains, mighty forests, and vast plains. Roads connected towns, making trade and travel common. The Darkfall changed all that. Eternal dusk struck the land, crops became hard to maintain, perils spread from swamps and forests, and natural beasts gave way to monsters. What was once a haven became a realm of nightmares.

### Forests

The dense forests of Ostoya could provide timber and game, but those entering the woods are likely to become prey.

- **Stillborn Forest** (south): Thick yew and oak with wide-reaching branches and coiling roots that make travel slow and perilous. The weaving canopy blots out moon and stars. Creatures prowl the dense terrain; bright lanterns and rustling steps advertise travelers as dinner. Even experienced hunters run afoul of the horrors that emerged during the Darkfall — far too monstrous to playact as nobles.
- **Sunstretch Forest** (north, along Grey Spine): Massive grey redwood and fir sentinels that once witnessed the sun's warmth. Deep within lies the Solar Grove, tended by a circle of druids who renew hope that light may return to northern Ostoya.
- **Dark Wood** (central): Thinning black trees bridging secessionist Raevo with the rest of the Empire. Sullen landscape scarred by crossed roads, host to carnivorous plants.

### Waterways

The rivers flowing through Ostoya are essential to its trade and transportation — deep veins of water coiling across the nation, feeding settlements with fresh supplies and enabling rapid movement of troops and information.

- **Roiling River**: Runs from beneath Mt. Vengeance through Malkovia to Raevan Bay. Starts as scalding rapids heated by volcanic activity, cooling in temperature and speed as it travels eastward.
- **Lake Iorta** (south of Fallowheart): Feeds the Tithewater River (south through Stillborn Forest to Glasswater Lake and the port city of Lunsk) and the Great Arteriyan River (east, bisecting Ostoya through Crowsbend before splitting near the Black Mire).
- **Black Bay**: Nov Ostoya's coastal harbor — a gateway to, and guardian against, the other nations of Etharis. Tremendous naval trade and whaling.

### Mountains

The Grey Spine Mountains act as a defensive border, a source of valuable resources, and a funnel for travel between the Ostoyan and Bürach Empires. Several paths exist through the mountains, though all are difficult and guarded. Immigrants, merchants, and wanderers traverse the Grey Spine at great risk and expense.

- **Elian Pass**: The original migrant route between Grabenstein and Fallowheart. Heavily guarded by both empires. Travelers are invasively inspected for contraband, interrogated as potential spies, and — if permitted to pass — must pay a heavy tax.
- **Valley of Mists**: Northern passage, narrow and choked with cold fog that never dissipates. Often completely covered by towering cliffs above, becoming a labyrinth of beckoning caves. Bones of lost travelers are frequently found within. Those who escape report being racked with despair, believing they would never find their way out. Survivors rarely take the return trip the same way.

### Weather

Ignoring the weather in Ostoya can be deadly. Howling winds tear through trees. Rain falls near-daily, turning black earth into thick mire. Fog ranges from wispy ground-blankets to curtains concealing lurking predators.

Regardless of weather, Ostoya never sees the sun. Few still alive remember its warmth. Most Ostoyans have never been outside their home villages and have never seen direct sunlight. The youngest generations make up stories about what they imagine it looks like. Ostoya's fields are black and difficult to tend. Travelers have a saying across the Grey Spine: warmth in Ostoya is like honesty in Altenheim.

The exact cause of the eternal darkness is a mystery. But the vampiric nobility revel in its presence.

## History

Ostoyan history has been written in blood.

### Founding

The first migrants to flee the Bürach Empire settled this land as their own, paying in lives to keep it. They founded scattered settlements around farming, lumber, and mining, spreading to the shores and establishing Nov Ostoya on the natural harbor of Black Bay, which became an international hub of seafaring trade.

The Bürach Empire forced a simple choice: pay imperial taxes or die. Ostoya refused. Thus began the first war between the two empires. Ostoya scrambled as a young nation, ill-prepared for conflict, but the Bürach could only assault through the Elian Pass, and Ostoya's provinces were rich with resources. The war was incredibly demanding for both sides. Victory is still sung today — Ostoya survived the mages of Erlefurt and black powder of Stehlenwald. It was at Fallowheart the Bürach Empire finally broke, the fortress Kaer Vena withstanding every siege.

But Kaer Vena was not built by Ostoyan hands. It existed long before any migrants journeyed through the Grey Spine. Something ancient slept beneath the soil.

### The Darkfall

The second Bürach-Ostoyan war came when the fracturing Bürach Empire made a common enemy of Ostoya. Years of bloody fighting unfolded, until a world-shaking event occurred.

The entire realm shook. Ravines spread across Ostoya like opening veins. The sun turned orange, dripping from the sky like sparks of golden liquid falling beneath the horizon. Darkness crept into the land. From beneath the earth, tides of undead monstrosities charged from the fissures to devour the living.

Nov Ostoya was the epicenter. Beneath the city, a vast necropolis was revealed by the earthquakes — teeming with undead, pulsing with dark energy like a heartbeat beginning to awaken. The aristocracy sent troops to investigate, then panicked and ordered the rifts collapsed with magic, sealing their own soldiers beneath the earth. This act sealed the fate of Ostoya.

### The Tragedy of Morgen Hund

Morgen Hund was a true champion of Ostoya. Born to traveling merchants, he grew up on the road, visiting every province. He learned to fight defending his family's caravan, then enlisted when war broke out. His valor and hatred for the invaders made him popular among troops. He rose to leadership as fellow soldiers died around him.

After Fallowheart was captured by the Bürach Empire, Hund was called back to Nov Ostoya as military envoy. Estspine had been lost and Büracher soldiers were advancing into Terinima and north to Raevo.

During Hund's stay in the capital, the earth shook and the sun disappeared. Morgen Hund was chosen to lead soldiers down into the necropolis. His orders: slay the undead, find the source of the scourge, and end it. With a battalion at his back, Hund descended. Neither he nor his soldiers ever returned alive.

After days of waiting, with ghouls continuing to emerge, the Grand Duke ordered the rifts collapsed. Arcane explosions buried the necropolis once more.

### The Sanguine Curse

Morgen Hund found something in the darkness beneath Ostoya. Something ancient. Something that had slept for untold centuries, awoken by the Darkfall. It changed Hund, transforming him into an undying and fearless monster that hungered.

Thought dead for weeks, Hund miraculously returned to Nov Ostoya, starving and furious. Betrayed by the Grand Duke, Hund began his slaughter in the capital by decapitating the Empire's leadership. He seized the military and drove the Bürach armies back across the Grey Spine over several bloody years.

But Hund was not alone. A horde of vampires rose with him, blood-starved after centuries buried beneath Etharis. These undead horrors aided Hund's crusade for vengeance. In return, he gave them the sovereignty of Ostoya.

Hund reinvented himself as Klaus Vorgen the Betrayed. In the years since, nobody knows what happened to Vorgen. Some say he was killed by another Court member; others claim he buried himself in the City Below. A few believe he's still alive somewhere in Ostoya, grieving over what he has wrought and hoping for redemption.

### The Crimson Court

The Ostoyan Empire is under the tyrannical rule of the Crimson Court — seven ruling vampire nobles who, through tenuous alliance, control every aspect of Ostoyan life. No true loyalty exists among them. Each noble vies for the head of the table, employing schemes that can take decades to bear fruit. Yet they know their grip on Ostoya only remains strong through shared strength.

Currently, the Court is ruled by **Grand Duke Drago Koshevek**. The remaining six members each rule over a province of the Ostoyan Empire.

### Vampiric Bloodlines

The sanguine curse is ancient. Its manifestations have divided into vampiric bloodlines, varying greatly in appearance, abilities, and attitudes. A vampire's bloodline may be determined by mortal heritage or the nature of their sire.

- **Soman**: Claim to be divine beings destined to rule, descended from a death god's blood. Dress lavishly, drink from wine chalices. Bend mortals through manipulation while playacting at benevolent sovereignty. Their grace and power enables them to mask as humanoids more easily than other bloodlines.
- **Upyr**: Claim to be the first and most ancient bloodline, having abandoned humanity and any pretense of appearing mortal. Crooked, emaciated frames — the very image of a living corpse. Rest in coffins filled with homeland soil. No machinations, no masquerade. Only the thirst. They shepherd mortals like a flock, caring nothing for worship or fear.
- **Fzeg**: Unholy combination of lycanthropy and vampirism. The ferocity of werewolves paired with ceaseless bloodlust. Most physically deadly of the bloodlines — cleaving claws and bloodsucking bite. Their difficulty lies in stopping.
- **Strigoi**: Pallid, gangly, wretched creatures. Bulging eyes above pointed fangs. Unrivalled nighttime predators who stalk from above — crawling upside down on cavern ceilings or beneath bridges. Feed on blood, flesh, and bone, making mutilated corpses of victims. Maintain mortal intelligence and cunning but have far less patience for courtly pretense. Many hunt freely through Ostoya's cities.
- **Ecoukrylo**: More batlike than humanoid. Sensitive ears and leaf-shaped noses enable hunting in pitch blackness over great distances, swooping on leather wings. Tend toward strange behaviors — hoarding trinkets, shrilling while feeding, excessive grooming. Despite their quirks, ecoukrylo are devious shapeshifters who disguise their true form within a noble masquerade.

## Provinces

Ostoya is divided into six provinces. Each lesser Court member is given autonomy over one region. Rulership is both honor and burden — failure to keep a firm grip is weakness, and weakness is an invitation to be removed from the Crimson Court, and possibly from Etharis entirely.

### Estspine

Home to the first Ostoyans, the province that gave birth to an empire. Its soil is stained with the blood of sacrifice and vengeance.

Estspine is under immense pressure — trapped between the Bürach Empire to the west, the Crimson Court to the south, and the Raevo Rebellion to the north. The province is a gateway to the Empire and a powerful symbol of resistance, independence, and pride.

**Fallowheart** looms above the river flowing from the Grey Spine. By ruling over Fallowheart, the Crimson Court legitimizes its reign. Raevo aims to liberate it, but both sides recognize that fighting over Fallowheart would create opportunity for a Bürach invasion. A tense standoff endures.

Economy: lumber, produce, and minerals exported via rivers. Estspine benefits from neighboring the Bürach Empire — daring merchants brave the Elian Pass to bring foreign wares. Stehlenwald black powder for Soman wines and Raevan talismans.

#### Notable Individuals

**Viscount Langkirk Warlow.** The most obsessive and pitiable Court member. Warlow didn't earn his position — he was raised to it by Koshevek. Whispers say the Grand Duke deliberately chose a weak ruler for Fallowheart, one he could easily manipulate. Other rumors claim the Viscount is expected to fail, giving Koshevek pretext to destroy him.

Warlow is an ugly, insecure strigoi who plays the part of noble badly — pretending to hold courtly dinners while horrifically devouring dismembered limbs before his subjects. Anyone who dares comment on his bad decorum or weak position is likely to end up on his plate.

**Castellan Gavril Bosković.** Stoic knight from Castalore who marched south with a mandate to liberate Fallowheart. Civilian lives within the city have stayed his hand from ordering an assault. Instead, he camps his army on the northern bank of the Arteriyan while his agents quietly foment rebellion within Fallowheart. If a civilian uprising were to occur, the Castellan is ready with soldiers to support them.

### Raevo

Some say Raevo was destined for rebellion. The province was founded when a young diviner, Lissandra, dreamed of a shadow stretching from Nov Ostoya. She followed her visions to the high cliffs of the north, where she saw a light. There she founded the fortified city of Castalore.

Raevans exhibit the spirit of resistance that began Ostoya. The commoners loathe the undead with righteous passion and consider anyone who serves the Crimson Court a traitor.

Since secession, Raevo thrives under a governing **magocracy**. It has become the epicenter of unrestrained magical study, spellcraft, and artifice. Magics deemed too dangerous elsewhere are the very strength Raevo uses against the Court's preternatural power.

Challenges: frequent snow and punishing rains, harvests no better than the rest of Ostoya without sunlight, restless dead requiring funerary rites that include decapitation, seasonal Valikan raids from the north, and undead incursions from the south — the Crimson Court exacting its blood tax one way or another.

**Castalore**: Largest Raevan settlement. Designed as fortress first, settlement second — an impregnable city on a mighty cliff with massive stone walls and layered spiral districts resembling a coiling python. Higher districts are more affluent. At the very top sits the Nightingale Tower, the magocracy's seat of governance. Never occupied by either the Bürach Empire or the Crimson Court.

Internal tensions exist: commoners don't fully trust mages, so loyalty is shared between the magocracy, the Order of Dawn, and the Solar Grove druids. While outwardly a coalition, the Order's ideals can hinder the magocracy's pragmatic rulership.

#### Notable Individuals

**Viscountess Nebüma Zeilgor.** Brilliant and inquisitive strigoi. Replaced Virgilia Petinscu after informing Koshevek of Petinscu's failures to bring Raevo to heel. Aware her appointment and life will be brief unless she shows progress. She and her strigoi progeny slink north to sow discord and weaken Raevo from inside. A shadow sorceress and spy, unafraid to bloody her own taloned hands. Advises the Grand Duke on religion, history, and arcane matters. Her ultimate desire: access to the Ravencourt Sanctuary, making her quest to capture Raevo a personal ambition.

**Mina Taliesin, the Lady of Masks.** First Minister of the Council of Mages for decades, defending her rule against strife, coups, and assassination attempts. Expansive spy network — some suspect she has a contact within Soma itself. Always conceals her face behind decorative masks, earning her moniker. Most believe she hides scars from an assassination attempt, but her enigmatic history and ties to the Ravencourt Sanctuary have spawned conspiracy theories. Seeks to destroy the vampire aristocracy but schemes cautiously — she will not trade the Crimson Court's bloodthirst for Bürach conquest.

**General Artemios Dulka.** Leader of the Order of Dawn. Has fought vampires since boyhood. Born a Soman noble before the Darkfall, his father was killed in Vorgen's cleansing of the nobility, forcing Dulka to flee north. Now elderly, he fights less in battles but personally trains new recruits with undimmed passion. His lifelong ambition: liberate the land that was once his.

### Terinima

The largest and central province, fed by the Great Arteriyan River which nourished its horizons into arable farmland. Terinima is extremely valuable to the Crimson Court — its large population of farmers, ranchers, hunters, and fishermen makes the province the breadbasket for all of Ostoya and provides feed for the vampires' humanoid livestock.

Terinimans are people of the soil with few ambitions beyond keeping their families safe and fed. Tending sun-seeking crops is difficult, but residents work with what they have. They're honest, blunt, and sturdy. Their lack of ambition makes them unlikely to resist, and they're inclined to report dissenters if it spares their families cruelty. Distrustful of outsiders but loyal to each other, they band together against banditry and prowling beasts.

**Crowsbend**: An ever-growing hub of interprovincial trade, gossip, and entertainment. The one place most locals travel to. A muddy town of ever-expanding streets, centered on a riverside market clamoring with farmers, livestock, artisans, traders, and bards singing of distant sunlit realms.

#### Notable Individual

**Countess Augustine Vasinesca** (ecoukrylo): Among the most charming Court members, surprisingly for an ecoukrylo. Known as the "Blood Sommelier" — her keen smell detects the most peculiar or decadent blood. Teriniman subjects fear being brought before her; once she detects a new scent, she insists on adding them to her stills. Advises the Grand Duke on food production and supply. Her true value: finding the most delightful blood blends to please demanding vampiric palates. Popular at court, even if her compulsive chittering behavior is considered uncouth.

### Bezcodru

The province's borders start and end at the Stillborn Forest tree line. Known for lumber and ore. The Tithewater River flows through to Glasswater Lake, both critical for transporting resources. The waters, mountains, and woods make Bezcodru enviable — but all are home to fearsome beasts and deathless monsters.

Bezcodrans are coarse, blunt, always on guard. Under the suffocating boughs of the Stillborn Forest, anything could lurk in the rustling bush. They work, eat, sleep, and rarely leave home.

**Lunsk**: Most noteworthy settlement, built into the southern bank of Glasswater Lake. A port town without mercantile bustle. Large bonfires at its edges are patrolled by guards with backs to the flame, eyes trained on the woods. Villagers dim lights using red-tinted glass, trying to go unnoticed by the forest denizens.

Mercenaries and monster hunters can earn well escorting travelers through the Stillborn Forest, but the profession is lethal. Undead spirits drift between trees, ghouls follow the scent of warm blood, and deadliest of all are the hunting packs of fzeg — the Baron's own fledglings, permitted to hunt freely in his realm.

#### Notable Individual

**Baron Mihael, The Wolf** (fzeg): The closest thing to loyal among Court members. Prone to lycanthropic habits, he rules through violence more than any other. But he believes in the strength of the pack and rewards loyal servants richly. A fearsome and cunning warlord, greatly favored by the Grand Duke — much to other nobles' jealousy. He has ended many would-be usurpers, razed villages sheltering rebel sympathizers, and slaughtered vampire hunters who dared travel south of Raevo. All of Ostoya fears the Baron.

Given the rich barony of Bezcodru as reward for loyalty. Advises the Grand Duke on resource production and infrastructure. Built roads across the realm for swift troop deployment. Ensures fellow nobles have materials for castle defenses. When not required elsewhere, disappears into the Stillborn Forest. His fzeg retinue travels unseen between the trees, falling upon caravans or villages when the Wolf decides to collect his blood tax.

### Umbroi (The Black Mire)

Mud, muck, and misery. A dark, dank hellhole that swallows anything that wanders within. The ground is wet and thick, the air heavy with decay. Trees stooped into twisted shapes; ceaseless insect droning whittles at sanity. Thick with insidious plants that drag victims below, toxic fungi breathing spores. The thick peat mummifies humanoid remains. Even before the Darkfall, the Black Mire was renowned for tales of hungry corpses clawing from the mud. Glowing spirits lure wayfarers deeper. Hag covens sing terrible songs over bubbling cauldrons.

Umbroians are quiet, suspicious, preferring to mumble to themselves rather than converse. They survive by remaining hidden, using secret water channels between tiny settlements. Outsiders find the bog impossible to navigate without local aid — but the folk don't give up their secrets easily. When escorting travelers, they may take longer routes to keep the safest paths concealed. Coin holds little value; they trade in food, tools, or favors.

They share a morbid humor — sniggering at outsiders' fear of the bog, sometimes laughing when someone is dragged screaming into the water, even if it's one of their own. Rumors circulate of a growing cult of Beleth, the Arch Daemon of fear.

**Riven**: Largest settlement, on the southern edge. Stilted structures constantly sink and are rebuilt, causing the town to shift across the map. Visitors must usually navigate a completely different layout each time.

#### Notable Individual

**Marchioness Iulia Cutov.** Quiet and ruthless. Fools would assume her the weakest Court member — she plays this role gladly while biding her time. The heart of a schemer.

Rulership of the Black Mire was a deliberate insult — aside from peat, there's little value to extract. Her real role: patrolling the coastline with a fleet from Nov Ostoya, stopping Raevan spies from using the Mire as a hidden avenue into Ostoya. Advises the Grand Duke on naval power and seabound trade. Keeps Valikan reavers from the southern coasts and ensures Morencian merchants find passage through Castinellan blockades.

**Secret**: What no one knows — not even Vulpescu's spy rings — is that the veil of Umbroi has given Cutov the ability to speak to Raevo without the Court's knowledge. She holds secret talks with Raevan rebels and other nations, whispering promises of land and access to Ostoya's riches if they help her defeat the other Court members. She desires sole leadership of the Ostoyan Empire. The plan is precarious in its infancy, but the pieces are in place.

### Soma

The seat of power for the Ostoyan Empire, holding the majority of its wealth. Open plains of farmland, scattered woods, and a strong coastal border. Soma's defining landmark is **Nov Ostoya**, the City of Gargoyles. Built above the Black Bay, wealth flows through Nov Ostoya from all of Etharis. The city is a gaping maw draining blood from its own provinces. Streets divide into two districts: the dangerous Low Streets infested with gangs and bliss addicts, and the Crimson Heights, decadent neighborhoods of the vampire elite.

Nov Ostoya is still scarred by the Darkfall fissures. Beneath lies the **City Below** (Necropolis). Authorities try to keep entrances sealed, but minor earthquakes and curious explorers unseal them frequently. Ghouls and cursed creatures continue crawling out. The city is patrolled by the Crimson Court's armies like hounds protecting a flock — but folk fear their leaders as much as the monsters beneath the earth.

Somans range from affluent aristocrats to the desperate and destitute. They tend to be cutthroat. Gangs form in lower society while the powerful trade lives with the nobility to keep fangs out of their own throats. Trust is hard-won. In Soma, you watch your back and cover your neck.

#### Notable Individuals

**Grand Duke Drago Koshevek** (soman): Most powerful and ancient Court member. Cold, humorless, and considered cruel even by fellow nobles. Could be carved from marble.

The Grand Duke existed long before the first migrants crossed the Grey Spine. When Morgen Hund returned to the surface, Koshevek stood behind him. Koshevek remade Hund as Klaus Vorgen, unleashed the vengeful fledgling upon Ostoya's mortal leaders, and soon after, Vorgen was never seen again. All fear to be beneath Koshevek's piercing glare. His appetite for power and blood is ceaseless — he has ended loyal servants simply to quench his thirst. Yet his malice is calculated: he rewards those who reinforce his position while crushing dissent at its whisper.

Koshevek is aware of his limitations. Though he has led many war hosts, he avoids invading Raevo. He hates mortal magic and detests the acolytes of the Ravencourt Sanctuary, with which he perhaps shares a primordial history. Every deed since waking has appeared deliberate — as if planned for millennia.

**Countess Katalina Vulpescu** (soman): Blood, wealth, influence, strength, magic — all coalesce in Vulpescu. Alluring and flamboyant, the exact opposite of the Grand Duke. Infamous for hosting lavish, debauched parties at her estate near Voyd, playing the glamorous patron. This masks her true power: secrets.

Beneath the affable façade, Vulpescu is heartless and savage — she merely avoids getting dirt upon herself. Her network of spies, informants, and brutes rules a good portion of Nov Ostoya and surrounding Soma, extending like tendrils into the rest of Ostoya and beyond.

Many believe she knows secrets about the Grand Duke himself, wielded as a blade to Koshevek's ribs while the two outwardly appear affectionate. The rest of the Court know to be wary. She advises the Grand Duke on national intelligence and foreign relations.

## Other Locations of Interest

### The Ravencourt Sanctuary

Miles from the Raevan shore, a blanket of fog called the Mistwall obscures the horizon. Those who sail into it become hopelessly lost. The Mistwall conceals an island home to one of the most secretive places in Etharis: the Ravencourt Sanctuary.

In Ostoya's distant past, the seer Lissandra discovered a derelict boat on the Raevan shore containing a wax-sealed envelope with ten names, including her own. She assembled her crew and sailed into the Mistwall, believed to have perished until they returned seven years later. Lissandra was no longer with the group. And each of the others had changed.

Every seven years the boat returns with nine mages, and ten new pupils depart with their names in the envelope. To be chosen is a great honor. All ten are inexorably in attendance when the names are read — yet never are all ten expecting to be called. Only nine return. What happens to the tenth is unknown. Are they sacrificed to the mysterious Keeper? Do they remain as servants or teachers? None but the Keeper know.

The Sanctuary itself is a crumbling castle above shoreline cliffs, enveloped by ceaseless storm. Lightning is constant. Debris from the castle drifts through the air, including entire towers orbiting the central keep like an orrery.

Graduates describe the interior as dreamlike. Doors don't always open into rooms that make physical sense. A small hallway may lead to a resplendent library of arcane knowledge unseen elsewhere in Etharis — the same door the next day opens into a scorch-marked laboratory with lurking monstrosities. Lodgings are damp; the only fire to be had is conjured with magic.

Magic is taught entirely without restraint. Students read tomes forbidden in Erlefurt or Tol Leyemil, commune with spirits, conjure daemons for interrogation, and may make pacts. Spells offensive to the Arch Seraphs are practiced openly. Students who cannot endure such trials return scarred rather than empowered.

**The Keeper**: This mysterious entity can appear human but is not. Acts as guide more than teacher, whispering from quiet darkness and encouraging each pupil to follow their deepest ambitions. Omnipresent within the grounds — often in more than one place at a time. Sometimes takes the shape of a raven, other times a loved one, rival, or a student's greatest fear. The eyes are always the same — rapturous and deep, pupils a subtly swirling cosmic void.

Nobody is certain of the Keeper's intent. Conspiracists claim it heads the Thaumaturge, a secretive mage cabal most don't believe exists. Others ponder if the Keeper is the Sanctuary itself — a place where magic coalesces and calls to those able to grasp it.

### The Solar Grove

Hidden deep in the forests of western Raevo. At its center grows the **Solarwood** — a magnificent tree with silver bark and burnished gold leaves. Radiant light showers beneath its boughs. Grass and flowers burst with life and color among its roots. The druids seed and harvest plants within the tree's glow.

The Solarwood was cultivated by **Archdruid Sylina Florian**, old enough to remember the true sun. Well-read in her youth, Sylina heeded the ancient writings of Lissandra and anticipated the Darkfall, performing rituals to imbue a sapling with solar power. By the time of the Darkfall, the Solarwood had already towered above neighboring trees.

The Solar Grove is hallowed ground. Undead cannot come within the light; vampires are burned by its radiance. But secrecy keeps the grove safe — if the Crimson Court learned of its existence, they would stop at nothing to destroy it. This has made Sylina reluctant to join Raevo's rebellion.

Both Mina Taliesin and General Dulka know of the grove. Dulka envisions using the Solarwood's bark — exceptionally durable, able to deflect axe blows, warm to the touch, and deadly to vampires — as timber for weapons. Sylina is horrified and refuses to sacrifice the grove's sole hope, but has provided a handful of smaller branches. Solarwood weapons are therefore extremely rare, carried by veteran Order of Dawn knights.

### The City Below

Untold centuries before the Ostoyan Empire, eastern Etharis was home to a civilization whose very name has been lost. Its bones have been unearthed by the Darkfall.

The City Below is a labyrinthine necropolis concentrated beneath Nov Ostoya but reaching as far as Fallowheart and Malkovia, running through the land like veins beneath skin. Broken streets and ruined buildings — temples, statues, houses swallowed by the earth. The walls are decorated with magnificent murals. A forgotten language is scribed into the stonework, describing ancient myths and stories.

How deep the necropolis descends remains a mystery. It teems with zombies, ghouls, skeletons, and wraiths — believed to have once been citizens of the old civilization.

**Secret**: The Crimson Court spreads the lie that they are the most ancient and dangerous creatures to awaken from below. But archaeological evidence suggests something even greater is buried in the dark — something the vampires themselves dread and revere.

## Quest Hooks

### Saga of the Hidden Sun

A lone, wounded Valikan named Erik of Clan Morgöng arrives at the Solar Grove, claiming he sailed south to raid the Crimson Court's shores. His crew were slaughtered and he's been barely outpacing Viscountess Zeilgor's strigoi. Sylina fears Erik has led the strigoi to discover the grove. She needs adventurers to escort the Valikan back to his homeland — Erik promises Clan Morgöng will raise a warband to join Raevo's rebellion. The fastest route from the Solar Grove to Valika passes through the Valley of Mists.

Sylina offers Solarwood stakes to the party, provided they slay the pursuing strigoi. The group must then brave the Valley of Mists — treacherous terrain, deep natural dungeons, and fearsome monstrosities. They'll know they've made it when they first see the sun.

### An Evening to Remember

Countess Vulpescu is hosting an evening gala at her estate to assassinate Viscount Warlow — unsanctioned by the Grand Duke. She wants to make room in the Court for her consort, Lady Valentina Bergow.

She provides assassins with invitations and a yellow crystal: placed in Warlow's goblet, it dissolves instantly and turns the blood to acid. The challenge is spiking his drink unnoticed. Alternatively, the crystal could be fed to a mortal whom the strigoi then devours entirely.

Vulpescu maintains plausible deniability — she'll feed failed assassins to the Court without hesitation. The assassins could also choose to use the crystal against Vulpescu herself, or any other Court member at the gala.

### Like Lambs to Slaughter

Teriniman shepherd Ciril Meri hires guards to escort 600 sheep to Crowsbend market. Several days through Ostoya's eternal night, past restless cemeteries, City Below ghouls, crawling graves from ancient battlefields, and desperate bandits near Crowsbend.

Payment scales with survival rate: 6 leura/sheep if >70% survive, 5 standard, 4 if <40% make it.

### Minesweeping

The Burrowfell Mines are the largest iron ore source in the Empire, vital to Soma's war efforts. Baron Mihael watches closely — if production doesn't meet expectations, he raises his blood tax.

Foreman Cecilia Krieg needs exterminators. In the deepest tunnels, walls have collapsed to reveal entranceways into the City Below. New treasures have pleased the Baron, but undead horrors are crawling up the mineshafts and attacking workers.

The mines are a huge complex. Krieg offers ongoing payment for mercenaries to patrol and kill anything already dead. Wardens keep any City Below treasures. Baron Mihael may visit at any time — if production has slowed, Krieg's mercenaries won't be spared the blood tax.

### The Riven Rascals

Something in the Black Mire is stealing children. The Marchioness relents to hire monster hunters in exchange for a higher blood tax. A distraught child, Blair, witnessed her friends being taken. She knows she's being given as bait but insists on accompanying the hunters.

Deep in the Mire, Blair points to glowing lights her friends followed — floating wisps bobbing playfully with echoing sounds of children laughing. Following leads to two spirits beneath a crooked tree: a man and woman, tattered and mold-eaten, with will-o'-wisps orbiting them. Beneath the tree lies a horrific mound of small bodies.

The wraiths can be banished easily. If spoken to instead, they recount how their own child went missing in the Black Mire. They died searching and have been taking children since in confused spectral grief. Finding the fate of their lost child lets them depart peacefully — but investigating further could uncover something worse tied to Riven.

### The Raven's Apprentices

In a Raevan harbor village, strangers meet for the first time. A wizard reads their names from a wax-sealed letter, inviting them aboard a boat bound for the Ravencourt Sanctuary.

As apprentices, they will face dangers intended to challenge them individually and as a group. Each specializes in different magic. They practice arcane arts while exploring the Sanctuary's countless passages and hidden chambers — some doors may lead to other planes of existence.

Each apprentice develops their own relationship with the Keeper, who may adopt the form of lost loved ones or confront them with the horrors of their past. When seven years have passed, the apprentices return to Raevo. One carries a wax-sealed envelope of their own.

## The City of Fallowheart

Fallowheart is caught in the jaws of conflict. It lies on Ostoya's western border, the first bastion against Bürach invasion. Throughout its history, it has repelled countless assaults — any invasion is doomed without first capturing Fallowheart. At the same time, the battle for Ostoya's soul between the Crimson Court and Raevo is concentrated here.

### Geography

**Kaer Vena** casts a dark shadow over Fallowheart. The ancient fortress is built on a rocky crag in the Arteriyan River, causing water to crash around it. Virtually impregnable, it is the seat of power in Estspine — currently the blood fort of Viscount Warlow.

Fallowheart sits on the southern bank, its great walls enclosing the inner boroughs. Dark forest besieges the city on all sides; twisted branches scrape at the walls like clawing fingers.

The southern road leads toward the Stillborn Forest and Nov Ostoya, forking toward the Grey Spine and the Bürach-held mountain pass. A small Bürach fort garrisons at the pass's eastern base. Spies and scouts from both nations watch constantly; neither moves without the other noticing.

The bridge crossing the Arteriyan leads north to Malkovia and Castalore. Those roads are traveled by Raevo soldiers, where the Court's influence thins. Skirmishes between Raevo militias and Soma loyalists are common.

River travel via the **Brevic** docks is the fastest and safest transport, heavily favoring trade with southern provinces. Fishing in the Arteriyan feeds the city regularly.

- **Malach**: Small fishing village on the north bank. Rest stop for travelers, and a way in and out of the city unseen — for a smuggler's toll.
- **Blud**: Settlement clinging to the southern gate, grown around a former noble's estate now used as a tavern (the Baron's House). Travelers stop here for news before entering or leaving the city.

### History

The first emigrants discovered Kaer Vena — empty and silent, built by a vanished civilization. Its architecture was unlike any known human or dwarven stronghold: arching and eerily ornate, with skeleton murals lining corridors and sculpted bats above doorways. But a radiant sun was carved into the great doors — its likeness emblazoned above mantels and painted across ceilings. To the migrants, it was Aurelia's sigil, convincing them this land had been promised to them.

**Saint Dayla**: A farmwoman of Estspine whose rage at Bürach oppression, channeled through the Arch Seraph Solyma, transformed the Ostoyans from meek farmers into warriors of defiant fury. The first war of secession forged Ostoya into a nation. Dayla is revered; her bones are buried beside the cathedral that bears her name.

**The Darkfall at Fallowheart**: During the second Bürach war, with Kaer Vena fallen to invaders, Klaus Vorgen returned from Nov Ostoya bringing death. No longer concerned with preserving life, he ordered Ostoyans into battles they couldn't win to exhaust Bürach lines. When Ostoyans died, Vorgen raised them repeatedly until their corpses were too broken to serve. The Bürach retreated in terror. Strigoi and fzeg hunted them down without mercy. When his vengeance was satisfied, Vorgen gave Fallowheart to the Crimson Court to decide who should rule it.

### Notable Locations

#### Grinfield

Fallowheart's central and largest borough. Pointed architecture with steep tiled roofs and gothic archways, every surface engraved with thorny roses, skeletal bas-reliefs, or glaring owl faces — Kaer Vena's ancient macabre style influencing the entire city. Home to most public businesses, merchants, trade posts, and healers. Small multistory town houses line cobblestone streets.

- **The Marla**: Dominating five-story inn and taphouse at the heart of Grinfield, facing a main square with multiple intersecting streets. Largest inn in Fallowheart, excellent venue for gathering news. Recommended as the most comfortable lodgings — though folk warn against the top floor.
- **Cemetery**: A dark void among Grinfield's lights. Earth choked with corpses buried atop one another, ground uneven, tombstones lopsided. Fetid limbs poke from the soil, picked at by ravens. Near the center towers a mausoleum — the only other pre-Ostoyan structure, decorated with skeleton and gargoyle engravings. Rumored entrance to the City Below, but nobody has seen its interior for fear of the Gravekeeper.
- **Augustine Trading Post**: Public face of the Augustine Trading Company, which operates warehouses in Brevic. Large selection of resources, equipment, and occasional magic items. Also provides transport and message delivery. Officially neutral in the civil war, known to have contracts with both sides. Prizes access to vampire blood for Morencian research.

#### Brevic

Storehouses, ship masts, and the Belltower define Brevic's skyline. The northern corner meets the Arteriyan's muddy banks. Narrow, poorly lit streets wet with filth and fish offal. Folk are gruff, poor, and resentful — polite to anyone cleaner and better dressed, knowing the gold flows downriver.

- **Belltower**: Originally a watchtower to warn Kaer Vena of armies. Now barracks for rapid deployment of the Viscount's soldiers. Rumored hideout for the **Volitant** — underground enforcers loyal to the Crimson Court.
- **Fish Market**: Shanty stalls crowded into a muddy square. Cheap, damaged, or illicit goods alongside fresh fish. Always cacophonously busy — many eyes watching, but many places to get lost. Rumored battleground between the **Ebon Syndicate** and agents of the Volitant.

#### Cerest

The hill on which **Saint Dayla's Cathedral** is built. The great dome and steeples crown the hill like a shining beacon. A band of Raevo soldiers guard the steps — too few to stop the Viscount if he decided to bleed the cathedral entirely, but enough to chase away vandals and Soma loyalists.

The high arched windows are always lit by flickering candles. The congregation's singing spills into the city — an invitation for honest hearts to take shelter. Built to honor Solyma's prophet; Dayla's bones lie beside it. A symbol of resistance, though some consider the congregation mere agitators.

#### Est Vale

Eastern corridor between hills near Kaer Vena and the city walls. Larger dwellings of the wealthy, and barracks housing Fallowheart's garrison under the Viscount's command.

- **Violet Rose**: Upmarket taphouse where the wealthy liaise with the Viscount's circle. Its darker purpose: the privileged send substitutes to be fed upon, shirking blood tax obligations. Records ensure all taxes are paid.
- **Grisham's Tower**: Crumbling tower of mysterious age, predating the migrants. Fallowheart's walls awkwardly jut around it. Ornate iron-bound doors permanently barred, no light in windows. Approaching causes hair to stand on end — superstitious locals take this as proof of haunting. The educated detect a powerful eldritch aura.
- **Bridge Forts**: Two fortresses and twin watchtowers protecting the bridge to the northern bank. Water gates between supports can block river travel.

#### Kaer Vena

Great battlements watching over all of Fallowheart. Symbol of both Ostoyan resistance and Crimson Court oppression. Only a single stone bridge connects to the southern shore, protected by a fortified gatehouse. The Arteriyan flows treacherously around the crags beneath — landing boats is impossible, and the sheer cliffs beneath the castle walls can't be scaled.

Inside: cryptlike. High stone walls, small chambers, narrow passageways. Arched iron windows invite only darkness. Rotting doors, secret corridors, and gothic ornaments make it a sepulchral labyrinth.

Warlow's throne room is unimpressive. Flickering fireplace embers cast orange glow on cold stone. Heraldic shields, rusted weapons, and faded tapestries collect centuries of dust. The Viscount sits on a simple chair in a corner, attended by ash-faced courtiers shielding him from light. He listens from the darkness; a hunger glints in his eyes.

### Important Individuals

#### Viscount Langkirk Warlow

A hideous strigoi with yellowed bulging eyes, needle-like fangs, withered nose, and a crooked spine bent so far forward his hands could reach the ground if he wasn't leaning on a cane. Resembles a horrifying corpse dressed as a flamboyant noble. The frailty belies truth: Warlow is a powerful strigoi whose rare displays of strength produce sounds of bursting flesh and splintering bone.

Cruel, manic, and sometimes droll — masking withering rage and insecurity. Obsessed with power, fueled by anxiety about his subordinate Court position. He fears Fallowheart being snatched by an ambitious rival or a displeased Koshevek. The shred of civility he affords separates him from lesser monsters in his own mind. He playacts listening to petitioners and invites wealthy commoners to macabre dinners — guests must ignore the blood on his teeth and fingers.

Warlow shrewdly sorts allies from enemies, rewarding the former while plotting punishment for the latter. But he knows he can't simply kill at will. Maintaining resource production requires a functioning society, as does defending against the Bürach. He can't incite rebellion or weaken his army. He tolerates a measure of dissent but employs surreptitious weapons to eliminate threats while maintaining deniability.

#### Sister Abigail Florentine

Young acolyte who stepped up when Father Amriel was arrested for fomenting rebellion. Not yet ordained, but her valor and conviction make others follow. Publicly preaches kindness and the rule of law — she will not repeat her predecessor's mistakes. Privately, she knows she's a spiritual leader in wartime. Her cathedral is a beacon of hope; while it stands, Solyma's vengeance is possible.

Her congregation are the eyes and ears of the Raevo army camped on the northern bank. When the time comes, commoners will pour from Saint Dayla's Cathedral, pitchforks in hand, to overthrow the nobility.

#### Solyma's Champion

A **Pactbreaker** bound to Saint Dayla's Cathedral. Follows the commands of the leading priest, detecting those corrupted by malevolent influences. Currently serves as Sister Abigail's bodyguard. Anyone entering the cathedral with malintent must face the Champion.

Legends describe how Solyma forged celestial machines to defend Ostoya alongside Dayla's army. This construct is the last that remains in Fallowheart.

#### Stanislava

Dhampir leading the Fallowheart **Volitant**. Seeks favor from the Crimson Court by uprooting the Viscount's enemies in the city's underbelly, hoping to be legitimized into the nobility. Operates from the shadows, corrupting the city watch with coin earned through theft, blackmail, information brokering, and assassination.

#### Edward Vilgé

Career thief and assassin atop the **Ebon Syndicate** in Fallowheart. Born in Malkovia, no special loyalty — only coin. The Syndicate smuggles weapons and information to Raevo, earning Warlow's ire. His agents are hunted by the Volitant; his businesses targeted by city guards. Vilgé needs allies and will strike any bargain to keep out of the Viscount's fangs.

Headquarters: an unmarked boat in Brevic called the **Edict of Freedom**, prepared to escape at any moment. But the larger Syndicate would look poorly upon abandoning Fallowheart entirely.

#### The Gravekeeper

A lumbering revenant loosely wrapped in soiled rags, dragging a shovel through Fallowheart with the scraping sound of metal on cobblestones. Stalks the streets searching for cadavers to bury — breaking into homes, assaulting hearses, pulling dead vagrants from alleys. Remains are dragged through the streets and crushed into shallow graves without rites or ceremony.

Never harms anyone living who doesn't interfere — those who do end up buried themselves. Several attempts to destroy it have failed; when defeated, it always returns from the mausoleum rejuvenated and ready to resume.

Viscount Warlow has decreed it illegal to attack the Gravekeeper. Its undead visage gives credence to the Court's authority and keeps the populace afraid.

### Quest Hooks

#### Defeat the Gravekeeper

Sister Abigail secretly offers a reward to permanently destroy the Gravekeeper. This requires descending deep into the mausoleum's undead-infested catacombs to discover what curse binds it to eternal servitude.

#### Curse Breakers

The folktale of Thaddeus Grisham: an eccentric wizard who once lived in the Est Vale tower, advisor to the dukes of Kaer Vena before the Darkfall. The tower held prisoners given to Grisham, on whom he conducted experiments. When the moon hides and Fallowheart is darkest, some say the screams of his prisoners can still be heard.

Truth: Grisham was corrupted by Shadowsteel. He crafted curses and objects from the profane metal. His prisoners are now cursed monsters infesting the tower. Grisham himself haunts the highest level as a **Shadowsteel Ghast**, protected by arcane traps and conjured elementals. He's covetous of his Shadowsteel and arcane library, hostile to anyone unbidden.

Both Warlow and Abigail know the tower's treasures and secrets could be powerful in enemy hands.

#### Church and State

Two armies camp outside Fallowheart like rival wolves. Sister Abigail quietly searches for brave confidants to **assassinate the Viscount** — proving the Crimson Court isn't invincible. She can't risk open defiance; such provocation would give Warlow pretext to arrest her and demolish the cathedral.

Abigail only offers this quest to folk she trusts — those who have proven themselves. Entry to Kaer Vena requires deception (petitioning under false pretenses) or discovering secret passages in the cliffs leading to the castle tombs. Inside: loyal soldiers, vampiric spawn, and undead horrors beside the Viscount in darkened halls.

#### Snuff the Candle of Hope

The Viscount summons individuals of particular skill for an unscrupulous task: **kill every soul in Saint Dayla's Cathedral and burn it to the ground**. Unwilling to make martyrs, Warlow won't send soldiers. Saint Dayla's is consecrated ground — neither he nor his spawn can enter. The task is entrusted to those he can deny connection with.

The cathedral is defended by a small Raevo militia and Solyma's Champion, the Pactbreaker. Defeating the construct and slaying Sister Abigail will close the Court's grip on Fallowheart.

## The Free City of Malkovia

The "Free City" of Malkovia is conflicted in identity and allegiance. Bisected by the Roiling River and split between warring provinces, Malkovia was founded as an international market accepting business from everywhere in Etharis. After the Darkfall, both Raevo and Soma clutched for control of the city's commerce. Anything goes in Malkovian markets, so long as you pay the taxes.

No less than seven bridges provide access across the river. The city is a network of slick streets and cramped alleys, crowded with folk moving between markets and warehouses. Something is being sold on every street and corner — from shops, stalls, or sleeves. The affluent reap excessive profits while common citizens fight crushing poverty.

**Northside** favors the rebellion, denouncing the vampiric nobility as godless monsters. **Southside** favors the Court, preferring the security of a united empire and supernatural leadership. **Matriarch Mariska Király** insists the city remains independent, but her subordinates may have other ideas.

### Geography

Malkovia's architecture is as pointed, intricate, and arching as any Ostoyan city. Countless steeples, statues of weeping seraphs, hooded knights, and hackled werewolves beneath curved alcoves. Dark deeds go unnoticed in alleys, while cobblestone streets are witnessed by glowing lantern-lit windows.

Multistoried ornate terraces dominate the Northside, where the wealthy make their homes. The center of Northside is dominated by the wharf district. By contrast, Southside infrastructure has fallen into disrepair — the oldest parts flooded during the Darkfall and never restored.

#### The Roiling River

The river carves across northern Ostoya from Mt. Vengeance to the sea. It is the lifeblood of the city — essential for mercantile industry, food, transport, and waste. Pierced with a network of drains and pipes. Downriver is littered with filth and sewage. Impoverished "River Rats" throw nets into the waste or rake the riverbed hoping for small treasures.

The river averages nine hundred feet across through Malkovia. Tiny uninhabited islands dot the surface. Rushing waters cascade over shoals and between bridge supports, requiring skilled captains. The banks are thick with mud and marshland reeds — home to birds, fish, eels, and very often, monsters.

#### Fissure Lake

During the Darkfall, a destructive fissure opened on Southside, splitting the river. Undead poured from the earth as water torrented in, draining the river and dragging ships, buildings, and people into caverns below. A dam now seals off the lake. Fissure Lake remains a haunting reminder of the countless souls consumed by the Darkfall.

Filled with ghost stories — openings to the City Below may still exist underwater. Sodden corpses shamble onto the banks from time to time. Nobody dares fish or sail upon those waters.

The most frightening rumor: the lake is slowly draining. Year after year, the surface lowers. It is feared that one day soon it will be a wide-open door to the underworld.

### History

Spreading north from the Elian Pass, early migrants followed the Roiling River eastward until they arrived at a looming structure of rock and bone: an immense bridge. This solitary crossing enabled settlers to use both sides of the river. Here they began to build.

Malkovia quickly became a booming trade hub. Inevitably, illicit activity arose — smugglers and thieves found an attractive base of operations.

When the Bürach marched to claim Ostoya, Malkovia funded the war and recruited foreign mercenaries. During the Darkfall, the fissure unleashed undead on Southside, but the river flooded the chasm — saving the city while dragging many commoners into the darkness.

The Crimson Court seized power, Raevo rebelled, and Malkovia found itself on a new front. Both sides made promises and threats. Malkovia's preferred independence is absolute — its markets open to all, mortals and monsters alike.

### Notable Locations

#### Old Malkovia

Eastmost Northside district, where the first structures were built. Historic, ornate founding-era architecture. Proud bloodlines dating to the city's origins — wealthy but mostly old nobility of lesser houses.

#### The Ossuary Bridge

The most recognizable and infamous of Malkovia's seven bridges. Constructed from skulls, bones, and skeletons — pillars of vertebrae, ribcage columns, a wide street of teeth and bones laid like cobblestones. Discovered by the founding migrants as a lone edifice surrounded by swamp.

Carved along its length: "KREKI STOMALK OVIAVINDI." Originally only "MALK OVIA" was visible through mud, giving the city its name. The full phrase has since been translated from the City Below's language: "all are equal in death."

The Ossuary Bridge remains unshaken and undamaged even by the Darkfall. Every ship enters and leaves beneath it.

#### Ralakia (Lake Town)

Southside district flooded during the Darkfall and formation of Fissure Lake. Most of the water never drained, leaving permanent marshland. The most destitute district — a labyrinth of packed slums and tenements. Folk survive through grit, scavenging, and begging.

#### The Clocktowers

Three watchtowers on an eastern river bend, originally built against Valikan longboats. Purchased by gnomish engineer **Ciprian Johann**, who modified each with brass plates and internal mechanisms. Every tower has its own clockface; their collective chimes create an hourly symphony of thunderous bells echoing over Malkovia. Shipping crews synchronize to the clocks. What occurs inside is a mystery — Johann permits only himself and his apprentices.

#### Chimera Wharf

Largest Northside district — docks, workshops, warehouses, and union halls. Residents are dockhands, sailors, merchants, and craftsmen. Named for the monster hunters who helped found Malkovia, commemorated by a brass chimera-slaying statue in a modest square.

**Hunters Guildhall**: The older guild members are trained monster hunters; newer recruits are thuggish enforcers for the Matriarch and their de facto leader, Oleg the Butcher.

#### Óvrosz

West Northside. Home to the city's wealthiest, enjoying the clearest river water. The south end opens onto the **Jade Marsh** — a large midnight garden groomed on noble coin. Open to the public, though the nobility don't welcome unwashed commoners.

#### Mooreland Manor

The Roiling River splits around a low, crescent-shaped island called the Mooreland. Once nothing but grass and mud, now home to Malkovia's ruler. The noble von Dülvet household held it until the Matriarch's massacre. The lavish multi-story mansion and its gardens are used for business and pleasure. An invitation from the Matriarch is a lucrative but cautious opportunity — business on the Mooreland isn't always clean.

#### Miskavir

Most crowded Southside district, home to the **Etharin Market**. Countless shops and stalls shouting for attention. A gateway to the world — Charnelaise silks, Castinellan wines, Valikan tapestries, Büracher automatons all find their way to Ostoya here. Neither the Court nor the Council of Mages watches closely.

But Miskavir is perilous. Deals are cut as often as throats. The unregulated marketplace attracts thieves and smugglers, some selling body parts and viscera to be offered as blood tax. Where these parts are sourced would be a dangerous investigation.

#### The Drain

Deep underground beneath Miskavir — a notorious fight pit. Combatants fight to the death in a grated cage suspended above a funnel that catches all blood and gore, offered to the Crimson Court as blood tax. Fighters are encouraged to gut opponents, maximizing collected blood. A common gathering place for scum of all sorts. Voluntary fighters are rarer than involuntary ones — prisoners, unfortunate travelers, or political rivals.

#### Lupul

Far west Southside — the banking district. Most Southside nobility reside here, along with a garrison protecting coin repositories. Malkovia stores wealth south of the river to avoid potential Raevan taxation.

The **Ebon Syndicate** uses Malkovian banks to siphon funds. The arrangement is not entirely unknown — the Syndicate quietly supports Matriarch Király in exchange for sanctioned activities. In return, the Syndicate feeds intelligence, acquires difficult objects, and makes dangerous rivals disappear.

### Important Individuals

#### Matriarch Mariska Király

Born a street urchin, surviving through cunning and beaten-in skills. Her short life was almost ended when waters broke during the Darkfall. A monster slayer named Grivr rescued her. Strict training and painful mutations preceded adulthood — she was tempered into a deadly devourer-trained monster hunter. After Grivr's death, Mariska spent years as a mercenary, amassing wealth and connections with unions, syndicates, and merchants.

When the ruling von Dülvet household lost the people's faith, Mariska landed her militia on the Mooreland and took Malkovia. She dismantled trade regulations, flooding the city with wealth, and forged alliances to strengthen her forces. In Northside, a champion who keeps both armies at bay. In Southside, a thug wielding threats and violence.

Despite her success maintaining independence, Mariska is surrounded by vipers — those who would give the city to Soma or Raevo, and those who covet her seat.

#### Oleg the Butcher

Northside overboss, governing districts and enforcing laws on the Matriarch's behalf. His specialty: killing vampires who cross the river. His belt is laden with body parts harvested from prey. When dealing with mortal criminals, Oleg devours portions that inflict terrifying mutations — making himself stronger.

Trained by the Matriarch, his loyalty to Mariska has never been questioned. But he hates the vampires more than she does. Becoming a devourer has changed his biology into a dhampir — a fact he mourns and loathes.

Meets secretly with Raevan rebels, hoping to expand their influence in the city. Should conflict erupt, the Butcher will gladly wet his axes with the blood of the damned.

#### Ylana Svarog

Former exceptional student of the Ravencourt Sanctuary, constantly underestimated due to her gnomish stature. Enemies who consider her easy prey are reduced to windswept ashes.

Never at ease among Castalore's rigid bureaucracy, she preferred Malkovia's free spirit. Former court mage to the von Dülvets, she won Southside overboss by helping Mariska's coup. Despite coming to power alongside the Matriarch, Ylana has found her own wealth dealing with Crimson Court delegates and permits vampire-worship to spread. This erodes Mariska's authority — unlikely Ylana would permit it without assurances from Soma.

#### Marienne Marilyn

Born into nobility but bored by affluence. A dangerous thirst for excitement led to escalating thievery until she was caught and forced to flee in disgrace. Already connected to the Ebon Syndicate and Matriarch Király, she became the Syndicate's head in Malkovia — its most powerful agent in Ostoya.

A confidant and mediator between the Syndicate and the Matriarch. What she lacks in grit she makes up for in charm and cunning. While she and Mariska pretend close friendship, the Matriarch is ever aware of the knife at her throat, should her decisions inconvenience the Syndicate.

#### Father Elijah

A tragic martyr. A divine prophet. A bloodthirsty deceiver. Downcast from the heavens during the Gods' End, Elijah wandered Etharis without cause — faithless, spent. Until he became a vampire. His path is now strikingly clear. He is once more divine, the sanguine curse revealing what he calls an ancient truth: vampires are seraphs, born of a god buried in the City Below, with a mandate to kill the faithless and elevate the worthy to conquer death.

Elijah has proselytized across Ostoya, emboldening citizens with the promise of immortality. He draws crowds with miracles — both vampiric power and dwindling divine sparks. He weaves songs; the congregation dances in celebration of life and weeps at death's finality. He showers them with food from Terinima.

Even Malkovia's nobility find him fascinating. His masses of followers threaten Malkovian leadership with a new banner to unite beneath. Elijah plans to take the city for himself — not for the Crimson Court, but as a step toward godhood. What's greater than a Grand Duke? A god.

### Quest Hooks

#### Drowned Underworld

Grimhilde, a strange Ralakian local, recruits divers to extract treasures from Fissure Lake. She's found gold coins and trinkets on the muddy banks and is convinced ancient riches lie below.

The water is murky, filled with floating corpses that thrash weightlessly in the gloom whenever light draws near. Crushed buildings from the Darkfall are scattered along the bottom — a drowned city. Tunnels into the City Below proper can be found: bone-laced passageways and ancient chambers unseen for unknowable centuries. Some are submerged, others drained. The necropolis spreads from beneath Fissure Lake to other parts of Malkovia and possibly beyond.

Multiple expeditions possible, encountering different monsters and treasures each time. Grimhilde expects to share in recovered wealth — and may seek restitution from the Southside overboss if cheated.

#### Noble Prey

Lord Rathulf, wealthy Óvrosz noble, is a patron of the monster-hunting guildhall. He's posted a bounty on a beast stalking commonfolk from within the Jade Marsh — he believes it to be a rogue strigoi or ecoukrylo.

**Secret**: The monster is a powerful werewolf ravager — and the lycanthrope is Rathulf himself. He intentionally deceives mercenaries into pursuing him, then turns them into prey.

Oleg the Butcher is aware of Rathulf's scheming pastime. If Rathulf is defeated and Oleg is informed, the overboss feigns ignorance but pays the bounty on the Lord's behalf.

#### Gangs of Malkovia

Tensions threaten violent gang war. Monster hunters from the Northside guildhall have slain vampires in the south, openly displaying carcasses in defiance. Ylana petitions the Matriarch to punish Oleg. Guild hunters crossing bridges into Southside are attacked by Ylana's enforcers.

Meanwhile, Father Elijah's teachings spread into Northside. Oleg appears powerless to stop the religious infiltration; outlawing the faith enrages his own citizens.

Matriarch Király hires independent agents to investigate the destabilization. The investigation eventually uncovers **Darius von Dülvet** — a young monster hunter trained by Oleg, who secretly spreads Father Elijah's propaganda. He is the last heir of Malkovia's massacred ruling family, now old enough to fight, planning revenge on Mariska by destabilizing her authority.

The Matriarch's agents choose: capture Darius for Mariska, or aid his crusade. Either choice may change the fate of Malkovia.

#### For Whom the Bells Toll

Malkovia's clocktowers have stopped ringing. Commoners have gone missing nearby. The Matriarch sends investigators.

Inside the towers: arcane libraries hoarded with blasphemous books, eldritch laboratories filled with nightmarish constructs — half-living humanoids with bodies painfully grafted with fiery machinery and gruesome weapons. Manic scribblings of Ciprian Johann exalt his own brilliance.

In the highest levels, the mechanisms have become twisted and daemonic. Cogs are powered by fiery arcane objects. Malikirian imps flock like bats in the belfry.

**Ciprian himself is transforming into a fiend**, having grafted his own body with hellish prosthetics. Unless stopped, he begins capturing commoners to subject them to the same horrific procedures.
