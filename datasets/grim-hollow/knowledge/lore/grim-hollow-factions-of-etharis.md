# Factions of Etharis

The major factions of Etharis are useful as more than backdrop. They are employers, rivals, ideological movements, conspiracies, supply chains, and engines of recurring trouble. Most of them operate across borders, giving a GM a way to connect distant plots and explain why the same names keep appearing in different realms.

Almost all of them are driven by power, but not in the same way. Some want profit, some influence, some knowledge, some faith, and some simple survival. None are clean. Even the most sympathetic groups tolerate cruelty, secrecy, fanaticism, or collateral damage when the stakes are high enough.

Use these factions as persistent campaign actors. A faction should not feel like a one-scene quest-giver; it should feel like an institution with resources, habits, enemies, and internal contradictions.

## Augustine Trading Company

**Identity**: The Augustine Trading Company presents itself as civilization's indispensable merchant engine: trade, education, innovation, and prosperity. In truth, it is a predatory corporate empire based in Morencia and willing to use debt, blackmail, war, trafficking, and magical research to turn every crisis into profit.

**Core dynamic**: The Company is most dangerous when it appears reasonable. It does not need to conquer territory openly if it can own the grain stores, finance the war, insure the cargo, hold the debt, and write the contracts afterward.

### Leadership

- **Founder**: Manoch Augustus, once a priest managing church finances, discovered he was better at exploitation than piety. He built his fortune first through arms dealing, then predatory lending, then land seizure and rent extraction.
- **Current ruler in practice**: Treasurer Kolyhs "The Blazing Brain" Papinian, a gnome orphan turned financial prodigy. She learned early that truth is expensive, secrets are cheaper, and control matters more than honesty. She sits at the intersection of the Company, the Banker's Guild, and Morencian politics.
- **The Montarri**: The ruling council of five. Beyond Kolyhs, its most important figures are Senior Preceptor Melchora Rosalle, who runs the Augustine Centers and their research arm; Master of Ledgers Enzo Reiff, who oversees trade operations and logistics; Lord Barrister Anlon Katzenbach, who controls contracts, legal warfare, and loopholes; and Intendant of Compliance Odriana van de Nijveldt, who manages intelligence, dossiers, and covert enforcement.
- **Internal tension**: The Montarri scheme against the Company's enemies and against one another, but only within limits. Kolyhs selected them for a reason and remains the only one plausibly capable of removing another councilor.

### Operations and Methods

- **Economic domination**: The Company buys essential industries, fixes prices, sabotages competitors, traps nobles in impossible loans, and turns entire communities into debt-serf economies.
- **Political leverage**: It bribes officials, rewrites laws, subsidizes churches, and uses shell companies or proxy ownership to control governments without obvious occupation.
- **Talent extraction**: Augustine Centers recruit brilliant students through scholarships that become chains. Graduates earn prestige, then discover they are indebted specialists serving a machine that sees them as assets.
- **Disposable deniability**: Adventurers, mercenaries, and local intermediaries handle the dirty work when the Company wants plausible distance from a job.
- **Regional adaptation**: The Company changes its mask to fit local culture. In Castinella it hides illicit arcane trade behind more respectable goods. In Valika it turns clan rivalries into trade-route protection deals. If a market exists, the Company will reach it. If it does not exist, the Company will create the need.

### Secret Projects and Dirty Secrets

- **Project Warbreed**: A long-running effort to create controllable super-soldiers from lycanthropes and other cursed beings, especially vampiric hybrids. The Company is also interested in how the arisen fit into property law: if an employee dies and returns, does the Company still own their labor?
- **Project Fumarialis**: Development of magical and black-powder weapons, plus covert tools like humanoid-looking automatons for infiltration and assassination.
- **Project Mandragora**: Performance enhancers, loyalty-inducing drugs, and "treatments" designed not to cure illness but to create permanent dependency.
- **Dossiers on everyone**: The Company records secrets, failures, debts, indiscretions, and vulnerabilities for future leverage. Contracts are only one layer of coercion.
- **Whistleblower policy**: Those who expose the Company vanish, are ruined financially, or get assigned "suicide missions."

### Key Locations

- **The August Spire, Morencia**: Fortress, bank, palace, and nerve center. Beneath the public grandeur lies one of the Company's greatest secrets: a grotesque portal of enchanted iron and twisted flesh used to dispatch couriers and agents across Etharis. It is so expensive to operate that only the highest-priority crises justify it.
- **Trading hubs**: Public-facing storefronts and warehouses in major settlements. Respectable in front, illicit in the back. Trade Factors run them and report upward.
- **Augustine Centers**: Elite schools that function as recruitment funnels, loyalty factories, and research pipelines.
- **Secret sites**: Facility Null-9 beneath Grarjord for Warbreed research; Ashhold Alpha in the Corovan mountains for weapons trials; and the Unlisted Garden in Charneault for alchemical and fey-tainted drug development.

### Relationships

- The **Arcanist Inquisition** and **Watchers** despise the Company but still need goods only it can source.
- The **Ebon Syndicate** is both competitor and business partner depending on season and opportunity.
- The **Thaumaturge** uses Company supply networks for rare reagents, while risking theft of apprentices or research.
- The **Free Swords** understand the Company better than most: cold contracts, good pay, no illusions.

### Quest Hooks

- Escort a caravan carrying a cursed relic, only to discover the destination is a black site where spirits will be harvested.
- Rescue or abduct a uniquely transformation-resistant youth destined for Warbreed experimentation.
- Recover a murdered bookkeeper's missing ledger before the wrong people decode the Company's hidden transactions.
- Investigate warehouse fires and discover the cargo was caged daemons earmarked for research.
- Collect a defaulted noble debt and learn the family owns heirlooms and monster-lore the Company values more than coin.

## Company of Free Swords

**Identity**: The Company of Free Swords is Etharis's most famous mercenary organization: elite, widespread, expensive, and proud of its code. They sell violence for coin, but unlike most mercenaries they insist that honor, internal loyalty, and vengeance against betrayers are part of the product.

**Core dynamic**: They are genuinely more principled than many armed factions in Etharis, but they are still mercenaries. Their code restrains atrocity; it does not make them altruists.

### History and Reputation

- **Founding myth made real**: Darion Harkin deserted rather than massacre a village during a rebellion in Falaris. He then assassinated the tyrant King Vaclav II, bought the tyrant's keep with looted treasure, and turned a mutiny into a business.
- **Founding principles**: Stay loyal to the company. Be honest in your dealings. Take vengeance on traitors and oathbreakers.
- **Battle of Kovich Crag**: An early victory where the Free Swords held a pass against an Ostoyan counterattack, cementing their reputation for tactical flexibility and audacity.
- **The Silent Seven**: The defining legend. Two dozen mercenaries went to Kravburg to handle ritual murders and a daemonic threat. Seven came back. Half the town died. A daemon lord's corpse smoldered in the ruins. The survivors said only, "We killed the daemon. We saved the people." Everyone suspects the real story was worse.

### Leadership

- **Yrsa Bitterwind**: Current overall leader, a ferocious Valikan warrior from Clan Völgr who took command after defeating Brom Ven Torrig in single combat and effectively inheriting his company. She is brutal, charismatic, and hungry for worthy opposition.
- **Her long game**: Many believe Yrsa ultimately wants to return to Valika and claim the power denied her there. Some fear the Company's growth is serving her personal saga as much as the institution.
- **Chandra Veln**: Yrsa's mysterious elven second-in-command and spymaster, proof that the Free Swords are more sophisticated than the tavern image of hard-drinking sellswords suggests.
- **Hans Tova**: A prominent leader and tactician, son of one of the Silent Seven, with the weight of Company myth behind him.

### Structure, Culture, and Methods

- **Local autonomy**: The Company is built from largely independent chapters. Shared culture matters more than centralized bureaucracy.
- **Ranks**: Recruit, Free Sword, Veteran or Specialist, and Captain. Same-rank members receive equal shares; advancement is about trust and proven value.
- **Specialists**: Battlemages, sappers, infiltrators, and other experts exist within the company, though not all chapters favor the same mix.
- **Two faces**: On campaign they are disciplined, practical, and famously sober. Off duty they are social, extravagant, and eager to spend hard-won coin.
- **Payment matters**: They do not romanticize war. They expect compensation and may punish lords who think mercenaries can be cheated after the battle is won.
- **Honor as business model**: Reliability is part of what clients buy. That creates constant tension when profit, survival, chapter politics, and moral edge cases collide.

### Pressure Points and Internal Fault Lines

- **The Red Swords**: A rumored off-books splinter tendency inside the organization that takes the highest coin regardless of ethics.
- **Duty vs contract**: A notorious incident during the Ostoyan civil war saw Free Swords slaughter one another over conflicting loyalties. The argument never truly ended.
- **Mage problem in Castinella**: Chapters operating there are forced to navigate Arcanist Inquisition oversight.
- **Yrsa's temperament**: Her personal ferocity wins battles and scares clients, captains, and rivals alike.

### Key Locations

- **Harkin's Hold**: The founding keep, now a sacred historical outpost and pilgrimage site. Darion Harkin's grave and the original tenets are preserved there.
- **Löwengard Fortress**: Main fortress in Bürach, strategically placed for campaigns into multiple realms. Barracks, armories, training yards, craftsmen, and high-level war rooms make it a true military hub.
- **Lions Dens**: Urban guild-style halls used for recruitment, negotiation, and representation. The grandest sits in Voros, politically vital for operations in Castinella and Charneault.

### Quest Hooks

- Investigate a captain's murder at Löwengard and determine whether it was revenge, factional intrigue, or evidence of a Red Swords cell.
- Aid an overstretched chapter that expected one werewolf and found an entire pack.
- Resolve a contract dispute where Free Swords have effectively taken a village's livelihood hostage over unpaid protection.
- Support or stop a renegade chapter that slid from survival banditry into outright predation.
- Act as deniable contractors for a chapter that cannot openly intervene because of local politics, especially in Castinella.

## Ebon Syndicate

**Identity**: The Ebon Syndicate is Etharis's dominant underworld network: thieves, smugglers, spies, assassins, extortionists, black marketeers, and quiet political meddlers. It thrives in cities, prefers shadow to banner, and treats morality as a service someone might pay for.

**Core dynamic**: The Syndicate can play villain, fixer, patron, or uneasy ally without changing its underlying nature. It helps when doing so buys leverage.

### Origins and Myth

- **Founder**: Hiram Greyman, a bandit who escaped death in Altenheim's hedge maze and took a hawk moth as a sign of favor. The moth became the Syndicate's symbol.
- **The title "Greyman"**: As Hiram became more hidden, the office eclipsed the man. Later leaders inherited the title rather than just the organization.
- **The Red Gondola**: The most famous Syndicate mystery. During the chaos around Castinella's failed invasion of Morencia, an unmarked ship full of butchered soldiers was found, and a blood-red gondola bearing black roses shaped like a hawk moth was left near the Augustine leadership. No one knows what proof or warning it carried, but after that the Syndicate's war with the Augustine Company moved back into the shadows.

### Leadership and Structure

- **Natalia Koscheii**: Current Greyman. Ostoyan-born, minor nobility by blood, criminal by choice and talent. Her ancestry links back to the Somans who founded the Order of Dawn. She became famous for surviving repeated assassination attempts.
- **Natalia's style**: Less overtly savage than some predecessors, more politically disciplined, and obsessed with loyalty. She routinely infiltrates her own branches to expose disloyal operators.
- **Lieutenant chain**: Local gangs report to bosses; bosses answer to regional lieutenants often styled "Grayman"; these in turn answer to Natalia.
- **Notable lieutenants**: Darion Aufir, the polished ogresh ruler of Grabenstein's underworld, and Kallista de Locra, brutal Grayman of Morencia and possible sponsor of attempts on Natalia's life.
- Regional cells vary widely in tone, methods, and slang, but all understand deniability, debt, and reputation.

### Methods

- **Coin as tool, not goal**: Money matters because it buys influence, silence, exits, officials, love, evidence, and second chances.
- **Control from the shadows**: The Syndicate prefers to manipulate power rather than claim it openly. Proxies, patsies, freelancers, and "helpful bystanders" are standard tools.
- **Public ambiguity**: It will sometimes expose corruption, rescue a victim, or remove a monster if doing so secures gratitude, debt, or leverage. This lets locals rationalize dealing with criminals who occasionally solve problems the state cannot.
- **Trade specialties**: Smuggling, assassinations, mage extraction in Castinella, spy work in noble courts, and acquisition of secrets other factions wish to bury.

### Recruitment and Agents

- **The Shadow Academy**: Not a school building but a training philosophy. Recruits learn by doing, with mentorship and escalating practical tests.
- **Selection logic**: The Syndicate values useful instincts more than polish. Servants, clerks, stewards, street gangs, and lovers can all become nodes in a wider intelligence web.
- **Failure policy**: Recruits who repeatedly embarrass the Syndicate disappear. Some are killed. Some are released under watch. The uncertainty is intentional.
- **Street enforcers**: Deliver warnings and visible violence when subtlety stops being efficient.
- **Elite assassins**: Build mission-specific toxins and prefer the death to look like the victim deserved it, caused it, or never existed.

### Geographic Profile

- Strongest in **Altenheim** and across much of **Bürach**.
- In **Ostoya**, it is more effective in Raevo than in provinces where the Crimson Court's own spy webs dominate.
- In **Castinella**, mage smuggling is lucrative and morally ambiguous; the same network may help one mage escape and sell out another.
- In **Charneault**, old institutions make Syndicate growth difficult, but dark mist, fear, and weakening trust in knightly protection create fresh openings.

### Key Location

- **The Grey City** beneath Altenheim's Ebon Halls: a hidden underground fortress wrapped in tunnels, deadfalls, poison chambers, hidden doors, sewer links, and river routes. It is headquarters, myth, and logistical marvel all at once.

### Quest Hooks

- Free a detained assassin so a Syndicate-backed killing can destabilize a tyrant's household.
- Work for a charming patron collecting relics, only to discover the acquisitions serve Syndicate arcane ambitions.
- Chase petty thieves into a Syndicate tavern and realize the larger operation wants the amateurs silenced.
- Smuggle a mage out of Castinella while deciding whether the Syndicate contact is helper, extortionist, or both.
- Learn the Syndicate wants the party to abandon their current investigation, then uncover why their quest threatens a hidden balance of power.

## Thaumaturge

**Identity**: Thaumaturge, formally the Grand Thaumatic Order of Archmages, may be the most influential faction in Etharis or only the most persistent conspiracy theory. There is no hard proof of the Order's existence, but nearly every major magical event in history has at some point been blamed on it, credited to it, or both.

**Core dynamic**: Thaumaturge works best as an ambiguous force. Even when the PCs interact with one of its agents, they should not receive clean answers about whether they met a member, a rival, a fraud, or a piece on someone else's cosmic game board.

### What the Order Is Believed to Want

- **Knowledge as power**: Tomes, relics, magical sites, planar lore, lost histories, gifted spellcasters, and strange phenomena are all natural targets.
- **Subtle influence**: The Order allegedly prefers invisible manipulation over overt rule. If history can be nudged instead of forced, so much the better.
- **Long horizons**: Their schemes are imagined on scales of decades, centuries, or more. That makes them capable of appearing compassionate, monstrous, or absent depending on when one catches a fragment of the plan.
- **Cosmic utilitarianism**: If common lives are candles, then whole villages can be spent to prevent something worse. This is the single most useful moral frame for portraying Thaumaturge suspects.

### Current Rumors and Active Threads

- **Sacred Artifacts**: Interest in legendary artifacts has spiked. Rumors vary wildly: the Order has some and wants the rest; it lost them; it is racing an immortal enemy; it already knows how to use them.
- **Aetheric contamination**: Strange arcane traces and rising encounters with aetheric aberrations have led many to suspect that some Thaumaturge members use unstable planar routes or traffic with the Aether Kindred.
- **Magical rescues**: Many escaped mages tell stories of impossible interventions against the Arcanist Inquisition: teleportation, charmed guards, rooms of enemies suddenly obliterated, or a stranger appearing at exactly the right moment.

### Suspected Leaders and Members

- **Raakan el-Adakar**: An archmage whose body was never recovered from Cold Iron Keep.
- **The Ravencourt Keeper**: So secretive that even students do not know who truly rules the Sanctuary.
- **Martorius**: The mage whose castle appeared impossibly atop a mountain in Bürach.
- **Mina Taliesin, Lady of Masks**: Archmage ruler in northern Ostoya and enemy of the Crimson Court.
- **Professor Franciszka Welgirson**: The Emperor's disembodied tutor, source of endless court gossip.
- **Odis**: Elven scholar from Sarmar Academy, now roaming Valika to study Gormadraug and coldfire.
- **Volarius of Aloran**: Knight-sorcerer on Charneault's Royal Council, popular and suspect in equal measure.
- **Pharazorthok**: Not a member but a terrifying theory: that an aetheric god-entity manipulates the Order's senior minds.
- **Council theory**: Many believe the Order has no single head at all, only a caucus of archmages too powerful to rule one another directly.

### Agents and Magical Profile

- **True members**: Exceptionally powerful mages and archmages with idiosyncratic capabilities rather than standardized ranks.
- **Initiated mages**: Experts in deception, illusions, battlefield confusion, teleports, switch spells, and custom arcane tricks.
- **Archmages**: Counterspell masters, planar rift-openers, teleporters, summoners, and collaborative ritualists able to combine power for catastrophic effects.
- **Pawns and fronts**: Colleges, patrons, cults, expeditions, and rival mages may all unknowingly do work that ultimately serves Thaumaturge interests.

### Suspected Locations

- **Ravencourt Sanctuary**: Protected by a supernatural Mistwall and surrounded by mystery. It both "produces" and "devours" mages, depending on who tells the story.
- **Erlefurt's Magister College**: Prestigious, secretive, and thick with forbidden-access rumors.
- **Sarmar Academy**: A secluded Charneault institution devoted to deep magical knowledge.
- **Castle Martorius**: A perfect site for impossible meetings if Martorius allows them.
- **Cold Iron Keep**: A prison fortress with magical safeguards and inmates worth interrogating forever.

### Quest Hooks

- Retrieve a magical artifact from a sealed crypt while competing with a mysterious mage who may be ally, rival, or cleanup crew.
- Accept an invitation to Sarmar Academy and discover the real task is dealing with a corrupted spirit or proving oneself to hidden observers.
- Board a suspicious "Ravencourt" boat before its proper seven-year cycle and uncover an abduction plot targeting promising mages.
- Investigate a grotesquely impossible magical murder that looks less like assassination than arcane demonstration.
- Rescue a condemned mage from Castinella for a patron who offers extraordinary rewards and even less transparency.

## Watchers of the Faithful

**Identity**: The Watchers of the Faithful are a young but rapidly expanding religious movement devoted to the Arch Seraphs and the Eternal Dogma of Sagrado Valieda. Unlike the Arcanist Inquisition, whose power is direct coercion, the Watchers fight primarily with preaching, organization, intelligence, and crowd-shaping zeal.

**Core dynamic**: They are dangerous because they can turn conviction into mass action. The Inquisition arrests individuals; the Watchers can make whole towns denounce, riot, or reform around a holy message.

### Ideology

- **Public goals**: Revere the Arch Seraphs; follow the sacred laws handed down through the sects; and destroy what is unholy, including aberrations, daemons, fey, undead, heretics, and in some sects almost anything deemed impure.
- **Hidden fourth goal**: Theocratic supremacy. The leadership ultimately wants worldly governments bent under organized faith and public pressure.
- **Self-image**: Champions of the people, defenders of the poor, correctors of corrupt clergy, and guides toward a new holy age.
- **What makes them slippery**: Many of their criticisms are accurate. Exploitative nobles, corrupt priests, and monstrous powers really do exist. The trouble is what the Watchers are willing to unleash in response.

### Leadership

- **Structure**: Six sects, one for each Arch Seraph, each led by a High Priest. Together they elect a Divine Speaker.
- **Divine Speaker Nola Cirdanal**: Current leader, aligned with Empyreus. Courageous, relentless, and aggressively expansionist. She deploys evangelists even where local rulers do not want them and often backs rhetoric with armed escorts.
- **Audres D'Xeo**: High Priest of Aphaelon, favoring a calmer and more temperate approach.
- **Carma Aruejo**: High Priest of Morael, inconsistent and politically pivotal because her vote is hard to predict.
- **Lawirsen Kheshev**: High Priest of Zabriel, quiet but influential through spy and librarian networks.
- **Srokkush**: Dragonborn High Priest of Solyma, more militant than even Nola in his appetite for righteous violence.
- **Velten Giessers**: High Priest of Miklas, healer of the poor and natural political rival to Nola because mercy wins devotion as effectively as fury.

### Methods and Reach

- **Evangelist networks**: Charismatic priests inflame crowds, redirect grievances, expose or manufacture heresy, and turn moral rhetoric into social power.
- **Pilgrimage economy**: Pilgrims, donations, missionary networks, and holy logistics give the Watchers continental reach without formal occupation.
- **Traveling shrines**: Capillarante wagons unfold into mobile chapels and often carry food, coin, banners, and cudgels useful for both ministry and unrest.
- **Stationary cells**: Many local Watchers hide behind the identities of ordinary clergy while quietly reporting to Toletum.
- **Foreign strategy**: They preach in Bürach, pressure Charneault, seek converts in Valika, and hate the Crimson Court. Their doctrine travels well wherever rulers are corrupt, weak, or visibly impious.

### Relationship with the Arcanist Inquisition

- The Watchers and Inquisition are allies only in the loosest sense.
- The Inquisition sees the Watchers as useful clergy who lack the will for necessary violence.
- The Watchers see the Inquisition as a blunt, violent instrument that can be pointed at "heretics" and competitors.
- Each uses the other. The Watchers pass along reports and moral framing; the Inquisition performs arrests, killings, and public terror.

### Key Location

- **Grand Temple of Toletum**: A starburst cathedral with chapels for each Arch Seraph, a central Empyrean nave, and impressive religious art designed to overwhelm visitors.
- **Hidden underlevels**: Secret passages, listening holes, a vast scriptorium, intelligence archives, and concealed shrines for relics.
- **Relic truth**: Each sect claims to possess a true relic. Only Zabriel's sect definitely does: the Tome of Saint Lysimachus, a genuine source of holy knowledge.

### Quest Hooks

- Defuse a holy mob stirred against an allegedly heretical noble and expose the Watchful Priest who engineered the unrest.
- Rescue a captured missionary in Valika who refuses rescue because he thinks he can convert his raiders.
- Recover a relic from vampire-held territory and decide whether the mission's "holy necessity" justifies abandoning innocents.
- Investigate a village-wide witch panic only to learn the Watcher behind it has fallen to a daemon of deception.
- Escort a missionary through hostile lands and watch faith, politics, and survival tangle together.

## Arcanist Inquisition

**Identity**: The Arcanist Inquisition is the anti-magic religious order at the heart of Castinella. Founded by Montego Valieda after his claimed communion with Empyreus, it teaches that arcane magic is a curse that endangers both wielder and world.

**Core dynamic**: The Inquisition should feel oppressive even when it is technically hunting something genuinely dangerous. In Grim Hollow, anti-magic paranoia is not entirely irrational; the horror lies in what the institution has become while acting on that truth.

### History and Present Character

- **Sagrado Valieda's vision**: After unifying Castinella, Montego took the title Sagrado and built a new order to identify, arrest, and control the magically gifted.
- **Early phase**: The first model was coercive but not yet openly monstrous. Captured mages were confined, "reformed," and repurposed for seraphic service.
- **Dragonborn alliance**: Valieda secured dragonborn support by offering them a restored place in Ember Cairn, tying their future to the Inquisition's success.
- **Hardening over time**: Under successive leadership, and especially under Lord Inquisitor Crevax, humble confinement turned into imprisonment, fear, and cruelty.
- **Internal doubt**: Some within the order believe it has lost its way, but the structure is so paranoid that even voicing such concerns risks becoming the next prisoner.

### Leadership and Culture

- **Lord Inquisitor Crevax**: A towering dragonborn zealot, physically imposing and ideologically uncompromising.
- **Propaganda as weapon**: The Inquisition relies on spectacle, symbol, rumor, and fear as much as formal authority.
- **Visible authority**: Unlike shadow factions, it wants to be seen. Its emblems are displayed proudly and are meant to frighten before force is ever needed.
- **Moral simplification**: Many ordinary people support or tolerate it because its worldview is emotionally easy: magic is dangerous, therefore those who wield it must be controlled.

### Headquarters

- **Ember Cathedral, Ember Cairn**: An enormous structure built into a hollowed rock formation, equal parts temple, fortress, prison, and bureaucratic labyrinth.
- **The underground maze**: Quarters, cells, chambers, hidden routes, and twisted passageways known best by the Inquisitors themselves. To be "thrown into the Cathedral's maze" is both literal threat and cultural terror.

### How to Use the Inquisition

- They are excellent recurring antagonists because they combine legal authority, religious legitimacy, popular fear, and enough truth about magic's risks to avoid cartoon villainy.
- They can also be temporary allies against truly catastrophic arcane threats, which is often more disturbing than simply fighting them.
- They work especially well when the PCs do not know whether to hide from them, manipulate them, or let them loose on a worse enemy.

### Quest Hooks

- Arrive in a village just as Inquisitors begin hunting mages and discover the locals may support them.
- Chase relic thieves into Camont only to learn the Inquisition is using the same case as a trap for magically gifted operatives.
- Attempt to save an ally scheduled for a very public execution.
- Decide whether to help the Inquisition stop an herbalist conspiracy that is real, even if the Inquisition itself is abhorrent.
- Investigate a "miracle child" healer while a true-believing junior Inquisitor tries to turn sanctity into a blasphemy charge.

## Crimson Court

**Identity**: The Crimson Court is the ruling body of Ostoya: seven vampire nobles who govern through terror, patronage, blood taxation, and the promise that obedience is safer than what waits outside the walls.

**Core dynamic**: The Court is both a state and a nest of predators. It rules Ostoya collectively, but each member is also scheming against the others. That tension makes the Court stable enough to dominate the land and unstable enough to generate endless intrigue.

### Political Reality

- **Origin**: The Court emerged after the Darkfall, when the undead plague from the City Below transformed Ostoyan nobility and changed the balance of the war with Bürach.
- **Public bargain**: The living obey, pay blood taxes, and hope their rulers protect them from worse undead horrors and imperial enemies.
- **Real order**: Every noble house is part player, part pawn. Assassination, manipulation, and hidden alliances are routine.
- **Grand Duke Drago Koshevek**: The oldest and most powerful member, master strategist and terror-artist, obsessed with defeating Bürach.
- **Provincial autonomy**: The other six members rule their provinces with broad latitude so long as they do not openly undermine the Grand Duke's larger ambitions.

### Means of Rule

- **Blood tax**: The living are harvested because submission to the Court is still preferable to being left defenseless before Ostoya's greater horrors.
- **Spawn hierarchy**: Each Court member creates and commands lesser vampire nobles and lieutenants.
- **Mortal servants**: Some serve from fear, some from opportunism, some from genuine loyalty, and some because they hope to be elevated into vampirism.
- **Undead soldiery**: The Court gladly uses lesser undead as disposable troops and terror weapons.
- **Aesthetic cruelty**: Some members compete in how nightmarish they can make their undead armies, adding grotesque armor and grafted limbs simply because fear itself is political currency.
- **Disdain for rivals**: The Court despises liches and other undead sovereigns as illegitimate imitations, though it may still traffic with monsters from the City Below when useful.

### Key Locations and Symbols of Power

- **Provincial estates**: Every Court member has a known stronghold, and uninvited visitors are often turned into prey, entertainment, or bargaining pieces.
- **Blackharbour, Nov Ostoya**: Grand Duke Drago's fortress. Rare full Court gatherings happen here, and invitations often imply that someone's seat is threatened.
- **Masquerades and private courts**: The Court's social life matters politically. Ballrooms, salons, and blood-soaked feasts are venues for bids, betrayals, and recruitment of expendable adventurers.

### Pressure Points

- Drago's anti-Bürach obsession is not universally shared.
- Some members want expansion, others security, others simply personal dominance.
- Rival nobles within the Court may sponsor the same adventurers against each other.
- Mortals working for the Court often believe they can navigate vampire politics safely until they learn they were never more than useful livestock with names.

### Named Figures from Current Intrigue

- **Baron Mihael the Wolf** hires off-book retrieval teams.
- **Countess Katalina Velpescu** fields her own competing agents.
- **Viscount Langkirk Warlow** is associated with theatrical cruelty and grotesque undead displays.
- **Marchioness Iulia Cutov**, **Countess Vasinesca**, and **Viscountess Zeilgor** each illustrate how internal Court politics regularly turn into assassination plots or desperate diplomatic gambits.

### Quest Hooks

- Retrieve a relic from the City Below while competing mercenary teams work for rival Court members.
- Hunt an experimental undead creature unleashed by a necromancer trying to avoid the blood tax.
- Broker or sabotage the Grand Duke's attempt to bring another vampire ruler into the Court.
- Attend a masquerade where the real objective is an assassination the patron cannot be tied to.
- Escort a soul-consuming war weapon to the front and decide whether exposing it is worth making the Court even more murderous.

## Monster Hunter Guilds

**Identity**: Monster hunter guilds are not one centralized faction but a continent-wide professional subculture made of small lodges, famous specialists, apprenticeships, and competing methodologies. They sit somewhere between trade guild, martial school, and grim survival profession.

**Core dynamic**: Monster hunters are among the most practical recurring allies in Etharis. They have valuable expertise, but their priorities are not automatically heroic. They may work with the PCs, against them, or view them as quarry.

### The Four Main Approaches

- **Carvers**: Study anatomy, weak points, and habits until a monster becomes a solvable problem.
- **Trappers**: Prefer containment, control, relocation, and engineered solutions over pure slaughter.
- **Occultists**: Use anti-magic training and arcane knowledge to combat supernatural threats.
- **Devourers**: Consume or internalize aspects of the monsters they fight. Effective, feared, and often socially shunned.

### Organization and Culture

- **Origins**: Shared techniques produced early guilds, but modern groups are fragmented and local.
- **Apprenticeship model**: Most hunters are taught by a veteran rather than trained in standardized academies.
- **Mobility**: Many guilds have no permanent hall at all. Hunters travel, take individual commissions, and temporarily join adventuring parties or mercenary companies.
- **Scale**: Larger guilds can rival minor mercenary companies, but accomplished hunters remain rare enough to be known by name.
- **Isolation of devourers**: Where devourer guilds exist, they are private and often socially sealed off from other hunters.

### Notable Figure

- **Weaver Faloenea**: A Dreamer occultist and leader of Altenheim's Nightshield. She is famed for dream-divination that can probe a monster's true nature. Crucially, she refuses to use that gift on the Great Beast, which suggests either principle, fear, or knowledge she does not want to share.

### How Hunters Feel in Play

- They know what they are doing in a way many militia, nobles, and priests do not.
- They can be arrogant because expertise keeps them alive.
- Their methods reveal setting tone. Carvers feel forensic, trappers practical, occultists eerie, devourers transgressive.
- They are a natural bridge between horror investigation and combat-heavy play.

### Quest Hooks

- Find a veteran hunter's missing apprentice before the quarry finishes the hunt in reverse.
- Become the target of a guild because one PC is transformed, cursed, or tied to a monster power.
- Compete with or join a guild on a high-paying noble bounty.
- Investigate a murder with guild help only to realize the guild itself has begun taking humanoid targets.

## Morbus Doctore

**Identity**: The Morbus Doctore are plague doctors, physicians, anatomists, and researchers who emerged in the wake of the Weeping Pox. They combine medicine, alchemy, surgery, and arcane practice in ways that make them potentially lifesaving and morally horrifying.

**Core dynamic**: They are best used when the party cannot dismiss them as simple villains because they are often the only people who can even attempt to treat a crisis. The horror comes from what they consider acceptable in pursuit of knowledge and cure.

### Public Face and Reputation

- Their arrival usually means disease is near.
- Their blood-stained boots, masks, vials, and coats announce competence and dread in equal measure.
- Many locals believe the Doctore revel in suffering, spread disease intentionally, or use patients as raw material.
- Some rumors even claim they helped cause the Weeping Pox in the first place.
- These accusations are not entirely baseless, but neither are they always fair. Some members sincerely want to save lives and simply work inside a monstrous institution.

### Structure

- **Compartmentalized pyramid**: Members typically know only those directly above and below them.
- **Adjuncts**: Public-facing doctors and nurses who maintain trust.
- **Orderlies**: Hired muscle, carriers, chemists, assistants, and practical workers who do everything the masked doctors do not want exposed.
- **Residents**: Typical plague doctors apprenticed into serious research and field treatment.
- **Fellows**: Senior experimenters with broad autonomy over methods and local networks.
- **Trustees**: The hidden top five, never publicly unmasked.
- **The Attending**: Voice of the Trustees, cold and relentlessly utilitarian.

### Operating Logic

- **Knowledge must be preserved**: Members share internally so discoveries are never lost.
- **No external sharing**: Research belongs to the organization.
- **Patients as data**: The boundary between treatment and experimentation is extremely weak.
- **Regional variation**: Because Fellows have wide discretion, one Doctore enclave may be grim but useful while another is a secret charnel laboratory.
- **Moral slippage**: Even decent members are surrounded by incentives that reward detachment, compartmentalization, and "necessary sacrifice."

### Leadership Mystery

- The Trustees are almost mythic within the organization. Many members have never seen beneath their masks.
- Stories say they were among the first to face the Weeping Pox and were changed by failure into obsessive seekers of absolute medical understanding.

### Quest Hooks

- Retrieve an escaped contagious patient who claims the Doctore infected him on purpose.
- Accept an experimental cure for a terrible affliction and deal with the side effects.
- Smuggle out a remorseful Resident carrying a library of medical knowledge and a death sentence from his own faction.
- Guard a medical caravan whose route seems to leave fresh outbreaks in every village it visits.
- Quarantine a village during a pox outbreak and discover a hidden Doctore branch is testing inoculations against placebos on living people.

## Order of Dawn

**Identity**: The Order of Dawn is an Ostoyan anti-undead militia rooted in exile, revenge, and the worship of Solyma. Based in Raevo and scattered frontier strongholds, it exists to destroy the undead and someday reclaim Ostoya from the Crimson Court.

**Core dynamic**: They are easy to sympathize with and difficult to trust. Their cause is just. Their methods are often terrifying.

### Culture and Leadership

- **Founder population**: Ostoyans who refused vampire rule and fled north.
- **General Artemios Dulka**: A Soman noble driven by the dream of a free Ostoya and by an all-consuming hatred of vampires. Outside that hatred he can seem benevolent; in its presence he becomes merciless.
- **Training**: Members are raised or drilled from early life to see undead eradication as sacred duty rather than profession.
- **Self-righteousness**: They respect courage and anti-undead action, but often look down on outsiders, including the Raevan mages who sheltered them.
- **Loyalty within**: Once accepted, members receive fierce solidarity and trust.

### Methods

- **No compromise with undeath**: Graves are opened, corpses burned, and tainted ground cleansed to prevent future rising.
- **Research through desecration**: Captured undead are dissected and studied, because piety and forensic hatred are not seen as contradictory.
- **Collateral tolerance**: The Order will accept civilian risk if it believes that is the surest way to destroy an undead threat.
- **Distributed strongholds**: Ruined forts, reclaimed churches, and hidden safehouses inside Court-controlled territory support long war rather than open conquest.

### Divisions

- **Sentinels**: Front-line knights and battlefield anti-undead specialists.
- **Stalkers**: Scouts, infiltrators, and assassins. Elite blessed stalkers deal radiant damage instead of poison.
- **Cleansers**: Priests and exorcists focused on incorporeal undead and corruption.
- **Blessed tattoos**: Each member bears a mark in holy ink that prevents resurrection, a striking statement that service to Solyma should never end in undeath.

### Quest Hooks

- Stop or support an Order plan to burn an entire village to guarantee a vampire noble dies with it.
- Break a siege on an isolated Order fortress using old tunnels and questionable allies.
- Decide the fate of a captured dhampir noble whose guilt is far from clear.
- Serve as distraction while the Order pursues its real assassination target elsewhere.
- Cleanse a village of undead only to find the "monster" protecting it may be connected to Dulka's own tragic past.

## Prismatic Circle

**Identity**: The Prismatic Circle is an ancient Valikan druidic order that keeps the western Thrull clans violent, sacrificial, and blood-soaked in order to prevent the awakening of Gormadraug, the Great Prismatic Wyrm. Whether their doctrine is true, self-serving, or both, it has shaped northern culture for generations.

**Core dynamic**: The Circle is not evil merely because it is savage; it is evil because it has normalized atrocity as cosmic necessity. Its members believe the world ends if the blood stops flowing.

### Belief and Practice

- **Founding vision**: Kybard, an elemental druid, proclaimed that Gormadraug was not slain, only sleeping, and that unending sacrifice would keep it dormant.
- **Political result**: The Circle pushes the Thrull clans toward raiding, warfare, and ritual violence, preserving a culture of bloodshed as sacred obligation.
- **Sentinel Woods rituals**: Scattered groves where captives, livestock, criminals, southerners, and political enemies are slaughtered over several agonizing days. Druids often enhance suffering with primal magic.
- **Coldfire escalation**: The return of coldfire has made the Circle more extreme. It interprets the phenomenon as proof that the Wyrm is stirring.

### Leadership and Power Base

- **Grand Druid Hjadana**: Spiritual ruler of the three Thrull clans, effectively a theocrat in all but name.
- **Chief Nolgr Magnusson**: Clan Völgr warlord and Hjadana's crucial ally. His warriors are the Circle's military arm.
- **Tyburn on Holgar**: Seat of power where a living fortress of dense trees marks the Circle's sanctum.
- **Formal structure vs reality**: The order claims a communal tradition, but Hjadana has ruled unchallenged for decades and critics tend to disappear into the sacrificial system.

### Opposition

- **Kandar resistance**: Eastern Valikan clans oppose the Circle and the conflict has escalated from customary raiding into open warfare.
- **Cult of the Great Wyrm**: Led by Grenhildr, once Hjadana's protégé, this faction wants Gormadraug to awaken and believes the primordial's return would preserve true northern culture.
- **Guhjdäl the Revenant**: A druid once sacrificed by the Circle, now returned with resurrection power and slowly building an army from those she saves.
- **Quiet dissent**: Lesser clans and hidden supporters fear the Circle but do not all believe its doctrine should justify endless butchery.

### Why the Circle Matters Outside Valika

- Its raids produce refugees, captives, and regional instability.
- Its prophetic claims tie directly into one of the setting's largest apocalyptic threats.
- It can serve as a religious antagonist, a grim ally against coldfire, or a source of morally impossible bargains.

### Quest Hooks

- Rescue captives before they are delivered to a Sentinel Wood sacrifice.
- Recover a primordial artifact for a druid patron and realize the item will be used against one of the Circle's enemies in a false-flag atrocity.
- Defend a Kandar village from a Thrull raid and discover the Circle was really hunting a hidden warlock of Gormadraug.
- Bargain with a Circle druid for rare anti-coldfire resources in exchange for betraying someone who publicly opposed the order.
