# The Valikan Clans

Valika covers the northernmost region of Etharis. Six major clans--Rune, Syr, Volgr, Mithra, Morgong, and Limgri--dominate, with minor tribes scattered through harsh wilds. The land divides between Thrull (western, raiding-focused, Prismatic Circle influence) and Kandar (eastern, diplomatic, seeking to change Valika's barbaric reputation). Grarjord stretches along the southern border with the Bürach Empire.

While outsiders think only of Thrull's bloodthirsty raiders, the perception has begun to change. Population growth along southern borders as southerners arrive for diplomacy and trade. The tradition of oral storytelling shapes each generation, ensuring the land's strength.

Clan Mithra, Clan Morgong, Clan Limgri, Clan Rune, Clan Syr, Clan Volgr

## Geography

Varied terrain requiring preparation for travel. The region was once the Primordial Land, constantly in flux and uninhabitable. Today, sudden elemental changes are infrequent but not unheard of. Coldfire sweeps from Volgen in the far northeast, consuming land into frozen wasteland. In coldfire's wake, an ice bridge connects Volgen to the mainland. Many fear continued spread could transform the entire region into barren tundra.

Mighty fjords along the coastlines lead into steep ice-covered peaks descending to open tundra (most of Grarjord and Thrull). Snowcapped forests dot fjord edges south of Halsfjord (Clan Rune seat). North of the Teeth (series of fjord-cut peninsulas giving the northlands the appearance of a great beast), frozen rivers wind from perpetually frozen Lake Frostmere, which shares its name with an ice fortress on its surface.

The Thockan Pass cuts through the Haeysor Mountains to Borgund (Clan Syr seat). Northwest: Holgar Strait providing access to the island of Holgar and Tyburn (Clan Volgr seat, primary launch point for reaver fleets).

East of Borgund: forests bleed into open tundra before climbing the Grensfal Mountains and Foxwood Range, nominally delineating Kandar's boundary. Hrist (Kandar capital, Clan Mithra seat) lies on the southeastern mountain edge along the valley of the Isbryteralva river. Summerhelm and Runeheim rest near the Skuggiskogr Forest.

Further east: Njucka Mountains protect Cold Iron Keep. The Ormralva River flows to Shadow Storm Gulf. Mountains continue east to meet the coldfire bridge and Fort Kentigern. The isle of Cinderghast lies in smoky haze off the northern coast, counterpoint to frozen Volgen where the Snow Queen's palace sits in the Astrik Mountains.

## History

Mostly passed down via oral tradition. Greatest legends compiled into the Lord Edda by scholars at Hrist.

### The Great Prismatic Wyrm

Before humans, mighty primordials roamed the north, drawing power from the sacred elements. Greatest was Gormadraug, a colossal serpent worshiped even by his peers. Of all the stories, none sow as much fear as descriptions of the utter destruction left in his wake--coldfire erupting at his every step, consuming all living things.

The Seven Heroes sought to end his reign: Kentigern of the Wastes, the sorceress Rune, Syr the Seven-Bladed, Volgr Clad-in-Iron, Limgri Lightbringer, Morgong Shadestrider, and Mithra of the Silver Harp. They fought through elemental hordes, were swallowed by the Great Wyrm, and hatched a daring plan from within to pierce his heart.

Many believe Gormadraug will one day rise again to consume all of Etharis. Many clans take this tale literally. Woe to those who stand between these fundamentalist Valikans and their goals.

### Thorgard's Saga

Thousands of years after Gormadraug's fall, the clans were divided over countless squabbles. Thorgard, an orc of Clan Volgr, decided to unite them. Aided by a hag with visions of the future, he quested for Kentigern's legendary weapon Bane-Slayer--a magical double-bladed sword wreathed in ice and flames.

Thorgard traveled clan to clan, gathering forces by diplomacy or force. A cunning plan drew southern invaders into open tundra through coastal raids, where united clan strength defeated them. Thorgard then set out west with a mighty fleet to conquer more lands--and was never seen again. His saga remains a staple of Valikan storytelling.

### The Prismatic Circle

Elemental druid Kybard claimed prophetic visions proving the Seven Heroes hadn't vanquished Gormadraug--he merely sleeps beneath earth and sea. Kybard claimed that a future where the Great Wyrm awakened could only be prevented by sating the creature with magic and mortal blood.

The druids of the Prismatic Circle travel between ritual sites called Sentinel Woods, making great sacrifices: magical trinkets from the southlands, stolen livestock, and humanoid victims--both condemned prisoners and thralls marked as offerings. The total fallen over centuries of raiding, defenders killed, and sacrificial victims might never be counted. With each raiding season, ground beneath the Sentinel Woods grows darker with blood.

Led by Grand Druid Hjadana, who steadfastly upholds Kybard's visions. Her fanaticism drives vicious cycles of raiding and death. Hjadana's control of Thrull clans, especially Clan Volgr (whose warriors serve as the Circle's military arm), ensures enforcement.

#### Grenhildr and the Cult of the Great Wyrm

Once Hjadana's protégé and expected successor. Separated from her party after a ritual sacrifice, lost in a blizzard, presumed dead. Returned to Tyburn with a tale of being saved and fed by prismatic drakes, sheltering in a cavern where Gormadraug spoke to her. She claims the Wyrm's long sleep causes his power to fade, and the north faces apocalyptic ruin if that power ends.

Her followers are the Cult of the Great Wyrm. Much to Hjadana's horror, their beliefs spread even in Thrull where the Prismatic Circle holds most sway. A schism within the Circle.

### Raiding and Thralls

Valikan raids inspire deep fear across Etharis--tales of Clan Volgr's berserker fury, Clan Syr's arms and armor, Clan Rune's destructive magic. When longships sail south in spring, coastal settlements from Kulen Sound to the Gulf of Lions secure their goods and people.

Valika's harsh winters deplete population. Desperate and immoral clans bolster numbers through captives. Thrall-raiding is so ancient that many Valikans in Thrull have no perspective to question it. The north's rocky fields, mines, fishing boats, and hunting require more labor than the population can provide. So millennia ago, clans looked south.

Clans raid each other endlessly too, but local raids effectively trade members back and forth, so they focus more on resource theft. Thrall raids target the south through reaver fleets.

### Politics

**Clan Rune.** Most representative political structure among major clans. Overseers elected for each settlement. Larger towns have three-person overseer councils. Halsfjord governed by the Council of Rune--thirteen elected councilors. Long-codified written laws with appeals process to the Council.

**Clan Syr.** Politics tied to the Jarnforning, a hierarchy of labor, responsibility, and authority entered at age ten. Twelve tiers (nivaer) from apprentice to clan master. Each settlement led by three wardens from the highest local tier, voted by peers.

**Clan Volgr.** Hierarchy established by dominance. Strongest rise to authority, but strength reckoned many ways--tactical acumen and leadership valued alongside martial prowess. Chief Nolgr Magnusson has ruled nearly thirty years. As clan leader, also Keeper of the Three Tribes (title from Prismatic Circle), effectively master of all Thrull-based clans.

**Clan Mithra.** Representative democracy. Villages hold things (moots) electing overseers. All over fifteen can vote. Overseers appoint a Voice (usually from best musicians and skalds) to travel to larger jarldom things. Jarls elected but tend hereditary, blurring democracy and hereditary monarchy. At the fall equinox althing, jarls elect a monarch. Current: Queen Andrea Helsing of Hrist.

Greatest foes: Thrull clans, whose incursions forced standing armies. Queen Andrea prefers diplomacy but violence seems the only answer Thrull accepts.

**Clan Morgong.** No centralized government. Jarl Summerhelm holds hereditary title. Village leaders typically the eldest survivors of raids and winters. No written records as a matter of pride. Laws memorized by a lawspeaker (skald) who interprets disputes on precedent. Murder and sexual assault punishable by execution. Sexual assault forbidden even on raids. Intentional damage to watercraft equally grave.

**Clan Limgri.** Little central government. Families operate independently, gathering four times yearly on solstices and equinoxes. Jarl Angrboða Grata of Solheim and druid Taala Brightstar speak for the clan. Few codified laws; communities enact their own. Gravest transgressors abandoned in the wilds (near-certain death).

Hospitality is the one governing law: strangers never denied food and drink. This generosity exploited by Volgr scouts who report nomad locations to raiding parties. Unwilling to change cultural laws, Limgri petitioned Clan Mithra for extended outposts. Queen Andrea provided warriors in exchange for Limgri taking control of Cold Iron Keep.

## Provinces

Borders in Valika are nebulous, flowing into each other without much regard for boundaries unless a river or mountain delineates the divide. Clan members may cross into rival territory without knowing.

### Thrull

From Holgar island in the far west to Grensfal Mountain foothills. Dominant culture in Valika, defining the raiding culture by which the region is known. Though each clan has its own governance, most follow the Prismatic Circle's edicts encouraging constant conflict to appease Gormadraug.

#### Important Individuals

**Grand Druid Hjadana.** Presides over ritualistic sacrifices from Tyburn on Holgar. Whether victims are Valikan or foreign is often of little concern--only that blood is spilled. Her closeness to Chief Nolgr and the Circle's presence in the Volgr clan seat ensures strong bonds between druids and warriors.

**Grenhildr.** Caused a schism of faith within the Prismatic Circle. Claims Gormadraug's sleep causes his power to fade and the north faces ruin if it ends. Her Cult of the Great Wyrm spreads even in Thrull to Hjadana's horror.

**Nolgr Magnusson.** Chief of Clan Volgr, effectively leader of all Thrull. Personally leads raids for three decades. As Keeper of the Three Tribes, invested by the Prismatic Circle with authority over Clan Rune and Clan Syr, at least ceremonially. Master of ceremony; charisma and magnetism nearly as deadly as his axe hand. Proudly acknowledges his relationship with Hjadana as the foundation of his power. Rumors he fathered at least one of her children.

**Ordun Doransson.** Chief of Clan Rune. Imposing figure with shadowed complexion and sun-bright hair, under-shaved and braided. Protected against elements by magic, goes bare-chested even in coldest weather. Positions himself as Nolgr's equal. Clan Rune's pride and independence growing.

### Kandar

Reaches northeast to Volgen (once island, now connected by coldfire bridge). More populated regions near center at the Grensfal Mountains and Thrull-adjacent disputed lands. United troops of Mithra, Limgri, and Morgong keep watch. More temperate Summerhelm provides a southern border; Runeheim further south is nominally within Kandar.

Despite diplomatic efforts, foreigners still first associate Valika with Thrull's violence. Facial tattoos, the wild reputation, and Thrull stereotypes leave Kandar fighting against prejudice.

#### Important Individuals

**Queen Andrea Helsing.** Leader of Clan Mithra, elected war chief of united Kandar clans. Dedicated to Kandar's survival free from the Prismatic Circle. Welcomes trade with other nations and pursues military or magical alliances. Savvy at working outside the rules--frequently has agents hire adventurers for deniable tasks related to the war effort. Some missions walk a thin line along the edge of honor.

Youth spent traveling Kandar as bodyguard to Urda Longsight, a Mithra seer. Learned patience, listening, and how knowing someone's wants creates leverage. Once a golden-haired shield maiden, now graying from age and worry. Shows battle scars with honor.

**Taala Brightstar.** Speaker of Clan Limgri. Raised among wandering Limgri, marked as a seer young. Ran with reindeer in reindeer form as a child. Devotes time to manifesting the Lightbringer philosophy of beauty and peace. Elderly druid who prefers animal forms but position requires human presence. Fears dual disaster of Thrull war and coldfire expansion. Views themselves as spiritual heir to Limgri Lightbringer. Seeks to support the Order of Kentigern, petitions for protection from Volgr raids, and reaches out to the secretive Redclaws (who remain elusive, perhaps because Taala is skilled at shapeshifting but not a true shapeshifter).

**Thrulldr.** Enormous werebear leading the Redclaw druids. Claims heritage to Kentigern. Hulking appearance in both forms--human body covered in fur-thick hair, braided beard down chest. Distrusts druids who haven't undergone permanent transformation. To him, druids without a second shape aren't close enough to the wild to be trusted. The Redclaws are primarily druids and shapeshifters; the final test is transformation into a werecreature, determined not by skill with nature magic but strength of heart and will. Widespread following, all beholden to Thrulldr as alpha.

For clanfolk fearing Thrull invasion or coldfire, the Redclaws offer hope for another path--one embracing their harsh natural world rather than fighting it.

**Njavesh Luunsdottir.** Leader of the Fox Whisperers druid order. Born in the druidic circle, gifted caster and shapechanger with deep connection to awakened foxes roaming the northern tundra. Wide information network from decades of wandering and secrets the frostbite foxes return. Those injured while traveling through fox territory, or those who respectfully request audience through foxes, can gain access to Njavesh.

**Abbot Sanrun.** Head of the Order of Kentigern (formerly contemplative monks, now coldfire defenders). Bald, grudgel features, old enough to remember before the coldfire crisis yet still appearing in his prime. Mournful aura beneath welcoming friendliness. Originally Clan Mithra, fully devoted to the Order. Dismayed at lack of funding since the border war. Believes Kandar and Thrull must unite against coldfire, though his calls are generally ignored.

Expanded recruiting beyond Kandar into southern kingdoms. Particularly interested in evokers (combat coldfire directly) and alchemists (create tools: alchemist's fire, burning oils, exploding casks). Runs tight organization with ranks. Training grounds within Fort Kentigern are extensive; work with ice drakes, polar bears, and giant lynxes requires many skilled riders and handlers. Druids who fail the Redclaw test frequently make their way to the Order, where Sanrun welcomes them.

### Grarjord

The entirety of the north is often called Grarjord by southerners. Encompasses much of the land from Bürach Empire border along the coastline and across open tundra. Borders between it and Thrull or Kandar are tricky.

Open tundra home to many minor clans enjoying relative freedom from major clan conflicts. Very few resources worth squabbling over, so very little oversight. The downside: unforgiving land, bitter cold, barren soil, monstrous beasts lurking outside campfire light.

### Locations of Interest

#### Skarfanes

Former watch post and reaver fleet waystation. Proximity to the Bürach Empire and their prized Erlefurt led to capture by the imperial fleet. Rather than pushing into Grarjord, the empire declared Skarfanes a free city and neutral trading post. Today, clan traders largely control it, protected by imperial garrison forces.

Sprawling settlement of huts and homes clustered around longhouses, surrounded by formidable stockade wall. Four gates; many locals live outside the walls. After three generations, intermarriage has created a mixed culture unique to Skarfanes.

#### Halsfjord

Clan Rune seat on a fjord peninsula. One of the most easily protected cities, a fact residents surely appreciate given their intimate knowledge of raiding. Boasts magical knowledge rivaling Bürach Empire colleges. Stealthy raids by war bands are a constant threat, seeking to enslave knowledgeable mages or artificers.

Walls fortified by years of enchantment. Magical beacons blaze at night guiding ships and merchants. Magic is the leading commodity Clan Rune trades for food and sundries. Named for sorceress Rune, who used powerful magic to imprison Cinderghast's fire elementals before retiring here. The schools that formed attracted the world's best sorcerers and artificers.

#### Hrist

Clan Mithra seat and Kandar capital. Bustling city inviting people from across Etharis. Three circles: outer (foreigners without valuable skills, soldier barracks, less reputable merchants), middle (most Valikans and skilled craftspeople, the Kaupang market, the Skald Library renowned throughout Etharis for relics and artifacts), inner (Great Hall where Queen Andrea holds court, oldest Mithra family residences).

#### Tyburn

Clan Volgr seat and Thrull capital. Bustling port spoken of with equal parts fear and reverence. Fearsome longship fleets depart from the deep harbor on Holgar's southern corner. The Prismatic Circle's base of operations, making Thrull effectively a theocracy.

#### Borgund

Clan Syr seat in the Armarka Mountains. Upper levels spread along stepped mountainside terraces lined with stone-walled houses. True size apparent only upon entering Syr's Well--slow-sloping ramps descending into the mountain's depths. Long tunnels radiate out like spokes, with hundreds of chambers and halls sharing space with legendary underground forges.

Half-year of winter night is no concern for those dwelling within. Warm warren of magical rune light; forge heat balances stone chill; elaborate ventilation tunnels cycle fresh air. Manufacturing hub for hallmark Syr weapons and armor. Classrooms and training forges for young clanfolk and thralls. Many thralls elect to join Clan Syr after service, dedicated to metalwork artistry, making Borgund the most diverse settlement in Thrull (more dwarves, elves, and southern folk than anywhere else).

#### Summerhelm

Second largest Kandar settlement, seat of Jarl Birger. Tall stone wall casts shadows creating perpetual twilight. The Dream Market: growing center of illicit commerce as the Jarl's attention dwindles. Ill-gotten weaponry at reasonable prices, magic charms both real and fake. Construction more southern in style. The Summer Palace resembles an Ostoyan castle, likely once held by Ostoyan ancestors, but deeper inside reveals non-human original builders.

#### Solheim

Small city on Shadow Storm Gulf. Named for a grand sun temple that fell into disrepair after the gods fell, now a decaying reminder in the city center that faith alone cannot save you. Smallest of Kandar's three cities. Shipyards are the remaining pride, along with the great hall where Jarl Grata stores her things. Overarching atmosphere of doom.

#### Fort Kentigern

Originally a contemplative monastery, now the last shield against coldfire. High keep with thick stone walls shaped by craftspeople and mages. Curtain walls 10 feet thick with defensive siege weaponry: catapults, trebuchets, ballista hurling flaming pitch, enclosures for evokers to hurl fireballs.

The Order of Kentigern gathers here, not for contemplation but as defenders of the living. No clan allegiance within the fort; past is forgotten upon joining. Majority drawn from Valikans but people from all Etharis counted among them. All skills welcome against coldfire. Many die in the line of duty.

Despite all efforts, coldfire continues to advance. Volgen's few remaining settlements shrink as residents flee or die.

#### Cinderghast

Volcanic islands. Tales say Cinderghast was always better suited to fire elementals. The Svalr tell of a time when the settlement of Varra flourished there. When sorceress Rune trapped the salamander Ixlalu with a curse, the land erupted into poisoned landscape. A vast ruin covered in ash and buried rock where an ancient city once stood.

Curse blocks certain areas to mortal travel: visible destinations that you walk toward but find yourself returned to your starting point. This is the spell binding Ixlalu and his army. The bonds are weakening; smallest fire elementals beginning to escape.

#### The Foxwood Range

Narrow vale in Grensfal Mountain foothills. Network of warren tunnels and dens for frostbite foxes to whelp and raise young in spring. Unique geography ensures large dispersal for autumn and winter trapping. Frostbite fox fur is resilient and weatherproof, an important part of clan culture.

Fox Whisperers (moon druid order led by Njavesh) shepherd the packs and ensure hunting doesn't deplete numbers. Packs led by awakened fox elders patrol the tundra seeking lost travelers, focusing on newly released thralls attempting the arduous journey south. Foxes lead charges along safe trails and hunt to feed those unable to feed themselves. Almost mythical status among thralls: sighting the foxes means luck and certain survival.

#### The Sentinel Woods

Prismatic Circle's altars of sacrifice. Great oaks standing vigil above the underworld where Gormadraug sleeps, their roots drawing blood downward. Oaks shouldn't grow in this landscape--an even darker magic makes its presence known in the eternal shadow, steeped into blood-black soil.

Four druids at each sacrifice speak Gormadraug's tale aloud, then flense each victim's skin with ritual chanting and razor-sharp knives. During humanoid sacrifices, stolen magic trinkets are incorporated. Bodies left for crows and wolves over long months. Next druids enter a different grove section where previous victims have rotted to scattered bones.

Clan Volgr patrols circle Sentinel Woods seeking intruders who might prevent or revive sacrifices.

## Quest Hooks

### Echoes of the Beast
Hunting parties returned to Borgund whispering of a great skeletal stag head in the fog. Mutilated bestial corpses found along trails. A scout warns of a gnoll warband roaming the tundra. Hunting grounds endangered. Wardens request elimination of gnoll incursions and determination of whether the Beast remains. Characters must deal with creatures lurking in cold and gloom, mutated beasts, and Clan Syr patrols offering possibilities for combat, roleplaying, or exploration.

### The Creeping Cold
Queen Andrea receives urgent aid request from Cold Iron Keep. Only route: crossing Lake Jakkan, home to a terrible beast said to sport coldfire antlers and drag souls beneath the surface. Latest transport ship arrived late--every soul aboard frozen solid, strange glowing puddles coalescing into Coldfire Oozes. Half-dozen guards claimed. Oozes hacked into pieces unintentionally proliferated their number.

Transport ship rests too close to shore for the warden's comfort. Oozes vulnerable to fire, but lighting the wooden ship could dump them into the lake. Unknown if they can freeze the lake solid (doom for the keep). Characters should seek creative solutions. And don't forget about the beast in the lake.

### Sheltering Boughs
West of Halsfjord, village healer Sarvenka missing near a Sentinel Wood. Found dead by an Ordeal Tree. Her latest scroll contained instructions to gain a tree's blessing--the tree chosen was an Ordeal Tree and her intentions somewhat noble. Under full moon, Redclaws patrolling the Sentinel Woods have been drawn by blood scent and fallen prey to baser instincts. Four Werebear Ascetics and five Werewolf Ravagers creep upon the party.

### A Crucible of Fire
Order of Kentigern hires expedition to Cinderghast to research flame-bearing monsters. A group of Malcinder and their Alpha have made a cozy home in tunnels--content to be observed and even allow small samples harvested if approached with care. Not as benign: a pair of Mjorks and Mjork Charger causing the actual volcanic uptick. Hostile but potentially valuable to capture alive for Fort Kentigern researchers.

### The Fancy of Foxes
Traveler Angr lost in blizzard above the circle of shadows during perpetual darkness. Her brother ill, food running out, she braved the tundra to hunt an elk. Two Frostbite Foxes have been tailing the party; now they circle firelight, beckoning into the storm. Following the foxes (tracking, perception, creative means) leads to Angr's snow burrow--hypothermic, dehydrated, ravenous. Once rescued, she asks help hunting the elk she tracked, promising gratitude and hospitality.

## Cold Iron Keep

Remote prison fortress in the Jakkan Mountains. Silent battlements loom two hundred feet above Lake Jakkan's glassy surface. Guests must cross the lake--not all who enter will exit again.

Built in forgotten centuries by wicked mage Mordu, who used spells to raise it from living rock. Filled with perilous magic, secured against the outside world. Mordu sent servants to abduct innocents for blood sacrifice. The beings he directed offerings to are unrecorded. Heroes trapped and killed Mordu; folk stormed and ransacked the Keep, leaving it to the elements.

Clan Mithra discovered the empty ruin and garrisoned it. Centuries later, Queen Andrea entrusted it to Clan Limgri (convinced it lacked strategic value) in exchange for conversion to a prison.

### Commander Bodil Garðr

Former Røkkva skald and doomsayer whose performances sparked unrest in Solheim. Forced into exile as warden. Garðr believes doom is to be embraced, not feared. She transformed the Keep into a haven modeled after her image: refurbished Guest Halls teeming with skalds, scholars, and adventurers.

She offers "hospitality" not citizenship. Revocation means property confiscation and ejection into the elements. Favored prisoners get relaxed treatment and may be sent on secretive missions. Uncooperative inmates face elaborate punishments including experimentation. She is crafty and vindictive.

Three administrative boards: Correctional Affairs (Constable Ulherik Vander), Hospitality Affairs (Constable Heiga Iðun), Provisional Affairs (Constable Agnes Harlech).

### Structure

#### Guest Halls (ground floor)

Kettle Row: drinking wells, food carts, miniature taverns, apothecaries. Selection surprisingly good for such a remote place. Maker's Market: suppliers for adventurers heading into the Deepvaults; Hospitality Affairs ensures cheating or thieving is done with Garðr's approval. Chalkstick Hall: six frozen fountains; a rift in the floor called Chalkstick Chasm, overgrown with pale cold-weather succulents ("chalksticks"), provides dangerous climb bypassing magical seals to the Deepvaults.

#### Offices of the Warden (upper levels)

Most visible portion of the castle. Commander Garðr and her circle hold the reins behind closed doors. Battlements tower over Lake Jakkan with parapets, towers, barbicans, arrow loops, and murder holes--somewhat understaffed as Garðr diverted garrison to peacekeeping.

Hall of Black Rot: highest reception hall for nightly revels of most favored guests. Stained by namesake mold removed during refurbishment. Invitation marks one as most respected.

Seersalir: highest tower crowned by a scrying device Garðr has spent years restoring. Under guidance of discreet scholars, she believes she's unlocked a key to understanding Mordu's magic. Keeps close eye on prison and world outside.

#### The Cells (below Guest Halls)

Some two hundred prison chambers for enemies that can't be dealt with by clan law: cultists, political foes, giants, primordial druids, monstrous creatures. Alongside: a dozen laboratories for experiments on non-compliant inmates.

### The Deepvaults

Extensive, mysterious, diverse. Layered hundreds of feet into mountain roots, perhaps beyond. One could spend a lifetime exploring and barely scratch the surface.

**The Creep.** Uppermost level, plagued by curtains of coldfire moving in irregular patterns. Allows careful plundering by adventurers who keep their wits. Disproportionately armed with Mordu's traps. Half mapped; new monstrous denizens appear regularly.

**Fountain of Twilight.** Hidden vaults on the south end of Lake Jakkan, originally accessible only from the deepest levels. Centuries of dripping water wore chambers into a cascading waterfall two hundred feet high. Waters feed bioluminescent fungal forests thriving in cold twilight. Few prove skilled enough to enter, from lake above or vaults below.

**Hidden Lake.** Labyrinthine chambers partially flooded by water from the Fountain of Twilight. Tides rise and fall along grand staircases and ramped corridors. Elevated balconies and balustrades form a shoreline through the maze. Giant oyster beds congregate, visible only at rare instances. Their pearls are the most valuable in Etharis; some said to deposit souls rather than pearls.

**Flooded Deepvaults.** Sealed by magic and deathly cold water. Most mysterious and inhospitable features. Mordu's most obscure magic lies inaccessible. Those who stumble upon the seals hear bellowing calls from beyond to open the doors and let the flood rise.

**Bruhvelving.** Most remote known chamber, accessed via twelve magical seals in the form of animated relief sculptures: archfey, beast, butcher, fool, lamb, raven, ruler, scholar, troll, warrior, wyrm, and youth. Each holds secrets about the Deepvaults and can be beguiled or entreated. Said to conceal passages to even deeper vaults. May have no physical connection to Cold Iron Keep other than the sealed portals--possibly installed in a separate part of the mountains.

### Approach to the Keep

Lake Jakkan: several miles wide. Road from Hrist plunges beneath the surface. No boats kept on shore by Garðr's order. Ferryman Huskol watches for approved passengers. Crossing takes hours and costs nothing, raising the question of why Huskol serves and what he lives on.

A fearsome shapeshifting creature said to lair beneath the lake, combining features of caribou, whale, and dire wolf. Often appears to inmates crossing for the first time.

Ferryman's Sty: island of detritus near the center where Huskol lives. Giant beams and metal plates protrude from broken stone rubble. Tales tell of a village buried when Cold Iron Keep was magically raised. Huskol was already living there when Clan Mithra explorers first sighted the Keep. Old and bent but strong-armed and keen-eyed, he sleeps for days, waiting for passengers to hail him using an ivory horn. His devotion seems to be to the Keep itself, not its warden.

### Important Individuals

**Astar, Queen of Midnight.** Ancient medusa known in Clan Mithra folk tales as the Barrow's Eye. Trapped nearly a century ago in an iron mask with a smiling child image after a three-day battle of wits. Sealed in a mirror-walled cell. Nurses hatred with cold detachment. Her whispered divinations and unsought counsels are as dangerous as her gaze.

**Fregr Ulfborn (the Winter Wolf).** Retired adventurer holding court in a shabby drinking hole called the Torchbearer in Kettle Row. Weathered face and silver hair belie axe-hand strength. His guild the Vaulters boasts fifty members and dares to openly disobey the warden. Turf war brewing that will finally clear space in the Guest Halls.

**Gorm, Heir of the Wyrm.** Red-haired madman burning continuously with coldfire head to foot. Whispers echo through the Keep: he's seen the Great Wyrm's cataclysm, coldfire obeys his commands, he moves freely through the Deepvaults with the warden's blessing. Garðr wasted no time tattooing magical controls on him and sending him into the Creep. If his prophecy is true and his touch upon Valikan snow heralds the end, that suits Garðr.

**Jormund the Dreamer.** Frost giant vampire in a thirty-foot quartz sarcophagus, kept in torpor by enchantment. Dreams so vividly that images can be scried from Seersalir. Garðr fears him too much to wake, but others in the Keep can think of many uses for such an ally.

**Riven Kreig.** Prismatic Circle fanatic who absorbs life essence at death and wears victims' faces. Apprehended in Hrist's streets. Refuses to show true form (if it could even be identified). Garðr hopes to turn him to Røkkva. Until then, a delicate laboratory experiment.

**Tove av Eikenbu.** Clan Mithra runesmith, Røkkva convert. Thin, wiry, fifties. Maintains austere, disquieting focus on studies despite the Keep's raucous culture. Perhaps the only person Garðr fully trusts. First of twelve prominent scholars who took residence when Garðr became warden. Instrumental in developing the runic Geas tattoo controlling prisoners.

### Cold Iron Keep Quest Hooks

**Beguile the Seal of Bruhvelving.** Since entering the Keep, many descriptions of Bruhvelving heard but few claim to have reached it. Down Chalkstick Chasm, through the Creep, into unmapped Deepvaults. Choose a seal--the choice is as important as contriving to open it. Whether by guile, knowledge, or bitter sacrifice. Beyond the wards and monsters, Bruhvelving may hide truths about the beginning and end of existence.

**Black Rot Revels.** Days of petitioning have landed a stolen invitation to the Hall of Black Rot where real power rests. Siv Hrothigvar, purveyor of vilest delights, will be in attendance and off guard. What foul crimes to commit for a place beside her and avenge the innocent souls she preyed on?

**Forage the Fountain of Twilight.** Plague spreading through Guest Halls. Medicine scarce until new shipments. Victims rise from deathbeds with a taste for living flesh. Rare fungal growths in Fountain of Twilight hold the secret. A party skilled enough to navigate the vaults must bring them back before inhabitants tear each other limb from limb.

**Interview With a Vampire.** Investigation reveals prisoner Jormund appears in tales alongside the Great Prismatic Wyrm and hero Kentigern. If the vampire frost giant is over a thousand years old and harbors hatred for the Wyrm, he could be an invaluable ally.

**Lost in Ferryman's Sty.** Search for the deathless head of Økerhardt, one of Queen Drejv the Betrayer's five decapitated heirs. Research accounts of the lake creature. Avoid or defeat it to scour the scrap heap. If Økerhardt can be coaxed to speak, his knowledge may reach back to Mordu's era.

**Oysters of the Hidden Lake.** Where tides rise and fall along the Hidden Lake's corridors, giant oyster beds congregate. Their pearls are Etharis's most valuable. Some deposit souls rather than pearls. Perhaps a dark patron sends the party for a specific soul, or the Vaulters require one as a token to join.

**Revolt at the End of the World.** The Vaulters have learned of Garðr's Røkkva devotions and plan to hasten the end. Frightening tales of her experiments, decadent revelries, and favoritism toward Kandar's enemies. Now is the time to rise up and end her reign.

**Rescue in the Creep.** Dungeon delver Averik Olherdottir called for help via Sending. Companions killed by a wraith; she jumped blindly and broke her legs. No food, exhausted from crawling to avoid coldfire. Few days to rescue her by picking out her trail amidst treacherous passages, avoiding spectral former companions, navigating ever-shifting coldfire.

**Uninvited Guests.** Failed to impress the warden; shut out from the Keep. Alternative entries: dive into the lake creature's lair and follow a narrow chute into the Fountain of Twilight; scale glacial cliffs above the third battlement to enter the Offices of the Warden; or impersonate prisoners and get locked into a cell hoping to court Garðr's favor.
