**Chapter 4: The Charneault Kingdom**

# Chapter 4: The Charneault Kingdom

Siraj Fakhri

A land of broken vows, mystical fey, and a tense but strained peace between once-warring cultures, the Charneault Kingdom is a seemingly idyllic realm atop a tinderbox of complex intrigues and inevitable conflict waiting to ignite.

Unfolding in the southwestern corner of Etharis, beneath the rugged Rock-Teeth Mountains and the dense forest known as the Grove Maze, Charneault is renowned for its meandering hills, vast sky, and clear rivers. It’s said that the residents of Charneault, both the elves and northerners who maintain a tentative peace, hold a closer connection to the land and the Spirits of Nature than any other nation. They also have the most to lose when the whims of those spirits take a horrific turn.

For generations, an impenetrable fog has descended over the lands of Charneault. The mist was harmless for centuries, just another natural sight to behold in a land of countless beauties and mysteries. In recent times, however, the fog has transformed into a malevolent force. Within the mist, plants grow twisted and beasts become wild. Roads and towns are plagued by wicked spirits and malevolent creatures wherever the fog rolls in.

The king of Charneault, Thibault de Sauveterre, and the elven Archdruid, Aetherion, work to discover the truth behind this sudden change. Despite their shared efforts, the kingdom remains divided in their opinions. Many elves believe the dark mist is a spiritual reckoning: a vengeful awakening in the land for the suffering inflicted upon their ancestors. Other settlements see nature itself as the problem, the dark mist a threat to be vanquished.

The Charneault Kingdom now faces the specter of its own history, an ambiguous future, and the mist which encompasses it all. Through this, Charneault’s generous, fertile landscape belies a culture built upon mutual distrust, moral hypocrisy, and a well-earned fear of the unknown forces that exist just beyond the veil of nature.

## Geography

The natural barriers that protect Charneault create a profound veil from the ugliness of Etharis. Travelers who brave the twisting roads through these primal boundaries hardly believe the strange beauty they witness in the Land of the Thousand Rivers. Nevertheless, wayfarers are warned to remain alert. The splendor of this mystical realm belies the hidden danger of the fey, at whose whim awe can easily become terror.

### The Grove Maze

The Kingdom of Charneault is nearly impossible to reach on foot. The Grove Maze chokes the pass between the Grey Spine and the Rock-Teeth Mountains, and creeps southward to partially conceal the Thousand Rivers from the east.

The forest is ancient. It’s a tangled labyrinth of deeply wooded foothills, misted valleys, cascading rivers, and forested wetlands. The Grove Maze conceals scenic beauties, but it has just as many dark corners. Careless wayfarers become lost within. Even veteran explorers must watch the paths they’ve wandered, lest the Grove Maze rearrange itself to ensure there’s no way out.

The twisted boughs of the trees creak heavily, as if the forest is constantly moving. Maps and landmarks can’t be trusted. A beautiful green vale with a stream like a blue, silk ribbon running through its center may reveal itself to an explorer, never to be found again when they return just a day later. The Spirits of Nature show whatever they will of the forest, and they conceal anything they desire to be left undisturbed. The Grove Maze is the final resting place of many would-be trespassers who try to pierce such barriers, their corpses feeding the vegetation at the gates to the Realm of Faerie.

Folks who the archfey permit into their realm speak of their experiences in the Grove Maze as if they’d wandered through a dream. Some claim to have seen the glittering spires of a palace nestled in a flower garden, while others report fleeing from a green-scaled dragon snaking through a moldy bog. A wintery glade is dusted by a pillar of snowfall. Birds bicker in plain language before falling silent as wayfarers approach. Yet a person can explore the Grove Maze for a lifetime and witness none of these peculiar sights if the Spirits of Nature don’t deem them worthy or interesting enough.

### Land of the Thousand Rivers

Countless rivers flow through the heartland of the Charneault Kingdom between its rolling hills. Dense woods and fertile farmland spread across the entire kingdom, making the realm rich in natural resources. Aside from the larger cities, villages tend to be small. Each nestles beside a brook or spring teeming with fish and overgrown with foliage. The waters sparkle as if lined with diamonds. Between the curtains of silvery mist that drift over the land, Charneault is truly breathtaking.

The southwest portion of the Charneault Kingdom extends into a peninsula, with the Emerald Sea to the west and the Gulf of Lions to the east. The difficulty of traversing the Grove Maze makes maritime trade a necessity. Charneault’s largest port is arguably Morencia, which remains its own city-state but whose independence is partially protected by the kingdom. This arrangement helps Charneault keep inland roads closed to other nations, shielding itself against the Bürach Empire’s imperialism or Castinella’s intolerance toward primal mysticism.

Yet life isn’t without difficulty. The untamed nature of the wilderness means small settlements are preyed upon by dire wolves and brigands, who hide easily in the fog-laden valleys. The Spirits of Nature are capricious and covetous. Children go missing often. They might be kidnapped on a forest’s edge or unknowingly exchanged with a wechselkind. Faeries take up residence in newly built homes if the timber used was cut from a tree that once belonged to them. Entire communities may disappear into the wilds to dance around a cauldron, forgetting their mortal lives to become playthings for the fey.

### Changing Seasons

The ever-changing seasons transform the Charneault Kingdom. The four Courts of the Fey are made manifest in Etharis, and the folk of the Thousand Rivers celebrate each season for its own blessings and challenges. Those who revere the archfey perform rites as the seasons change to ensure favor with the Court who currently rules the land.

In the spring, heavy rain falls, followed by rays of sun painting the realm in lush greens and flowering colors. Yet the woodlands become wilder as the Spirits of Nature turn mischievous. Hunters and lumberjacks avoid the deep forests in spring. The allure of satyr songs drift on the breeze, and the trees twist their branches to trap explorers within their groves. Those seduced by the beauty of spring become lost forever in the verdant wilderness.

During summer the rivers recede. The days become warm and dry. Field hands know to avoid working beneath the heat of the midday sun, lest they be visited by a maiden or crone wielding a sickle, or the undead pestas she leaves in her wake. Those seeking relief from the hot sun are wise to leave certain pools or brooks alone. Fish-fey spirits called gaspers swim beneath the surface, waiting to drown careless fools who plunge their filthy hands in Charneault’s pristine waters.

Autumn turns the realm brilliant shades of orange and brown. Cruel-spirited faeries awaken scarecrows and farm tools to wreak horror upon undeserving peasants. As the last of the grain is harvested for winter and put into storage, the final rites are performed to express gratitude to the Spirits of Nature and ensure they don’t feel slighted by what has been taken from the land.

As winter grips the realm, the rivers freeze in the kingdom’s north. Commoners huddle inside their firelit homes to endure the cold, dreaming of spring while sickness takes hold. Stories claim the Winter Queen watches Charneault through her frozen mirror and inflicts cruelties for each offense against the Spirits of Nature that went unpunished in the previous year. True or otherwise, malevolent fey slink into settlements looking to devour sufferers of such illness or sorrow. Their true names unknown, though monster hunters call these spirits grief eaters.

### The Mist

The mists that permeate Charneault seem magical to behold, but in truth originate from magma vents along the nation’s southern coast and beneath the Emerald Sea. That’s not to say the mists aren’t an instrument of the archfey. The largest of these heated fissures lies beneath a lake, inside a caldera where the Temple of Mist is built. Within that thick fog, a circle of druids speak with the Spirits of Nature directly. Their rituals, and the Temple itself, are never visible from outside the crater.

Sometimes the mist is harmless: a light veil that passes gently over the lands and hides the frolicking pixies. Silvery hosts of sprites are said to move unseen within the mist, marching to secret battles waged between the Fey Courts. When the mist has come and gone, mushrooms crop up in faerie circles. The goats have been milked and the milk stolen. The grass is longer, the trees regrown their leaves, and pollen drifts upon the breeze. But this gentle passing of the mist is no longer always the case.

In recent times, the mist has turned grey and frightening. The residents of Charneault flee when the dark mist comes to embrace the land in an umbral fog. Those who cannot escape must shelter as best they can.

Within the dark mist, the weather changes to that of other seasons. Fertile soil turns to dust, and crop fields lay barren in spring, while a blanket of snow freezes folks inside their homes during summer. In winter, a flash flood from melting rivers and pelting rain can wash away whole settlements.

The dark mist turns the natural foliage hostile. Spores become noxious. Vines awaken to strangle wayfarers. Treants begin bleeding a sickly sap, attracting swarms of vicious insects.

The Spirits of Nature themselves appear corrupted by the dark mist. Pixies lose their wings and transform into vengeful pucks—corpse gardeners who fertilize their groves with peoples’ innards. Sylvan archers stalk into settlements and hunt the occupants as if for sport. Entire towns become a pale-spun nest of giant spiders. The sounds of fey merrily singing are gone—only mortals screaming in the darkness, and the echoes of whimsical laughter like children playing a game.

## History

Fey Conflict

Before there was a kingdom called Charneault, there was Caer Neiada, the “land of countless rivers” in the Elvish tongue. A verdant paradise of rolling hills and fertile wilderness that was isolated from other nations, Caer Neiada flourished on its own for centuries before the Era of Expansion.

### The Ulufey

The first settlers of Caer Neiada called themselves the ulufey: “mortal fey” in Elvish. Believing they were the direct descendants of the Spirits of Nature, the children of the archfey, in everything they did they gave respect and offerings to the seasonal courts. The ulufey considered Caer Neiada to be their birthright.

They hunted from the land, but always buried their bones and offal to nurture the soil. They gathered more often than farmed, always spreading the seeds to grow in new places. They felled trees whilst planting others. They listened for the voices on the wind and in the streams, the Spirits of Nature, who tutored them in the primal magics of their ancestors.

The cultures of the ulufey stretch back into the mists of history before the Era of Expansion. Fragments of their great works still exist in ancient stonework, faded tapestries, and half-remembered songs that the elves still celebrate to this day.

But much of it was also lost. Humankind first appeared in Caer Neiada like the embers of a forthcoming wildfire, drifting in from beyond the Grove Maze. When they first encountered the ulufey, they adopted the word into the human tongue—”elf.”

### Arrival of the First Men

Passing through the Grove Maze was not a simple feat for the folk of central Etharis. Many tried and failed. For a time, humans believed the Grove Maze and Rock-Teeth Mountains denoted the southern edge of the world. Eventually explorers did push through, and after them migrants, shortly before armies.

Beholding the natural splendor of Caer Neiada, many folks believed this place was a haven from the mud and black forests of central Etharis, a gift given to them by the gods. The first tribes settled in the northeastern part of the region, where the great rivers facilitated agriculture. They hunted freely and fed the scraps to their animals. They cut trees to build their homes and feed their fires. They tilled the earth and planted foreign grains. Without heeding the Spirits of Nature, the settlers drank deeply of all the land had to offer.

Nearby elves were horrified witnessing the ravaging of their lands. Worse, they feared the wrath of the archfey. Conflict between cultures was inevitable, as the elves tried to expel these foreigners from their realm and the northerners clung to their new homes. Armies arose on both sides, culminating in generations of war.

### Pact of the Sacred Land

Many songs and chivalric tales illuminate the exploits and battles between the northern settlers of Caer Neiada and the elves who dwelled there since time began. Some of these accounts are recorded as history. Others are fables about heroes who may have lived but have had their tale retold so many times, the truth has become lost.

What nobody questions is the name of the first man who would be king of a realm called Charneault: Aymeric Noblecoeur. Once a knight in service to Castle Lamesdhonneur, Noblecoeur was said to have been knocked from his horse by an arrow and washed away in the river currents.

He was found deep in Caer Neiada by an elven sage named Thuridan Sarador, who bathed his feet in a stream. A leader among his own people, Sarador sat and spoke to Noblecoeur, explaining his vision for a united realm. Two cultures, one kingdom. The dream of Charneault.

Historians who don’t believe this fable write that Aymeric Noblecoeur sought to bring northern domination to the land under a banner of diplomacy. Lord Sarador, knowing that an endless cycle of war would end with the extinction of the elves, chose to offer peace. Both leaders paid a personal toll for the ensuing pact. For their sacrifices, the sun dawned on the Charneault Kingdom.

The Pact of the Sacred Land outlines the peace between the northern folk and the elves, and their respective responsibilities in sharing the land. The Bondmakers commune with the Spirits of Nature directly. The king and his noble court rules, but the Bondmakers are their counsel to the whims of the archfey. The chapter knights protect the kingdom against invasion and maintain order between its peoples. Wealth and produce is shared. The sacred sites of elves are never disturbed.

Of Thuridan Sarador, the elven sage crowned Aymeric Noblecoeur and formed the Circle of Bondmakers. Counselling his friend, Sarador revealed one final demand that was made by the Spirits of Nature. King Aymeric Noblecoeur, and each regent that would succeed him, must send their firstborn child to the Temple of Mist. There, they would be trained in ancient customs. The heir would then ascend to the Fey Courts, never to be seen again. All attempts to circumvent this tribute have brought disaster upon the people of Charneault, and so it is still upheld diligently to this day.

### The Circle of Bondmakers

There are two main faiths in the Charneault Kingdom. Belief in the Four Divines—Aurelia, Galt, Ulmyr, and Maligant—migrated south with the northern tribes. But the Gods' End did not impact Charneault as it did other realms. Many offer their prayers to the Arch Seraphs in the gods’ absence, yet the kingdom’s continued prosperity is said to be in no small part thanks to the Spirits of Nature.

The elves would not call their relationship with the fey a religion. They view the Spirits of Nature as their true ancestors and the Courts of the archfey as a ruling authority above their own. The fey are not distant gods who answer the prayers of the downtrodden through goodwill. Elven customs are far more transactional. The Spirits of Nature demand that the wilderness is maintained: for every animal hunted, fruit plucked, and tree felled, an offering of equal value is provided in gratitude for the paradise the Spirits of Nature have cultivated.

Offering prayers to the archfey is considered a faux pas. Though they are celebrated, it’s improper to make appeals for their aid without an appropriate gift given to earn their favor. In recompense for an entire realm, the regents of the Charneault Kingdom have paid a steep price.

The Circle of Bondmakers are a community of druids whose responsibility is maintaining the connection between the people of Charneault and the Spirits of Nature. They wander the Kingdom, strengthening or restoring the bonds with the Spirits, wherever the fey cause trouble.

The Bondmakers’ are a divisive group due to their secrecy and their connection to the archfey. Many suspect that their loyalty to Fey Courts outweighs their loyalty to the crown. They conceal their rituals and customs. Not even their own knightly escorts are permitted within the Temple of Mist, the druids’ sanctum in southern Charneault.

Folk who’ve witnessed the cruelty of the fey distrust the Bondmakers innately. While fey can certainly be malicious, the druids contend that their wickedness is often the result of an insult inflicted by a commoner or community. In regions where the land is held sacred, the Bondmakers act as counselors, spiritualists, and exorcists. In places where chapels to the Arch Seraphs are built, the druids are thought of as pact makers and heretics.

Nevertheless, the two faiths coexist in Charneault, often within the same settlement. For centuries they’ve influenced each other. In modern times, northerners have created their own way of connecting with the Spirits of Nature through knighthood and quests. Elven ideals of romance and beauty have suffused the warrior cultures of the north to give rise to the Chapter Knights.

### Knighthood

Olha Nykytiuk Chapter Knight Knighthood is the greatest aspiration of any child who comes of age in the Charneault Kingdom. The traditions of the Chapter Knights date back to before the founding of the realm and have their roots in both northern and elven cultures. A knight is expected to exemplify many virtues: courage, strength, and honor as required by any warrior. Justice, temperance, and mercy. A knight may be needed to act as a sheriff or judge in defense of the realm’s peace. Faith, generosity, and hope. The knights are the light of Charneault, and their ability to spread beauty and mirth is as important as their skill in swordsmanship. The Chapter Knights are highly codified institutions that prioritize differing virtues. All serve the Charneault Kingdom. Yet their specific purposes and ideals differ. Becoming a Chapter Master, the leader of each knightly order, involves years of dedication and service to a chapter’s cause and virtues.

To be king or queen of Charneault, one must be regarded as a knight of the highest caliber. When a new ruler is to be coronated, the Chapter Masters convene and decide who among them shall be the next regent. Often this is merely a formality. The heir apparent is customarily a child or relative of the previous ruler, prepared from a young age to become a Chapter Master and eventually ascend the throne. But intrigues do happen.

There are five major knight chapters recognized as the most renowned in Charneault. Other, lesser chapters exist, though they attract fewer squires and tend to be specific in their cause. Knights-errant also wander the land looking for deeds to enact, though they bring no honor upon themselves. A knight without a chapter has no authority recognized by the crown.

#### Vermeil Guard

The Vermeil Guard acts as a personal guard of the Bondmakers, traversing Charneault with them wherever the druids may need. However, the Vermeil Guard are not allowed to witness any of the Bondmakers internal ceremonies or customs. They are responsible for escorting the firstborn of a new regent to the Temple of Mist as well.

The shield of the Vermeil Guard is green and gold, featuring crowned antlers and a sprig of holly. Their loyalty is to the throne and the Spirits of Nature in equal measure.

#### Iron Sparrow

The Iron Sparrow oversee the general defense of Charneault, with subchapters stationed outside the Grove Maze, along the Rock-Teeth Mountains, and on the coastline. Anyone entering Charneault from foreign lands likely comes across an Iron Sparrow patrol.

These knights carry a blue and silver shield, which displays a fortress that defends Charneault, and the song sparrow of their namesake.

#### Fairweathers

These knights patrol the mainland of Charneault, keeping the peace and rebuilding settlements after a conflict or the mist passing through. They’re also in command of the justice system, though with matters that occur close to Tol Leyemil, they defer to the Acen Elavain.

The Fairweathers ride with a yellow shield adorned with an ivory mounted knight carrying a sword and the scales of justice.

#### Dawn Dreamers

The Dawn Dreamers protect Tol Leyemil, though they live on the outskirts of the great city. Their members have elven heritage. Though their knights rarely range far from the elven stronghold, some can be found throughout Charneault investigating threats to their home.

The Dawn Dreamers shield is the color of autumn. A bare tree spreads its branches above symbols of elven culture, a lyre and a bow: both stringed instruments of beauty and destruction.

#### Severe Templars

The most contentious of the knight chapters is the Severe Templars, whose purpose is to map the land of Charneault and study its environments. Many elves aren’t comfortable with these Chapter Knights’ research, which they view as intrusive to the Spirits of Nature. Nevertheless, the Severe Templars’ explorations could uncover truths about the realm that have laid hidden for centuries.

The Severe Templars view their work as bringing to light the true extent of archfey power in Etharis, which they believe is an insidious force. Their loyalty is to the crown and no other. Displayed on their black shields is a coroneted knight alongside a flaming torch, which banishes the darkness of the wilderness and burns the Severe Templars’ foes.

### The Quest

Any citizen of the Charneault Kingdom can rise to knighthood. Most who do are inducted into a chapter at a young age, educated collectively before being chosen as a knight’s squire.

Squires are expected to maintain their mentor’s weapons, clothing, and equipment. They’re responsible for the good health of their and their master’s horses. Each squire must memorize and embody the ideals of their chapter, and through their duties learn the skills and virtues necessary to be worthy of knighthood. When at last they’re deemed ready, each squire must embark on a quest.

Lower-born commoners not recruited by a chapter must instead arm and educate themselves. Their road is far more difficult. These squires leave home to embark on their quest without any of the preparation or resources offered by a chapter.

All squires carry a blank shield. During their quest they must accomplish a worthy deed of great courage. Once achieved, the squire can assert the right to bear a coat of arms on their shield. Most are knighted by the chapter who trained them. Solitary squires must find a chapter willing to induct them, or else wander the land as a knight-errant.

Sometimes during their quest, a knight or squire might meet a Spirit of Nature and gain their trust through noble acts or behavior. Many renowned knights are those who have managed to enter the service of such a Spirit by taking an oath to protect and serve them, becoming guardians of the wilderness itself. In return, the knight is blessed with primal magics that vary depending on the nature of the Spirit to which the oath was taken.

Other squires might swear vows to an Arch Seraph and become anointed. It’s not unheard of for divine power to manifest among the ranks of the Chapter Knights, and those who accomplish it are revered as paladins of the highest virtues.

### The Dark Mist

After generations of relative peace between the cultures of the Charneault Kingdom, relations took a grim turn when King Thibault de Sauveterre ascended the throne.

The King’s firstborn daughter, Tiphaine de Sauveterre, knew it was her destiny to walk to the Temple of Mist and disappear forever: a sacrifice she made happily for the kingdom she loved. But the Princess’ heart was also given to another.

Sir Guilhem le Preux was a famed knight of the Vermeil Guard. He’d sworn oaths to the Spirits of Nature. He’d slain werewolves and toppled giants. And, despite the Pact of the Sacred Land, he also married Princess Tiphaine in secret.

When Thibault de Sauveterre was crowned, Sir Guilhem wept at his king’s feet. The knight begged that Tiphaine be spared. It was the Princess herself who took her husband’s hands and instructed him to be merry, for the sacrifice of their great love would forge the mightiest Kingdom.

But it was not to be.

Sir Guilhem broke his oath to the Vermeil Guard and forsook his duties, unable to bear walking beside his wife to the Temple of Mist. He swore instead to never love another and to live dishonorably as a knight-errant. But swiftly the Spirits of Nature came to him. It’s said a song sparrow perched upon his shield and chirped in his ear, warning Sir Guilhem that the Princess was in grave danger.

The knight stirred his horse to follow Tiphaine’s escort, his own Chapter brethren, but Sir Guilhem arrived too late. The Vermeil Guard were defeated. The Bondmakers were slaughtered. All was in ruin, and Princess Tiphaine was missing. Yet the hoof tracks of her captors led onwards to the Temple of Mist. Her husband pursued.

What happened next is the subject of mythmaking. Nobody is certain who Princess Tiphaine’s assailants were, and her fate, along with Sir Guilhem’s, remains a mystery.

Stories whispered in taverns say the Severe Templars did the killing, intentionally breaking the Pact of the Sacred Land in malice, and the dark mist is the archfeys’ retribution. More elaborate conspiracies claim Diyanthar, a sorcerous elf of grand ambition and no honor, abducted Princess Tiphaine to enact his own fell ritual in the Temple of Mist. Perhaps Sir Guilhem interrupted that ritual too late for its effects to be completely prevented, and from that day forth the mist was tainted—sometimes as silvery as ever, other days dark and filled with terrors.

A romantic tale imagines Guilhem and Tiphaine reunited and fled far from Charneault to be happy in a distant land. But such fairy tales are rarely true, and both have been seen as apparitions in the dark mist, calling for help to lift their curse.

## Provinces

Lucas Torquato

Charneault is comprised of seven provinces, each with its own expressions of elven and northern culture. Gran Croix is the realm of the Grove Maze. The western provinces of Beaumont, De la Fontaine, and Faucher have higher elven populations and greater reverence for the Spirits of Nature. Gagnon, Lamar, and Chaplin are home to more urbanized settlements and tend toward worship of the Arch Seraphs. Dividing the provinces in this way doesn’t paint the nuances of Charnelaise society, and influences from the Realm of Faerie are found everywhere.

Before the Pact of the Sacred Land united them, each province was its own territory or kingdom. Some were ruled by a noble house whose descendants still govern to this day, now bearing the titles of duke or duchess. It’s common for these noble families to include Chapter Knights among their lineages.

Honor and reputation are influential in Charneault, even if the heads of house are not a knight themselves. Marriage and trade are other levers of power. As ambition circles the kingdom’s throne, those who cannot wield a sword to wear the crown instead offer their children and riches to remain within the king or queen’s graces.

### Chaplin

Bordering most other provinces, Chaplin is the crown jewel of the Charneault Kingdom and the birthplace of knightly virtue. It’s the seat of the Crown’s authority. It’s also the wealthiest of any region, though its riches are not based on resources or even crown taxes. Folks pour into Chaplin from across Charneault in the hopes of witnessing greatness.

The annual Games of the White Dove take place each summer at Castle Lamesdhonneur, where knights and squires from every chapter compete for personal glory. Likewise, the Tournament of the Pink Rose is held at Houiville. This springtime festival emphasizes contests of poetry, song, and painting over that of martial prowess. Smaller competitions are held in every village throughout Chaplin year-round, and squires who survive the mud, blood, and pressure of local contests aspire to be remembered as champions of the Victor’s Circle at the capital.

Chaplin immortalizes its heroes. The shields of knights long passed hang upon the outer walls of Castle Lamesdhonneur, reminders of their now-distant courageous deeds. The halls of Houiville tell these stories in greater detail. Woven tapestries depict knights blessed by Arch Seraphs, druids speaking with woodland creatures, and villagers banding together to defeat a ravening werewolf.

While Chaplin worships acts of glory and heroism, such stories of the Charneault Kingdom are often a lie. Within Castle Lamesdhonneur, endless conspiracies unfold. While the highest power rests with the crown, any regent is beholden to competing interests. The Royal Council are the crown’s closest advisors, comprised of dignitaries who represent the interests of the nobility. While the ducal houses are meant to speak on behalf of their people, many use their influence in Castle Lamesdhonneur to further their personal desires and interests.

The group known as Acen Elavain are the voice of Tol Leyemil. Loyalty from half the realm relies on the elves feeling (if not always being) heard, and their wishes granted whenever the crown is able. But only the Chapter Masters can make a king. This gives the Knight Chapters inordinate power in deciding the future of the Charneault Kingdom, and what a previous regent’s legacy will be.

Largely, the decrees of the king are obeyed. Peace would not reign otherwise. Even so, this doesn’t stop plots and bargains from being arranged behind closed doors and in whispered hallways. The Charneault Kingdom, and Chaplin particularly, is a realm where courage, faith, and generosity are upheld as the ideal virtues of leadership. But secrecy, ambition, and cruelty are the true tools of power.

Tensions in Chaplin have only risen as the dark mist spreads. In the face of mounting calamity, Castle Lamesdhonneur has been transformed into a refuge for many Charnelaise seeking shelter against the horror outside. The once-vibrant city is rife with nervous fear. In a time when he is most needed to bring hope, the noble King de Sauveterre has retreated from public life and left the Severe Templars to maintain order within Chaplin.

#### Notable Individuals

King Thibault de Sauveterre. Former Chapter Master of the Iron Sparrow, King de Sauveterre struggled to claim the throne in the face of dire competition from Hermand le Sévère. Thibault prevailed, though narrowly. Kingship was never something he aspired to, especially knowing the sacrifices he would need to make. It was his deeply held belief in the knightly virtues that made him an inspiring choice from among his peers.

Thibault’s supporters thought he would be a lion among kings once he ascended the throne. Instead, he became a broken man almost instantly after the tragedy that befell his daughter, Princess Tiphaine, and the subsequent spreading of the dark mist.

Frightened, indecisive, and disaffected are the words whispered behind the King’s back. Thibault’s reputation as a knight have sustained the love of his people, but without the leadership of the crown, his court has descended into violent suppression and grabs for power. Without the combined loyalty of his subjects, the King is powerless to fight the dark mist.

Though most doubt he could find the strength for it, bringing all people together beneath his bloody sword may be Thibault’s only option left to unite his realm.

Hermand le Sévère. The man who would be king. Chapter Master of the Severe Templars, Hermand de Sévère, has an indomitable reputation as a knight who rarely fails at his goals. While he’s celebrated for his swordsmanship and fearlessness, many a knight and squire have lain dead at his feet having faced de Sévère in combat during a tourney.

Losing the crown to Thibault de Sauveterre is considered Hermand’s only failure in life. Yet in defeat he appears to have found success. His voice holds great influence in Castle Lamesdhonneur, and he attends more councils than the King. Though he has no official political power aside from his role as Chapter Master, whenever de Sévère is present in a room, decisions tend to be made toward his preferences.

Hermand joined the Severe Templars as a child. He dedicated his entire life to serving his chapter, and under his command the Severe Templars have become one of the most feared machines of warfare the Charneault Kingdom has seen.

The Templars’ contempt for the Spirits of Nature is no secret. The dark mist only affirms their crusade to burn the archfey out of civilized lands wherever their influence is felt. Strong leadership from the crown has prevented open hostility between the elves and Templars in years past, but King de Sauveterre’s retreat from public life has emboldened the Severe Templars to extend their authority beyond their usual bounds. This hasn’t helped rumors that Hermand de Sévère ordered the killing of Princess Tiphaine.

### Gagnon

Gagnon is the easternmost duchy of Charneault and borders the Castinellan Provinces. Forts are built along the rivers that divide the two nations. Open hostility is rare, but Charneault is alert to the intolerance Castinella expresses towards druidic magic and the Spirits of Nature.

Gagnon is the home to the Iron Sparrow, whose knightly tradition is entwined with an appreciation of the inherent beauty in the natural world. Music is common in the streets of Camont. Sometimes it’s a knight singing ballads by a fountain, reciting the Iron Sparrow's deeds that keep the realm safe.

Neighborhoods in Camont are lush with verdant courtyards, potted flowerbeds, and overhanging trees. The extent to which the architecture reflects elven influences can be striking for a city this far east—a result of Camont’s close proximity to the Grove Maze. Nevertheless, worship of the Arch Seraphs is common in Gagnon, especially given their eastern neighbors.

#### Notable Individuals

Duchess Sylvana Briar Lorraine. The young duchess of Camont is no more than five years old. Made an orphan by the recent death of her father, Duke Lothair Lorraine, intrigues loom around Sylvana, as her father’s nobles’ scheme to make her ducal throne their own.

Complicating matters, the Duchess appears to be cursed. When she’s bored, which is often, pixies flit from thin air to torment her servants. When she’s angered, nearby objects burst into flames. When Sylvana is amused, bubbles escape from her ears and mouth, which has earned her the moniker the Bubbling Duchess.

The nature of Sylvana’s curse is unknown, though she seems quite incapable of controlling it. It remains to be seen whether she needs protection from her court, or whether the court needs protection from her.

Steward Edwardine Evrétte. The elven steward to Duke Lothair Lorraine, governance of Gagnon has fallen to Edwardine until Duchess Sylvana comes of age. The Steward actively searches for a solution to Sylvana’s curse. Meanwhile, others in the court believe that Edwardine holds a secret about Sylvana’s abilities that threatens civil unrest if revealed.

### Lamar

Lamar is a bridge between east and west Charneault. Its midlands are mostly farms and roads, where commoners make humble livings whilst dreaming of knightly adventures and mystical Fey Courts.

Though often unseen, the Spirits of Nature are never far. The many bridges that cross the countless rivers, and guide stones marking directions beside the roads, are frequently destroyed—presumably by mischievous faeries. Superstitious folk who fear fey retribution tear down structures built too close to sacred woods or faerie mounds.

Mont de Lancey is the ducal house, and a coastal fortress built upon a rugged bluff brimming with rare minerals and stones. The town that circles the mountain has mined the rock for generations, earning considerable wealth for the ruling house. Their familial signet rings are inset with gemstones.

Alongside the mountain another castle is built, this one the stronghold of the Fairweathers—Castle de Esquire. The aspirants trained within are renowned for becoming exceptional squires, coveted by other chapters. Though a squire is always permitted to join any chapter that welcomes them, Castle de Esquire steadily swells the ranks of the Fairweathers for the protection of the realm.

The spread of the dark mist has brought a massive influx of students to the school, and Mont De Lancey acts as a symbol of hope and persistence against the mist.

#### Notable Individuals

Vivian Descoteaux. Chapter Master of the Fairweathers, and mother to the current Duchess of Lamar, Vivian Descoteaux is an acclaimed knight. Formidable and determined, she is fixated on being the one and only savior of Charneault’s ideals and its citizens.

Vivian wasn’t pleased that Thibault de Sauveterre was given the kingship, but she herself was not vying for it. A king rots in their golden cage at Castle Lamesdhonneur. The real work of leading happens on horseback, charging a foe down with sword and lance. King de Sauveterre is too kind in her eyes. His reliance on diplomacy is a weakness. She is a woman of deeds and few words, believing that it’s in words where dishonesty hides.

Vivian is, underneath her steel facade, a deeply scarred individual with delusions of grandeur and a willingness to sacrifice anything or anyone while thinking she is saving them. She intentionally leaves villages unprotected to bait her foes. She’d sooner burn down grain stores and slaughter livestock than leave them for brigands or rebels. To Vivian Descoteaux, justice exists only at the end of a sword.

Emy Descoteaux. The third born child to Vivian Descoteaux, Duchess Emy Descoteaux is a disappointment to her mother. More comfortable with a book in her hand than a sword, Emy never showed much aptitude for knighthood nor much interest.

Duchess Descoteaux is a puzzle solver. She works to make the mines beneath Mont de Lancey more efficient and the exportation of Lamar’s gems and produce more lucrative.

When the dark mist rolls over Mont de Lancey, the Duchess instructs her people to flee into the city’s walls for better safety: another disheartening strategy to her mother. Vivian would rather have fresh corpses to study after the dark mist disperses.

> I watched Lady Vivian slay a whole troupe of twisted goblins once. They came out of the mist, shrieking and gibbering like they do. Those were the last sounds they ever made.
> —Fairweather Knight

### Grand Croix

The Grove Maze has its own provincial name: Grand Croix. Most settlements are along the great forest’s edge, though some communities choose to live within its fortress of trees. These villages are intimately shaped by elven culture. Homes are sometimes built aloft, among the branches of enormous trees, and connected by arching bridges and spiraling staircases. The Spirits of Nature may secretly reside in these hamlets, hiding themselves when they hear horses approach.

The Severe Templars are active in Grand Croix, charting the Grove Maze and maintaining the roads between villages both outside and among the trees. They burn back the encroach of nature. They build walls to bar the wilderness from reclaiming newly cleared lands. They slay malevolent spirits, when within the Templar’s authority to do so. But the Severe Templars are not always welcome when they travel in Grand Croix.

The capital of the province is Besion, an ancient fortress built by Raulish hands on what had once been a southern frontier. The city’s changed its name and sovereignty many times through its long history, between the Bürach Empire and Charneault. Yet its identity has become firmly Charnelaise.

Beneath the stones and soil of Besion are the prehistoric foundations of a gnomish settlement. Their people are common in Grand Croix, sharing a deep affinity with the Spirits of Nature, and this discovery has made it unclear if their ancestors migrated south from Rauland, or journeyed north from the Grove Maze.

### Beaumont

The land of the elves, Beaumont is the region that surrounds Tol Leyemil. The elves rarely delineated specific borders in the days of Caer Neiada, and therefore Beaumont is considered to be anywhere in northern Charneault from where Rock-Teeth Mountains are visible.

With rugged foothills, coastal access, and a rich forest, Beaumont has the greatest diversity of resources among the provinces. The fish from the rivers, fruits from the trees, and metals from the base of the mountains provide plenty to trade with other regions. Boats built in Beaumont are also famed for their sleek designs and speed on the water.

Founded long before the Bürach Empire, Tol Leyemil is the last stronghold of untouched elven culture: a time capsule of life in ancient Caer Neiada.

The city teems with murals of long-forgotten history and music unheard in other realms. Tol Leyemil is a mystical place, even to elves born outside its bounds, appearing to exist between the mortal world and the Realm of Faerie.

The Dawn Dreamers fervently protect Tol Leyemil. They guard the surrounding forest so that interlopers never glimpse the elven city before encountering one of their patrols. The knights are clad in cloaks more than mail. True threats to the city are often riddled with arrows before they even realize they’re being watched, unless confronted by an antlered knight riding atop a stag.

The Acen Elavain, or the “Assembly of Elves,” is the ruling council of Tol Leyemil. However, their political power stretches further than the elven city, advising the crown of the Charneault Kingdom and negotiating on their people’s behalf.

The assembly is far from united. Some members view Tol Leyemil as the twin capital of Charneault, a vital part of the Kingdom with a shared duty to the realm’s prosperity. Others would isolate their people within the elven city, believing Tol Leyemil to be the last true fragment of their dying culture. There is even distrust for the Bondmakers among the Acen Elavain, who view the druids as enabling northern cultures to dominate Caer Neiada.

The wider kingdom deems the Acen Elavain as either a pragmatic voice for the elves, without the Bondmakers’ spirituality, or bureaucrats who distract the king and look only to the interests of their own city.

#### Notable Individuals

Virion Venfir. While no individual member is intended to have more authority than the others on the Acen Elavain, Virion’s voice travels further and with more influence than the rest of the assembly. A musician of spectacular talent, Virion wields his popularity with the elves of Tol Leyemil to advance his own political and personal glory. Rivals view the bard as an irresponsible showman. While the dark mist slithers through Charneault, Virion has done nothing to help eradicate its scourge. This doubly frustrates his detractors. An orator of Virion’s undeniable talents might actually be able to shake King de Sauveterre from his grief-stricken torpor. What the Acen Elavain don’t realize is that they are merely an instrument for Virion Venfir to complete his own hidden aims. Let the dark mist consume the Charneault Kingdom, and let it be reborn as Caer Neiada anew. Gonzalo Vega Virion Venfir

### De la Fontaine

Olha Nykytiuk Elf Bladeguard Deep in the Charneault Kingdom are the mystic lands of De la Fontaine. Here the mist is ever present. The green hills and wide valleys always look as if painted in watercolors, studded with tiny villages of mostly elven heritage. There are few bridges. The many rivers are crossed at natural fords or on ferries made of grass. Agriculture is small and usually haphazard, including wild growing orchards, thickets of berry bushes, and haggard livestock roaming free in unfenced pastures. De la Fontaine seems an unwealthy province to its neighbors. But the small life suits its residents. They prefer hearing fairytales told over a warm fire, rather than participating in them.

Bonneseaux Village is the largest settlement of De la Fontaine, with a greater mix of heritages than the rest of the region. Nevertheless, it is small. Situated on a sloping hill on the south side of a lake, the village is almost entirely surrounded by water, and houses are built upon stilts to remain dry. The lake has risen in recent years. Lights are seen dancing across its surface, and folk are found drowned even within their own homes. The locals say the water spirits within are disturbed by the dark mist.

Scattered across De la Fontaine are the crumbling bones of elven statues and towers. The elves sing of this region like it were a wilted flower that once blossomed with beauty and culture. Now folks fear to roam near such ruins. Malevolent spirits are said to haunt them.

At the south of De la Fontaine lies the Temple of Mist itself, making the Bondmakers and their knightly escorts a common sight. A druid is far more common than a priest in most villages. They perform rites and offer guidance to ensure the Spirits of Nature are appeased, yet the Bondmakers have no answer to the dark mist. Whispers across the kingdom have begun to wonder if the current calamity is one of the Bondmakers’ own making.

#### Notable Individuals

Duke Hugo Plomierre. The halfling duke of De la Fontaine, Hugo Plomierre, spends much of his time on the Royal Council at Castle Lamesdhonneur, or as a guest at Camont or Mont de Lancey. His ducal estate at Bonneseaux Village is unimpressive, and the Duke much prefers the comfort of larger cities.

Though Hugo is a shrewd politician on behalf of his people, and of himself, he leaves most of the local troubles in De la Fontaine to be handled by his reeves.

Grand Shaman Aetherion. Aetherion is a powerful archdruid, named as leader of the Bondmakers in the aftermath of the slaughter that claimed Princess Tiphaine. Given the epithet “Fey Sighted,” it’s said the Spirits of Nature are willing to show this elf glimpses of the future. Whatever horrors he has witnessed, Aetherion remains silent as he works to undo them.

Despite this, Aetherion is a jovial and mischievous elf who enjoys spending time in others company. He was a companion to King de Sauveterre during Thibault’s questing days. Since the tragedy that unleashed the dark mist, a rift has opened between the two men. Aetherion did not go to the King in his grief, instead rushing to the Temple of Mist to discover what manner of catastrophe had occurred. If ever he learned the fate of Princess Tiphaine or Sir Guilhem le Preux, Aetherion has not disclosed it. This is a betrayal the King will not forgive.

Aetherion now rides across the Charneault Kingdom, recruiting knights and mercenaries to accomplish deeds that combat the dark mist. Yet his goals are not always clear in his requests. Only the Fey Sighted can see the tapestry of time, weaving together a future he desperately wishes to prevent.

### Faucher

In the furthest western reaches of Charneault, the land rots. The grass thins and turns grey. The trees distort and lose their leaves. The riches of the wilderness disappear, and Spirits of Nature fall silent near the boughs of the Dark Oak Grove.

Long before the Pact of the Sacred Lands ended generations of war, the region called Faucher was a true garden paradise. It teemed with the Spirits of Nature and was said to grow in abundance every plant found elsewhere in Caer Neiada. The capital, Tol Kerdywel, was a city of white towers as beautiful as Tol Leyemil remains to this day.

The ruler of this land was an elven sorceress named Thaelkinei, today remembered as the Lich Princess. Only the oldest elven texts contain any mention of her. She believed she could control the Spirits of Nature through arcane magic, intending to use this power to expel the northern tribes from Caer Neiada. It’s told she wept above Tol Kerdywel when she heard of the bloodshed inflicted upon her people.

But Thaelkinei’s magic turned vile. She drained the primal world of its vitality to suffuse herself with power. She used binding rituals to usurp the will of the fey, and whomever she could not command, she reaped their souls instead. Had Thaelkinei’s aspirations succeeded, she would not have saved Caer Neiada but transformed it into a realm of living corpses and called that peace.

In the end, the Lich Princess was her own undoing. Her body rotting, her magic spent, she was defeated by the first coalition of elves and northern folk. Undying but trapped in perpetual decay, Thaelkinei rests in the city of Tol Kerdywel, surrounded by the land she once adored. It now lies in ruin.

Faucher is a province of few inhabitants. The Dark Oak Grove is a place of death and decay. Its once magnificent trees rot from the inside out, undead wolfen husks stalk the underbrush, and stagnant mires bubble with acid.

Tol Kerdywel is accursed to the elves. Those who venture into it are marked by the Lich Princess’ malice, their flesh withering with decay. But rumors of unimaginable treasures and eldritch secrets lure explorers into the old city nonetheless.

#### Notable Individuals

Diyanthar, the Dark Elf. Once a member of the Bondmakers, Diyanthar made a name for himself as a magical prodigy. Knowledge is currency to the Dark Elf, and for this reason he yearns to learn the secrets of the archfey. But desires such as his are not why the Circle of the Bondmakers was created.

Diyanthar believes that elven magic has weakened due to the presence of northern folk in Caer Neaida. He works tirelessly to gain back the powers he views as lost to history, which led him to the Rotting City of Tol Kerdywel. What he learned in that fell place he has taught to others, forming his own circle of mages collectively called the Dark Elves.

Diyanthar has not been seen since Princess Tiphaine vanished, leading to the suspicion of his involvement in the dark mist curse.

Thaelkinei, the Lich Princess. Sealed away in the Rotting City, Thaelkinei was regarded as a sorceress with no equal in the times of Caer Neiada. Though she has been silent now for many centuries, the endless decay of the Dark Oak Grove is a reminder that she was never destroyed.

Now, the realm fears Thaelkinei has found a new disciple in Diyanthar, the Dark Elf: a surrogate who could resurrect the Lich Princess or complete her blasphemous vision for Charneault. Whether she’s involved in the dark mist curse or not requires brave souls to journey into Tol Kerdywel and discover the truth for themselves.

### Other Locations of Interest

The following are other locations of interest in Charneault.

#### The Shaded Haven

The Shaded Haven is an isolated coastal village that serves as a sanctuary to many lycanthropes who seek respite from their curse. With a thriving fishery, large stretches of farmland, and a grove-like herb garden, the community is entirely self-sufficient. Yet few visitors come to trade, superstitious of what might happen close to the full moon.

Despite such fears, the Shaded Haven is a peaceful place. The locals live ascetic existences, tending to their chores and listening to the Spirits of Nature, with whom they feel a primal kinship. They assuage their predatory instincts through focused martial practice. The wulven heirs of the Shaded Haven must decide whether to adopt their parents’ quiet lives or venture out into a world that may never accept them.

However, where there is lycanthropy, there is the always the potential for disaster. The Shaded Haven fears visitation from the dark mist, dreading how it may distort their primal nature or force them to defend their homes with tooth and claw. The only question is how extensive the carnage will be.

#### The Sailgraves

Off the southern coast of Lamar lies the Sailgraves, a chain of islands and swampland that remain mysterious to the residents of Charneault. The mist never seems to leave the islands. Some believe the Sailgraves are a place where the veil between the mortal world and the Feywild wears thinnest. The temperate waters are home to merfolk who croon in the deep mist. They sit upon crumbling sea vessels and watch over the countless bones of would-be explorers. Anna Verhoog

Few who venture to the Sailgraves return. Reports suggest there had once been settlement upon the islands, but the Sailgraves are now deemed sacred to Charneault and to be left undisturbed. This doesn’t stop would-be treasure hunters from visiting. Stories say faerie gold, or treasures from a lost elven kingdom, are buried on the islands waiting to be claimed.

Many of these rumors come from Menchâtel, a tiny village on the Charnelaise coast closest to the Sailgraves. The residents in Menchâtel are used to folk disappearing to the islands, and don’t dissuade those who attempt the voyage. Instead, they make a good living providing boats and supplies for adventurers, benefiting from the regional coin that’s made their township wealthy. Local superstitions believe the folk of Menchâtel have a pact with a sea hag or some other Spirit of Nature to lure explorers to their doom upon the Sailgraves.

> They can say that the mists are natural or supernatural, daemonic or faerie made. But I know the truth. It's the breath of the Lich Princess, and it's coming for us all.
> —Drunk Merchant

## Quest Hooks

The following quest hooks can be used to launch adventures within the lands of Charneault.

### Princess of the Grove

The princess of Charneault, Tiphaine, is missing. It’s been years since she and her husband, Sir Guilhem le Preux, disappeared on the road to the Temple of Mist. Commoners claim to hear their voices and witness their apparitions within the dark mist, calling for help and each other’s names as if they are lost.

Every knight and adventurer available to the King’s command is tasked with keeping an eye open and an ear to the ground for information that could reveal Princess Tiphaine’s fate. Many false leads and superstitious tales spring from this long but worthy quest. In helping others throughout the kingdom, clues can be gathered that ultimately point toward the Dark Oak Grove.

The most common tales of Tiphaine and Sir Guilhem point to the dark mist. Perhaps their spirits reside within the putrid forest of the Dark Oak? Or maybe they’re prisoners in the dreaded Rotting City of Tol Kerdywel itself? Another possibility is that all of this is a ruse by the Dark Elf, Diyanthar, luring souls toward his stronghold in the fell woods. Questing knights have already vanished within the Dark Oak Grove. Their valiant souls could be used to empower Diyanthar’s magic, or possibly revive Thaelkinei.

Whichever the case, only formidable warriors and powerful mages should dare step foot within the Dark Oak Grove. Discovering the truth of Tiphaine’s fate could be as perilous as facing the Lich Princess herself.

### Water’s Edge

Menchâtel is teeming with accounts of the Sailgrave Islands. Stories of isolated elven enclaves, seabound monsters, mystical plants, and of course long-lost treasures attract all manner of explorers, researchers, and pitiless fools, all hoping to strike it rich.

Franklin D’jéun is one such fool. The lone squire wishes to earn the honor of painting his shield if he can return from the Sailgraves having accomplished a personal quest. Franklin doesn’t desire faerie gold or elven artifacts. He simply wishes to recover the signet ring of his uncle, who was lost to the Sailgraves a decade earlier.

Franklin has searched for brave warriors and explorers to accompany him on his quest. Spending any time in Menchâtel, the locals seem almost too eager to offer support and advice to see adventurers on their way. Investigating the village closer could yield some clues as to what lies on the islands. Are the villagers somehow involved in the fate of Franklin’s uncle?

The turbulent waters surrounding the islands hide enormous sea beasts lurking under the surface. Sailing is difficult, and the sea is crowded with wrecked and capsized vessels. These ships could be boarded and looted though at the risk of hearing the sirens’ song.

It’s impossible to imagine what horrors and perils await on the Sailgraves. Will the islands be green and lush, untouched by centuries of strife upon the mainland? Or are the Sailgraves as foreboding as their reputation suggests? A doorway to the Realm of Faerie from where none can escape, like Franklin’s uncle, and now the young squire himself?

### Wood Song

The commonfolk of Hildebury are disappearing. Once a vibrant village nestled next to a wood in the heart of Chaplin, its smithy has done dark, its mill is abandoned, and the stables are filled with unclaimed steeds. The noticeboard at the center of town is plastered with notices of missing people. A dwarven woman stands nearby, clutching one such notice in her hands.

Torgga Lerryn is Hildebury’s baker, and she just used the last of the town’s flour. Worse, Torgga sent her daughter, Amber, to take the final baked loafs around to the surrounding farms to make any coin she could. A day later, Amber hasn’t returned. Torgga cares less about the bread or money; she just wants her daughter returned.

Anyone who asks around the local farms discovers that half are as derelict as the village. Some of the others remember seeing Amber. She would have needed to use old forest roads leading between the farms. Further investigation may discover Amber’s tracks, the abandoned breadbasket, and a clearing filled with countless footprints that lead nowhere.

As dusk begins, the clearing comes alive with the sound of music and dancing. All the missing villagers crawl from the brush. They thrash and skip and prance around to jaunty music, whilst laughing and shrieking as the moon rises. At the center of the glade is an old horned satyr, playing his music on a merry set of pipes.

The Hildebury folk seem unharmed, aside from their mania. The satyr’s song may even enchant others to join. But if the music is interrupted, the revelers may turn savage, and the satyr is unwilling to release his captive audience.

### The Wolf & Loon

Wood Song

The Wolf & Loon is a wildly popular traveling theater troupe, headed by a wulven acrobat named Mattéo and a fortune teller named Ariel. They can be found putting on productions and participating in festivals in towns and cities across Charneault. Whilst their reviews are raving, they’re prone to quickly wearing out their welcome. People tend to go missing when the Wolf & Loon are in town.

If their caravan rests too long on a settlement’s edge, a knight may be fetched to ask them to leave. Failing that, paid mercenaries are just as good. The pair rarely seem eager to fight, despite Mattéo’s reputation as an acrobat and swordsman. Part of his act is rending steel with his claws.

Speaking to the pair reveals that they’re eventually driven out of most towns. Their troupe consists of many spurned by superstitions—a wechselkind duo who perform pantomimes, a dhampir alchemist who brews tinctures, and a disembodied scholar who tells histories of distant lands. Many such folk of different talents and backgrounds have joined and left the Wolf & Loon troop. Ariel herself is the most mysterious of them all.

Always hooded and behind an elven mask, the robed fortune teller speaks softly and ever in riddles. Some think she was a Bondmaker with the fey sight, whilst others believe she is a Spirit of Nature herself. Her eyes are too pale for a humanoid’s. The silvery feathers that decorate her cloak might actually protrude from beneath.

Whatever Ariel is, she always welcomes strange or unwanted people. The true purpose of the Wolf & Loon is helping the disenfranchised seek closure, or retribution for the injustices of their past: the wechselkind whose mortal parents disowned them, the dhampir whose lover tried to stake them, or the wulven whose lycanthropic father still hunts across Charneault.

Whether the Wolf & Loon are a harmless troupe righting wrongs in the world, or a vengeful scourge who need to be stopped, is a question that needs an answer if they’re to continue travelling the Charneault Kingdom.

## Castle Lamesdhonneur

Bram Willemot

The capital city of the Charneault Kingdom, Castle Lamesdhonneur, once stood as the beacon of humanity’s glory and the cradle of all knighthood. From within its fortified walls, the king and the Royal Council guided the kingdom’s future with valour, the Knight Chapters gathered to train and carry out their noble duties, and the common folk labored tirelessly to feed and arm the guardians of the land. For generations, people from across Charneault looked to Castle Lamesdhonneur as the pinnacle of what humans can accomplish when guided by the virtues of honor and justice.

However, beneath the polished steel of sword and shield lay a pervasive tarnish that crept into the hearts of knights and common folk alike. Fear of the Dark Mist has warped honor and justice into a violent and wicked force that fuels the flames of vengeance and prejudice. The flames are further stoked by the Knights of the Severe Templar, who seized the opportunity to grow their influence. The once lively city is now a battleground where dogmatic ideologies clash in the streets, and blades are pointed not just at monsters but also at friends and neighbors.

### Geography

Castle Lamesdhonneur is a fortified city built at the heart of the Charneault Kingdom. The eponymous castle sits at the very top of a hill, supported by a stone foundation that juts out over the rest of the city. Densely packed homes, shops, and workshops radiate from the foundation along the slope. Vast agricultural fields stretch along the base of the hill, enclosed by a colossal wall that defines Castle Lamesdhonneur’s border.

This wall is nicknamed the Shield, as it’s the city’s first line of defense against intruding danger. Lance-like towers jut forth from the ramparts and pierce the sky. When a knight falls in battle, their personal shield is hung on the wall, protecting the king and his citizens even after death. These shields gently rattle in the wind, echoing like the sound of an army marching in unison.

#### Terrain

Castle Lamesdhonneur took advantage of the natural landscape to secure its place as a seat of power. Cascading waterfalls and steep slopes make it difficult for intruders to reach the city walls, and large rivers form a natural moat behind the main keep. Agricultural floodplains yield bountiful harvests to feed the booming population, while thick forests provide both natural cover and hearty wood. Brooks flow around the city, providing plentiful fish and fresh water.

Castle Lamesdhonneur has felt the passing of many seasons. The wind blowing across the rolling hills spreads spring pollen before summer's warmth nurtures new life. In autumn, the wind turns cold, painting the trees red and brown. The rains of winter freeze into falling snow before spring's arrival once again sends warm breezes dancing over the hills.

However, the influence of the Dark Mist makes the seasons less predictable. Spring and autumn rain brings extreme flooding. Summer scorches the land to droughts, withering away crucial farmland, and winters now include long bouts of freezing rain, regularly breaking trees beneath beautiful but destructive layers of ice.

#### Unyielding Architecture

The fortified city is primarily built of granite, as the gray stone can withstand centuries of erosion. In the evenings, flecks of minerals catch the light of the setting sun and bathe the city in a subtle red glimmer. At daybreak, mineral veins reflect the rising sun and illuminate the city in gold.

Within the city itself, crowded buildings form labyrinthian streets. The architectural style of the city contains a blend of trends from across the ages, as more modern wooden homes and shops sit alongside centuries-old stone brick establishments. Colorful tiles and roofs have turned drab gray with time and neglect, especially as the city’s resources are redirected toward the upkeep of the knights.

### History

The original Castle Lamesdhonneur was built as a human bastion south of the Grove Maze. Bathed in sun atop its hill, the castle was a beacon of strength and safety against the wilderness of the Thousand Rivers. Tales of many famous knights begin in Castle Lamesdhonneur. Its legend is therefore written as the home of virtue and heroism.

Shortly after the Sacred Pact was signed, King Aymeric Noblecoeur chose Castle Lamesdhonneur as his capital. It soon became the centre of political and military power in Charneault, establishing the Royal Council and supported by the Chapter Knights. Barracks, training grounds, chapels, and colleges were built to train and educate a new generation of knights. Serving as a knight was the highest aspiration and honor in Charneault, drawing hundreds to Castle Lamesdhonneur. The city quickly expanded outside the original castle walls when the number of knights outpaced the initial castle size.

As the castle’s importance grew, so did its population. People flocked to the castle to be of service to the Chapter Knights, settling in the farmland outside the castle and offering their devotion and labor to the crown. More defensive walls were built to accommodate these new citizens, transforming the lone castle into a thriving walled city.

#### Devoted Populace

Through the centuries, as more heroes and tales originated from Castle Lamesdhonneur, the city’s legend grew more fanciful. Each person dedicated their hearts and lives to the service of the king, the knights, and the romantic grandeur of the kingdom’s declared ideals. Skilled artisans flocked from around the kingdom to hammer swords, forge armor, or stitch banners in the hope a worthy knight would one day bear them. Equestrian farmers raised horses with aspirations of being chosen for a noble steed. Lyricists competed to compose poetry of heroic deeds, that they might borrow the fame of a knightly patron.

This endless devotion placed the knights of the realm upon a pedestal. But they were, of course, not angels. The working class was often exploited by knights trading on their renown and courtly favors to avoid paying a fair wage. Other knights instead paid extra to secure the loyalty of skilled artisans, breeders, or bards, creating complicated networks of allegiances across the city.

#### Local Knight Chapters

A handful of Knight Chapters were created not to serve the realm but to protect Castle Lamesdhonneur itself. While not as famous or glamorous as the more notable Knight Chapters, these local orders perform crucial duties for the city’s wellbeing. These chapters include the Gravekeep Guardians and the Silent Order.

The Gravekeep Guardians. This enigmatic group is tasked with the grim duty of caring for the dead. They gather the fallen, perform burial rites, and guard the cemeteries. Though often viewed with morbid fascination, these knights show great compassion toward the living and dead.

The Silent Order. This chapter acts as the personal guard for the royalty and all nobility. As they are present among confidential conversations, all members of the Silent Cavaliers must swear an oath of secrecy. The more extreme members have their tongues removed so that they are never tempted to speak out of turn.

#### Present Turmoil

After the Dark Mist descended upon the Charneault Kingdom, King Thibault withdrew from city affairs to focus on saving his daughter, Princess Tiphaine, and breaking the Dark Elf curse. Sensing an opportunity to fill the power vacuum, many of the Knight Chapters jostled for stewardship of the city. Ultimately, it was the Knights of the Severe Templar that successfully staked their claim on city leadership as the arbiters of law and order.

In the name of protecting the city from the threat of the Dark Elves, the Severe Templars rule Castle Lamesdhonneur with a cold iron fist. Their fervent anti-magic and anti-elf agenda bleeds into the streets, sowing discord and distrust among family and friends. The other Knight Chapters attempt to either temper or embolden the Severe Templars, invoking rivalries and testing allegiances across the city.

#### Grim Utilitarianism

Upon taking power, the Knights of the Severe Templars blamed this crisis on a complacent and overly permissive culture within Castle Lamesdhonneur. They declared that the city has strayed from the true ideals of knighthood, its people distracted by romanticism and lavish ceremony.

To right this wayward course, the Severe Templars seized control of goods and services, reallocating them towards the chapter’s coffers so that they can best protect the city. Art and other forms of personal expression are starved for funding, considered a waste of money and resources. Poetry and song about the golden era of knighthood has been silenced, so that only words approved by the Severe Templars may spread.

#### Draconian Law

To keep the Dark Mist away from the city, the Knights of the Severe Templar have implemented strict laws and even harsher punishments. It is illegal to do many things without permission: sing a song of the fey, paint a mural of a forest, or grow a garden of wildflowers. Anyone caught breaking these ordinances are often fined, sometimes jailed, and even executed.

To facilitate these new capital punishments, scaffolds were built in Belforétte for public hangings and beheadings. These execution sites are now used almost daily, their victims ranging from the most noble knights to the lowliest peasant. The bodies are hung in the streets, their blood and offal providing a stark warning to any who’d cross the Severe Templars.

#### Uneven Justice

Elves and the practice of magic have not been outright banned within Castle Lamesdhonneur. However, elves and magic users are often falsely accused of crimes, giving the Severe Templars reason to torture and execute them in the name of order. Meanwhile, common folk who commit warped forms of vigilante justice against suspected sympathizers are lauded for their acts.

This has led to a plague of street violence, where innocent people are ripped from their homes and beaten by their neighbours until they falsely confess. They’re then marched to the gallows to be executed, while their attackers are rewarded with coin. The most desperate even turn on their friends and family, seeking just an extra bit of food and water for their betrayal.

As the number of elves and mages dwindle within the city, the flame of prejudice and misguided justice has turned to other acceptable targets. People suspected to have non-human ancestors, worshippers of the Spirits of Nature, and those who’ve sworn loyalties to Knight Chapters with elven bonds are all at the mercy of the citizens’ volatile whims. Even people who merely whisper or mutter their displeasure with the Severe Templars are framed for crimes and hauled off for execution.

#### Forbidden Nature

Suspicious of the Spirits of Nature inhabiting gardens, the Severe Templars have banned live plants from Coronette, Berthald, and Belforétte. Family gardens trampled under iron boots, generations-old trees were chopped into firewood, and stubborn weeds pulled from the cobblestone. Even the Sacrecoeur, the tree symbolizing the strength of the king, wasn’t spared this dreadful fate. Knights of the Severe Templar regularly spray the streets with an alchemical concoction that withers any last bit of greenery and sickens living creatures who breathe it in.

### Notable Locations

Castle Lamesdhonneur is roughly divided into four main districts. These districts are sectioned off both by defensive walls and elevation, and entry points are guarded around the clock.

#### Coronette

The original Castle Lamesdhonneur is home of the royal family and the Royal Council. It towers above the entire city upon its hilltop stone foundations. From afar, the palace looks modestly unremarkable, built for sturdy function rather than extravagant style. However, a keen eye is quick to notice that every inch of the walls is painstakingly carved with scenes of knights engaged in battle, courtly love, and great deeds of honor.

Within Coronette’s courtyard was the Sacrecoeur, a mighty maple tree planted and nourished at the founding of Castle Lamesdhonneur. Kings were traditionally coronated under its crimson boughs, and its leaves were considered tokens of good fortune. However, the Sacrecoeur began to rot after the appearance of the Dark Mist, and it was cut down altogether by the Knights of the Severe Templar as part of their strict new edicts.

#### Throne Room

At the centre of the main keep is the grand throne room, where meticulously designed stone arches lead the eyes toward the highbacked throne upon a crimson dais. Hundreds of people used to jostle for space here, seeking audience with the king while the Silent Order kept vigil along the walls. After the Dark Mist descended, public audiences were discontinued and the throne room all but abandoned. A thick layer of dust clings to the velvet curtains, and the crystal candelabras are barely lit.

Most of the actual governance work is done in the Council Chamber, a private room attached to the throne room where the Royal Council can convene and discuss matters in private. The silence of the throne room is only interrupted by the echoing shouts from the Council Chamber, as King Thibault and the Knight Chapters attempt to keep the kingdom in some form of order.

#### Belles-voux

Hidden within the maze of castle corridors and chambers is Castle Lamesdhonneur's greatest secret—not a treasury filled to the brim with priceless artifacts or a library of arcane tomes, but a lushly verdant enclosed garden once treasured by Princess Tiphaine. Delicate white roses blossom beneath swaying purple lilac bushes, imparting a distinct perfume to the air. Soft grass spotted with vibrantly red poppies and pale pink valerians gently cushion footfall to a hush. Few beside King Thibault knows of this garden’s existence, and he alone tends to it.

#### Berthald

Jutting from the stone foundation that the castle rests upon, Berthald is built upon a rugged ridge that descends from the mountain’s summit. It was once the vibrant home of the Knight Chapters and noble families. A parade of pennants and flags in the colors of all the Knight Chapters flew proudly above the parapets. The sound of clashing steel was followed by uproarious cheers as the knights sharpened their skills in the jousting arenas and training grounds.

Now Berthold is dull and grey, the ceremonial grandeur snuffed out in favor of a new grim reality. Knight Chapters with close ties to the elves have retreated from the city, their abandoned headquarters seized by the Knights of the Severe Templar. Roofs are cloaked with black as if in a state of perpetual mourning, and non-knight nobility sequester themselves in the safety of their homes.

#### The Black Cross

This is the main headquarters for the Knights of the Severe Templar within Castle Lamesdhonneur, and it acts as the central hub for all municipal governance. Like many of the historic Knight Chapters headquartered in this part of Berthold, the Black Cross has a dining hall, barracks, armoury, training grounds, and stable. Only the most elite of the Severe Templars live within the Black Cross’s walls, but many more attend every day to train and receive orders.

The Black Cross wasn’t always property of the Severe Templars. When the Dark Mist arrived and the Knights of the Severe Templar grew exponentially in size and influence, they successfully petitioned to take over the headquarters for the Vermeil Guard at the center of Berthold, arguing that the amenities were wasted on a chapter that spent most of its time outside the city. Upon taking over the headquarters, the Severe Templars swiftly destroyed all elven gifts displayed within the walls and gutted centuries-old architecture.

#### The Victor’s Circle

There was a time when the Victor’s Circle was filled with adoring crowds. Knights jousted on the pristine white sand for the glory of their chapter, as the epitome of chivalric might. While the Knights of the Severe Templar consider jousting for entertainment wasteful folly, they recognize that events of sanctioned competitive violence are useful for honing swordplay and redirecting frustration. Now, the Victor’s Circle plays host to a more deadly kind of competition, where knights face off against monsters.

The Games of the White Dove have been a long tradition, where knights and squires can capture the glory of accomplishment without leaving the city walls. Knights from any chapter are allowed to compete, and even squires are invited to step into the ring and earn their coat of arms. The grand prize is permission to wear the symbol of the White Dove upon a knight’s heraldry. It serves as a reminder that the purpose of knighthood is the quest for peace and beauty—combat is only the means.

Behind the scenes the Severe Templars pull strings to ensure their champions conquest in the games. In this way, they enforce the chapter’s image as an order of righteous strength.

#### Belforétte

The most populous part of the city, Belforétte is where the majority of Castle Lamesdhonneur’s citizens live and work. Everyone from the lowly apprentice to the master craftsman joined forces in symbiotic harmony to support the once-great institution of knighthood. Taverns and city squares once burst at the seams with friends and neighbours bonding over chivalric tales and cheerful singing.

Those light-hearted days are a distant memory. Jewellers, brewers, and other businesses declared as frivolous have been forcibly shuttered, their owners and families forced onto the street. Blacksmiths, armourers, farriers, leatherworkers, weavers, and healers toil at all hours of the day and night to satisfy the ever-growing demands of the Severe Templars. Blood runs through the gutters, fed by public executions and brutal street violence.

#### The Stone Anvil

The Stone Anvil, named after its granite sign carved in the shape of an anvil pierced by a sword, is the largest surviving pub in Castle Lamesdhonneur. Dark wooden tables crowd the stone brick interior, furs and monster hides hanging from the wall as grisly trophies of glorious quests in a far gone past. While it serves ale from across the kingdom, the Stone Anvil’s in-house brewing serves up beer thick enough to chew and strong enough to knock out a horse. Despite the barkeep’s boasting, the beer wasn’t the reason that the Stone Anvil continued operations after most other alehouses shut down.

When the Severe Templars declared that taverns were a wasteful distraction, the Stone Anvil’s owner Armand Basile discreetly made an offer to the chapter—the pub would be allowed to stay open in return for prioritizing service to the city’s knights. Knowing that a full prohibition on alcohol would only breed resentment in the streets, the Severe Templars agreed. While ordinary citizens now avoid the soldiers’ tavern, the occasional visiting noble is allowed in, if they don’t mind the volatile combination of alcohol, knightly ego, and readied blades.

#### Ferforger

With the sheer number of squires clamoring to become a Chapter Knight, armor and weaponry are in extremely short supply. The Ferforger was established to keep up with the demand, a smithy staffed by inexperienced but zealous citizens who devote their spare time to support the knightly orders. They hammer iron into crude breastplates and crooked swords over roaring furnaces, laboring until their hands can no longer grip their tools. Those who collapse are carried away to recuperate for the day, and another person is always at the ready to step in and continue the work with little disruption.

> Good beer, but don't look anyone in the eye.
> —Traveling Merchant at the Stone Anvil

#### Verdeaux

The largest district is Verdeaux. It includes horse paddocks and large burgage plots inside the city walls, whilst farmland and fisheries feed Castle Lamesdhonneur from the foothills further out. Rolling fields and blooming orchards once flourished year-round, and fish nearly leapt out of the rivers into awaiting nets. While life may seem small compared to the exploits and adventures of knights, the folk of Verdeaux are proud of what they contribute—for it is their work in the foothill that allows the city to stand strong.

Unfortunately, Verdeaux has suffered the most from the dangers of the Dark Mist. Crops on the outermost edges wither prematurely. Farmers and fishers are barely able to feed themselves as the fruit of their labours are taxed by the knighthood. All the meanwhile, monsters prowl the foothills, eager to feast upon the vulnerable.

#### Shrine of the Fields

This was once a humbly picturesque shrine attended by the farmers and fishers who gave thanks to the Spirits of Nature for the bounties of the land. Wooden walls were painted with locally sourced pigments that echoed nature’s vibrancy, the thatch roofing allowed streams of golden daylight to filter into the main chamber, and a single stained-glass window honored the serenity of the landscape. The shrine’s sole caretaker was a half-elven recluse named Faerynthia, who only appeared in the evenings to sweep the floors and light the incense. When the Knights of the Severe Templar took over Castle Lamesdhonneur, the Shrine of the Fields was condemned. Faerynthia was arrested on suspicion of conspiring with the Dark Elves, and she was swiftly beheaded at the threshold of the shrine. The handful of farmers who attempted to intervene were unceremoniously cut down. The bodies and the shrine were then set aflame, and nobody was allowed to approach even as the fire spread to the nearby fields. Daniel Alessi Hermand le Sévère

These days, very few dare to approach the burnt-out husk of the shrine. Black nettles grow in formerly fertile soil, and an unearthly fog clings to the walls. Those brave or foolish enough to enter the shrine’s doors speak of sweeping done by unseen hands, groans that echo like earth tremors, and a headless ghost lingering beneath the shattered stained-glass window.

#### The Burning Maw

Dug into the heart of Verdeaux is a great bonfire that burns every hour of the day, belching out plumes of black smoke and leaving behind a soot on the crops below. This fire pit is used by the Knights of the Severe Templars to dispose of refuse, profane magical or elven artifacts, and bodies of criminals who don’t deserve to be buried in the city cemeteries. The people who are given the bleak task of tending the fire wear masks to avoid breathing in the smoke and smell of charred flesh.

> I left Stehlenwald to escape the smoke. Now look at me.
> —Attendant to the Burning Maw

### Important Individuals

Notable people associated with Castle Lamesdhonneur include those described below.

#### King Thibault de Sauveterre

The current ruler of Charneault, King Thibault de Sauveterre is popular among the people but embattled by the Royal Council and Knight Chapters. Though he spent a significant amount of time as a young knight in Castle Lamesdhonneur, it was only after he was coronated that he called the city home. He barely had time to enjoy what the city had to offer before the Dark Mist crisis began, and he now sequesters himself within Coronette and rarely makes public appearances.

#### Hermand le Sévère

Hermand le Sévère is the Chapter Master for the Knights of the Severe Templar. His austere manner and flinty gaze unnerve many, but his decades of knightly accomplishments are renowned and respected, made even more impressive by his dedication to using human strength without the assistance of magic or Spirits of Nature. These days, most of Hermand’s time is spent in the Council Chamber, speaking few words but nevertheless swaying opinions toward an authoritarian rule that would snuff out the elves, magic, and the Dark Mist.

#### Josseline d’Eon

Second-in-command of the Knights of the Severe Templar, Josseline handles the Knight Chapter’s day-to-day operations within Castle Lamesdhonneur. Born to a renowned family of the Vermeil Guard, Josseline’s path to knighthood was redirected when she witnessed the chaos sowed by the Dark Mist during her squire quest. In her mind’s eye, she still sees monsters tearing through knights like wolves through lambs. She watched thieves burn and pillage villages. She witnessed elves hang and spear innocent forest travellers.

Determined to never let such chaos befall her beloved home city, she joined the Knights of the Severe Templar and quickly climbed the ranks with her sharp strategic wit and even sharper sword. Josseline is respected and feared even among her colleagues, known across the chapter for her unreadable composure and unbreakable devotion to Hermand le Sévère.

#### Lady Mireille de Bayeux

Lady Mireille was widowed and left to run the Bayeux estate after her wife, Ser Apolline de Bayeux, was executed by the Severe Templars on charges of treason against the crown. Firm in her belief that Apolline was framed for daring to speak up against Josseline d’Eon, Lady Mireille gracefully navigates the treacherous political landscape to build a network of other dissenting nobles in Berthold.

Her status as a widow to a traitor has severely damaged Lady Mireille’s reputation and access to noble privileges. That doesn’t stop her from opening her home to other dissenters or seeking audiences with powerful political figures. While she’s under constant surveillance by the Severe Templars, her gentle demeanour is just disarming enough to let her continue her work in secret.

#### Armand Basile

Armand was born to a farming family in Verdeaux, but he always had a better eye for business than agriculture. With a warm attitude and a charming smile, he was able to win the hearts and coin purses of customers, selling sub-par product for the price of premium. His business grew from his family’s crops to his homemade beers, and then to the establishment of the Stone Anvil within Belforétte.

Armand’s welcoming charisma is now turned toward the business of keeping knights happily drunk, though his opportunism has also borne him a new industry—selling secrets overheard in the Stone Anvil. Of course, such prized intel is only available to those willing to pay hefty sums and swear an oath of secrecy.

#### L’Hibou

Daniel Alessi L'Hibou L’Hibou is a reformist revolutionary who preaches about the sins of the knights, from the Severe Templar’s extreme autocracy to the other Knight Chapters’ passive negligence. From beneath their red cloak and steel owl mask, l’Hibou urges the people of Belforétte and Verdeaux to disavow the knights and remake society to service the disenfranchised. Their somewhat radical words are accompanied by extreme acts of violent sabotage in Berthold, done in the hopes of destabilizing the institution of knights and sparking a revolution. Few have been able to glean anything about l’Hibou’s identity, including the Severe Templars who are hot on their heels. Some say that l’Hibou wears dented knight armour with the coat of arms torn away underneath the cloak, while others swear that l’Hibou’s limbs can transform into twisted blades.

### Quest Hooks

The following quest hooks can be used to flesh out adventures set in Castle Lamesdhonneur.

#### Elven Execution

In Belforétte’s main marketplace, a group of black-clad templars march an elven man to the gallows. The elf’s face is battered and bloodied, and one of his ears has been crudely lopped off. A crowd of common folk gathers around the scaffold, jeering and hurling slur-filled insults at the elf, eager for some entertainment in their otherwise work-filled day.

Through swollen eyelids, the elf makes eye contact with the characters. The characters can read his bloodied lips as he pleads his innocence and promises a reward if he’s saved from his impending execution.

#### Creature Capture

A tournament is in three days, but the Knights of the Severe Templar have yet to acquire the appropriate monsters for the contest. The characters, alongside other mercenary crews, are hired to track down monsters, capture them, and transport them to the Victor’s Circle alive and in fighting shape. While the Severe Templar are open to any kind of monster, Josseline d'Eon pays extra for anyone who supplies a cursed monster for the tournament.

#### Unwitting Smugglers

Armand Basile hires the characters to escort a caravan of empty barrels back to the brewer it came from outside the city. What Armand doesn’t know is that a group of elven refugees are hiding in the barrels in the hopes of being smuggled safely out of the city. Now the characters must escort this precarious contraband through the thronging streets of Belforétte and past the gate checks conducted by the Severe Templar. If they’re caught, Armand will be quick to throw the blame onto the characters.

#### Owl in Flight

Chased by shouting severe templars, a figure in a red cloak and an owl mask turns the corner and crashes into the player characters. In the ensuing tussle and grapple, the mysterious person—l’Hibou—looks the characters in the eyes and says, “If you believe in freedom and justice, then let me go!” The characters only have a few moments to choose before the Severe Templar catch up.

If the characters let l’Hibou escape, the vigilante leaves behind a simple iron key and encoded coordinates to a hideaway.

> You would not catch me saying a bad word about the Severe Templar.
> —Lady Mireille de Bayeux

#### Sickle and Blood

In the relatively peaceful fields of Verdeaux, a meek and humble farmer named Pierre attacked a visiting Wind Walker with a sickle. This erratic act is the latest incident in a recent pattern, where farmers have suddenly turned their farming tools against knights only to then have no memory of the attack. Pierre’s partner Francis, who swears that Pierre would never hurt a fly let alone a person, begs the characters to investigate what lead to this sudden change in behavior.

#### A Knight’s Dying Wish

Groans from an alleyway in Belforétte lead the characters to an unarmoured knight bleeding out onto the cobblestone streets. The knight is barely able to explain that she was attacked by an unknown assailant, her sword and shield stolen after she was stabbed multiple times. In her final breath, she asks that the characters to recover her shield and hang it upon the border wall, where it can continue to protect the city even after she dies.

#### Stifled Forges

The furnaces of the Ferforger have gone stone cold and halted all production. Not even the most experienced blacksmith can get the fires relit. People now whisper that the Ferforger has been cursed by a vengeful mage. The Severe Templar, eager to get the forge back in operation, hire the characters to either fix the forge or find who’s at fault.

#### Sewer Squire

Nobles in Berthold complain of a rotting smell that has permeated their homes. While investigating, the characters discover the dismembered arm of a squire next to an open sewer grate, their blank shield splattered with still-warm blood. Upon closer inspection, the characters note that the squire’s shoulder is covered in bite marks, as if it was gnawed clean off.

#### Rampart Phantom

Rumors abound about a ghost appearing atop the ramparts of the Coronette. This apparition appears to be of an elven woman, and yet she bears a striking resemblance to the missing Princess Tiphaine. A local herald named Léonide asks the characters to watch the ramparts and confirm if the rumors are true. It would be even better for Léonide if the characters are able to somehow communicate with the ghost.

#### Mireille’s Messenger

Lady Mireille de Bayeux sent along a confidential letter of sympathy to Ser Nicodème le Doux, one of the last remaining Vermeil Guard that still stays in the city despite the Severe Templar’s increasing hostility. However, Lady Mireille’s messenger has disappeared along with the message. Fearing that the letter may have fallen in the wrong hands, Lady Mireille asks the characters to trace the messenger’s last steps and recover the message.

## Tol Leyemil

Erel Maatita

One of the most beautiful places that remain in Etharis, and the last refuge of the elves, Tol Leyemil blooms from the depths of the forest like a glade of vibrant wildflowers. Music flows through its stone archways. Art flourishes in every overgrown street. The scent of flowers upon the air and sound of flowing water create a quiet sense of peace and unity. But this calm exists only because the wars are long over. Tol Leyemil is a city of mourning.

Most folk, even other Charnelaise, only ever hear about Tol Leyemil’s splendor. The city is closed off to all other species. The most any uninvited traveler can hope to glimpse are the moss-covered spires and insect chewed corpses hung outside the city from the boughs of trees—other wayfarers who did not heed the elves’ warnings to stay away.

Tol Leyemil is protected by the Dawn Dreamers, who patrol the surrounding forest and the invisible barriers created by the landscape interweaving with the Realm of Faerie. However, with the Dark Mist distorting the Spirits of Nature and devastating Charneault, the residents of Tol Leyemil are faced with an existential danger. No longer can they thrive by remaining hidden. The very Fey Courts they’ve relied upon for their protection are corrupted by the mist, so the elves dread their demise at the hands of their own deific ancestors. Yet they also fear losing what has long been the only living remnant of elven history, making them reluctant to seek unity with the rest of the Charneault Kingdom.

Unrest brews. Trust among the leaders of Tol Leyemil is thin. The city has withstood the near annihilation of its people, but with the enshrouding mist and the rot it brings, Tol Leyemil now lies on the precipice of destruction.

### Geography

Tol Leyemil is built above the rapids of a flowing river, which runs through the forest and all the way to the distant Emerald Sea. Water cascades down into the valley where Tol Leyemil is cradled, beneath the cliffs that descend from the Rock-Teeth Mountains. The songs of birds fill the valley. The smells of pollen, bark, nettle, and earth drift upon the air. There is no city more harmonious with nature; it feels as if it grew from the forest rather than being built my mortal hands.

#### The Glade

The buildings of Tol Leyemil separate the forest to create a wide glade. Winding paths and arching bridges find their way into the city, meandering between tree trunks and menhirs carved to resemble hooded sentinels. These ancient stones, clung with moss and ivy, represent the Spirits of Nature who protect Tol Leyemil. Yet few elves remember their names.

The streets inside Tol Leyemil aren’t built for carriages or wagons. Tree roots are permitted to displace the flagstones, vines drape into narrow archways, and thick foliage blossoms to obstruct laneways entirely. Twisting boughs reach through buildings. The limestone spires and ivory towers rise above the trees, the boulevards below nestled in leaves and dappled with sunlight. The air glows in the evenings by the light of fireflies.

Yet beneath all this beauty lies much grief. A fountain sculpture of dancing sylphs is cracked and dry, the faeries who once played in its waters having retreated into their own realm. Faded murals depict ancient places in the Thousand Rivers that no longer exist. Weathered statues honor distant heroes whose deaths did nothing to protect Caer Neiada.

Music is a constant in Tol Leyemil. But the melodies are soft and mournful. If the elves did not keep their histories alive in the glade through song, it would all be forgotten from Etharis entirely.

#### The Great Falls

The Great Falls are a breathless sight. Rushing water descends in crystalline curtains from high in the mountains behind Tol Leyemil, joining multiple wellsprings to cascade into the rapids that flow beneath the city. Colors arch within the roiling mist. Rumors imply that the Great Falls speak to those willing to listen, and the laughter of faeries can be heard beneath the splashing waters. Traditions say the falls descend from the Realm of Faerie itself, bringing whispers of wisdom from the Fey Courts.

There are indeed secret places beneath the waterfalls. An intricate cave system, unknown to many elves, naturally honeycombs the cliffs above the city. Some caves, veiled by water, lead into moss-covered grottos that catch the rain. Other tunnels are flanked by weathered statues sculpted from the rock walls, the memory of whether they were made by elf hands or their ancestors long faded from history. Ancient mines carve into the belly of the Rock-Teeth Mountains. Rarely do the elves still use them. The deepest tunnels are said to flicker with light, which if followed, eventually leads into the realm of goblins and other cantankerous fey.

#### The Seasons

The seasons are felt keenly within Tol Leyemil. As the dominance of one Fey Court wanes into the next, the city transforms. The summers are green and scorching, with shade from the trees the only solace from the overbearing humidity. In autumn, the city is painted in fiery hues. The breeze turns cold, and mist from the Great Falls drifts through the glade. In winter, snow blankets everything, and some of the brooks freeze. Spring heralds the return of warmer days, with blossoming plants and birdsong once more filling the glade.

Witnessing the transcendental beauty of the city has inspired countless artists to capture it in song and on canvas. This is the closest most in Charneault will ever come to witnessing Tol Leyemil for themselves.

As the seasons change, so too does the presence of the Spirits in Nature. In summer, quick winged sprites steal fruit and coins. In winter, homes are invaded with shadow spirits and the caves of the Great Falls glow with goblin campfires. These fey are hunted as pests in distant parts of Charneault. In Tol Leyemil, they are accommodated as servants of the Archfey, and some even celebrated as elf kin, though the city is divided on who should be welcomed and who should not. Despite a significant population of talented druids, even the Bondmakers have disparate loyalties to the differing Fey Courts.

### History

At the height of Caer Neiada, there were many elven cities like Tol Leyemil. Now it is the last. The city was established beneath the beauty of the Great Falls and is thought to be one of the most ancient settlements in Etharis. An oasis to commune with the Spirits of Nature.

The entrances into the Realm of Faerie are copious near the elven city. The waters. The woods. The caves. Even a limestone archway down a narrow street could be a road to the Fey Courts. Therefore, the elves believe the valley where Tol Leyemil is built was chosen as a sacred place, where the barriers to the Feywild wear thin.

#### Last Elven City

When the Pact of the Sacred Land gave rise to the Charneault Kingdom, securing peace within the realm, Tol Leyemil’s seclusion was written into those ancient laws. While every other elven city was razed or abandoned, Tol Leyemil endured.

Elven hymns sing of the city’s sanctity. It was spared because the Spirits of Nature willed it, veiling Tol Leyemil against the fires of the north. Such a precious gateway to the Realm of Faerie would always need the stewardship of elves, so humans and other species would never be permitted inside.

Chivalric histories paint Thuridan Sarador as the reason Tol Leyemil survived. As Sarador sat beside the river speaking with Aymeric Noblecoeur, the elven sage described his home with such beauty that it moved the future king to tears. Agreeing that the city should remain safe, the two leaders enshrined it within their sacred pact.

But such fairytales are not believed by all. There’s no doubt Sarador retreated to Tol Leyemil for a time, while the rest of Caer Neiada burned. But within the city’s records, which human eyes have never seen, it’s shown that Sarador’s true home was devastated long before he fled to Tol Leyemil.

In truth, Tol Leyemil was likely chosen to be the last elven city simply because it was the only refuge that remained when the fires of war finally receded. But its survival was costly. The elves preferred their blood to flow through the Thousand Rivers than cede what little they had left.

#### The Acen Elavain

Klyde Sosa Angry Fey Tol Leyemil isn’t led by nobility. Instead, citizens are chosen at random to serve as members of the Acen Elavain, the “Assembly of Elves,” holding vast political power whilst representing the voices of their people both within Tol Leyemil and the broader Charneault Kingdom. The council is large and often fractured in opinions. Half the assembly are isolationists who view themselves as dissidents in an occupied realm, protecting the last shards of their heritage. Others roam abroad the Thousand Rivers, giving voice to elves throughout Charneault. They advise the regency on the needs of the elven people and seek restitution when they believe the Pact of the Sacred Land has been broken.

The arbitrary method of appointing new members to the Acen Elavain ensures a diversity of ideologies. Yet in practice, skilled orators and cunning partisans tend to dominate the less intrigue-minded members of the Assembly. Power vacuums emerge when a councilor’s tenure ends. Alliances are redrawn when new members are randomly appointed. The chaotic machinations of the Acen Elavain mirror the elves’ cultural ties to the capricious Fey Courts. Power can shift in Tol Leyemil as swiftly as the seasons change.

These traditions predate the Pact of the Sacred Land, when the Bondmakers arguably took authority away from the Assembly. Though some citizens seethe about the druids, self-appointed influence in Tol Leyemil, the Bondmakers connection to the Spirits of Nature makes them a powerful voice within the city.

### Notable Locations

Tol Leyemil is small enough to not be separated into boroughs or districts. The buildings cluster close together in the vale. Nevertheless, there are some locations of notable significance.

#### Hall of Many Voices

Charneault Fey Court

This great building near the center of Tol Leyemil houses the Acen Elavain. The council chamber seats all twenty members of the assembly, and despite being a hallowed place the hall frequently echoes with cacophonous hostility.

Rarely is every councilor in attendance. Many travel abroad throughout Charneault, preferring not to waste their time trapped in the council chamber, or are in fact afraid for their safety in the Hall of Many Voices. Seats are instead occupied by advisors who negotiate and vote on their councilor’s behalf. These advisors hold no official power, yet their voices are influential in the ears of the assembly, and their political knowledge is vast. They are yet another layer of the machinations that unfold within the Hall of Many Voices.

Branching from the council chamber are many corridors that lead to parlors, terraces, and gardens where councilors conduct private discussions. Intrigues are hatched in these idyllic surrounds. Bargains are made. Cutting through the bureaucracy of the Acen Elavain is often difficult, given the size of the council and the number of divided loyalties. The Fey Courts. The Bondmakers. The crown of Charneault. Tol Leyemil itself. Therefore, the rules of the assembly are not always followed, occasionally to violent ends.

There is fear in the Hall of Many Voices. While the Acen Elavain fails to address the Dark Mist, struggling with inner turmoil over its loyalty to the Charneault Kingdom, rumors prowl that Dark Elves are among the assembly, disciples of the sorcerer Diyanthar.

#### Sarmar Academy

The Sarmar Academy is home to mages of profound talent. While other arcane institutions teach command, Sarmar teaches patience. Magic is learned through careful petitioning of the Spirits of Nature to reweave the energies of the primal world into spells. A calm wind becomes a crushing gale. Water is conjured and shaped into a dagger of ice.

The Academy has a central building—an elven citadel with many hanging towers like the branches of a tree. But this place is only for meditation and gatherings. The true lessons occur in the wilderness of the forest, in a garden filled with music, around a bubbling cauldron beneath the moon, or surrounded by scrolls in a candlelit library. By whichever method one weaves their magic, the Academy provides mentorship.

Apprentices are not specifically inducted into the Sarmar Academy. Rather, tutors seek gifted pupils and offer them mentorship, providing guidance and resources to help an apprentice cultivate their skills. Mages do not graduate or receive commendations. The elves don’t license their mages like other nations do. Magic isn’t merely a weapon for achieving power. It’s a form of spirituality and worship, used to commune with the Spirits of Nature and draw upon the elves’ ancestral strength. It’s a way to record history through ritual and song. A method by which to shape the natural world rather than become lost within it. The Sarmar view themselves less as academics and more as philosophers who view the world through experience rather than purely study.

When an apprentice’s studies are considered complete—which can come at any time and not as the result of some predetermined achievement—the mage is free to pursue life as they wish. Many join the Bondmakers. Others make their skills available as spell-swords of the Dawn Dreamers or seek their ambitions in the wider Charneault Kingdom.

The focus on patience and observation makes studying with Sarmar last decades and is considered above the capabilities of most other species—especially those of shorter lifespans. Yet Sarmar tutors aren’t forbidden from mentoring a human or halfling. Such apprentices never visit the Academy tower in Tol Leyemil, but a new home has been made for them outside of the city.

Aemanyir House was founded by Alren Corynthios, and lies south of Tol Leyemil, on the forest’s edge in Beaumont. This small settlement was built to mimic the function of the Academy Tower, a place of contemplation and magical practice. Many in Tol Leyemil refuse to recognize Aemanyir House as a branch of the Sarmar Academy, yet pupils trained there are done so in the same traditions that Alren learned in Tol Leyemil.

> That was the best decade of my life.

#### The Preservation

The most pivotal documents recording Caer Neiada’s history before the Era of Expansion are held within the Preservation. The elves who tend this massive library also guard it fervently. Even the Acen Elavain and Sarmar academics must request permission to view specific records, and some archives are only available to the Preservationists themselves.

The Preservation only exists in whispers to most of Tol Leyemil. Most elves aren’t even aware of its location within the city, and in truth the archive isn’t consolidated in a single place. The identities of the elves entrusted with maintaining the Preservation, usually past disciples of the Sarmar Academy, aren’t known publicly. They waft throughout the city in white robes and enclosed, ornate masks like Spirits of Nature. Rumors say that the Preservation does not sit entirely within the city, but that parts of the archive extends into the Realm of Faerie itself.

The Preservationists hold a small measure of power within Tol Leyemil. Certain murals or statues cannot be moved without their permission. When a relic of the city’s history needs to make room for a modern structure, the Preservationists move the object into the archive. When an elven artifact is brought to Tol Leyemil from foreign lands, it’s within the Preservation’s purview to confiscate the object and lock it within their library.

What powerful relics and mystical tomes sit within the Preservation can only be guessed. While the stated purpose of the Preservation is ensuring elven culture never vanishes entirely, folk in the broader Charneault Kingdom aware of its existence fear the elves have stockpiled a repository of ancient weapons and eldritch knowledge.

#### Faerie Roads

Tol Leyemil sits on the cusp of the Feywild, and countless hidden roads lead to and from the city through the Realm of Faerie. Few can navigate these paths safely, and many live a lifetime in the city without realizing they exist.

Curtains of flowering vines conceal a city laneway. Stepping through leads to a forested wilderness. On a warm summer’s day, ice forms at a pond's edge. In its depths lies a gateway to the Winter Court. Entering the same door twice is fraught with danger. A building beside a busy boulevard might exit to a mountaintop, or a day could pass on the street whilst only a few hours were spent within.

The Faerie Roads appear and disappear within the city at the whims of the Spirits of Nature. A small cupboard could become the entrance to a twilight garden when a faerie wishes to summon a mortal servant into their realm. That same cupboard contains nothing but clothes to anyone the fey did not invite. These hidden roads allow the Spirits of Nature to converse with those they wish in secret, leaving it unclear who within the city are servants to the Fey Courts.

The Bondmakers, Sarmar, and even a few among the Dawn Dreamers can navigate the Faerie Roads with less difficulty. Such skills are used to traverse Charneault swiftly. The Faerie Roads can lead from somewhere like the Grove Maze to Tol Leyemil without ever leaving the forests. The Bondmakers use this talent to petition the Spirits directly. Elven rangers can pass through the Feywild unseen, while intruders near Tol Leyemil get lost within the Faerie Roads surrounding the city without ever realizing they left the mortal realm.

#### The Autumn Spire

Outside the valley of Tol Leyemil rises a white tower, draped with ivy and domed with gold. Myths say it was built by the fey. Today it’s the fortress of the Dawn Dreamers, Chapter Knights dedicated to the protection of the elven city.

The Dawn Dreamers date back to the elven warriors whose lives bought the safety of Tol Leyemil. For many years, their ranks were only elves. As the Charneault Kingdom became more united, other species squired to join theses knights, whether through a deep lineage traced back to Tol Leyemil or romantic ideals to serve the Spirits of Nature. In either case, non-elves are not allowed to live in the city. Many never even see it. It’s the oath of the Dawn Dreamers to lead a life of violence in protection of a peace they can never enjoy.

The Autumn Spire's barracks are wreathed in their own beauty. Tapestries, paintings, and sculptures line the halls, weaving histories that remind the knights what they fight for. All squires are taught music. It’s a virtue prized almost as much as courage and swordsmanship. The Dawn Dreamers learn elvish songs of anguish, filling their hearts with a beating rage that encourages no mercy for trespassers in the woods. The instruments of legendary knights, relics of the chapter's history, are enshrined within the tower.

Beneath this nobility are the roots of wickedness. In the spire's basement hide vile interrogation chambers, unknown even to Acen Elavain or the king and his predecessors. Keeping Tol Leyemil safe demands brutal methods to ward against threats from within.

### Important Individuals

Notable people associated with Tol Leyemil include those described below.

#### Virion Venfir

Charismatic. Beautiful. Cunning. Virion Venfir is all three and in equal measure. A current and influential member of the Acen Elavain, Virion was an astonishing musician before being elevated to join the assembly. His fellow councilors underestimated his eloquence and confidence. It was assumed Virion would show no interest in governance and lazily wait out his tenure in the assembly, but instead he’s become one of the most well-known and influential members of the council. Virion is extremely popular among the citizens of Tol Leyemil. Through his music and bardic tales, Virion is seen as a true voice of the people, and many in the assembly wish to tie their reputation with his.

As a younger elf, Virion was a refugee who came to Tol Leyemil when his village was burned by folk superstitious of the fey. The trauma he experienced quietly turned to rage when he saw the elven paradise—this place where his people lived carefree. Where the Spirits of Nature were revered, not hated. Where the buildings were limestone and painted with murals, not huts speckled with mud beside a brackish stream.

Virion quietly welcomes the Dark Mist. He believes it could wipe the land clean of non-elven cultures, creating a new Caer Neiada. He uses position and charm to influence others to this idea. But Virion is no fool. He hides his true feelings beneath a disguise of compassion for the plight of elves living outside the safety of Tol Leyemil.

#### Alren Corynthios

An archmage of the Sarmar Academy, Alren Corynthios began traveling the Charneault Kingdom in search for talented spellcasters to mentor. His apprentices were frequently unorthodox. A human who had been chosen for a pact with the archfey. A gnomish druid who could whisper with the Spirits of Nature and bend the weather to their whims. An ogresh apothecary talented with decoctions.

Frustrated he couldn’t bring these apprentices back to Tol Leyemil and draw on the resources of the Academy Tower, Alren eventually founded his own branch of the Sarmar Academy: Aemanyir House. Reeve of this small Beaumont community, Alren has since been joined by colleagues who aid him to train new apprentices in the Sarmar traditions.

Alren’s apparent ethos is unity. He believes the more skilled mages Charneault can draw upon, regardless of heritage, the more chance the realm has to survive the Dark Mist. But his view isn’t shared by all in Tol Leyemil. It isn’t certain whether the Dawn Dreamers, protection should extend to Aemanyir House, or if the knights should seek to destroy it as a vulnerability for elven secrets. Other knight chapters are equally wary. The Severe Templar argue the settlement is a cabal of fey idolaters who should be disbanded before they become dangerous.

#### Eifre

A squat sylvan creature, Eifre is a craftswoman who travels the breadth of Charneault via the Faerie Roads. She barters and sells her wares. Most of what she trades she claims to have found or fashioned herself, though given her puckish nature, few in the kingdom trust her. Most assume she’s a thief.

Eifre is a frequent visitor in Tol Leyemil, bringing desired commodities that can’t be obtained inside the city. Sometimes it’s as simple as a pouch of sugar. Other times it’s a master-crafted arrow dipped in the sunlight of the Summer Court, or a hunting horn that conjures woodland spirits from the Realm of Faerie. Eifre trades for antiques, old books, or magic items unique to Tol Leyemil, and then disperses them throughout the realm.

Many elves disapprove of Eifre disseminating artifacts of their history outside the sacred city. She’s even been accused of pilfering from the Preservation itself. But as a mostly benign nature spirit, she enjoys a degree of reverence that has kept her from harm whenever she visits Tol Leyemil. If ever she’s in actual danger, Eifre somehow manages to vanish just in the nick of time.

> Let me see what I have in my pack that might tickle your fancy? Need a goblin skull that's been burned in the fires of the Summer Court, by any chance?
> —Eifre

#### Benoît the Brave

Once a young boy orphaned by a flash flood, Benoît joined the ranks of the Dawn Dreamers after the knights found him in the woods grappling with a red fox over a rabbit. Dirty, scrawny, and starving, Benoît might have been left for dead. But his tenacity earned him his moniker and a place among the elven knights. Decades of dedicated service to the protection of Tol Leyemil lead to Benoît rising to the rank of Chapter Master: the first of mostly human heritage to lead the Dawn Dreamers. With a loud laugh and an easy smile, Benoît has made his reputation as the merry guardian of Tol Leyemil. Bards love him. His brave deeds weave a hero’s tale. But the Acen Elavain despise him, disgusted that their last refuge is protected by a descendant of the north. Omar Samy Benoît the Brave

Benoît has spent his entire life justifying the ties to his elven heritage. Even now, he’ll never be permitted to settle in Tol Leyemil. Yet there is no violence, no torture, no dishonor Benoît would not commit to protect the elven city. He would cut the crowned head from King Thibault’s shoulders if it was in the interest of his people, and Benoît dreams of a day when Hermand de Sévère stands at the end of his blade. For now, the brave knight must vent his frustrations on the brigands who dare approach Tol Leyemil without invitation.

### Quest Hooks

The following quest hooks can be used to flesh out adventures set in Tol Leyemil.

#### A Thousand Hues

New paintings have begun appearing on city walls inside Tol Leyemil, usually depicting exquisite landscapes so detailed they almost appear lifelike. Rivers with splashing rapids. Grasslands beneath a twilit sky. The artist behind these breathtaking images isn’t known, but those who admire the work swear the paint moves on its own—the waves of a coastline crash into seafoam. Clouds drift lazily across the heavens.

There’s no doubt these artworks are magical. The Sarmar Academy wishes the painter to be found so they can be offered mentorship, though another concern motivates the search. The Academy believes that each painting is a gate to the Faerie Roads. Folk have disappeared near the paintings, and capricious fey have had an easier time entering Tol Leyemil. The artist may even be a Spirit of Nature themself. Even more concerning is how the paintings change over time, transforming from idyllic landscapes into shadowy scenes clouded by creeping mist.

The Sarmar Academy will reward anyone who discovers the identity and location of the mysterious painter. Their artwork is stunning. Yet in Tol Leyemil, great beauty often obfuscates ill intent.

#### At Any Cost

Sir Benoît, Chapter Master of the Dawn Dreamers, is deeply suspicious that Dark Elves have infiltrated the Acen Elavain. Deeper investigation is hindered by the assembly’s distaste for the knight. Therefore, Benoît has turned to hiring agents he trusts but can also deny association with.

Discovering which members of the assembly have associations with Diyanthar or the Dark Mist may be a quest that leads to places distant from Tol Leyemil. Unearthing such information will not come easily. Sir Benoît sanctions any means necessary to discover the identities of the Dark Elves and welcomes any suspects to the Autumn Spire for more thorough interrogation.

#### Book of True Names

Many primal artifacts and books of lost knowledge rest within the Preservation. Master Joséph Windorff is an archmage who would very much like to secure such a tome, which he believes exists in Tol Leyemil. The book in question contains the true names of the Archfey. An ogresh who has spent decades studying primal magic, Master Windorff believes the book will bring him immeasurable power over the Spirits of Nature.

The archmage hires mercenaries of renown to undertake this quest. There are several possible approaches to infiltrate the elven city.

The direct route would be through the forest that encircles Tol Leyemil. This path undoubtedly leads to conflict with the Dawn Dreamers, whose strength and numbers should not be underestimated. The knights will lay woodland traps and deadly ambushes. They might seek to mislead interlopers down false paths away from the city or into the midst of a monster’s lair. The Dawn Dreamers ranks include skilled rangers and knights, supported by war druids who use the wilderness as their weapons. Trying to sneak past their patrols could lead to a game of cat and mouse, as potentially deadly as confronting the Dawn Dreamers head on.

Alternatively, Master Windorff has some knowledge in how to navigate the Faerie Roads. One could avoid the Dawn Dreamers entirely by risking the twisting paths of the Feywild instead. Though equally perilous, this route would be more hidden. Yet who’s to say what forbidding plants and eldritch creatures might be encountered in the darkest corners of the Realm of Faerie?

Even inside Tol Leyemil, discovering the location of the Preservation and gaining access is a whole other challenge. The Preservationists are skilled mages and warriors in their own right, sworn to protect the contents of their sacred library. And if rumors of the Preservation are true, it’s likely a massive complex as twisting and difficult to navigate as the Feywild itself. What other monsters or traps guard it? What further elven artifacts or knowledge might it contain, if such treasures can be procured whilst escaping the Preservation alive?

#### The Winter Queen

Winter pours into Tol Leyemil. Icy breaths of mist flood the city streets from the Faerie Roads, and the Great Falls threaten to freeze entirely. The trees have turned blue with frost. Rime carpets the flagstones and gardens.

Worse than this unnatural invasion of winter is the cold that has settled into the hearts of the elves in Tol Leyemil. Disaffected, impatient, and cruel, the denizens of the city have turned upon each other. Anger rises easily as the cold brings despair. Conflict over withering rations erupts as folk become desperate. Some elves sit listlessly in their homes, as if life itself had been drained from them. Others weep despairingly while the shadows snicker at their pain. Tol Leyemil has become a hunting ground for unseelie horrors.

The cause for this enduring winter isn’t certain, though the Bondmakers have divined a course of action. A mirror is to be constructed by arcane means, which can be used to travel to the Winter Court and discover what dread has befallen it. Has some offence occurred within Tol Leyemil to invoke the Winter Queen’s wrath? Or have tendrils of the Dark Mist begun to infiltrate the Fey Courts directly?

To create this mirror, the icy shards must be collected. Each are forged by enacting a dark ritual. This ritual can be taught by the Bondmakers to any magically gifted, but it requires the death of a person whose heart is saturated by cruelty, hatred, or despair. Turning to agents they trust for help, the Bondmakers have begun collecting shards of the mirror. How many are required remains to be seen, as do the horrors that await in the unseelie realm of the Winter Court once the mirror is complete.
