**Chapter 4: The Charneault Kingdom**

The Charneault Kingdom is fairytale horror: a land of breathtaking beauty that conceals amoral cruelty at every level. Rolling hills, clear rivers, and elven forests make it the most visually idyllic nation in Etharis — which only makes the violence more disturbing when it surfaces. The fey are not allies or guides; they are capricious, ancient, and casually destructive. The chivalric knighthood is noble on its face and rotting beneath it. And lately, the mist that has always clung to this land has turned dark, bringing madness, corrupted nature spirits, and something that may be divine punishment. The tone oscillates between pastoral beauty and sudden, arbitrary horror.

## Geography

Charneault occupies the southwestern corner of Etharis, nearly impossible to reach overland — the Grove Maze (a sentient, rearranging forest) and the Rock-Teeth Mountains block access from the north and east, while the Emerald Sea and Gulf of Lions form the coastal borders. Within these natural walls lies a kingdom of meandering hills, vast skies, and a thousand named rivers. Dense woods and fertile farmland fill the interior between scattered villages.

**The Grove Maze** is a labyrinthine ancient forest where paths vanish and re-form at the will of the Spirits of Nature. Maps are useless. Travelers who the archfey do not wish to pass through simply do not. Corpses of trespassers fertilize the vegetation at its edge.

**The Temple of Mist** lies within a caldera in southern Charneault, inside a permanent fog where druids commune directly with the Spirits of Nature. Its rituals are never visible from outside the crater. The firstborn child of every Charneault king is sent here to disappear forever — the price the Spirits of Nature demand for the kingdom's prosperity.

## History

**The Ulufey and the First Men.** The elves (ulufey, "mortal fey") inhabited Caer Neiada for uncounted centuries before northern humans broke through the Grove Maze. The humans felled trees, farmed without offering, and took without asking. Generations of war followed.

**The Pact of the Sacred Land.** Knight Aymeric Noblecoeur and elven sage Thuridan Sarador brokered a peace that became the foundation of the Charneault Kingdom: shared land, shared governance, an elven Bondmaker council to counsel the crown, and one unbreakable tribute — each regent's firstborn child walks to the Temple of Mist and is never seen again. Every ruler who has tried to circumvent this has brought disaster.

**The Circle of Bondmakers** are the druidic intermediaries between the crown and the Spirits of Nature. Secretive, trusted by neither side fully, suspected of loyalty to the fey over the kingdom. They wander Charneault maintaining the bonds, and only they may enter the Temple of Mist.

**The Dark Mist.** King Thibault de Sauveterre's firstborn daughter Tiphaine was betrothed by sacred custom to the Temple of Mist. She also secretly married Sir Guilhem le Preux, a celebrated Vermeil Guard knight. When the time came, Guilhem broke his oath and tried to save her — arriving too late, finding the Bondmakers slaughtered and Tiphaine gone. He rode into the Temple and neither returned. The mist turned dark. Whether the Severe Templars murdered the escort to break the Pact, or a sorcerer named Diyanthar abducted the Princess for a dark ritual, or Guilhem's interference corrupted something he almost stopped — nobody knows. Both Sir Guilhem and Princess Tiphaine have been seen as apparitions within the dark mist.

## The Dark Mist

The mist originates naturally from magma vents along the southern coast but is also an instrument of the archfey. In its normal state it is harmless — sprites march through it, faerie circles appear in its wake, goats get milked by invisible hands. In its corrupted dark form, it is a region-destroying catastrophe.

When the dark mist descends: crops turn to dust in spring, floods wash away settlements in winter, seasons invert. Soil becomes hostile — vines strangle, spores poison, treants bleed sickly sap that draws swarms. The Spirits of Nature inside the mist are corrupted: pixies lose their wings and become corpse-gardening pucks; sylvan archers hunt village occupants as sport; entire towns become spider nests. The mist's passage leaves silence where there was music, and laughter where there should be screaming — the sounds inverted.

## Cultural Flavor

**Knighthood** is the highest aspiration. Five major chapters each embody different virtues: the **Iron Sparrow** (border defense), **Fairweathers** (domestic peace and justice), **Dawn Dreamers** (protection of Tol Leyemil, elven membership), **Vermeil Guard** (escort of Bondmakers and royal tribute), and **Severe Templars** (mapping and studying the realm — increasingly authoritarian and anti-elf).

A squire earns knighthood through a quest that produces a worthy deed, then claims the right to paint their own coat of arms on their blank shield. Squires who impress a Spirit of Nature may take primal oaths; those who impress an Arch Seraph become paladins. This culture of honor-through-deeds coexists uneasily with the current Severe Templar regime in Castle Lamesdhonneur, which has replaced most deeds with executions.

The fey and the Arch Seraph faiths coexist across the kingdom. Elves practice transactional reverence with the Spirits of Nature — always offering equal value for what is taken. Northerners pray to Arch Seraphs and the old gods. The Bondmakers are distrusted by both groups.

## Key NPCs

**Hermand le Sévère** — Chapter Master of the Severe Templars, a flinty, austere knight of genuine martial renown who pursues power through authoritarian governance. He has positioned the Severe Templars as the dominant force in Castle Lamesdhonneur: suppressing elven culture, burning sacred sites, executing dissidents. He speaks few words in the Council Chamber and does not need many.

**Virion Venfir** — an astonishing elven musician elevated to the Acen Elavain (the elven governing council of Tol Leyemil) who became unexpectedly influential through charm and eloquence. Privately he welcomes the dark mist — he believes it could cleanse Charneault of all non-elven cultures and restore Caer Neiada. He conceals this behind expressions of compassion for displaced elves.

**Benoît the Brave** — Chapter Master of the Dawn Dreamers, the first of mostly human heritage to lead the elven city's knightly protectors. The Acen Elavain despises him. He has spent his entire life justifying his elven ties and will sanction any violence necessary to protect Tol Leyemil, including torture and assassination. Cheerful and beloved by bards in public.

**Alren Corynthios** — archmage of the Sarmar Academy who founded Aemanyir House, an off-site branch that trains non-elven students in elven magical traditions. His stated ethos is unity; the Severe Templars call it a fey cabal and want it disbanded.

**L'Hibou** — a revolutionary in a red cloak and owl mask who preaches against all the knight chapters and commits violent sabotage in Castle Lamesdhonneur's districts. Identity unknown. Possibly wearing dented knight armor with a torn coat of arms beneath the cloak. Possibly something more than human under the hood.

## Featured City: Castle Lamesdhonneur

The capital sits on a hill, the eponymous castle jutting from stone foundations above the city like a crown above a body. It was once the heart of Charneault's chivalric ideal — the place where the knight chapters trained, where the common folk labored to feed and arm the guardians of the realm. Now the Severe Templars have seized control, shuttered businesses they deem frivolous, executed elves in the marketplace, and remade the city into an authoritarian fortress. The streets smell of the Burning Maw's smoke. The jousting arenas have been repurposed for monster fights. The sacred maple tree that kings were crowned beneath was cut down. Former allies of the elves have retreated; their chapter headquarters are now Severe Templar barracks.

### Notable Locations

**Coronette** — the original castle, seat of the royal family and the Royal Council. Its walls are carved with scenes of knightly virtue — now presided over by a king who barely leaves and a Council Chamber echoing with shouted disagreements.

**The Victor's Circle** — the old jousting arena, now hosting the Games of the White Dove: knights fighting captured monsters rather than each other. The Severe Templars rig the results to glorify their champions.

**The Burning Maw** — a great bonfire pit in the Verdeaux district, burning constantly, disposing of bodies, elven artifacts, and anything the Severe Templars have condemned. Tended by masked workers. Black smoke coats the surrounding crops.

**The Stone Anvil** — the last surviving pub, kept open by owner Armand Basile's deal with the Severe Templars (a tavern of knights is less dangerous than a street of resentful ones). Basile supplements his income by selling secrets overheard from drunk knights, to whoever can pay and swear an oath of silence.

### Key Individuals

**King Thibault de Sauveterre** — genuinely popular with the people, embattled by his own Council. Since the Dark Mist crisis began, he sequesters himself in Coronette. He tends in secret to the hidden garden his disappeared daughter Tiphaine loved. He has not publicly appeared in months.

**Josseline d'Eon** — Hermand's second-in-command, born into the Vermeil Guard but turned to the Severe Templars after witnessing Dark Mist horrors during her squire quest. Strategic, unreadable, and utterly devoted. Runs the chapter's daily operations within the city.

**Lady Mireille de Bayeux** — widowed when the Severe Templars executed her wife on fabricated charges of treason. Quietly building a network of noble dissidents under the guise of gentle grief. Under constant surveillance. Her soft demeanor is the only thing that keeps her alive.

## Featured Location: Tol Leyemil

The last elven city — hidden in a forest vale, closed to all other species, its moss-covered spires glimpsed only by travelers who don't heed the warning offered by the insect-chewed corpses hung from the surrounding boughs. Within: music, art, flowing water, the scent of flowers. Tol Leyemil is a city of mourning for a people who have survived near-annihilation and built beauty over the grief of it. Now the Dark Mist threatens even this refuge, corrupting the Fey Courts the elves rely on for protection. The city's governing assembly (the Acen Elavain) is deadlocked by divided loyalties and rumored to be infiltrated by dark elves serving the sorcerer Diyanthar.

### Notable Locations

**Hall of Many Voices** — the Acen Elavain's council chamber, usually half-empty (many councilors send advisors rather than risk attending personally). Intrigues hatch in the attached parlors and gardens. Violence occasionally disrupts sessions.

**The Sarmar Academy** — a school of primal magic that teaches patience over command: spells are learned by petitioning the Spirits of Nature to reweave the world's energies. True lessons happen in forests, gardens, and candlelit libraries — not lecture halls.

**The Preservation** — a secret archive whose location even most elves don't know, tended by white-robed masked archivists. Holds the oldest records of Caer Neiada and rumored to extend partly into the Realm of Faerie. The Preservationists can confiscate elven artifacts brought from outside; folk in the wider kingdom suspect it contains a stockpile of ancient weapons and forbidden knowledge.

**The Autumn Spire** — the Dawn Dreamers' fortress outside the city vale: beautiful tapestries and enshrined instruments above, hidden torture chambers in the basement below, kept secret even from the Acen Elavain.

### Key Individuals

**Virion Venfir** (see above) — the charming, secretly extremist councilor who uses bardic popularity as political cover.

**Alren Corynthios** (see above) — the archmage whose inclusive approach to magical education has made him both the city's greatest outward-facing asset and an internal controversy.

**Eifre** — a puckish sylvan spirit who travels Charneault via the Faerie Roads, trading obscure goods (sunlight-dipped arrows, spirit-summoning horns, bags of sugar) for Tol Leyemil artifacts she probably stole from the Preservation. Mostly benign. Accused of many things. Vanishes whenever genuinely threatened.

**Benoît the Brave** (see above) — the celebrated, eternally unwelcome Chapter Master who would murder a king to protect the elven city he can never live in.
