# The Bürach Empire

A dying empire of relentless greed and power struggles. Its faith is fractured, its people divided. The Hearthkeepers control information flow to slow the empire's dissolution, but ambition outstrips might. The Bürach people would rather blaze into glory than fade quietly.

Once ruling nearly all of Etharis, the empire overextended past the Grey Spines and couldn't maintain its territory. New nations carved from its borders pushed it back to the continent's heart. Now a plagued lion, determined not to die in silence.

Provinces: Abendland, Unterland, Bürach Empire (core), Rauland, Nordenland.

## Geography

Vast deep forests cover the empire, the tree line slowly receding to feed constant lumber demand.

### Western Bürach

Dense forests range from scenic to impenetrable canopy. The heartland favors oak, pine, and birch, but western pockets receive humid air bursts that transform flora into tangled jungle. Hidden valleys flood suddenly, drowning unwary travelers.

The western coast hugs the Emerald Sea — monstrous waters and Valikan raiders make coastal Abendland as treacherous as the deep forest. Mercenaries and monster hunters patrol the woods for coin.

**Walstein** — a ruined city of ash and soot, now a camping ground for adventurers and Imperial soldiers training against the area's abundant monsters.

Forest roads are treacherous but traversable; lumberjacks have widened trade routes over the years. All roads lead to **Altenheim**.

**Altenheim** — the capital, crowning the southern shore of Lake Osea. The "City of Joy" flourishes by draining wealth from surrounding provinces like a leech.

### Southern Bürach

The Rock-Teeth Mountains dominate the south — narrow, dangerous roads wind to ancient mines and forgotten settlements. Home to **Stehlenwald**, a tiered city carved from granite and marble resembling a celestial stairway. Its forges spew fire endlessly, trading in metalworks, jewelry, tools, and weaponry.

Above Stehlenwald sits an enormous palace of opalescent towers — home to **Martorius**, an archmage who claims to predate the empire. He swears no allegiance to any nation but takes keen interest in the empire's future.

### Eastern Bürach

**Unterland** — marshland with weathered buildings and battle-hardened soldiers. Roads are reinforced and regularly repaired as swamps try to swallow all structures.

**Olstenburg** and **Vollendorf** rely on trade routes passing through them; surrounding land yields little beyond rich hunting grounds.

**Grabenstein** — fortress city blocking the Grey Spine mountain pass, guarding against Ostoya. Its forges burn and towers are guarded, but ancient walls crumble from repeated conquest and rebuilding.

### Northern Bürach

**Nordenland** — constant rain, storms, and river flooding. Homes are regularly abandoned to floodwater; most who can't tolerate the seminomadic lifestyle flee to **Landhaus** or **Runeheim**.

**Mt. Vengeance** — an active volcano that awakened during Gods' End and grows more restless. Intense pulses of arcane power emanate from within. Ash spreads as far as Duesenberg. Researchers note eruptions and tremors are increasing. Of those who investigate, only scorched survivors return with tales of monsters lurking within.

The Norden fear the mountain casts judgment on the empire. Others believe it's a portal from the Netherworld spewing hellish monsters.

### Varied Landscapes

Marshlands, forests, mountain valleys, and coastlines give Bürach diverse natural resources. What one region lacks, another provides through trade. But hazards and monsters now disrupt commerce, darkening the national mood.

## History

Founded in the Era of Expansion with the crowning of Emperor Indorius I. The smaller kingdoms of central Etharis first united through faith in the Four Divines: **Aurelia** (healing/protection), **Galt** (law/sovereignty), **Ulmyr** (wisdom/foresight), and **Maligant** (war/might).

Hearthkeeper priests discovered a boy named Thancred who restored a lamb to life. They named him Emperor Indorius I. The Four Divines bestowed four Sacred Artifacts:

- **Aurelia's Breastplate** — divine protection for his bloodline
- **Galt's Circlet** — golden crown of sovereignty and just law
- **Ulmyr's Eye** — wisdom and foresight, with warning: "Consistency brings comfort while complacency can only bear the fruit of calamity"
- **Dämglesvir** (Might of Heaven) — Maligant's longsword; its sacred name revealed when it cut the emperor's finger. "A sword wielded by a coward can cut nothing"

With these blessings, the empire conquered region after region — marching, negotiating, and slaughtering until nearly all Etharis flew their flag.

### Gods' End

The Sacred Artifacts contained fragments of each god's very essence — far more than divine power.

The Hearthkeepers' Edict of Eternal Blood decreed only the emperor's direct descendants could rule. Purity through inbreeding produced **Leopold I** — cruel from childhood, imprisoning servants for minor infractions and ordering executions as entertainment at feasts.

Leopold I declared the empire would worship only the "One True Deity" but never named which god. Civil war erupted as each province championed their favored divine. The gods themselves quarreled, their ideals tarnishing into obsessive rhetoric demanding absolute devotion.

Assassins from the four provinces killed Leopold I. As divine influence waned, **Aurelia** realized the gods' battles would destroy Etharis. She shielded the mortal realm at the moment of catastrophe. Angels fell from the sky like comets — the gods were no more.

### Aftermath

The Hearthkeepers crowned a new emperor with forged Sacred Artifacts; shared history kept Bürach from dissolving entirely. But the civil war left deep divisions — border disputes, paranoia, and hostility toward travelers.

The true Sacred Artifacts went missing. Leopold's assassins tried to bring them to Nordenland's wizards but never arrived. Most believe the assassins, loyal to different provinces, quarreled and separated the treasures. They have been lost to history.

A theological account says the Artifacts blazed with holy light and vanished — only one who reunites all four is worthy to wield them and claim Indorius I's legacy.

### Rising Shadows

A powerful, corrupting presence stalks the empire — where it wanders, death follows, hearts are poisoned, minds turn to wickedness.

Provincial manifestations of the corruption:
- **Abendland**: many-limbed mutants with twisted flesh infest the forests, attacking wayfarers and villages
- **Nordenland**: corrupted flora — trees with human limbs and faces; blood and flesh polyps rain from the sky
- **Rauland**: wolf-headed monsters prowl the hills, depopulating settlements
- **Unterland**: corrupted warriors with armor melted into flesh, weapons fused to limbs, hunting indiscriminately

Its sigil — a deer skull crowned with many antlers — appears scrawled on church doors and in basements. Described as a behemoth wreathed in shadows, a rotting corpse whose bony crown scrapes clouds. Its trumpeting shriek transforms people into monsters.

It has no name but **"the Great Beast."**

## Provinces

Four provinces: Abendland (heart), Nordenland (mind), Rauland (industry), Unterland (sword).

Each began as its own kingdom. Abendland's fertile woods made it wealthy through trade. Nordenland was defined by magic and philosophy. Rauland resisted conquest through technology and fortification. Unterland kept a long military tradition from its warring tribal roots.

At the empire's height, provinces worked in harmony. Since Gods' End, cultural divides have deepened; mutual prosperity has given way to fear and self-interest.

### Abendland

Aurelia's province — birthplace of the Hearthkeepers and (by scripture) Emperor Indorius I. Aurelia instructed her first priestess Amara to strike the ground with a spear, producing the wellspring that became Lake Osea. Rich in farmland and timber, but religion has always been Abendland's most valuable commodity.

**Current crisis**: Emperor Aratron II was lost at sea. The pregnant Empress Valeria was sheltered by the Hearthkeepers. After she gave birth, they quickly coronated the infant and shielded him from outside influence.

The citizenry cries for governance. Faith in the Hearthkeepers has waned with divine magic's absence. In response, the nobility established the **Säullan Council** — self-appointed leaders from each province who negotiate trade and resources. As Emperor Odalric I matured, the Council superseded the Hearthkeepers as his trusted advisors.

**Key tension**: Pope Murrich has grown weary and ineffectual. Odalric I distrusts the church and permanently installed the Säullan Council as advisory body. The Hearthkeepers quietly search for Murrich's successor.

**Bishop Karlos Reinach** — a rising figure whose prophetic sermons have captivated crowds and drawn the Emperor's attention. He preaches that Gods' End was the first omen of apocalypse; the Great Beast is the second. The Hearthkeepers officially brand mention of the Great Beast as heresy, making Reinach controversial within the church. Rumors link him to the Celestial Gate doomsday cult. His history before the Hearthkeepers is mysterious.

#### Notable Individuals

**Emperor Odalric I** — coronated practically in the womb. Raised between Empress Valeria and Pope Murrich. Once introduced to the Säullan Council, he became fascinated with the outside world. Studies economics (Lady Büchandt), strategy (Lord General Roemer), production (Master Lorand), and philosophy (Professor Welgirson). Uncertain whether he can distinguish honest guidance from political coercion.

**Gatekeeper Chantal Sommerkin** — supposedly dead; the Hearthkeepers had her assassinated. She led the **Celestial Gate**, a doomsday cult preaching the Great Beast as divine retribution. Her writings still circulate. Disciples infiltrate every tier of society, aiming to turn Abendland into a theocracy. Their shift from violent overthrow to subtle influence suggests Sommerkin may still be alive.

### Nordenland

Ancient, pre-recorded-history cultures rich in magic. The Norden weave arcane practices with spiritual communing and fear magic less than other Bürach citizens. Western Norden hew to imperial traditions; further north and east, they resemble Valikan neighbors.

When Indorius I was crowned, Ulmyr became part of the united pantheon and Nordenland joined the empire. Eastern settlements resisted, resenting Raulish logging, Hearthkeeper proselytizing, and Unterland soldiers enforcing laws. Their anger never waned.

**Post-Gods' End**: The Norden abandoned imperial oaths and reverted to old traditions — communing with primal spirits, refusing to fell trees without beseeching them. Some villages sacrifice to the Primordials; others whisper to the fey folk.

**Ulmyr's Gate** — a lost city. Arcanists performed a ritual to part the veil between Etharis and the gods; the entire city was ripped into the Ethereal Plane. This became the cautionary tale that led to heavy sanctions on magic.

**Erlefurt** — regional capital, began as a mage college. Graduates are considered among the most meticulous and powerful mages in Etharis. Maintains cautious approach to magic's dangers.

**Order of the Golden Bough** — druids practicing old ways in east Nordenland's wilderness. Viewed with contempt by the empire. They defend boundaries with primal wards, but these protections are failing as nature fouls in the Great Beast's presence.

All Nordenland's great minds are bent toward the Beast's destruction, offering riches and magical secrets to anyone who can recover the Sacred Artifacts.

**Mt. Vengeance** spreads ash across the empire. Horrors spew from the mountain — more than fire and smoke. Erlefurt adepts study it from its slopes but need adventurers daring enough to go within.

#### Notable Individuals

**Archduchess Maelfa Erlekind** — last heir to Erlefurt's founding house. Assertive leader who has never bowed to an emperor, maintaining autonomy through diplomacy. The empire accepts her rule because trusted mages are too valuable to risk alienating. Rumored to have support from the **Thaumaturge** — a secretive cabal said to control global politics from the shadows.

**Alfric, the "Red Hart"** — Archdruid of the Golden Bough. Patrols Nordenland's borders disguised as a giant red stag. Scarred by Imperial assassins, he treats all outsiders with suspicion. Despite this, he secretly trades knowledge with trusted Erlefurt mages to fight the Great Beast.

**Professor Franciszka Welgirson** — archmage, planar expert, court mage and tutor to Odalric I. Säullan Council member. She was present during Ulmyr's Gate's destruction. Despised at court as the "Wraith of Welgirson Hall." Courtiers wonder what eldritch schemes she whispers to the impressionable emperor.

### Rauland

Province of Galt — dwarven and gnomish fiefdoms in the southernmost central Etharis, spanning the Rock-Teeth Mountains to the Grey Spine. Culture built on the nobility of labor and self-reliance.

Built the mountain cities of **Stehlenwald** and **Grabenstein**. Stehlenwald endured centuries of siege before yielding sovereignty in exchange for open trade and provincial self-governance. The empire came to rely on Raulish craftsmanship — smithing, architecture, construction. Forge fires burned ceaselessly.

**During Gods' End**: When Galt's madness struck, Stehlenwald imposed strict curfew. Artisans were worked ceaselessly, factories opened, new inventions discovered — all to outpace other provinces in the god's name. Rauland emerged as a husk: exhausted people, spent resources.

**Post-Gods' End**: Still the empire's industrial epicenter but reliant on Unterland metals and Nordenland timber. Quality has diminished.

**The Great Beast's impact**: Monsters emerge from mine tunnels. Horrors of flesh and stone attack settlements. In Stehlenwald, feverish artisans build golems from muscles and sutures — the Beast's influence poisons their minds. Every fortification has been reinforced, but what use are walls when a creature's presence transforms commoners into monsters?

#### Notable Individuals

**Archduke Ulrich Eizenherz** — dwarven lord of Rauland. Once fair-minded and loved; now reclusive, paranoid, terrified of the dark. Rumored to see the Great Beast in his dreams. **Secret**: longstanding rumor that he holds a Sacred Artifact in the Quartz Palace vaults. Perhaps his connection to it allows the Beast to touch his mind.

**Master Izsak Lorand** — Säullan Council member. Gnomish tinkerer disinterested in politics; advocates for craftsmen's resource needs but otherwise sketches designs during discussions. Highly favored by Odalric I for his inventions and philosophy: "To be brave is to be scared but kind. To be fearless is to be foolish and dead."

### Unterland

Descended from warring marsh tribes who worshipped Maligant, the war god. The Unterfolk rose as the strongest through cunning warfare — surgical precision over brute force.

**Key victories**: destroyed the Vorkan tribe by sabotaging a dam (winning without losing soldiers, though many innocents perished). Trapped Falaris invaders in marshland ambush. Captured Grabenstein using siege engines designed by Maligant's priests.

With Grabenstein's forges, the most formidable military in Etharis rose from Unterland — the sword of the empire.

**Post-Gods' End**: Soldiers' loyalties split between nation and god. Thousands died in pointless battles. Legions scattered, soldiers left on distant warfronts.

**Olstenburg** — regional capital, home to the **Academy of Swords** (dating to Maligant's earliest teachings). Every able-bodied youth serves two years of training. Produces pikemen, swordsmen, archers, heavy cavalry, scouts, and rangers. Monster hunter guilds have also emerged — old army strategies fail against swarming monstrosities.

The garrison at **Grabenstein** fears Ostoya may reach across the Grey Spine.

#### Notable Individuals

**Lord General Vassily Roemer** — beloved strategist and diplomat, Säullan Council member. Unquestioned dedication to the empire, but forced to divert troops everywhere with never enough resources. Relies on mercenaries. **Secret tension**: Unterland's military leadership worries he's too distracted by Altenheim politics to see the rising threat of the Tormach cult in his own province.

**Lady Yule Büchandt** — Academy-trained, widely traveled master of commerce. Puts trust in coin over loyalty or faith. Rivals say she accepts bribes. Vital to the empire's function — ensures councillors have financial resources to hold the nation together.

**Captain Damon Andval** — leads the cult of **Tormach**, Arch Daemon of war (Maligant's dark lieutenant, representing pure violence over Empyreus's honor). Believes Empyreus has failed as deity. Builds support among soldiers, preaching that Tormach's strength can cleanse Ostoya, subdue Valikans, and destroy the Great Beast. Many fear he'll overthrow Lord General Roemer.

### Other Locations of Interest

#### Walstein

Razed during Gods' End by Norden mages in Ulmyr's name. Now a giant cemetery of fire-blackened ruins, haunted by monstrous horrors. The event left a permanent cultural wound between Abendland and Nordenland, and helps justify strict magic regulation.

**Current state**: Monsters make it a gruesome but lucrative hunting ground. Mercenary guilds use old buildings as bastions. Vagrants and criminals camp inside; a makeshift market trades in rumors, equipment, and plundered treasures. Imperial soldiers garrison there for training. Erlefurt mages are occasionally seen searching the ruins — their interest known only to themselves.

Terrible rumor: Walstein's destruction may have been the Great Beast's first visitation.

#### Gräffendam

A towering dam of obsidian-black granite spanning a plunging valley. Its reservoir water remains just above freezing, can only be altered by arcane means, and enhances crops and potion potency. Bottled as **Aquanos**, it has made the surrounding aristocrats enormously wealthy.

**Gildreath** (atop the dam) — a wintery wonderland of wealth, evergreen trees, and perfect gardens. The privileged live in excess.

**Ünterdam / "The Shade"** (below) — frigid hive of crime and poverty. Stacked buildings climb the dam wall like barnacles. Citizens receive a pittance despite handling all Aquanos transport.

**Secret**: The **Ebon Syndicate** controls Aquanos shipping, receiving a cut of revenue and unchecked authority over The Shade in exchange for helping Gildreath avoid Imperial taxes and quashing unrest.

#### Castle Martorius

The wizard **Martorius** is brilliant, eccentric, and cruel. His castle appeared over Rauland after Gods' End — glittering spires of indeterminable dimensions, rooms rearranging like puzzle pieces, patrolled by alien automatons, with illusionary servants and a menagerie of bizarre creatures.

Both Hearthkeepers and Säullan Council accommodate him; in return he offers counsel. No one believes he's benevolent. Some wonder if he's even of this world. His agents search across the empire for the Sacred Artifacts.

## Quest Hooks

### Aberrations

An urchin in an isolated village sells a strange creature's eye — it brings horrible nightmares but tremendous luck. At evening, the village is assailed by aberrant beasts: two-headed wolves, grotesque giant rats, a bear with bones growing through its skin.

The eye inflicts visions of possible futures on its possessor. Bestial abominations follow it wherever it travels, attacking each night.

**Key questions**: Could this be Ulmyr's Eye — a Sacred Artifact? Where to seek safety — the Hearthkeepers, Odalric I, Erlefurt's colleges, or Martorius? Discovery of a Sacred Artifact would shift the empire's power balance as all factions seek to control it.

### Usurper of Olstenburg

A warlord calling himself **Indorius VII** claims to wield Dämglesvir. Soldiers defect to his growing legion near Olstenburg. **Captain Andval** has declared for him, mustering Tormach-devoted warriors — though his true motive may be using any pretense to sow division and recruit for the cult.

**Faction responses**:
- Lord General Roemer marches from Altenheim to bolster Olstenburg's garrison
- Professor Welgirson supports diplomacy — wants to verify if the weapon is genuine
- Lady Büchandt hires spies and assassins to end the rebellion with one death
- Emperor Odalric I sequestered by Hearthkeepers for protection
- Nordenland and Rauland's intentions unknown — killing the current emperor could curry favor with a usurper

Whether Indorius VII has a true claim, believes he wields a god's weapon, or is an opportunist with daemonic backing — nothing is certain. The empire teeters on the edge.

### The Sacred Grove

Deep in Nordenland's woods, villagers leave human sacrifices at a sacred grove tended by a **puck** (fey creature). In return, fruit regrows, animals remain plentiful, and felled trees regenerate within days. Not all sacrifices are willing — some are abducted from neighboring settlements, generating missing person reports.

**Twist**: Killing the puck reveals the sacrifices' fate — their bodies have been transformed into plantlike horrors, bark-skinned with horrified faces visible in the foliage. The village's community does not survive without their fey patron.

### Field Experience

**Vulf Avorrs** — Erlefurt student hiring an escort for pilgrimage to Walstein. Fascinated by outer planes and the ancient Aether Kindred. He probes ever deeper toward Walstein's center with unflagging determination.

**Secret**: Part of Vulf's magic comes from an otherworldly patron whose voice drives him toward some secret in the ruins. It could be a Sacred Artifact, an arcane object, or Vulf's imprisoned patron — a Daemon calling out to compel young warlocks to release it. His loyalties may not survive the patron's promises.

### The Heart of Vengeance

Mt. Vengeance thrums with arcane energy. Winged gargoyle-like creatures circle the crater. Those near the summit report a hideous voice chanting from within the mountain's throat.

**Professor Illiana** hires adventurers to delve inside. **Lord Hansjörg** offers supplies. Inside: a labyrinthine dungeon of heat-filled tunnels and sulfurous chambers branching from the central volcanic shaft. Lava cascades from wall cracks; tunnels collapse during eruptions. Elemental monsters lurk within. The deeper one descends, the clearer the voice.

**Possible discoveries**: A portal to Primordial planes, an Arch Daemon forging a fiend army, or a prison built by the gods for an Aether Kindred — reawakened after the gods' destruction.

### Mark of the Beast

The Great Beast's sigil — deer skull with many antlers — appears in missing people's journals, basement walls, and scarred into disgraced nobles' skin. Each sighting marks **Celestial Gate** presence.

The Hearthkeepers deny the Beast's existence, blaming troubles on the cult. Pope Murrich hires hidden agents to investigate — a quest spanning the empire, following leads that could implicate powerful figures in every province.

The cult attempts to assassinate investigators. Rumor: the dead prophet **Sommerkin** has been raised as a monster — the immortal voice of the Great Beast. Only her permanent destruction can halt the cult's ideology.

## The City of Stehlenwald

Ash-covered and smoke-shrouded. Thick clouds from ever-lit furnaces block sunlight; falling soot covers quality cobblestone streets. The heat vented through granite and marble walls makes it cozy in winter, stifling in summer. Clockwork creations roam the roads — iron horses pull wagons, gas lanterns glow through thin pipes, lightning arcs through glass tubes. Old and majestic despite its industrial decay.

### Geography

Stehlenwald is carved into the mountain in four tiers, like a staircase toward the sky made of metal, granite, quartz, and clockwork. Really two cities: the overcity (open to air) and the undercity (tunnel network below). The overcity feels labyrinthine even aboveground — high walls, close stonework, claustrophobic to non-dwarves.

#### The Four Tiers

**Gate Tier (First)** — markets, businesses, shops, guard stations. The only tier most visitors see. Clockwork cleaners scrub ash and soot after markets close.

**Artisan Tier (Second)** — workshops, smithies, gnome inventor studios. Walled off to contain dangerous work. Constant sound of hammers, chisels, and occasional industrial accidents.

**Home Tier (Third)** — residential area with domestic businesses. City Guard stationed here for easy access to all tiers.

**Lord's Tier (Fourth)** — administrative district. The Quartz Palace, guildhalls, military headquarters. Streets lit by windows and lightning-filled glass tubes. Guards patrol with weapons displayed; some officers carry blackpowder firearms.

#### Waterworks

Steam pipes radiate constant heat year-round. Snowmelt feeds aqueducts; underground, water powers steam-driven factories and home heating. Wood-burning banned by Imperial fine — the city uses coal and gas. Experiments with harnessed lightning promise a revolutionary power source.

#### Lightningworks

Glass tubes in buildings carry racing arcs of blue lightning, speed increasing toward midday. At exactly noon each day, an explosion echoes from the **Den of Disasters** (second tier) — then the lightning stops. This powers many clockwork machines and experiments.

Led by **Professor Leguntor**, a lightning manifestation expert. Side effects: rampant static electricity each afternoon, blue hazes around objects and people, momentary sky darkening and strange smell before each daily explosion.

### History

Built atop ancient dwarven mines. Gnomes joined the dwarves; together they constructed walls against surface threats. The overcity was carved from mountain ridges while old tunnels became the undercity's first roads.

**Era of Expansion**: Humans attacked but were repelled for decades — dwarven stubbornness and gnomish craftiness held firm. Discovery of adamantine and blackpowder made Stehlenwald untouchable by torch or blade. Eventually hunger forced the city to negotiate — joint rulership was agreed, and Stehlenwald joined the Bürach Empire.

The Great Beast's emergence brought mutated creatures to both surface and undercity tunnels.

### Notable Locations

#### Gateside Plateau

Farms and hamlets outside the main walls providing sustenance. No noteworthy single location. Unemployed and those driven mad by the city's workings are deposited outside walls — their shanties lean against the city wall for support. Further from walls, farms grow wealthier.

#### Gate Tier Details

**Hübsches Gelding** (Shopping District) — warmly lit streets of storefronts: tailors, clockmakers, jewelers, toolsmiths. Gas lanterns and gear-driven moving signs.

**Brass Abbey** (Materials District) — warehouses and storage. Perfect for illegal backroom deals; bulk material exchanges. Mercenary guards for shipments.

**Stehlenwald Stalls** — open-air market, the Gate Tier's heart. Citizens and permitted visitors sell everything from bread to questionable arcane mechanical objects.

#### Underways and Undercity

Old tunnels filled with ductwork, cables, and arcane trappings. A maze showing stories of failed sieges. Nearly impossible to navigate without experience. Workers secure and enhance water and energy supply. Illuminated by glass tubes of glowing green bioluminescent chemicals (not torches — the air is thin and gas runs through the tunnels). Soldiers deny entry to non-workers.

A smaller underground city serves those who prefer subterranean living. Many dwarves proudly inhabit it, looking with derision on surface-dwellers.

### Important Individuals

#### Lord Reggard Laurendor

Head of the ancient dwarven Clan Laurendor; administrator of Stehlenwald. Carries himself with grace and homespun wit.

**Goals**: Marry his eldest daughter **Janka** to the Archduke's son **Adalbronz** — positioning her to eventually join the Säullan Council. Convinced Stehlenwald's strength will collapse before the empire falls; wants Janka to undermine the empire's relentless consumption of city resources from within.

**Secret**: He and Archduke Ulrich were once friends, but Reggard now quietly waits for the Archduke's death (a mercy, given Ulrich's paranoia). Seeks to verify whether a Sacred Artifact is locked beneath the Quartz Palace. His obsession with long-term ambitions has left Stehlenwald's daily administration to subordinates.

#### Professor Alarica Deiderich

Specializes in lightning manifestation. Slight, stooped, standoffish. Architect of the Lightning Speed Chamber. Assistants go missing with alarming regularity.

**Secret**: Stehlenwald's only resident **dhampir** — hides features behind tinted goggles and covered skin. Devours her assistants, then uses their remains to create **the arisen** — composite corpses given life through combined magic and science. She fawns over her creations and encourages true students to experiment with this new life.

#### Detlef Hagen (Laboratory Assistant)

Young human who traveled to Stehlenwald to study under Deiderich. Ambitious but impatient — his assembled creations are poorly made. Other assistants fear his constructed horrors. May become another of Deiderich's missing students if he doesn't improve.

#### Lady Gouden Burkta (Head Alchemist)

One of the dreamers found deep in the Rock-Teeth Mountains. Created the **bioluminescent algae lanterns** used in the tunnels — light without magic or traditional energy. Maintains a large algae pool in her private laboratory.

#### Master Nitter Soyar (Craftsman)

Gnome in charge of blackpowder production. Works with the **Ebon Syndicate** to smuggle weapons in and out of the city. Heads the **Scrap Guild** — orphaned children who collect discarded materials throughout the city. Loves machines and tends many of Stehlenwald's clockwork creations.

#### Dr. Veirgeld Reisenheim (Surgeon)

Dwarven surgeon responsible for Stehlenwald's medical needs. Accepted the post reluctantly in exchange for funding his true passion: **prosthetics**. More fascinated with limb size and functionality than actual symptoms. Jokes circulate about patients waking to find him sawing off a particularly fetching limb as a model. The humor masks a genuine risk — he's one interesting case away from crossing the line between medical curiosity and butchery.

### Quest Hooks

#### The Diligence of Believers

**Lady Burkta** needs help urgently — a group is digging an unauthorized, structurally unmapped tunnel. With blackpowder easily obtained on the black market, an explosion could trigger devastating rockslides inside Stehlenwald.

The diggers are mostly newly created **arisen**, led by **Zayga** of **Canary's Lament**. Inside the tunnel: a massive furnace with vats of bubbling blood and writhing muscle, arisen grafted to the machine. It resembles a giant beating heart.

**The cult's goal**: They see humanity as canaries in a coal mine. They seek to build a new god — the **Dread Machine** — in the mortal realm. It speaks construction secrets into Zayga's dreams. Powered by arisen life force, it's too large to move through the tunnels.

**If fought**: Each cultist death makes the Dread Machine pulse faster, its furnace turning blue. After all are slain, the heart begins beating on its own.

**If left alone**: The cult grows in power across Stehlenwald; people are hungry for something to believe in.

Other sects may exist across Etharis — in Morencia, Malkovia, or elsewhere — building other dreadful constructs.

#### A Student's Greatest Failure

**Detlef Hagen** has lost track of a dangerous escaped arisen — large, muscular, poorly built with mismatched bloodshot eyes. Despite grotesque appearance, it's clearly intelligent: speaks formally, reads books, learns rapidly.

**The arisen's perspective**: Recounts the pain of waking with lightning, striking out at his creator in confusion. Has an intelligent child's mind — no understanding of who or what he is, but capacity for rapid learning alongside roiling rage and lack of empathy.

**Choices**:
- If the arisen is killed: Detlef pays and may introduce the party to Professor Deiderich
- If the arisen lives: Detlef is furious, refuses reward. The arisen wants Detlef killed and will ally with anyone who helps seek revenge for the crime of his creation
