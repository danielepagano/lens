# Chapter 13: Divinity in Etharis

## Divine Magic in Etharis

Divine magic exists but is diminished. The Gods' End catastrophically reduced the population of divine casters; truly talented clerics and paladins are now extremely rare, treated as living prophets — celebrated or feared. Common priests lack real power. Healing is not available at temples; resurrection is nearly impossible. Apothecaries, surgeons, and herbalists have replaced the clergy for medical needs.

Some theologians argue divine and arcane magic are the same force drawn from different sources. The Hearthkeepers call this heresy. What is clear: divine magic still flickers in Etharis, and those who wield it matter.

## The Aetheric War and Gods' End

**The Aetheric War.** The gods were not alone in the cosmos. The Aether Kindred — incomprehensible beings of pure chaos predating the gods — gnawed at the edges of creation. Fearing the Kindred would eventually devour everything, the gods launched a preemptive war. It was catastrophic. Most of the pantheon was destroyed or consumed. Only four young gods survived, having held back from the battle: **Galt the Builder**, **Maligant Warclad**, **Ulmyr the Wise**, and **Aurelia of the Hearth**.

**The Gods' End.** The four surviving gods chose to build the Bürach Empire as a model civilization, granting its first emperor Indorius I four holy artifacts to rule wisely. Over generations, the Bürach Church's Edict of Eternal Blood mandated inbreeding to preserve the imperial bloodline — weakening it. The final result was the Mad Emperor **Leopold I**. When Leopold touched the divine artifacts, his fractured mind fused with all four surviving gods simultaneously, driving them to madness. The four gods then turned on each other and destroyed one another. Etharis has been godless ever since.

## Arch Seraphs and Arch Daemons

In the gods' absence, their former celestial servants — Arch Seraphs and Arch Daemons — have filled the vacuum. They are not gods: they can only embody narrow aspects of their forebears' domains, which makes their edicts extreme and unyielding. Whether a being is called Seraph or Daemon is culturally dependent — the same entity may be venerated in one region and despised in another.

### Arch Seraphs

**Empyreus** — Arch Seraph of Valor. Patron of righteous warriors and the Castinellan Inquisition. Drives followers toward zealotry — punishing heretics, mages, and anything perceived as corrupt. His righteous brutality unwittingly serves the Arch Daemon Beleth. Depicted as a knight in heavy plate with blade-wings.

**Zabriel** — Arch Seraph of Truth. Inherited the domain of knowledge; values truth above all, hates lies and ignorance. Her priests run churches of learning. Her inquisitors hunt criminals with clinical precision, never considering context. Depicted as a figure with a glowing, featureless face carrying a tome of every name in existence.

**Aphaeleon** — Arch Seraph of Temperance. Patron of the strong-minded and spiritual; nurtures those seeking peace and enlightenment. Opposes Sitri, the Arch Daemon of hedonism. Austere monks and peaceful healers follow him; his followers also offer absolution to the genuinely repentant.

**Solyma** — Arch Seraph of Justice (also called Lady Vengeance). Has sworn silence until all cosmic evil is defeated. Patron of the downtrodden, monster hunters, and the Order of Dawn in Ostoya. Her silence offers no guidance, leaving followers prone to extremism and vigilante justice.

**Morael** — Arch Seraph of Sacrifice. Patron of martyrs; teaches that sacrifice is the truest virtue. His most ardent followers (the Flagellants) have become terrifying — burning taverns, dragging nobles from homes, interpreting suffering as spiritual purity.

**Miklas** — Arch Seraph of Mercy. Former servant of Aurelia. Cannot fill Aurelia's full domain; offers only aid to those already suffering. Worshipped by doctors and apothecaries. Her followers tend toward nonviolence but can become zealously paternalistic — holding the "cursed" against their will.

### Arch Daemons

**Beleth** — Arch Daemon of Fear. The Trickster; finds mortals hilarious. Delights in watching people create what they fear most — Empyreus's zealots become torturers, witch hunters see mages everywhere. Worshipped by assassins and bards who wield fear as a weapon.

**Venin** — Arch Daemon of Deceit. Mistress of lies, illusions, and insanity. Rewards those who assassinate Zabriel's priests. Her followers — assassin guilds, spy networks, information brokers — eventually lose track of what is real.

**Sitri** — Arch Daemon of Hedonism. The Tempter; whispers desire into mortal hearts until want becomes craving becomes all-consuming addiction. Glorified by Ostoya's vampires. Cultists believe they're attending a party; they're being harvested.

**Malikir** — Arch Daemon of Pride. The most powerful Arch Daemon; prefers to be called "God." Seduces ambitious leaders, knights, and rulers — they begin honorably, become tyrants. All who rise in Malikir's favor come to a terrible end.

**Tormach** — Arch Daemon of Wrath. Patron of pure carnage; strategy and honor are cowardice in his view. Brother to Empyreus. Growing cult in Unterland's armies. His truly devout become berserkers.

**Gorodyn** — Arch Daemon of Avarice. The King of Red Coins; teaches that greed is survival and virtue. Worshipped at every level of society from pauper to noble. Concentrated in Morencia's markets and the opulent cities of Altenheim and Gräffendam.

## The Aether Kindred

The Aether Kindred are incomprehensible beings of pure chaos that predate the gods. After the Aetheric War, they appeared to sleep — but their presence has returned. Abominations spawn from their dreams. Warlocks and some sorcerers may unknowingly draw power from them.

**Vraigoroth** — Kindred of Devouring. An enormous consuming void or writhing mass of flesh that devours existence and incorporates all it consumes. Its worshippers believe all existence was once part of it and will be again; the devouring is inevitable. Polyps of Vraigoroth can bubble into the mortal realm through holes chewed in reality.

**M'rorcameleth** — Kindred of Dreams. Slithers into mortal minds through sleep; torments dreamers with nightmarish visions of inevitable futures. Nobody who dreams of it wakes the same. Oneiromancers who successfully contact it may manifest psionic powers — or become puppets.

**Pharazorthok** — Kindred of Knowing. A vast tentacled tree with countless eyes that supposedly holds all of reality in place. Its followers claim it sees and knows everything. Survivors of its knowledge-visions become sorcerers and cult leaders teaching fragments of cosmic truth.

## Primal Magic in Etharis

Primal magic draws from nature's power — elemental forces and living things outside the purview of gods or mortals. Two cultures practice it: the elves of Tol Leyemil (who commune with Spirits of Nature) and the Valikan Clans (who draw from the Primordials of the Elemental Planes).

### The Primordic Cycle

In the beginning, the Great Prismatic Wyrm **Gormadraug** was the first living thing — consuming all elements until his body encircled the world. The four Primordials (Morakesh, Alondo, Citrolach, Ilhara) emerged from or were born to him, all suffering under his tyranny. The hero Kentigern and six companions, aided by all four Primordials, slew Gormadraug — whose body became the mortal realm (flesh into earth, spine into mountains, blood into frozen seas). The Primordials believe Gormadraug merely slumbers. The return of coldfire suggests his resurrection approaches.

### Primordials

**Lady Morakesh** — Primordial of Fire; rules Flamebourne. Capricious and easy to anger; rewards lavishly or incinerates without warning. Commands azers, efreeti, and fire giants. Druidic blood-and-fire cults burn sentient sacrifices in her name.

**Alondo** — Primordial of Ice and Water; rules the Endless Sea. Wishes to be left alone; has no love for mortals. The most conscientious Primordial, actively working to prevent Gormadraug's return — though rumors tie him to the coldfire outbreak.

**Citrolach** — Primordial of Earth and Stone; rules Stonereach. Progenitor of stoneborn trolls and earth elementals. Worshipped historically by dwarves and gnomes. Legendarily stubborn — "easier to move a mountain."

**Ilhara** — Primordial of Air and Shadow. The most ambitious Primordial; her spies lurk in shadows and candle smoke. Seeks to fill Gormadraug's vacant throne. Important to Clan Morgöng spies. Requires a whispered dark secret to summon.

**Gormadraug** — The Great Prismatic Wyrm, Primordial of All Elements. Slumbering, not dead. Worship forbidden throughout Valika. The Primordials actively smite any druid who tries to invoke him, fearing even drawing on his power might wake him.

### The Faerie Courts

Before the Era of Expansion, the Faerie Courts were worshipped as deities in Charneault. The elves believe they are archfey — possibly seraphs cast down in the Aetheric War who cultivated a second paradise in the Realm of Faerie. In modern Charneault, the Courts are revered alongside (and sometimes instead of) the Arch Seraphs. Many humans view the fey as capricious devils rather than divine beings, even while training mages and making pacts with them for primal power.
