# Chapter 7: The City of Liesech

Liesech is a poisoned apple many powerful hands have tried to grip. The city-state festers inside the Bite Bay like a rotted tooth just north of the Charneault Kingdom. On a clear day, the peaks of the Rock-Teeth Mountains are seen in the distant east. But there are few clear days in Liesech.

Travelers that find a stop in Liesech necessary are cautious of the creeping green haze in the air, the disturbingly serene looks on the peoples’ faces, and the dreadful clamor of The High Belfry’s bells that abruptly captivate Liesech’s citizens, before they return to their day. Visitors don’t stay long enough to learn more.

Liesech is the ‘seed’ of The Weeping Pox, the deathly disfiguring and mentally dismantling plague. Those that brave a prolonged stay here soon learn that behind every friendly smile is abject terror.

## Geography

White Horn Analita von Raiza A green haze perpetually hovers over Liesech’s skies, ostensibly blamed on the vapors coming off the waters. It does not hinder the day’s offering of warming light nor prevent the moonlight adding to a hauntingly beautiful, emerald glow that blankets the city. On the clearest nights, it is visible for miles around. Liesech is primarily reached by boat. The Bite Bay’s open waters and deep-sea ridge allow vessels to anchor closer to the shore. Transiting ships can skirt the coastlines in inclement weather, but even so, ship captains must be vigilant. Surrounding waters harness turbulent currents that have been known to throw vessels off course into sea stacks, exciting sea monsters rumored to dwell in nearby waters. This area is called the “Eye of the Sea.” It has dark, historical significance in Liesech’s story. Liesech’s plentiful channels and ditches keep the wild tide swells at bay. Landforms corral coastal winds, keeping temperatures in the region mild and warm most of the time. Waning sea storms sprout light rains during warmer times, barely enough to aid in agriculture. Outsiders opine that Liesech’s sparse rainfall is the result of the haze that hangs over the bay city. Locals shrug their shoulders when asked.

During the wintry season, snowfall is dry and winds are merciful. Liesechers watch for regional predators, who fearlessly wander from the forest, mountains, or waters into the city in search of food to keep them during hibernation.


## History

Liesechers trace their origins to the story of Otto Bouwengracht, whose family was influential when the city was just a large fishing village. Each family eked out a living with their meager catches, never quite managing enough to live comfortably. Bouwengracht was ambitious and daring. He decided to fish out beyond safe waters, where he hoped to net larger catches. He did indeed net his biggest catch in the waters of the Eye of the Sea.

A powerful creature called The Filth Grazer sensed the ambition of the fisher and invaded his thoughts in a soothing, feminine voice. She offered Otto a bargain—she would provide a font of knowledge beyond the minds of Etharis’s greatest scholars and the counsel to use it to turn Liesech into a dominant, thriving city, with wealth beyond his dreams.

All the Filth Grazer desired in return was to feed upon the city’s waste, including their dead. Otto was required to also find other devotees to fulfill the Filth Grazer’s charge, under guise of ceremonial sea offerings. Bouwengracht agreed.

Otto Bouwengracht became a wealthy fisher and merchant. His family, and the families of others who trusted him, rose to prominence. Liesech prospered under his leadership. Bouwengracht used his acquired genius to develop canal and irrigation channels that would allow for easy transport of supplies and waste from the city, while also keeping the ‘unexplained’ damaging tides ebbing from the Eye of the Sea.

Furthermore, Otto Bouwengracht helped to construct a remarkable fleet of sailing ships. He sent his fleet to establish trading routes across the Emerald Sea and further. Liesech continued to grow, and with the revered Bouwengracht family as its voice, Liesech pronounced itself a free city.

The Bürach Empire and the Charneault Kingdom benefited from Liesech’s ports. As a midway point for trade ships traveling between regions, this provided crews with needed respite and Liesech’s merchants opportunities to acquire goods from afar. However, Liesech’s tradeway taxes grew beyond the Bürach Empire’s ability to tolerate.

Without fear of retaliatory response by the Liesech-allied Charneault Kingdoms, the empire boldly planned to bring the Bouwengracht family back under Bürach rule.

### Annexation by the Bürach Empire

The Bürach Empire discussed a resolution to Liesech’s autonomy. They could ill afford other regions demanding independence. Ultimately, they decided to punish Liesech by unleashing a monster.

Analita von Raiza’s chained coffin was discovered beneath the soil of Rauland. Starved by centuries of bloodless imprisonment, the Bürach Empire believed von Raiza could be wielded as a pawn. She was freed from her imprisonment and sent to Liesech as an emissary of the Empire, where she was to use her vampiric gifts to compel the Bouwengracht Family to agree to the Bürach Empire’s terms of annexation.

With the staging of an agent in Liesech, the empire planned to slowly influence mercantile matters to their advantage and disburse the Bouwengracht’s wealth as they desired. This plan promised discretion without destabilizing Liesech’s mercantile industries.

Alas, the entire enterprise relied on trust in a vampire’s word. The Bürachers believed von Raiza was a trained monster they could manipulate through her need for blood. They did not realize her ambitions matched their own

### The Red Mourning

Upon arrival in Liesech, von Raiza immediately sensed that a strange pall covered the city. As she began her investigation of the city and its power structure, she noticed the dark influence within Liesech was especially strong around the Bouwengracht manse.

Her vampiric hunger and her intellectual curiosity were piqued. Through her natural charm, von Raiza’s meeting with the family was soon arranged; their domination came easy. She learned much about Liesech’s founding family, all of the intricacies among Liesech’s holdings, and most importantly, the true source of the Bouwengracht’s rise to power: the bargain with the Filth Grazer and the ceremony that keeps the creature at bay.

After completing her mission, von Raiza enjoyed her newfound luxury. As the Büracher leaders foresaw, her basic vampiric nature returned. While her hunger was sated by Liesecher blood, her hunger for power grew. As the Bürach Empire embraced the puppet Bouwengracht’s support of annexation and tariff reduction, their imperious demands continued. Countess von Raiza grew tired of being their puppet. With the knowledge she now had, the vampire acted.

On the morning of the next annual Ceremony of the Nurturing Sea, the Bouwengracht family, along with other noble families, were found brutally killed—the blood entirely drained from their bodies. While word of these deaths shocked many, von Raiza was not fazed. Shortly after these deaths, the vampire felt Liesech’s magic wane. The Filth Grazer responded. On the eve of the day known as “the Red Mourning,” von Raiza flew to the Eye of the Sea to deal with the jilted otherworldly force.

### Battle with The Filth Grazer

The deaths of the Bouwengracht family and their supplicants severed the contract with The Filth Grazer, abruptly ending its long-satiating feeding. The Filth Grazer’s vengeance was immediate. On the evening of the Red Mourning, a long, deep-droning wailing rippled from the Eye of the Sea. After the rage-filled crying faded, destructive swells of flotsam and waste rolled into Liesech’s shores; toppling buildings, drowning many, and delivering The Filth Grazers minions—undulating, many-mouthed and tentacled monsters who consumed any in their path.

Analita von Raiza had to act. While brave Liesechers took up steel against the monsters, the vampire flew out to the Eye of the Sea to destroy the Filth Grazer. But how can you fight a force that transcends corporeal form?

The vampire spilled more blood of the Liesech noble families who worshiped the Filth Grazer through necromantic rituals intended to poison the Filth Grazer and break its hold on the city. In retaliation, the Filth Grazer sent monsters from the deep to slay anything in their path.

Analita responded by sending scores of animated corpses into the ocean, leaving the Eye of the Sea clotted with blood and viscera. If the rumors are true, those very same corpses were sent back into the city bearing the Weeping Pox.

In the end, the seas calmed. The newly crowned Countess von Raiza returned to her home at the High Belfry. The Filth Grazer minions returned to the sea. In the wake of the monsters wrath, the city mourned. Numbing shock and engrossing gratitude consumed survivors. They began to rebuild. Many wondered; who won the battle at the Eye of the Sea?

Countess von Raiza’s and the Filth Grazer had endlessly attacked one another, with no clear victor. Desperate to defeat the monster, the countess had used powerful magic to reach into the Filth Grazer’s mind to dominate its will. Instead of finding anything like a mortal mind, the Countness found the same soothing voice that had spoken to Otto Bouwengracht long ago.

The voice promised a wealth of knowledge, luxury, respect, and servitude—at a very small price: the Filth Grazer’s appetite for Liesech’s waste must be sated, and a future tither would be paid. As show of power, the Filth Grazer devoured Countess von Raiza’s traumatic memories. Among the Liesechers, the creature did the same—supplanting any fearful thoughts of the Filth Grazer with pleasant ones. No longer suspicious, the vampire agreed to the Filth Grazer’s terms and sealed the bargain in her blood, a vampire’s most binding act. She settled down in Liesech to enjoy the fruits of her deal.

### The Weeping Pox

In the days following the conflict, Liesechers mourned the loss of their first families. Countess von Raiza nursed her wounds and observed the processions from afar. During this time, the Filth Grazer’s penance was paid. When the bargain at the Eye of the Sea was sealed, the Filth Grazer’s reality-bending essence shrouded the city again. It placed within each corpse of the honored dead a virus, which quickly spread among funeral attendees and those they contacted. Once the funeral skiffs delivered the Bouwengracht dead to the Filth Grazer, it delivered its revenge. The disease, named the Weeping Pox, was the manifestation of the Filth Grazer’s bargain with Countess von Raiza and its vengeance for the vampire’s disruption of the ages-long alliance. The symptoms and consequences of the disease were like nothing Etharis had never seen. Lucy Lisett Weeping Pox Victim

After first exposure, the victim expressed hyper-vigilance and excitability, followed by bouts of delirium and fever. In its final stages, flesh takes on a bone-white pallor, and two bright red pustules appear on the neck. Right before death, melancholia sweeps over the victim as it weeps all the blood from its body and loosens a final powerful, painful wail.

It wasn’t until Countess von Raiza’s vampiric minions sought out other blood servers did she realize what had happened. The victim’s blood that sated the countess was also infected. The vampire’s nature repelled the Weeping Pox’s mortal effects, but blood nourishment came with a terrible price. The vampire became grotesque and twisted, with boils and pox and sores that never healed. The Weeping Pox was the Filth Grazer’s warning to von Raiza. She dared not dishonor the blood bargain.

As von Raiza suffered her penance, she continued to cultivate her influence. And the Weeping Pox claimed thousands. The countess sent representatives to lead the Nurturing Sea Ceremony processions, ensuring that her bargain to the Filth Grazer was fulfilled. She feigns concern over Liesech’s people—going so far as to request aid abroad to help cure the Weeping Pox.

In time, allied aid arrived, in the form of the Morbus Doctore. Their arrival sparked false hope, furthered by the countess’ façade. The masked countenances of the medically trained doctors are a constant reminder of the death and danger that strike down citizens of Liesech every day.

### The Spread of the Pox

Trade ships bound for the Bürach Empire and beyond brought the Weeping Pox with them. Realizing the contagion’s source, the empire sent a quarantine fleet to block maritime trade routes into Liesech. This move created incited outrage between the empire and other regional leaders, who still desired to do business in Liesech.

A conglomerate of Etharis’s aristocrats and merchants send vessels to dock in Liesech despite the blockade and the Weeping Pox. Missives sent to Countess von Raiza went ignored. The Bürach Empire’s hold in Liesech diminished for fear of the disease and Countess von Raiza’s apparent betrayal of their subterfuge.

The mercantile forces of all nations, in addition to smugglers, pirates, and black-market traders continue to use Liesech as a hub. Indecision among mercantile leaders in the Bürach Empire grows as they seek to learn how other regions benefit from trade in Liesech, where the deadly pox is in a state of perpetual pandemic.

Countess von Raiza, and perhaps the Morbus Doctore, claim that the Weeping Pox is now under control. They speak of new cures. But they have done so for years. The vampire’s ambition benefits Liesech, much like Otto Bouwengracht’s determination did ages ago. The city’s people share in its prosperity, but also its sickness.

### The Liesecher Way

In Otto Bouwengracht’s youth, the fisherfolk were humble and hard-working. Communal survival drove decisions. Business was conducted on barter and trust. Commonplace among them, was a friendly smile and greeting or a blessing to the old sea gods.

The Weeping Pox, Filth Grazer, and Countess von Raiza have changed that. Visitors are still met with pleasant greetings and smiles. There is always, however, something dark behind the pleasant expressions. While speaking, their focus may drift off and their speech may slow. Any mention of the Weeping Pox to locals is met with silent reverence, a head turn toward the bay, and on occasion, a tear. They then return to matters at hand.

Most Liesechers make their way from their homes to their fishing boats and businesses after five morning bell tolls from the High Belfry. After six evening bell tolls, business dealings move from district streets to inside buildings. Those folks out beyond six bells are believed to be under employment of the Gold Barge Company—or one of the foolish that risk becoming an addition to the accepted nightly disappearances.

The Weeping Pox is a part of everyday life in Liesech, and the populace does whatever is necessary to keep up their spirits in the midst of death and misery. Locals call the affliction a necessity. They insist it prevents overpopulation and keeps visitors fearful from staying too long. They note with feigned relief how the disease spares children—although the children still bear its disfiguring marks and spread the disease like anyone else.

Despite many losses. it has not claimed the city’s revered Countess von Raiza, who the locals say miraculously managed trade to break the Bürach Empire’s oppressive blockade. Citizens point to the arrival of the Morbus Doctore, who aid the sick and troubled.

### Culture

#### The Ceremony of the Nurturing Sea

This annual ceremony sees citizens come together to offer their thanks to the old sea gods, who provide their families continued nourishment and prosperity. The ceremony starts with a solemn, white-cowled, and white-robed procession that starts at the High Belfry and ends at Bite Bay’s docks.

After honorific prayer and song, families place their offerings on old boats that are then pushed out into the water. It is believed that the power of the sea currents, faith, and winds guide them to the old gods. Families return home with their devotion renewed. And the Filth Grazer and Countess von Raiza continue to honor their contract.

Something else may be watching this tradition in hiding from watery depths. Perhaps the old sea gods, or a representative of those ancient powers, are not as far gone as some might assume.

#### The Week of the Grand Citywide Market

Proclaimed by some to be the Bouwengracht family’s greatest trademark, this quarterly celebration of prosperity and trade brought ships from all reaches of Etharis. Liesech opens all of Main Town and Dock Town streets for entertainers and vendors to set up booths and stages. During this time in ages past, the High Belfry opened for visiting aristocrats to enjoy Bouwengracht family hospitality while exploring or solidifying business ventures.

Since the death of Liesech’s first families and the spread of the Weeping Pox, this celebration now takes place twice a year, with fewer participants. Visiting vessels undergo inspections by the Gold Barge Company and Morbus Doctore before debarkation. Affluent merchants and aristocratic guests are only invited to the High Belfry Open House after six evening bell tolls, for reasons explained by the countess’s majordomo: “The Countess prefers company at night when she is her most radiant.”

Despite these inconveniences, trade relations grow. The Market Week continues to draw more regional and renown celebrants, much to the ire of officials of the Bürach Empire, who refuse the countess’s invitations. Of recent note, the acclaimed magizoology scholar Professor Terrence Myron von Küfen was spotted setting up a vendor booth when he was surrounded by Morbus Doctore. Witnesses claim that he was escorted to The High Belfry and has not been seen since.


## Districts

### Hauthoff-Liesech (Main Town)

The city has grown from dirt paths, cabin homes, and shore-tethered boats. The population lives among the dark stone and shingle-roofed buildings and cobblestone streets. Each street intersection has a prominent water drain in the event of sea-storm deluges.

A bell post stands at each corner; only rang to alert the Morbus Doctore when someone has contracted the Weeping Pox or has died. Liesech’s sewer and street network connect to one main sewer pipe and road into Dock Town. While most locals travel by foot, the Golden Barge Company offer various modes of transport: bicycle, pony, or stagecoach. Only two structures are gated and walled off from the rest of the city: The High Belfry and Zielenhaus.

### Traughafen (Dock Town)

Liesech’s docks stretch across the Bite Bay’s shoreline, reaching both borders of the Castinellan Provinces and Rauland of the Bürach Empire. The Golden Barge Company sees to the city’s maritime fleet from its boathouses, shipyards, and training grounds.

Docktown’s businesses can provide visitors with a range of goods and services. Inns and taverns accommodate modest lodging needs. Most business arrangements are conducted within Handelzimmer buildings, where sanitized rooms are provided and monitored by the Golden Barge Company. This calms merchants fearing exposure to the Weeping Pox and grants them a respite from the reeking odor of fish scales and entrails that lingers about.

## Important Individuals

### The Bouwengracht Family

The family responsible for Liesech’s prominence is immortalized in history. However, accounts of Otto Bouwengracht’s bargain with the Filth Grazer are nowhere to be found. In Liesecher hearts, the Bouwengrachts and their supporters were the epitome of hard work, humility and kindness. With their mysterious and tragic deaths, the families’ manse and other holdings were assumed by Countess von Raiza.

As their temporary executor, the countess would guard these holdings until such time as any blood relative lay claim to them. The Bouwengracht family has familial ties to Rauland, yet none have stepped forward. Rumors that a Bouwengracht child, Wilhelm, was whisked away to safety by the families’ mother-nurse as the families murder took place.

As the city continues to prosper, open reverence of the Bouwengrachts dwindles. Ironically, Countess von Raiza lauds Otto Bouwengracht’s contributions to Liesech’s success in public conversations.

### Countess Analita Von Raiza

Liesech’s interim leader is one of the oldest known vampires. Nearly impossible to kill, she was captured and imprisoned by the Bürach tribes of centuries past. Confinement to a sarcophagus carved and enchanted by old magic nearly shattered her mind.

Before that end, she was released by powerful Büracher loyalists and pressed into service as an agent of the empire. After fulfilling her mission in the subterfuge and assimilation of the Bouwengracht family, she manages the aftermath of her betrayal of the Bürach Empire.

This placed her face-to-face with the Filth Grazer, in prolonged battle that left her disfigured by the Weeping Pox. In the end, the Bouwengracht influence ended, a blood contract was made, and her rise to power and prominence began. She still nurses her wounds in the High Belfry. Her advisors, the vampiric Cult of the Black Lady, aid her in stately matters and see to her nourishment. This helps the countess improve relations abroad, carry on Liesech’s traditions, and monitor the Weeping Pox and Morbus Doctore.

### The Filth Grazer

The origin of the otherworldly thing that dwells within Liesech’s waters is a great mystery. Those that encounter the Filth Grazer are left with wracking disbelief as to what they sensed. But in the mind of Countess von Raiza, the image is vivid: a miasmic mass of eyes, mouth and tentacles that speaks with a cacophony of soothing, alien-like voices.

The creature, or at least its most-used physical form, feeds on all forms of waste: physical and metaphysical. The Filth Grazer incorporates it into its being, fueling its intellect and persuasive power. Its waste carries a sliver of its sentience with it. This flows through sea water, rises into the air, clings to other life forms. This creates a far-reaching telepathic bond, which the Filth Grazer uses to torment victims as it sees fit.

In vengeance against Countess Analita von Raiza for killing the Bouwengracht’s and other minions, the Filth Grazer excreted a powerful waste sludge among its tidal attacks on Liesech. This sludge’s mark remained. It festered. It was the catalyst for the Weeping Pox. Every pox carrier is a thrall for the creature to sate its hunger, expanding its knowledge of Etharis and spreading its influence.

## Important Factions

### Cult of the Black Lady

In order to execute Liesech’s annexation, Countess von Raiza needed help. She beguiled a group of Liesech’s youth to slowly generate curiosity and mystery among Liesech’s elite. They did their job well enough—within days of the countess’s arrival, she received an invitation to attend a gathering at the High Belfry Manse of the Bouwengracht family. She thanked her help with a special gift—vampirism.

As von Raiza’s spawn and family, they perform overt and covert acts to bolster her popularity and to discreetly remove any opposition. They hunt for people unaffected by the Weeping Pox to help their mistress recover with untainted blood.

The cult has vampiric and non-vampiric members, allowing them to protect the High Belfry and the countess, day or night. They distrust the Morbus Doctore and are hyper-protective of the countess during the healers’ visits to the High Belfry. During the day, cultists claim they have heard the Countess cry out in her sleep about someone some powerful force in the sea. They don’t know who or what that is.

### Golden Barge Company (G.B.C.)

The company’s founders trace their history to the Bouwengracht family. Their ancestors were devotees of Aphaleon, the Arch Seraph of Temperance. They dismissed Otto Bouwengracht’s prophetic claim that the fishers would grow rich through offerings of their dead to the ocean, claiming that this blasphemed the reverent honoring of the dead.

While they did not participate in the Ceremonies of the Nurturing Sea, they served the village loyally. This commitment continues to be the core of the Golden Barge Company duty. They protect and transport Liesechers and their interests within the districts. They do not discriminate when there is profit to be made. The company boasts a staunch fleet of vehicles, tools and supplies.

In their long service to Liesech, they have come to understand the importance of commerce, and the power they wield serves that commerce. They are not afraid to remove obstacles to free trade through means that might cause others to blanche. They employ thieves and assassins when necessary, chalking it up to the greater good.

They know the city streets, sewers and waterways well and use the most expedient routes to complete their tasks. Few among their leaders suspect that the events of the Red Mourning and the Weeping Pox are related, and trust that Aphaleon will reveal the truth to these tragedies in time.

### Morbus Doctore

The Morbus Doctore formed shortly after the Weeping Pox claimed its first victims. Healers and apothecaries from the city were at a loss, and they reached out to colleagues and contacts from across Etharis. Slowly, help arrived.

Before long the presence of these healers was felt. Wearing dark long coats, hats, and bird-like masks, they solemnly set to task. With Countess von Raiza’s and the Golden Barge Company’s assistance, a city area was cleared, and high iron walls erected around it. This quarantined area is called Zielenhaus. In the following weeks, plumes of a mysterious green smoke rose from Zielenhaus’ chimney stacks. It spread over Liesech’s skies and then settled into a ghostly haze. Locals continue to ponder its purpose.

Behind Zielenhaus walls, the Morbus Doctore bring the afflicted. Painful examinations and extractions take place; often with the same result: death. Even so, the Morbus Doctore are seen as merciful seraphs by most. Even though few are cured of the disease, suffering is lessened. They contain the suffering behind their walls. They treat the sick with reverent respect. They play with the children. They offer hope for a cure of the Weeping Pox.

A dark shadow looms over the organization, however. Whispers of procedures and experiments taking place in Zielenhaus with no connection to the disease echo through Liesech’s streets. Sightings of plague-masked figures, covered in blood and skulking through dark alleys, have become more frequent. Whether these rumors are true remains to be seen.

## Notable Locations

### Eye of the Sea

At the center of Bright Bay are dark, turbulent waters where, historically, ship crews that failed to heed warnings were lost and never seen again. Yet the mystery of the area continues to be the stuff of minstrel musing and tavern tales. Its waters are believed to hide anything from utopian settlements of enchanting sea creatures, gilded schools of fish that can feed families for a lifetime, and great treasures left behind in the death of the old gods.

It was such stories that drove young Otto Bouwengracht to row out to the Eye of the Sea, where he encountered none of these things. He came under the sway of a being called the Filth Grazer, whose presence lends truth of Liesech’s historical prosperity. Countess Analita von Raiza is the only being that knows that the Filth Grazer’s corporeal form resides there.

### The High Belfry

Liesech’s first major structure was a village town hall that sat at the village center. As families prospered, they collectively renovated this structure. It would become the single-story home built off of the old town hall, and with a small chapel within it. This grew to become the multi-story manse that housed the entire Bouwengracht clan, with a council hall and a chapel that also serves as the cities bell tower.

It was never referred to as the Bouwengracht Mansion. Otto Bouwengracht was humble; instead, choosing the name High Belfry. Here Liesech’s leaders made decisions, meted out justice, entertained foreign guests and worshipped the old gods of the sea.

Since The Red Mourning, the manse has been renovated to accommodate Countess Analita von Raiza and her servitors, The Cult of the Black Lady. The High Belfry has visitors on special occasions, namely during the Week of the Grand Citywide Market, but those visits are kept to public areas of the mansion, council hall, and church.

### Zielenhaus

The Morbus Doctore’s iron-walled compound encompasses a four city-block region. Its gates open only to Morbus Doctore members—with one peculiar exception: Liesech children often stand at the gates and call for the doctors to come out and play with them. Some of the healers do for a bit—and even offer some sweets, before ushering the children home. Or at least most of the children.

Within its many dark-stoned structures are a labyrinth of cells, examination rooms, laboratories, libraries and vaults filled with instruments the Morbus Doctore need to carry out their efforts against the Weeping Pox.

Its tallest structure is the Morbus Plinth. It’s a factory-like complex built of thick iron plating and strong rivets. Inside are the Morbus Doctore’s most secret laboratories, including the central dispersal chamber that expunges experimental waste into the sky, feeding the ever-looming green haze.

## Quest Hooks

The following quests hooks provide ideas for adventures that might take place in the city of Liesech.

### Golden Knights

The Golden Barge Company seeks mercenaries to complete a series of night-time supply deliveries throughout Traughafen. Recently, deliveries have not reached clients, and its employees have vanished. Continued failures threaten to mar the reputation of the G.B.C.

An iron, banded barrel, has drawn the attention of a skulk of plague carrion, who want the barrel, filled with samples of the Weeping Pox brought from abroad for the Morbus Doctore, to study. This barrel has also caught the interests of the delivery robbers, Cultists of the Black Lady, who believe that the content may hold the secret to curing the Pox.

This draws characters into a confrontation with senior cult leaders, who arrive to accept these gifts. If the thieves and their paramour are defeated, the characters arrive at the Zielenhaus gates.

### The Relic

The Froth Church was Liesech’s historical house of worship, founded by the Bouwengrachts to not only worship the old gods of the sea, but to veil Otto Bouwengracht’s eternal contract with the Filth Grazer and founding of the cult and the Ceremony of the Nurturing Sea into tradition.

In the wake of the Red Mourning, the church was destroyed. Liesechers don’t recall anything about it—their memories were consumed by the Filth Grazer. It’s ruins jut from a sandbar near Liesech’s shore, and none venture out to it. The church’s tithe coffer, ceremonial instruments, and gilded altar still remain somewhere among the ruins. Over time the ruins have sunk further into the bay, areas consumed by the sea may now be occupied. At worse, something touched by the Filth Grazer essence remains.

### Captured Envoy

Ivik Garpok is a wulven diplomat who serves the Crimson Court of Ostoya. He was sent to Liesech to procure information on the Weeping Pox—or more precisely, on how to possibly weaponize it to use against the rebels from Raevo, the Order of Dawn, or other enemies of Ostoya’s rulers.

Ivik’s bodyguards were found dead in the streets of Liesech, and the diplomat is missing. The characters are asked by representatives of Liesech’s police force to quietly and discreetly investigate the disappearance and learn the fate of the wulven diplomat.

The characters can follow the trail of kidnappers who captured Ivik on behalf of the Order of Dawn. That group is looking for a way to turn the Weeping Pox into an affliction that can also affect undead in the same way it does living creatures.
