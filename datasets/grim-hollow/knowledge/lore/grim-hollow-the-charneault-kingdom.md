# The Charneault Kingdom

Charneault occupies the southwestern corner of Etharis, shielded by the Rock-Teeth Mountains and the Grove Maze. A kingdom of rolling hills, clear rivers, and dense forests, founded on a fragile peace between elves and northern settlers under the Pact of the Sacred Land. The elven Bondmakers serve as spiritual intermediaries to the Spirits of Nature (archfey).

A malevolent dark mist now plagues the kingdom. Once harmless fog has become a corrupting force that twists plants, drives beasts mad, and unleashes hostile spirits. King Thibault de Sauveterre and Archdruid Aetherion seek the cause, but the kingdom is divided: elves suspect spiritual reckoning for past wrongs; others see nature itself as the enemy. Charneault's generous landscape belies a culture of mutual distrust, moral hypocrisy, and well-earned fear of the unknown.

Factions: Iron Sparrow, Fairweathers, Severe Templars, Dawn Dreamers, Vermeil Guard.

## Geography

Natural barriers create profound isolation. Travelers who brave the twisting roads through these boundaries are warned to remain alert--the splendor of this realm hides the capricious danger of the fey.

### The Grove Maze

Charneault is nearly impossible to reach on foot. The Grove Maze chokes the pass between the Grey Spine and Rock-Teeth Mountains and creeps southward. An ancient, tangled labyrinth of deeply wooded foothills, misted valleys, cascading rivers, and forested wetlands. The forest constantly moves--maps and landmarks cannot be trusted. A beautiful vale may reveal itself one day and vanish the next. The Spirits of Nature show whatever they will and conceal what they desire undisturbed.

Folk permitted by the archfey speak of their experiences as if wandering through a dream: glittering palace spires in a flower garden, a green-scaled dragon in a moldy bog, a wintery glade dusted by snowfall, birds bickering in plain language. The Grove Maze is the gateway to the Realm of Faerie and the final resting place of many trespassers.

### Land of the Thousand Rivers

Countless rivers flow through the heartland between rolling hills. Dense woods and fertile farmland spread across the kingdom. Villages nestle beside brooks teeming with fish. The southwest extends into a peninsula (Emerald Sea west, Gulf of Lions east). Maritime trade through Morencia is essential since the Grove Maze blocks overland routes, shielding Charneault from the Bürach Empire's imperialism and Castinella's intolerance toward primal mysticism.

Dangers: dire wolves and brigands hide in fog-laden valleys. The Spirits of Nature are capricious and covetous. Children go missing--kidnapped at forest edges or exchanged with wechselkind. Faeries take up residence in homes built from their trees. Entire communities may disappear into the wilds to dance around cauldrons, forgetting mortal lives as fey playthings.

### Changing Seasons

Each season governed by one of four Fey Courts. Those who revere the archfey perform rites at seasonal changes.

Spring: heavy rain then sun, lush greens and flowering colors. Woodlands turn wild--satyr songs drift on the breeze, trees twist branches to trap explorers. Those seduced by spring's beauty become lost forever.

Summer: rivers recede, days warm and dry. A maiden or crone wielding a sickle visits those who work beneath the midday sun, leaving undead pestas in her wake. Fish-fey spirits called gaspers lurk in pools, waiting to drown careless fools.

Autumn: brilliant orange and brown. Cruel faeries awaken scarecrows and farm tools. Final rites express gratitude to the Spirits for what was taken from the land.

Winter: rivers freeze in the north. The Winter Queen watches through her frozen mirror and inflicts cruelties for each unpunished offense against the Spirits. Malevolent fey called grief eaters slink into settlements to devour the sick and sorrowful.

### The Mist

Originates from magma vents along the southern coast and beneath the Emerald Sea. The largest fissure lies beneath a lake inside a caldera where the Temple of Mist is built. Within the thick fog, the Bondmakers commune with the Spirits of Nature directly. Their rituals and the Temple are never visible from outside the crater.

Sometimes the mist is harmless: a light veil hiding frolicking pixies and sprite hosts marching to secret fey battles. When it passes, mushrooms crop up in faerie circles, goats have been milked and the milk stolen, trees regrow their leaves.

The dark mist is different. Grey and frightening, it brings unseasonable weather--dust and barren crops in spring, snow in summer, flash floods in winter. Foliage turns hostile: noxious spores, strangling vines, treants bleeding sickly sap that attracts insect swarms. The Spirits appear corrupted: pixies become wingless murderous pucks who fertilize groves with peoples' innards. Sylvan archers hunt settlers for sport. Towns become pale-spun nests of giant spiders. The sounds of fey singing are gone--only mortals screaming and whimsical laughter like children playing a game.

## History

### The Ulufey

Before the Charneault Kingdom, this was Caer Neiada, "land of countless rivers" in Elvish. The first settlers called themselves ulufey ("mortal fey"), believing themselves direct descendants of the archfey. In everything they did, they gave respect and offerings to the seasonal courts. They hunted but buried bones to nurture soil, gathered more than farmed while spreading seeds, felled trees while planting others. They listened for the voices of the Spirits of Nature who tutored them in primal magic.

Their culture stretches back before the Era of Expansion. Fragments survive in ancient stonework, faded tapestries, and half-remembered songs. When humans arrived through the Grove Maze, they adopted the word "elf."

### Arrival of the First Men

Many tried to breach the Grove Maze and failed. Eventually explorers pushed through, then migrants, then armies. Beholding Caer Neiada's splendor, many believed it a divine gift. Settlers hunted freely, fed scraps to animals, cut trees for homes, tilled earth with foreign grains--all without heeding the Spirits. Nearby elves were horrified, fearing the archfey's wrath. Conflict was inevitable, culminating in generations of war.

### Pact of the Sacred Land

Knight Aymeric Noblecoeur, found wounded in Caer Neiada by elven sage Thuridan Sarador, forged peace. Historians who don't believe the romantic fable write that Noblecoeur sought diplomacy-cloaked domination, while Sarador chose peace knowing endless war meant elven extinction.

Terms: the king rules with Bondmaker counsel to the whims of the archfey. Chapter Knights protect the kingdom and maintain order between peoples. Wealth and produce are shared. Sacred elven sites are never disturbed.

Critical price: every regent must send their firstborn child to the Temple of Mist for training in ancient customs, then ascend to the Fey Courts, never to return. All attempts to circumvent this tribute have brought disaster.

### The Circle of Bondmakers

Two faiths coexist. Belief in the Four Divines (Aurelia, Galt, Ulmyr, Maligant) migrated south with northern tribes; after the Gods' End, many turned to Arch Seraphs. But the kingdom's prosperity is attributed to the Spirits of Nature.

Elves view Spirits as true ancestors and Fey Courts as ruling authority. Their relationship is transactional: for every animal hunted, fruit plucked, and tree felled, an offering of equal value is given. Offering prayers to archfey without an appropriate gift is a faux pas.

The Bondmakers are druids maintaining the connection between people and Spirits. They wander the kingdom, strengthening bonds wherever fey cause trouble. Divisive: suspected of loyalty to Fey Courts over crown. They conceal their rituals; not even Vermeil Guard escorts may enter the Temple of Mist. Folk who've witnessed fey cruelty distrust them. But the druids contend fey wickedness often results from insults inflicted by commoners.

Over centuries the two faiths influenced each other. Northerners created their own connection to Spirits through knighthood and quests. Elven ideals of romance and beauty suffused warrior cultures to create the Chapter Knights.

### Knighthood

Knighthood is the greatest aspiration of any Charnelaise child. Knights exemplify courage, strength, honor, justice, temperance, mercy, faith, generosity, and hope. The Chapter Knights are codified institutions prioritizing different virtues.

To be king or queen, one must be regarded as a knight of the highest caliber. When a new ruler is coronated, Chapter Masters convene and decide. Usually a formality--heirs prepared from youth--but intrigues happen.

Five major chapters, plus lesser chapters and unchaptered knights-errant who hold no authority recognized by the crown:

**Vermeil Guard.** Personal guard of the Bondmakers. Green/gold shield with crowned antlers and holly sprig. Escort regent's firstborn to the Temple of Mist. Loyalty split between throne and Spirits.

**Iron Sparrow.** General defense of Charneault, with subchapters outside the Grove Maze, along Rock-Teeth Mountains, and on coastline. Blue/silver shield with fortress and song sparrow.

**Fairweathers.** Patrol the mainland, keeping peace and rebuilding after conflict or mist. Command the justice system, deferring to Acen Elavain near Tol Leyemil. Yellow shield with mounted knight, sword, and scales of justice.

**Dawn Dreamers.** Protect Tol Leyemil. Members have elven heritage. Rarely range far but investigate threats. Autumn-colored shield with bare tree, lyre, and bow.

**Severe Templars.** Map and study Charneault's environments. Many elves view this as intrusive to the Spirits. The Templars believe archfey power is insidious and their loyalty is to the crown alone. Black shield with coroneted knight and flaming torch. Under Hermand le Sévère, they've become one of the most feared war machines Charneault has seen. King Thibault's retreat has emboldened them to extend authority beyond usual bounds, fueling rumors they ordered Princess Tiphaine's killing.

### The Quest

Any citizen can rise to knighthood. Most inducted into a chapter young, trained as squires. Lower-born commoners must arm and educate themselves. All squires carry a blank shield and must accomplish a worthy deed of great courage to earn a coat of arms.

During quests, knights may meet a Spirit of Nature and gain its trust, becoming wilderness guardians blessed with primal magic. Others swear vows to an Arch Seraph and become paladins.

### The Dark Mist

After generations of peace, relations turned grim when King Thibault de Sauveterre ascended. His firstborn daughter Tiphaine knew her destiny was the Temple of Mist and accepted it happily, despite having secretly married Sir Guilhem le Preux of the Vermeil Guard.

When Thibault was crowned, Guilhem begged that Tiphaine be spared. She took his hands and instructed him to be merry, for their sacrifice would forge the mightiest kingdom. But Guilhem broke his oath, forsaking his duties, unable to bear escorting his wife. He swore to live dishonorably as a knight-errant. Swiftly a song sparrow perched on his shield, warning that Tiphaine was in grave danger.

He pursued but arrived too late. The Vermeil Guard were defeated, the Bondmakers slaughtered, and Tiphaine missing--though hoof tracks led to the Temple of Mist. Her husband pursued.

Theories: The Severe Templars broke the Pact deliberately; the dark mist is archfey retribution. Or sorcerer Diyanthar abducted Tiphaine for a fell ritual in the Temple, which Guilhem interrupted too late. A romantic tale imagines them reunited in a distant land, but both have been seen as apparitions in the dark mist, calling for help.

## Provinces

Seven provinces, each with its own expression of elven and northern culture. Western provinces (Beaumont, De la Fontaine, Faucher) have higher elven populations and greater fey reverence. Eastern provinces (Gagnon, Lamar, Chaplin) are more urbanized and tend toward Arch Seraph worship. Grand Croix encompasses the Grove Maze. Each province was once its own territory; some still governed by ancient noble families now bearing ducal titles.

Honor and reputation are influential. Marriage and trade are levers of power. Those who cannot wield a sword offer children and riches to remain in the ruler's graces.

### Chaplin

Crown jewel and birthplace of knightly virtue. Seat of the crown at Castle Lamesdhonneur. Wealthiest province--not from resources but from folk pouring in to witness greatness.

The Games of the White Dove (summer martial tournament at Castle Lamesdhonneur) and Tournament of the Pink Rose (spring arts festival at Houiville) draw competitors from every chapter. Chaplin immortalizes heroes: shields of fallen knights hang on castle walls, tapestries at Houiville depict their deeds.

The highest power rests with the crown, but any regent is beholden to competing interests. The Royal Council (noble dignitaries), the Acen Elavain (voice of Tol Leyemil), and the Chapter Masters (king-makers) all vie for influence. Courage, faith, and generosity are upheld as ideal virtues. But secrecy, ambition, and cruelty are the true tools of power.

Since the dark mist, Castle Lamesdhonneur has become a refugee shelter. The noble King de Sauveterre has retreated from public life, leaving the Severe Templars to maintain order.

**King Thibault de Sauveterre.** Former Iron Sparrow Chapter Master. Supporters expected a lion among kings. Instead he became a broken man after his daughter's tragedy. Frightened, indecisive, disaffected. His reputation as a knight sustains his people's love, but without crown leadership the court has descended into violent suppression. Uniting the realm beneath his bloody sword may be his only option.

**Hermand le Sévère.** Severe Templars Chapter Master, the man who would be king. Indomitable reputation--many knights and squires have died facing him in tourneys. Losing the crown to Thibault is considered his only failure, yet in defeat he found success. His voice holds great influence in Castle Lamesdhonneur. Whenever he's present, decisions bend toward his preferences. The Templars' contempt for fey is no secret; the dark mist only affirms their crusade to burn the archfey from civilized lands.

### Gagnon

Easternmost duchy, bordering Castinella. Forts along border rivers. Home of the Iron Sparrow, whose tradition intertwines appreciation of natural beauty with defense. Capital Camont: lush verdant courtyards, potted flowerbeds, elven-influenced architecture despite eastern location. Music common in streets--knights singing ballads by fountains.

**Duchess Sylvana Briar Lorraine.** No more than five years old, orphaned by her father's recent death. Appears cursed: when bored, pixies flit from thin air to torment servants; when angry, nearby objects burst into flames; when amused, bubbles escape from her ears and mouth ("the Bubbling Duchess"). Nobles scheme for her throne.

**Steward Edwardine Evrétte.** Elven steward governing until Sylvana comes of age. Searches for a cure. Others believe she holds a secret about Sylvana's abilities that threatens civil unrest if revealed.

### Lamar

Bridge between east and west. Midlands mostly farms and roads. Spirits of Nature never far--bridges and guidestones frequently destroyed by mischievous faeries. Superstitious folk tear down structures built too close to sacred woods.

Mont de Lancey: coastal fortress on a gem-rich bluff. Familial signet rings inset with gemstones. Castle de Esquire: Fairweathers stronghold and squire training school. Aspirants trained here are coveted by other chapters. The dark mist has brought massive influx of students.

**Vivian Descoteaux.** Fairweathers Chapter Master, mother to the Duchess of Lamar. Formidable, determined, fixated on being Charneault's sole savior. Believes diplomacy is weakness and justice exists only at the end of a sword. Beneath her steel facade: deeply scarred, delusions of grandeur, willingness to sacrifice anyone while believing she is saving them. Intentionally leaves villages unprotected to bait foes. Burns grain stores rather than leave them for brigands.

**Emy Descoteaux.** Duchess of Lamar, Vivian's bookish third child. More comfortable with a book than a sword. Puzzle solver working to make mines efficient and exports lucrative. Orders people to shelter behind walls during dark mist--another disappointment to her mother, who'd rather have fresh corpses to study.

### Grand Croix

The Grove Maze has its own provincial name. Most settlements along the great forest's edge, though some communities live within, built aloft among enormous trees with arching bridges and spiraling staircases. The Spirits of Nature may secretly reside in these hamlets.

Severe Templars active here, charting the maze, maintaining roads, burning back encroaching nature, slaying malevolent spirits--but not always welcome.

Capital Besion: ancient fortress with gnomish ruins beneath. Whether gnomish ancestors migrated from Rauland or from the Grove Maze is unclear.

### Beaumont

The land of the elves, surrounding Tol Leyemil. Beaumont is considered anywhere in northern Charneault from where Rock-Teeth Mountains are visible. Greatest diversity of resources: fish, fruit, mountain metals, famed sleek boats.

Tol Leyemil is the last stronghold of untouched elven culture, a time capsule of ancient Caer Neiada. Teems with murals and music unheard in other realms, appearing to exist between the mortal world and the Realm of Faerie.

Dawn Dreamers protect it fiercely. Knights clad in cloaks more than mail, with antlered riders atop stags. Threats riddled with arrows before they realize they're watched.

The Acen Elavain ("Assembly of Elves") rules Tol Leyemil but wields broader political power, advising the crown. Internally divided: some view Tol Leyemil as Charneault's twin capital; others would isolate within the city, believing it the last fragment of dying culture. Some distrust the Bondmakers for enabling northern domination.

**Virion Venfir.** Most influential voice on the Acen Elavain. Spectacular musician wielding popularity for political and personal glory. Has done nothing to help eradicate the dark mist, frustrating detractors. Hidden aim: let the dark mist consume Charneault and let it be reborn as Caer Neiada anew.

### De la Fontaine

Mystic lands deep in Charneault. Mist ever-present. Green hills and wide valleys look painted in watercolors, studded with tiny elven villages. Few bridges; rivers crossed at fords or grass ferries. Agriculture haphazard: wild orchards, berry thickets, livestock roaming unfenced. Seems unwealthy but suits its residents.

Bonneseaux Village: largest settlement, on a sloping hill surrounded by water, houses on stilts. Lake has risen; lights dance across its surface; folk found drowned in their homes. Water spirits disturbed by the dark mist.

Crumbling elven ruins scattered across the province; malevolent spirits haunt them.

Temple of Mist lies at the south, making Bondmakers and escorts a common sight. Whispers wonder if the calamity is of the Bondmakers' own making.

**Duke Hugo Plomierre.** Halfling duke, shrewd politician who prefers comfort of larger cities. Leaves local troubles to reeves.

**Grand Shaman Aetherion.** Powerful archdruid, "Fey Sighted"--Spirits show him glimpses of the future. Named leader of Bondmakers after the slaughter that claimed Tiphaine. Whatever horrors he's witnessed, he remains silent as he works to undo them.

Jovial and mischievous, he was companion to King Thibault during their questing days. Since the tragedy, a rift opened: Aetherion rushed to the Temple of Mist instead of comforting the King. If he learned Tiphaine's or Guilhem's fate, he has not disclosed it--a betrayal Thibault will not forgive.

Now rides across Charneault recruiting knights and mercenaries. His goals aren't always clear. Only the Fey Sighted can see the tapestry of time, weaving a future he desperately wishes to prevent.

### Faucher

In the furthest western reaches, the land rots. Grass thins and turns grey. Trees distort and lose leaves. Spirits of Nature fall silent near the Dark Oak Grove.

Before the Pact, Faucher was a garden paradise. The capital Tol Kerdywel was a city of white towers. Its ruler was the elven sorceress Thaelkinei, the Lich Princess. She believed she could control the Spirits through arcane magic to expel the northern tribes. Her magic turned vile: she drained the primal world's vitality, used binding rituals to usurp fey will, reaped the souls of those she could not command. Had she succeeded, Caer Neiada would have become a realm of living corpses.

Defeated by the first coalition of elves and northern folk, Thaelkinei was never destroyed--undying but trapped in perpetual decay within ruined Tol Kerdywel.

Faucher has few inhabitants. The Dark Oak Grove: trees rot from inside out, undead wolfen husks stalk underbrush, acid mires bubble. Tol Kerdywel is accursed--those who venture in are marked by the Lich Princess's malice, their flesh withering. But rumors of treasures and eldritch secrets still lure explorers.

**Diyanthar, the Dark Elf.** Former Bondmaker, magical prodigy. Believes elven magic weakened due to northern folk. Studied in Tol Kerdywel, teaching what he learned to form the Dark Elves circle. Disappeared when Tiphaine vanished, raising suspicion of involvement in the dark mist curse.

**Thaelkinei, the Lich Princess.** Silent for centuries but the endless decay of the Dark Oak Grove proves she was never destroyed. The realm fears she has found a new disciple in Diyanthar who could resurrect her or complete her blasphemous vision.

### Other Locations

**The Shaded Haven.** Isolated coastal village, sanctuary for lycanthropes seeking respite from their curse. Self-sufficient with fishery, farmland, herb garden. Residents live ascetic existences, assuaging predatory instincts through martial practice. Wulven heirs must choose between their parents' quiet lives or a world that may never accept them. The community fears the dark mist may distort their primal nature.

**The Sailgraves.** Island chain off Lamar's southern coast, perpetually mist-shrouded. Veil between mortal world and Feywild wears thin. Merfolk croon in the deep mist, sitting on crumbling sea vessels watching over countless explorer bones. Few who venture return. Stories of faerie gold and lost elven treasures.

Menchâtel: tiny coastal village profiting from supplying adventurers. Locals don't dissuade voyagers but seem too eager to help. Suspicion of a pact with a sea hag to lure explorers to their doom.

## Quest Hooks

### Princess of the Grove
Tiphaine and Sir Guilhem missing for years. Commoners hear their voices in the dark mist. Every knight tasked with gathering information. Clues point toward the Dark Oak Grove. Perhaps their spirits reside in the putrid forest, or they're prisoners in Tol Kerdywel, or it's all a ruse by Diyanthar luring souls to empower his magic or revive Thaelkinei. Discovering the truth could be as perilous as facing the Lich Princess herself.

### Water's Edge
Squire Franklin D'jeun seeks companions to the Sailgraves to recover his uncle's signet ring lost a decade earlier. Menchâtel locals suspiciously eager to help. Turbulent waters hide sea beasts; wrecked vessels could be looted at risk of the sirens' song. The islands may be a Faerie doorway from which none escape.

### Wood Song
Hildebury villagers disappearing. Baker Torgga Lerryn's daughter Amber missing delivering bread. Tracks lead to a clearing with countless footprints leading nowhere. At dusk: all missing villagers crawl from brush, thrashing and dancing around a horned satyr playing pipes. The folk seem unharmed aside from mania. The satyr's song may enchant others. Interrupting turns revelers savage; the satyr won't release his audience.

### The Wolf & Loon
Popular traveling theater troupe: wulven acrobat Mattéo and fortune teller Ariel (always hooded behind an elven mask, eyes too pale, silvery feathers that might protrude from beneath her cloak). Troupe includes outcasts: wechselkind pantomimists, dhampir alchemist, disembodied scholar. People go missing when they're in town. True purpose: helping the disenfranchised seek closure or retribution for past injustices. Whether they're harmless or a vengeful scourge needs answering.

## Castle Lamesdhonneur

Capital city on a hilltop. The castle at the summit, supported by jutting stone foundations. Homes, shops, and workshops radiate down the slope. Agricultural fields at the base, enclosed by the Shield wall--where fallen knights' shields hang, rattling in the wind like a marching army.

Once the beacon of knightly virtue. Now fear of the dark mist has warped honor into violent prejudice, stoked by the Severe Templars who seized the power vacuum.

### Geography

Natural defenses: cascading waterfalls, steep slopes, rivers forming a natural moat. Built primarily of granite--at dawn, mineral veins illuminate the city in gold; at sunset, a red glimmer. Crowded buildings form labyrinthine streets blending modern wood with centuries-old stone. Colorful roofs have turned grey with neglect.

The dark mist makes seasons unpredictable: extreme flooding in spring and autumn, summer droughts, winter freezing rain that breaks trees under ice.

### History

Built as a human bastion south of the Grove Maze. King Aymeric Noblecoeur chose it as capital after the Sacred Pact. The city grew around the knight chapters, expanding outside the original walls. Working class often exploited by knights trading on renown. Local chapters: Gravekeep Guardians (care for the dead, guard cemeteries) and Silent Order (royal guard sworn to secrecy; extreme members have tongues removed).

### Present Turmoil

After the dark mist, King Thibault withdrew to save his daughter. Severe Templars claimed stewardship. Their anti-magic, anti-elf agenda bleeds into streets. They blame permissive culture, seize goods and services, defund art, silence unauthorized speech.

Draconian laws: illegal to sing fey songs, paint forest murals, or grow wildflowers without permission. Scaffolds in Belforétte used almost daily. Elves and magic users falsely accused, tortured, executed. Street violence: innocent people ripped from homes and beaten until they falsely confess. Attackers rewarded with coin. Targets expanding beyond elves to anyone with non-human ancestry, nature worshipers, or Templar critics.

Live plants banned from Coronette, Berthald, and Belforétte. Family gardens trampled, trees chopped, the Sacrecoeur (maple tree where kings were coronated) chopped down. Streets sprayed with an alchemical concoction that withers greenery and sickens those who breathe it.

### Notable Districts

**Coronette.** The original castle atop the hill. Home of royal family and Royal Council. Every inch of walls carved with knightly scenes. Courtyard held the Sacrecoeur--now a stump. The throne room is abandoned, covered in dust; governance conducted in the attached Council Chamber amid echoing shouts. Hidden in castle corridors: Belles-voux, Tiphaine's secret garden of white roses and poppies, tended solely by King Thibault.

**Berthald.** Built on a ridge descending from the summit. Once vibrant home of Knight Chapters and noble families with pennants flying. Now dull and grey. Chapters with elven ties retreated; headquarters seized by Templars. The Black Cross: Severe Templar HQ, formerly Vermeil Guard headquarters. Elven gifts and centuries-old architecture gutted.

**Victor's Circle.** Tournament grounds. Games of the White Dove tradition continues but now features deadly monster fights. Severe Templars rig outcomes behind the scenes. Grand prize: permission to wear the White Dove symbol, a reminder that knighthood's purpose is peace.

**Belforétte.** Most populous district. Taverns and squares once burst with chivalric tales. Now jewelers, brewers, and "frivolous" businesses shuttered. Blacksmiths and armourers toil constantly for Templar demands. Blood runs through gutters.

The Ferforger: citizen-staffed smithy producing crude weapons at frantic pace. Workers collapse and are immediately replaced.

The Stone Anvil: last surviving pub. Owner Armand Basile cut a deal with Templars: pub stays open in return for prioritizing knight service. He secretly sells intelligence overheard in the pub to those willing to pay.

**Verdeaux.** Largest district: horse paddocks, farmland, fisheries. Suffers most from dark mist. Crops wither; farmers barely feed themselves as harvests are taxed for knighthood. Monsters prowl foothills.

Shrine of the Fields: condemned by Templars. Caretaker Faerynthia (half-elven recluse) beheaded at the threshold. Defending farmers cut down. Bodies and shrine set aflame. Now haunted: sweeping by unseen hands, earth-tremor groans, headless ghost beneath shattered stained glass.

The Burning Maw: great bonfire pit for disposing refuse, profane artifacts, and bodies of criminals. Workers wear masks against charred-flesh smoke.

### Important Individuals

**King Thibault de Sauveterre.** Sequesters himself in Coronette, rarely appears publicly.

**Hermand le Sévère.** Austere, flinty. Decades of knightly accomplishments, dedicated to human strength without magic or Spirits. Spends most time in Council Chamber, swaying opinions toward authoritarianism.

**Josseline d'Eon.** Second-in-command of Severe Templars. Born to renowned Vermeil Guard family but redirected after witnessing dark mist chaos during her squire quest. Sharp strategic wit, sharper sword. Respected and feared for unreadable composure and unbreakable devotion to Hermand.

**Lady Mireille de Bayeux.** Widowed after wife Ser Apolline was executed on fabricated treason charges for speaking against Josseline d'Eon. Gracefully navigates treacherous politics to build a dissenting noble network in Berthald. Under constant surveillance but her gentle demeanour is just disarming enough.

**Armand Basile.** Stone Anvil owner, farm-born opportunist. Welcoming charisma now directed at keeping knights drunk while secretly selling overheard secrets.

**L'Hibou.** Masked revolutionary (red cloak, steel owl mask). Preaches against knight tyranny to folk of Belforétte and Verdeaux. Commits violent sabotage in Berthald. Identity unknown. Some say dented knight armour beneath the cloak, limbs that transform into twisted blades.

### Castle Lamesdhonneur Quest Hooks

**Elven Execution.** Templars march battered elf to gallows in Belforétte. Through swollen eyelids, the elf makes eye contact and pleads innocence, promising reward for rescue.

**Creature Capture.** Templars hire mercenaries to capture monsters for tournament. Josseline pays extra for cursed creatures.

**Unwitting Smugglers.** Armand Basile hires escort for "empty" barrels. Elven refugees hiding inside. Must pass Templar gate checks. If caught, Armand throws blame on the party.

**Owl in Flight.** L'Hibou crashes into party fleeing Templars. "If you believe in freedom and justice, let me go!" Leaves behind iron key and encoded coordinates to a hideaway.

**Sickle and Blood.** Farmers in Verdeaux suddenly attack knights with tools, then have no memory. Pattern of incidents.

**A Knight's Dying Wish.** Dying knight in Belforétte alley asks party to recover her stolen shield and hang it on the border wall.

**Rampart Phantom.** Ghost of elven woman resembling Princess Tiphaine appears atop Coronette ramparts. Herald Léonide asks party to confirm and attempt communication.

**Mireille's Messenger.** Lady Mireille's confidential letter to a Vermeil Guard knight has gone missing along with the messenger.

**Sewer Squire.** Dismembered squire's arm found by sewer grate in Berthald. Shoulder gnawed clean off.

**Stifled Forges.** Ferforger furnaces gone cold; no one can relight them. Rumored curse by vengeful mage.

## Tol Leyemil

Last refuge of the elves. Blooms from the forest like a glade of wildflowers. Music flows through stone archways. Closed to all non-elves--the most uninvited travelers can hope to glimpse are moss-covered spires and insect-chewed corpses hung from trees as warning.

Protected by Dawn Dreamers and invisible barriers interweaving with the Realm of Faerie. But the dark mist corrupts the very Fey Courts they've relied upon. Elves dread destruction at the hands of their own deific ancestors while also fearing loss of the only living remnant of elven history. Unrest brews; trust is thin. Rumors prowl that Dark Elves are among the Acen Elavain.

### Geography

Built above river rapids in a valley beneath Rock-Teeth Mountain cliffs. The Great Falls cascade from the mountains in crystalline curtains, rumored to descend from the Realm of Faerie with whispers of wisdom from the Fey Courts. Secret cave system in the cliffs: moss-covered grottos, weathered statues, ancient mines. Deepest tunnels flicker with light leading to goblin and cantankerous fey realms.

Streets not built for carriages. Tree roots displace flagstones, vines drape into archways, foliage obstructs laneways. Limestone spires and ivory towers rise above trees. The air glows with fireflies in the evening. Beneath beauty lies grief: cracked dry fountains (faeries retreated to their realm), faded murals of vanished places, weathered statues of distant heroes. Music is constant but mournful.

Seasons keenly felt as Fey Court dominance shifts. Summer: sprites steal fruit and coins. Winter: shadow spirits invade, goblin campfires glow in caves. These fey are hunted as pests elsewhere; here they're accommodated as archfey servants.

### The Acen Elavain

Citizens chosen at random to serve on the assembly. Vast political power representing elves within Tol Leyemil and the broader kingdom. Large and fractured: half isolationists protecting heritage shards, others roaming abroad giving voice to elves throughout Charneault. Skilled orators dominate less political members. Power shifts as swiftly as seasons.

Traditions predate the Pact. Some seethe about the Bondmakers' self-appointed influence, but the druids' connection to Spirits makes them powerful within the city.

### Notable Locations

**Hall of Many Voices.** Assembly chamber seating twenty councilors. Frequently cacophonous. Many travel abroad; seats occupied by advisors who negotiate and vote on their behalf. Branching corridors lead to parlors, terraces, and gardens for private intrigues.

**Sarmar Academy.** Home to profoundly talented mages. Teaches patience: magic learned by petitioning Spirits to reweave primal energies. Central citadel for meditation and gatherings; real lessons in wilderness, gardens, beneath the moon. Mentorship-based; no graduation or licensing. Magic is spirituality and worship, a way to record history through ritual and song.

Aemanyir House: branch outside Tol Leyemil for non-elven apprentices, founded by Alren Corynthios. Controversial--many in Tol Leyemil refuse to recognize it.

**The Preservation.** The most pivotal documents of Caer Neiada's history. Secret distributed archive maintained by white-robed, masked Preservationists. Location unknown to most elves. May extend into the Realm of Faerie. Even the Acen Elavain must request permission. Small measure of power: murals and statues cannot be moved without their consent. Feared to contain stockpiled ancient weapons and eldritch knowledge.

**Faerie Roads.** Countless hidden roads to and from the city through the Feywild. Appear and disappear at fey whims: a cupboard might lead to a twilight garden, or a door might exit to a mountaintop. A building could show different destinations on different days. Bondmakers, Sarmar mages, and some Dawn Dreamers navigate them to traverse Charneault swiftly. Intruders near the city get lost in Faerie Roads without realizing they left the mortal realm.

**The Autumn Spire.** White tower draped in ivy, domed with gold. Dawn Dreamers fortress outside the valley. Tapestries and music fill the halls. All squires taught music alongside combat--elvish songs of anguish filling hearts with rage. No mercy for trespassers. Beneath the nobility: hidden interrogation chambers unknown to the Acen Elavain or crown.

### Important Individuals

**Virion Venfir.** Charismatic bard elevated to the Acen Elavain. Refugee from a village burned by fey-fearing folk. The trauma turned to rage when he saw the elven paradise. Quietly welcomes the dark mist; believes it could wipe the land clean of non-elven cultures. Uses position and charm to influence others, hiding his feelings beneath compassion.

**Alren Corynthios.** Sarmar archmage who founded Aemanyir House for non-elven apprentices. Believes more skilled mages improve Charneault's survival chances. Dawn Dreamers divided on whether to protect or destroy the settlement. Severe Templars call it a cabal of fey idolaters.

**Eifre.** Squat sylvan creature, craftswoman traveling via Faerie Roads. Barters commodities into Tol Leyemil, from sugar to enchanted Summer Court arrows. Accused of pilfering from the Preservation. Enjoys fey reverence that keeps her safe; vanishes whenever in actual danger.

**Benoît the Brave.** Dawn Dreamers Chapter Master, first of mostly human heritage. Found as an orphaned boy grappling a fox over a rabbit. Loud laugh, easy smile. Bards love him. The Acen Elavain despise that their last refuge is protected by a northerner's descendant. Spent his entire life justifying elven ties. Would commit any violence to protect Tol Leyemil. Dreams of the day Hermand de Sévère stands at the end of his blade.

### Tol Leyemil Quest Hooks

**A Thousand Hues.** Magical paintings appearing on city walls--exquisite landscapes so detailed they appear lifelike, paint visibly moving. Each may be a gate to the Faerie Roads. Over time they transform into shadowy scenes clouded by creeping mist. The artist may be a Spirit of Nature. Sarmar Academy wants them found.

**At Any Cost.** Benoît suspects Dark Elves in the Acen Elavain. Hires deniable agents. Any means sanctioned; suspects brought to the Autumn Spire for interrogation. Investigation may lead far from Tol Leyemil.

**Book of True Names.** Ogresh archmage Master Windorff hires mercenaries to infiltrate Tol Leyemil and steal a tome of Archfey true names from the Preservation. Two approaches: through Dawn Dreamer patrols (traps, ambushes, misleading paths) or via the perilous Faerie Roads. Inside, the Preservation is a twisting complex guarded by skilled mage-warriors.

**The Winter Queen.** Unnatural winter pours into Tol Leyemil through Faerie Roads. Great Falls threaten to freeze. Trees turn blue with frost. Elves become disaffected, cruel, desperate--turning on each other. Unseelie horrors hunt the city. Bondmakers require a mirror forged from icy shards to travel to the Winter Court and discover what's happened. Each shard requires a dark ritual: the death of a person whose heart is saturated by cruelty, hatred, or despair.
