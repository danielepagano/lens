# Chapter 10: Folk of Etharis

The people of Etharis tend to be hardy folk, as one needs to be when dealing with the hardship and strife of a world lacking in sympathy but teeming with monsters. Whether thinking about the common heritages of a typical fantasy setting, the rare heritages unique to Grim Hollow, or the eldritch heritages of magical and terrifying origin, the folk of Etharis leave plenty of room for interesting and dramatic stories.

Humans dominate the world of Etharis. The elder peoples—the dragonborn, dwarves, elves, gnomes, and halflings—share the human nations with those people who undertook the conquests of the Era of Expansion. The rarer and more unsettling heritages share these lands as well, although their presence may raise some eyebrows—and far too often pitchforks and torches.

As described in the Grim Hollow Player’s Guide, the Heritage System allows the folk of Etharis to be far more diverse than a typical fantasy setting. The social structure of the different realms or locales sees an individual’s traits breaking with that of someone typical of their species.

Even physiological traits can differ greatly within species, as the interweaving of different people—not to mention the mutative magic prevalent throughout Etharis—can give someone traits much different than even those of their ancestors. If a player can imagine a character with unique abilities, the Heritage System likely has a way for them to build it.

The rare and eldritch heritages described in this chapter have a special place in Grim Hollow’s lore. These species, or in some cases, these created beings, might not have separate nations or even distinct communities within the landscape of Etharis, but they nonetheless have compelling stories about their origins or their relationship with the other folks who make of the population of the world.

Players might want to embody the lore of these less common heritages, playing a character that engages with the lore as a typical member of that heritage. On the other hand, they may want to be the exception of that heritage, breaking tradition and charting their own course within the social milieu of the world.

As with any campaign, the world of Etharis that you present to your players—and the stories of the people that make up that world—are yours to tailor for the stories and adventures taking place there. The folk described here, and their histories and legends, are a starting point for your imagination!


### Dragonborn

With scales like armor and eyes that betray an ancient lineage, the dragonborn move through the shadows of Etharis, inspiring both terror and reverence in those they encounter. Their draconic features—jagged scales, deadly talons, and fearsome jaws—mark them as living echoes of legendary beasts. Though they lack the massive wingspan and imposing stature of true dragons, dragonborn carry within them the living essence of these primal creatures: dragon’s breath and terrible majesty flow through their veins. Whispers claim the dragonborn’s lineage predates all others in Etharis. When human explorers first ventured into the southernmost territories during the Era of Expansion, they discovered in Castinella not a thriving civilization, but the hollow remnants of one—a kingdom already fallen to ruin. The granite metropolis of Ember Cairn stood as a crumbling monument to forgotten glory. Even the dragonborn themselves could not recall what calamity had befallen their once-proud society. Their prayers echoed unanswered through abandoned temples, their gods silent as the grave. They dwelled amid the broken pieces of their inheritance, like orphans in their own ancestral homes. Omar Samy

The human settlement of what would become Castinella required little effort. The dragonborn, plagued by spiritual emptiness and growing despair, abandoned their ancestral territories to wander Etharis in search of divine answers that never came. Those who remained were receptive to the teachings of human missionaries who spoke of The Aetheric War and the Divine Seraphs. A terrible revelation took hold: their gods had not abandoned them but had been obliterated by the Aether Kindred. This epiphany filled the void of their lost faith, transforming the dragonborn of Castinella into the most fervent believers in the Eternal Dogma, their devotion born from ancient wounds.

In recognition for their conversion, Castinella granted the dragonborn dominion over a fragment of their ancestral lands. From this meager foundation, they reconstructed Ember Cairn atop its own ruins. The dragonborn who were scattered across the continent began returning on grim pilgrimages to their reborn city, where they embraced the worship of the Divine Seraphs with desperate intensity. Finding their prayers answered at last—or so they believed—many dragonborn felt called to become clerics, missionaries, and inquisitors. They spread their adopted faith with a zealotry matched only by the searing flames that erupt from their throats—twin instruments of conversion that leave either ashes or believers in their wake.

#### Using Dragonborn in Your Campaign

Dragonborn make zealous inquisitors. They hunt heretics through mist-shrouded villages, and their intimidating draconic features make them perfect enforcers of religious orthodoxy. A fanatical dragonborn inquisitor can be a terrifying antagonist hunting your players for real or imagined transgressions against the faith. However, if the characters convince the inquisitor of their innocence, they could earn a potent ally.

Alternatively, dragonborn characters may question their culture’s typical religion. A young dragonborn adventurer may discover ancient texts suggesting their gods weren’t destroyed but imprisoned, leading to forbidden archaeological expeditions. This could unveil dark secrets about what truly happened to dragonborn civilization.

The party could ally with a dragonborn chaplain who witnessed the horrors of border conflicts firsthand. This weathered veteran has a pragmatic interpretation of the Eternal Dogma that prioritizes compassion. While maintaining respectable standing within the church, he provides succor to all who seek it, regardless of faith, heritage, or history. His position grants access to sensitive information, making him an excellent source of intelligence. The weary warrior-priest walks a dangerous line between official duties and personal principles, whose help risks discovery by more orthodox elements within his faith.

### Dwarves

Omar Samy Carved from the very stone they venerate, dwarves emerged in Etharis in times before history’s quill first touched parchment. Their presence as old as the continent itself, these stoic beings established their realms within the valleys and shadows of Etharis’s most primeval mountains: the Rock Teeth and the Grey Spine. Deep within these ancient ranges coursed veins of mithral and gold, glittering promises in the darkness. Those dwarves who extracted and transformed these precious metals into works of exquisite craftsmanship soon garnered renown across the lands of Etharis. Today, dwarves are most common throughout the Bürach Empire and Valika.

When the blood-soaked Era of Expansion descended upon the continent, dwarven clans retreated behind the walls of their greatest strongholds: the terraced citadel of Stehlenwald and the mountain fortress of Grabenstein. These bastions were once deemed unconquerable. The defenders of Grabenstein fought with grim determination to protect their ancestral holdings, enduring siege after relentless siege until finally their formidable walls crumbled, their thanedoms scattered to the bitter winds, and their mineral-rich mines were seized by human warlords whose descendants would later establish the foundations of the Bürach Empire.

The dwarven kingdom of Stehlenwald persisted, though not without cost. Rather than suffer their northern kin’s fate, the Stehlenwald dwarves delved further into the mountain’s lightless heart. There, in chambers untouched by surface light, miners uncovered a metal that defied their enemies’ weapons: adamantine. Clad in impenetrable adamantine armor and wielding axes, spears, and blades that cut through bone like paper, dwarven battalions succeeded in driving invaders back to their homelands. Victory came at a terrible price—Stehlenwald’s population was devastated by the twin specters of war and famine. For the survivors, isolation within their mountain sanctuary became their sole means of preservation.

By the time human kingdoms rose to prominence, the once-proud dwarves of Stehlenwald had recovered their strength, if not their numbers. They emerged from their mountain seclusion to find the world utterly transformed, their realm now encircled and vastly outnumbered by the Bürach Empire’s legions. Recognizing the single path that might ensure his people’s survival, the dwarf king negotiated a pact with the Bürach Empire. The dwarves would relinquish their territories and join the empire, provided they retained independent governance over their own affairs.

To this day, countless dwarves cannot bring themselves to forgive the immense loss of life and sovereignty suffered by their forebears. A common saying in Etharis holds that one might sooner move a mountain than change a dwarf’s mind. Though deep-seated grievances still fester within the Bürach Empire, dwarves have risen to become among the most affluent and accepted peoples throughout all the nations of Etharis—their outward prosperity masking generational scars that never truly heal.

#### Using Dwarves in Your Campaign

Consider portraying dwarven settlements as outwardly prosperous trade centers hiding dark secrets beneath. Their mountain homes and ancient mining tunnels might harbor forgotten shrines where traditionalists perform rituals to ancestors who died defending their sovereignty. These cults could be harmless expressions of cultural memory—or sinister groups planning vengeance against human bloodlines.

Dwarven characters make excellent representatives of generational trauma. A merchant dwarf who smiles at imperial officials by day might harbor centuries-old grudges by night. This duality creates morally complex NPCs who assist players while pursuing their own agendas of reclaiming lost artifacts or sabotaging imperial interests.

While in Liesech, the party might run afoul of a dark dwarven magistrate. The bureaucrat was expelled from the Magister College decades ago, finding a career in mundane public policy. They study the transformative curses of Etharis with particular fascination, viewing them as evolutionary pathways. The failed arcanist conducts experiments on unwitting subjects lured through his legitimate connections. Their most insidious scheme involves coins infused with essences harvested from lycanthropes, vampires, and other transformed creatures. These enchanted coins gradually alter those who handle them, creating subtle physiological and psychological changes. The twisted wizard meticulously documents these changes in coded ledgers, viewing the city as his experimental laboratory.

### Elves

Ancient and hauntingly beautiful, the elves were among the first to walk the primeval forests of Etharis. Their history intertwines with half-remembered myths from time immemorial. Tall and ethereal in their grace, these ageless beings claim descent from the nature spirits that first breathed life into the mortal realm. The dense woodlands and meandering riverlands of Caer Neiada—now part of the Charneault Kingdom—are their ancestral territories. Within these shadowed groves, they crafted realms of terrible beauty, forever bound to the whispered guidance of the forest’s ageless spirits. Etharis’s elves are still most commonly found under the boughs of Charneault. Gonzalo Vega

Elves possess unnaturally keen vision and deadly accuracy with bow and arrow. Their intimate communion with the woodland’s secrets and their fey bloodline allows them to vanish like ghosts within their territories. In Etharis’s dawn, their nimble and lethal armies inspired dread in all who encountered them. Yet centuries of unchallenged supremacy over their forest domains, coupled with their extended lifespans, cultivated a fatal arrogance within the elven psyche. They regarded themselves as elevated beings, particularly in comparison to humans, whom they dismissed as crude and ephemeral creatures. Thus blinded by pride, the elves failed to perceive the rising human threat until it was too late.

The Era of Expansion extracted a terrible toll from elven civilization. Their once-great kingdoms shattered, countless ancient forests reduced to smoldering ruins. Though their martial prowess never faltered, the elves found themselves overwhelmed by relentless human armies that burned and desecrated their sacred woodlands. Forced to retreat into the deepest, most inaccessible reaches of the forests, they clung to the desperate belief that the Spirits of Nature would shield them from extinction. When the elves eventually returned from their exile, they formed an uneasy pact with humanity that gave birth to the Charneault Kingdom—a compromise born of necessity rather than desire.

The profound grief of the elves for their vanished paradise now manifests in their haunting artistic expressions. They have composed soul-rending melodies of a lost golden age that echo mournfully through the trees when darkness falls. In these melancholic ballads, they name themselves ulufey—“mortal fey” and descendants of the fey sidhe. The faerie courts that once guided their ancestors did not perish during the Era of Expansion, and many elves harbor the silent hope that these Nature Spirits might yet aid in restoring their lands to their former, terrible splendor—though what price such beings might extract for their assistance remains unspoken.

#### Using Elves in Your Campaign

Elven enclaves—and nearly anything the elves create—are remarkable works of art, but each hides a certain darkness and melancholy. Visitors might find themselves unable to leave, stricken by profound beauty or encumbered with the unending sorrow of the long-lived elves.

The ulufey make compelling antagonists as ancient elves seeking vengeance for lost forests, perhaps kidnapping children from logging communities or binding human spirits to withered trees. Alternatively, they might serve as tragic figures—the last guardians of sacred groves whose protection requires increasingly desperate measures.

The party could encounter a once-celebrated guardian of Caer Neiada’s sacred groves, who survived the burning of her ancestral forest during the Era of Expansion by forming a desperate pact with an ancient, nameless entity hiding in the forest’s heart. Now seemingly ageless, she maintains her ethereal beauty through a horrific ritual requiring the lives of young humans with fey-touched bloodlines. Her court appears as a haven of preserved elven splendor deep within a recovering forest, but visitors who look closely notice how the trees grow in unnatural patterns, their bark inscribed with arcane symbols. Each of the lady’s dogmatic followers bear a notable self-inflicted scar—assurance they will never usurp their leader’s beauty.

### Gnomes

Restless of mind and slight of frame, gnomes are believed to share distant ancestry with dwarves, though they lack the stature and bulk of their mountain kin. These diminutive beings are marked by ceaselessly churning intellects and an unsettling gift for creation. Gnomes possess minds that never truly rest—clever, inquisitive, occasionally mischievous, yet bound by rigid cultural devotion to their obsessive crafts. The most terrifying mechanical marvels of Etharis have emerged from gnomish workshops. Thundering cannons, unsettling clockwork automatons, smoke-belching flintlocks, and devastating explosives all bear the fingerprints of gnomish ingenuity. For gnomes, the fevered process of creation often eclipses the creation itself. They conjure new inventions merely to demonstrate possibility, with little thought to consequence. A common saying among other folk warns that to locate a gnome in any populated area, one need only follow the trail of destruction to its smoking origin. Omar Samy

When the bloodshed of the Era of Expansion engulfed Etharis, gnomes abandoned their vulnerable settlements beyond the mountains’ protection, seeking shelter within dwarven strongholds like Stehlenwald. There, they shared in the grim fate of their cousins as human armies laid siege to the towering stone ramparts. While dwarven miners unearthed adamantine in the mountain’s depths, the unquiet minds of gnomish inventors conceived different applications for the mountain’s gifts.

Gnome alchemists labored without respite, combining volatile compounds and mysterious powders until they perfected a weapon of unprecedented devastation: explosives. As dwarven warriors clad in impenetrable adamantine charged the human forces, the air shattered with concussive blasts as gnomish artillery rained cannonballs and dynamite upon the enemy lines. Beneath this barrage, human forces broke in terror, witnessing what seemed a horrific new sorcery unleashed upon the world. Victory came at grievous cost to both gnome and dwarf. The gnomes followed their dwarven allies into centuries of isolation beneath the mountains—and when the dwarf king eventually led his people into the Bürach Empire, the gnomes accompanied them, trailing smoke and whispered secrets.

Strangely, gnomes maintain an uncanny connection to the natural world. Those who did not seek sanctuary within the mountains felt themselves inexplicably drawn back to ancestral territories deep within Etharis’s ancient forests—a peculiar affinity suggesting a shared lineage with the elves, though neither species acknowledges such kinship. While gnomes are common throughout the Bürach Empire, the often seek solace within Charneault.

#### Using Gnomes in Your Campaign

Gnomes provide Game Masters with perfect vessels for exploring themes of innovation gone awry and obsession’s dark consequences. Their inventive nature fits seamlessly into Grim Hollow’s gothic horror landscape when properly framed.

You could portray gnomish settlements as smoke-shrouded laboratory-towns where the line between genius and madness has long since blurred. Gnomish inventors might create clockwork creatures that develop unexpected sentience or explosives that produce supernatural side effects, like trapping souls or creating localized time distortions.

A reclusive gnome artificer makes an excellent recurring character—perhaps they supply the party with unusual gadgets while gradually revealing concerning signs of mental deterioration. Their workshop could feature increasingly disturbing experiments, with the gnome convinced they’re on the verge of a breakthrough despite the horrific costs. Strange mechanical chimeras whir in shadowed corners, half-alive and suffering. Walls covered in obsessive calculations contain subtle errors that worsen over time. The gnome’s inventions grow more effective yet ethically questionable—devices that harness pain, machines that whisper to users, or contraptions incorporating biological components harvested from missing townspeople. Most disturbing, the artificer genuinely believes their work serves a greater good, unable to recognize their own descent into mechanical madness.

### Halflings

Omar Samy As humanity’s relentless conquest of Etharis left countless civilizations broken in its wake, most peoples earned human wrath through resistance. Those who opposed the human tide were met with ruthless violence. Yet one people survived by embracing a different path—not through confrontation, but through calculated accommodation to the shadow of human dominance. The blood-soaked Era of Expansion touched halflings differently than other folk. These diminutive beings place little worth in material treasures such as gold or gemstones, making them willing to surrender such trinkets as tribute to the better-armed humans who offered a veneer of protection. Though possessing quiet courage and indomitable spirit, halflings harbor no grand ambitions of nation-building. Their modest settlements seamlessly transformed into provisions for human empires and kingdoms—fertile fields feeding the forces that had subjugated them. Halflings infiltrated human society by assuming roles deemed valuable: merchants who smile while calculating odds of survival, advisors who whisper half-truths, educators who teach only what is permitted, and scholars who record history as the victors wish it remembered. Though halflings privately abhorred humanity’s capacity for brutality, they maintained their silence—a calculated decision that ensured their continued existence through generations of human violence.

Halflings remain a tenacious yet outwardly peaceable folk. Most of these small-statured beings seek only tranquil corners in which to settle, far removed from the conflicts that periodically engulf Etharis. They cultivate appreciation for haunting melodies, exquisite cuisine, and moments of laughter when circumstances allow. They nurture a particular fascination for ancient lore, forbidden knowledge, and tales whispered in shadows. Yet this apparent docility masks an unexpected resilience. When cornered by threats they cannot evade through cunning or flight, halflings display a surprising ferocity. Chronicles of halfling adventurers who pursue hidden and dangerous knowledge rank among the most chilling tales recounted in hushed tones around their hearths.

For generations, halflings have methodically compiled the fragments of wisdom they gather into extensive private libraries devoted to particular subjects. Within these countless volumes reside centuries of accumulated knowledge concerning the peculiar interests and obsessions of these seemingly simple folk—domestic crafts that conceal secret purposes, the whispers of nature where no human ear listens, and histories unaltered by conquerors’ blades.

#### Using Halflings in Your Campaign

Halflings offer Game Masters unique opportunities to explore themes of hidden resistance and desperate survival under oppression. Their outward docility conceals depths that can generate compelling narratives.

A quaint halfling village could be an idyllic community harboring dark secrets beneath a pastoral facade. A simple small-town brewery ran by a halfling family might serve as a waystation in an underground network that smuggles forbidden relics or helps fugitives escape persecution. Behind their disarming smiles, halfling innkeepers could maintain elaborate spy networks, collecting secrets from world-weary travelers while appearing harmless.

A halfling librarian makes an excellent recurring villain—perhaps they’ve spent decades meticulously gathering forbidden knowledge while projecting an image of harmless scholarly dedication. This character could control an extensive network of seemingly innocent halfling travelers who collect dark artifacts or identify potential sacrifices for ancient rituals. Their specialized library might contain grimoires bound in human skin, records of successful possessions, or maps to places where the veil between worlds has thinned. When confronted, this villain could reveal generations of resentment against human dominance, twisted into a plan to summon entities that will restore “proper balance” to Etharis—regardless of the cost to innocent lives.

### Humans

Though ancient peoples like elves and dwarves claim the first kingdoms of Etharis as their legacy, humanity now casts its shadow across these lands. Humans infest every corner of the continent, from the bitter, unforgiving tundra of the North to the parched, cracked plains of the South. Historical records first note their emergence from the temperate woodlands in what would become the Bürach Empire—unremarkable creatures that the dominant races of Etharis dismissed as insignificant. Left unchecked, they multiplied with disquieting speed. Their adaptable nature and restless ambition eventually drove them beyond the boundaries of their birthplace. The blood-soaked chapter of history known as the Era of Expansion is often portrayed as an unending tide of violence that swept across the continent for centuries, as humans spread like a contagion to every corner of Etharis. In truth, this era encompasses countless separate conflicts that erupted in the wake of human migration. Humans did not initially dispatch armies, but settlers—seemingly innocuous in their individual intrusions. They felled ancient trees from the Grove Maze to construct their dwellings. They journeyed to the distant reaches of Valika to escape the yoke of their own southern kingdoms. When they claimed territories already inhabited by other peoples, conflict became inevitable. By the time the mighty kingdoms of elves and dwarves recognized the creeping danger of human encroachment, the opportunity for containment had already passed. The slaughter that followed forever altered the face of Etharis, washing it in rivers of blood. Omar Samy

Humanity’s terrible success stems from its adaptability. Their brief lifespans—mere moments compared to other peoples—cause their customs to wither quickly, preventing them from becoming ensnared by their own traditions. Every human culture bears the mark of another heritage in the territories they have claimed. In the duchies of the Charneault Kingdom, humans pay tribute to the Spirits of Nature as taught by the elves. The imposing fortresses and sprawling cities of the Bürach Empire incorporate dwarven stonework and architecture, appropriated rather than honored. In the present age, humans are acknowledged as the dominant people across Etharis, controlling vast swathes of land and sea. Yet their realms are far from uniform, each carrying its own blood-soaked history and amalgamation of peoples from other heritages—stories of conquest thinly veiled as cultural exchange.

Among all human vices, ambition proves the most pernicious. Humans maintain their stranglehold on positions of power within their societies. Many have laid claim to the High Throne of Altenheim, hungering to control the most formidable empire in existence. Known as the Era of Descent, the years following the Era of Expansion have witnessed the slow decay of each human domain. While common folk attribute this decline to the death of the gods, people of other heritages whisper that human hubris is the true cause. When no more realms remained to subjugate, humanity turned its greed, ambition, and violence inward—consuming itself as surely as it had consumed the lands of others.

#### Using Humans in Your Campaign

Humans provide Game Masters with perfect vessels for exploring Grim Hollow’s themes of corruption, ambition, and fall from grace. As the dominant heritage across Etharis, humans represent both the mundane terror of political power and the supernatural horror of those willing to make dark bargains for advancement.

If venturing north to Valika, characters may meet a renegade longship captain. Commanding the longship Frostfang with ruthless efficiency and unexpected honor, the captain was born to a prestigious Valikan clan. She fled an arranged marriage by stealing a vessel and gathering a crew of outcasts, fugitives, and wanderers. Now she sails the treacherous waters between her raiding past and a potential future as a legitimate merchant-adventurer.

Unlike most Valikan raiders, the Frostfang’s captain refuses to take captives, though she readily relieves southern ships of their treasures. A distinctive jagged silver scar runs across her right fist and occasionally gleams with faint blue light when her heart races—the result of a childhood encounter with coldfire.


### Dreamers

Suzanne Helmigh History consumes itself in endless cycles. Long before humans embarked upon their blood-soaked expansion, before elves sang in ancient groves or dwarves chiseled their first runes into mountain halls, another civilization rose to impossible heights before plummeting into oblivion. Beneath present-day Stehlenwald once stood the heart of a nameless empire thought to have eclipsed any that followed. For centuries, this civilization existed as whispered myths among elves and dwarves. Legends claim their downfall came when mortals dared challenge divine authority. Other tales speak of an empire devoured whole during the first cataclysm of The Aetheric War, leaving nothing but dust and silence. But something lingered in the dark. As humans spread across Etharis, forcing the Stehlenwald dwarves to carve deeper into their mountain sanctuaries, the desperate miners broke through chambers far below even the adamantine veins that became their salvation. These sealed vaults bore markings of inexplicable origin. When the ancient barriers were breached, forgotten enchantments dissolved, and the occupants of these lightless chambers stirred from a slumber that had lasted millennia.

In the final days before catastrophe consumed their civilization, a secretive order of mystics known as dreamers foresaw the approaching annihilation and conceived a desperate gambit for survival. Time flows differently in the realm of dreams, and these ancients wielded the forbidden magic of oneiromancy to sever themselves from time’s merciless current. Suspended in the liminal space between reality and dreamscape, they could exist in perpetual slumber for as long as necessary, outlasting the cataclysm that would erase all trace of their kind from the waking world.

Their desperate plan succeeded, but at a terrible cost. Existing for countless ages within the dream realm wrought profound and irreversible changes upon their minds. Upon waking, the dreamers discovered they could no longer distinguish between dreams and memories—both faded like morning mist from their fractured consciousness. What emerged was a people stripped of their own history, carrying only fragmented images and dreamlike impressions of a civilization that may have been real or merely the collective fantasy of their dream-addled minds.

Now these dreamers struggle to find their place in a world never meant for them. Predominantly found in settlements throughout the Rock-Teeth Mountains, where dwarven excavations first breached their ancient chambers, they maintain an uneasy coexistence with their discoverers. Whatever their true origins, they have demonstrated remarkable adaptability, their minds quick and bodies surprisingly resilient in this alien realm. Their prolonged slumber infused them with preternatural energy, allowing them to labor beyond even the legendary endurance of their dwarven neighbors. Yet sleep remains the state in which dreamers feel most authentic, most at home. They can slip into sudden slumber whenever no immediate task demands their attention—their consciousness sliding back into the dream realm as if drawn by unseen currents to a place more real than the waking world they now inhabit.

#### Using Dreamers in Your Campaign

Dreamers offer unique storytelling opportunities centered around lost knowledge, fractured identity, and the boundary between reality and dreams. Their presence in the Rock-Teeth Mountains creates natural adventure hooks.

Mysterious dreamers could be guides to ancient ruins only they can partially remember, their fragmented recollections revealing locations of pre-cataclysmic artifacts with dangerous powers. Their ability to slip between waking and dreaming states might allow them to perceive threats others cannot—spectral entities, dimensional anomalies, or psychic disturbances.

A dreamer NPC might experience prophetic nightmares of coming disasters, their warnings dismissed as delusions until too late. Alternatively, dreamer communities could unwittingly serve as conduits for ancient entities attempting to return through the dream realm.

### Grudgels

Ancient whispers murmur an age when orcs cast long shadows over the land. This heritage, rooted in the continent’s frozen North, is described in hushed tales as having spread like a plague to terrorize other peoples. Orcs were said to be fearsome warriors with an insatiable thirst for bloodshed. Yet accounts of their hordes nearly subjugating all of Etharis almost certainly represent the fevered imaginings of terrified outsiders, distorted by generations of fearful retelling. These same dark legends recount how the orcs abandoned their territories in a mass exodus—not fleeing from invaders or natural calamity, but heeding some mysterious ancestral summons to sail westward across the infinite sea. While historical records confirm that orcs indeed departed Etharis in a vast armada, a calling that would later echo in Thorgard av Holgar’s mind a century afterward, the notion that an entire people could simply vanish beyond the horizon strains credulity. The truth, as ever in Etharis, festers beneath layers of half-truths and deliberate obfuscation. Gonzalo Vega

Those who carry the bloodline of the orcs in present-day Etharis name themselves grudgels. Their appearance, at odds with the nightmarish depictions of their forebears, leads the ignorant to believe orcs and grudgels constitute entirely different peoples. Yet what truly divides them is merely the relentless march of time and the metamorphosis of culture across generations.

Grudgels remain imposing figures whose physical prowess has not diminished since the days when tales of orc warriors struck terror in the hearts of other folk. Yet even in the Valikan Clans where their numbers are greatest, grudgels demonstrate no greater inclination toward violence or the warrior’s path than any other people. Among their ranks are skilled artisans, solitary wanderers, wielders of arcane forces, and those who commune with the night sky. They alone preserve the arcane craft of forging stryllum—an otherworldly substance formed when starlight congeals into glass through processes they guard jealously. Grudgels are industrious, peaceful among allies, and possess an uncanny ability to maintain composure in dire circumstances. Yet they remain perfectly capable of unleashing terrible violence upon those who threaten their existence or seek conflict in the merciless wilderness they call home.

Beyond the North, grudgels remain such uncommon sights across Etharis that most are more likely to encounter terrified rumors of their kind than an actual grudgel in flesh and blood. While those who serve as protectors or sell their martial skills may exploit such fearful whispers to their advantage, the single thread that appears to bind all grudgels is a profound reluctance to discuss or acknowledge the ancient legends of their orc ancestors. Whether this aversion stems from a desire to distance themselves from their forebears’ violent legacy, or from some secret knowledge regarding the true reason for the orcs’ disappearance from Etharis, remains a mystery they keep locked behind their tusked grimaces.

#### Using Grudgels in Your Campaign

Grudgels can be at the center of stories dealing with forgotten history and misconception in Etharis. Their imposing presence combined with many grudgels’ unexpected calm demeanors creates a compelling character contradiction.

Consider introducing grudgel craftspeople who trade in mysterious stryllum artifacts with unusual properties—perhaps items that glow in proximity to supernatural threats or allow glimpses across dimensional boundaries. Their workshops might contain star charts mapping celestial alignments with ominous significance.

A grudgel NPC could serve as a stoic guide through northern territories, their knowledge of ancient paths suggesting familiarity with secrets predating recorded history. Their calm in the face of horror might stem from ancestral knowledge of worse things lurking beyond Etharis. As the party journeys with them, they might notice the grudgel performing subtle rituals at certain crossroads or placing carved stryllum tokens at seemingly random locations. When questioned, they offer only cryptic explanations about “keeping boundaries intact” or “honoring old agreements.” This behavior suggests grudgels aren’t merely descendants of orcs who left—they’re guardians maintaining ancient wards against whatever drove their ancestors to flee.

### Laneshi

Suzanne Helmigh Far beneath the churning waves that crash against Etharis’s eastern shores lies the sunless realm of the Llana’Shi Empire, home to beings that surface-dwellers call laneshi. Hauntingly alien in appearance, these pallid humanoids with writhing tendrils of kelp-like hair trailing from their skulls are creatures born of the abyssal depths, possessed of the unnerving ability to commune with the flora and fauna of their lightless domain. Laneshi society functions through a worldview of stark, uncompromising absolutes and fundamental duality. Light or dark. Acceptance or banishment. Ally or adversary. Their culture writhes along the permeable boundary separating life from death. They converse freely with spirits seeking guidance from beyond mortality’s veil and regard their eventual demise not with dread but as merely another state of being. Every element within laneshi society must occupy its predetermined place, their civilization built upon a rigid caste hierarchy that reflects this unwavering perspective.

The mystic caste encompasses all laneshi born as twins—a disturbingly common occurrence among their kind. The first-born twin is invariably inducted into the mystic caste, while its sibling is designated as their spiritual anchor. Through a potent necromantic ceremony, the second twin is sacrificed, its soul forcibly bound within the surviving sibling’s flesh. Each member of the mystic caste therefore houses two souls within a single vessel—one living and one eternally trapped—granting them perception into the realm of spirits and enhancing their necromantic capabilities. Mystics preside over burial ceremonies, crafting, construction, historical documentation, and the preparation of sustenance. The more laborious responsibilities of this caste are performed with the assistance of reanimated corpses—a sight that would inspire madness in most surface-dwellers who value the sanctity of final rest.

The warrior caste comprises not only the martial forces of the laneshi but those charged with diplomatic relations, cultivation of food sources, and the rearing and instruction of young. This caste roughly doubles the mystic caste in size, organized as a meritocracy where exceptional deeds elevate one’s standing within the hierarchy.

Laneshi warriors engage in perpetual conflict with other denizens of the deep. Yet simultaneously, the rulers of the Llana’Shi Empire have begun to direct their gaze toward the world above the waves for purposes yet unclear. Perhaps some ancient horror stirs in the lightless trenches of the ocean floor, driving the laneshi to seek desperate alliance with those who breathe air. Or perhaps there is terrible truth hidden within fearful whispers that these aquatic visitors have forged unholy pacts with primordial evils, and now the laneshi search for fresh territories to subjugate in service to an unspeakable master whose hunger knows no bounds.

#### Using Laneshi in Your Campaign

Laneshi characters are powerful tools for introducing cosmic horror, a core theme of Grim Hollow, to your campaign. Their alien culture and necromantic practices create natural tension when they interact with surface dwellers. Furthermore, the deep sea has always been a place as mysterious as the stars.

Your stories could feature laneshi emissaries suddenly arriving at coastal settlements to offer strange gifts or seek arcane knowledge. Their emotionless demeanor and casual use of undead servants would horrify locals, providing a distraction from much darker intentions.

A laneshi mystic makes an excellent recurring character—perhaps seeking surface allies against some greater deep-sea threat while struggling to understand surface taboos about death. Their dual-souled nature could manifest as sudden personality shifts or conversations with their imprisoned twin that others cannot hear. Players might witness the mystic arguing with themselves in moments of stress, their voice alternating between commanding and pleading tones. The imprisoned twin could possess knowledge unknown to the living host, revealing secrets at crucial moments or temporarily taking control in situations reminiscent of their sacrifice. This internal conflict might intensify as the mystic spends more time among surface dwellers, with the dead twin growing increasingly resentful of their eternal imprisonment or perhaps developing unexpected empathy for the surface world’s perspective on death—creating a moral dilemma that challenges the mystic’s rigid cultural assumptions.

### Ogresh

Suzanne Helmigh The prevalent cultures of Etharis whisper myriad tales of the ogresh, though few have encountered these imposing beings in the flesh. Folklore portrays them as reclusive, sagacious figures who serve as wellsprings of arcane knowledge and counsel for neighboring communities. Countless legends describe protagonists receiving cryptic guidance from an ogresh before embarking upon perilous journeys, while others speak of court advisors with distinctive broad features who whisper secrets into royal ears. Such narratives contain kernels of truth, as ogresh often possess insights gleaned during extensive wanderings in their formative years. Yet even beyond these mythic accounts, those familiar with the ogresh frequently regard them with a mixture of fascination and unease—as aberrations that have somehow assumed humanoid form.

The true rarity of the ogresh stems from peculiarities of their biological nature. Young ogresh develop at an achingly slow pace, and throughout a protracted adolescence spanning decades, they are driven by an insatiable wanderlust that borders on compulsion. This primal urge forces them to roam in search of territories suitable for permanent habitation—areas possessing abundant natural resources, established settlements of sentient beings, and a conspicuous absence of other ogresh. Upon selecting a location, an ogresh transitions into the second phase of their existence, marked by a voracious, almost unnatural appetite and an increasingly sedentary lifestyle. A single ogresh could effortlessly consume the surplus provisions of an entire village, explaining the evolutionary necessity of their wandering instinct and solitary nature.

Given their dependence on other folk for sustenance, many ogresh naturally develop an uncanny mastery of social manipulation. Yet some who have gazed too long into their deep-set eyes maintain that the ogresh capacity to extract insights from others possesses a disquieting, almost supernatural quality—as though they perceive more than merely what is spoken, gleaning secrets others would never willingly divulge. Whether this represents an evolved survival adaptation or something more sinister remains a subject of hushed debate in settlements harboring these enigmatic giants.


#### Using Ogresh in Your Campaign

Ogresh let Game Masters play with the expectations many players have about giants. These giants are not brutish or single-minded and can make powerful, if alien, allies. Their imposing presence and unsettling insight, coupled with a generally friendly demeanor, creates opportunities to present Grim Hollow’s themes of hidden darkness.

Consider portraying an ogresh as a village’s respected advisor whose counsel, while effective, subtly serves their own appetite for resources. This advisor might have decades of experience manipulating local politics to ensure continued prosperity—and surplus food—while maintaining a benevolent facade. Their home could be filled with mementos and journals from their wandering years that contain valuable but cryptic information about distant threats.

For adventures, an ogresh settlement site might contain remnants of previous civilizations they’ve outlived, with disturbing implications about their longevity and the fate of communities that no longer produced sufficient resources. Ancient ogresh might possess knowledge of forgotten rituals or the true origins of arcane curses.


### Accursed

The accursed stand as the most enigmatic and scarce among all heritages that walk Etharis—for each accursed exists as a bloodline unto themselves. Whether forged through aberrant magical convergence, delivered into the world through yawning planar rifts or shimmering extradimensional doorways, or embodying the final vestiges of a lineage presumed erased from history’s pages, an accursed being possesses traits that defy categorization, manifestations of a singular existence unbound by conventional heritage. This haunted designation stems not necessarily from their origins in malevolent enchantment, witch’s compact, corrupted arcane formula, or other traditional interpretations of “curse” within the realms of sorcery. Rather, “accursed” reflects the grim reality of how the wider populace of Etharis perceives such individuals—as walking omens, living aberrations whose very existence violates the natural order. Only those rare souls who venture beyond fearful whispers to comprehend the accursed’s true nature might glimpse the person beneath the unsettling exterior. This heritage exists to liberate imagination, encouraging the creation of characters whose essence transcends established bloodlines while remaining tethered to Etharis’s bleak tapestry. Siraj Fakhri

Unlike other heritages, the accursed rarely represent a collective people or shared culture, instead standing as solitary anomalies in a world unprepared for their existence. One such being might manifest as a halfling-sized corvid possessed of human speech, hatched from a petrified basilisk egg during the witching hour of a blood moon. Throughout the breadth of Etharis, no other creature shares their peculiar composition, their chosen heritage traits reflecting this singular nature. Another might embody the form and capabilities of a lizardfolk—a heritage absent from Etharis’s recognized bloodlines. Whether this accursed represents a solitary wanderer from some forgotten scaled enclave lurking within the fetid depths of the Black Mire, or the offspring of elven parents subjected to a malevolent sorcerer’s spite, their selection of heritage traits carves out a unique narrative within a world that instinctively recoils from their presence.

#### Using Accursed in Your Campaign

The accursed invite players and Game Masters to explore what it means to be truly alone in a world that fears the unknown. Many accursed may seek vengeance on a world that hates them simply for how they were born while others may find purpose in their situation and seek to change Etharis for the better.

The appearance of an accursed in a remote village might trigger superstitious panic, witch hunts, or desperate rituals to ward off perceived omens. These reactions create immediate tension as players must navigate between protecting the innocent accursed and addressing the legitimate fears of locals who may have experienced previous supernatural horrors.

Your campaign might feature a traveling carnival known for showcasing “living curiosities,” including an accursed known as the Ashen Songbird, whose haunting voice produces melodies thought impossible. This slender figure appears mostly humanoid but with skin that shifts between translucent gray and wisps of smoke, revealing glimpses of organs composed of smoldering embers. Their eyes glow amber, and when they sing, listeners hear multiple voices in impossible harmony.

The performer wears ornate manacles inscribed with binding runes disguised as decorative bracelets, preventing them from straying beyond the carnival grounds. The carnival master introduces them as a being born from the ashes of a great fire that consumed a village, though the tale changes regularly.

Between performances, the accursed remains confined to a reinforced wagon with a special chimney to vent the smoke that occasionally billows from their mouth. Fellow performers maintain their distance—some from fear, others from guilt.

### Arisen

Abdelkarim Guessous The arisen exist in hushed, trembling discourse throughout Etharis, all too frequently condemned as abhorrent anomalies beyond trust or salvation. Although they appear humanoid, each arisen stands as a singular construct born of arcane manipulation, obscure science, or the unholy marriage of both disciplines. Arisen are incredibly rare and often live lonely lives but are most often found in Ostoya and Stehlenwald, where dark arcane experimentation is commonplace.

No arisen enters the world through natural birth. They are not the undead, nor creatures restored from death’s embrace by divine intervention. Rather, each arisen is meticulously assembled and granted the spark of consciousness, typically by the hands of a creator whose motives remain their own. Arisen are indeed thinking beings, possessing distinct personalities, intellects, and lived experiences like all sentient creatures. Yet the question that haunts their existence remains—do they harbor souls within their fabricated forms? This metaphysical quandary plagues each arisen throughout their unnatural existence.

Arisen bodies consist predominantly of organic material. They are not mere automatons, though many bear mechanical components to varying degrees. One might possess a single limb of articulated brass and steel, while another’s vital organs float suspended in a network of fluid-filled vessels. Some arisen feature exposed wiring penetrating their flesh, connected to arcane power sources fused to their spines, while others appear deceptively ordinary save for telling scars and the enchanted gemstones embedded within their eye cavities.

Some arisen are entirely fabricated, constructed according to precise specifications or esoteric designs. Others begin their existence through seemingly benign experiments to replace failing organs or severed limbs, with successive procedures inexorably eroding all connection to their original selves. Still others emerge from living beings who perished or suffered catastrophic injury before being “reconstituted.” Regardless of the process that spawned them, an arisen’s creation typically proves traumatic—manifesting as fractured recollections of arcane laboratories and the first flickering moments of awareness, or as the persistent nightmares that constitute the only remnants of a former existence.

Their scarcity among Etharis’s populations and the grim rumors that cling to them like shrouds often force arisen into isolation. Yet not all succumb to nihilism in the face of their bleak origins and lonely existence. From that very trauma, many develop a penetrating self-awareness that fuels an insatiable curiosity and a uniquely profound perspective on matters of existence, mortality, and their precarious place within a world that regards them with fear and suspicion.

#### Using Arisen in Your Campaign

Arisen characters bring classic body horror to your Grim Hollow campaigns. Their unnatural existence raises profound questions about personhood and invokes visceral discomfort. In a world full of horrors, NPCs may rightfully fear an arisen, providing a great opportunity to create tension. Additionally, you could present a character arc wherein an NPC’s worldview is challenged by a particularly humane arisen.

Shadowy academies might secretly produce arisen as experiments in mortality, with escaped specimens becoming either allies or antagonists in your campaign. A brilliant but amoral physician operating from a hidden laboratory could create arisen servants using parts harvested from fresh graves, drawing the party’s attention when local cemeteries are desecrated.

The party may come into conflict with an arisen physician-wizard who works to improve the lives of other arisen. They have altered their appearance to be somewhat more accepted by the commonfolk and use the wealth they have amassed to lobby for and support their fellow arisen. Their medical work is dedicated to making life more comfortable for arisen.

However, their compassion toward arisen contrasts horrifically with their attitude toward “the soft.” Viewing humans, elves, and others as merely raw materials for experimentation, the arisen doctor abducts subjects under cover of darkness. These unwilling donors provide components for their procedures, their screams muffled by thick stone walls.

When confronted with their crimes, they display genuine confusion, unable to comprehend why society values “soft” lives while rejecting the arisen. This moral blindness makes them tragically sympathetic yet unquestionably monstrous, creating difficult ethical questions for characters who discover their work.

### Dhampir

A dhampir bears the weight of a terrible inheritance—a mortal half-damned by vampiric taint, existing in the agonizing threshold between life and undeath. These cursed beings carry the sinister aspects of their undead progenitors, yet their lingering mortality spares them from a vampire’s most crippling weaknesses. Like the predators whose blood flows within their veins, dhampir hunger for the crimson essence of the living, though they are not wholly enslaved by this craving if their resolve proves sufficient to withstand the constant whispers of thirst. Invariably born from other heritages, dhampir manifest with extreme rarity and display vast diversity in appearance and disposition. The sole trait binding all dhampir is the grim legacy of their unnatural conception. True vampires embody death itself, mimicking vitality by twisting corpses into undead servants or spreading their affliction to spawn more of their kind. Yet vampires cannot naturally reproduce or create anything genuinely alive. Thus, dhampir typically emerge from some manner of necromantic aberration—a child at death’s threshold revived through forbidden arts, an alchemist blending herbs with vampire ichor, or a desperate attempt to reverse vampirism gone terribly awry. Abdelkarim Guessous

Regardless of their origins, most dhampir endure an existence rooted in profound trauma and pervasive isolation. Common folk who fear these half-bloods view them as blood-thirsting abominations, despite the fact that most sustain themselves primarily through ordinary nourishment. Simultaneously, pure-blooded vampires despise them as diluted, imperfect shadows of true vampiric power. A dhampir’s life thus becomes an endless search for belonging, whether struggling to assimilate into mortal society or attempting to earn approval from their blood-drinking ancestors.

A dhampir who chooses to dwell among mortals often suffers relentless guilt. Their craving for blood may grow overwhelmingly intense as they desperately cling to their humanity and capacity for compassion. Dhampir are branded as monsters by the superstitious masses. Yet even those rare mortals who embrace a dhampir as family can never truly comprehend their internal battle against the predator that lurks beneath their skin, waiting for a moment of weakness to emerge.

Other dhampir surrender to their darker nature, embracing their bloodlust without reservation. Some even contend for position within Ostoya’s vampire nobility. Despite lacking conventional parentage, a dhampir may form a perverse familial bond with the vampire they consider their sire. Vampires who exploit this relationship gain a valuable servant capable of walking in daylight and integrating more seamlessly with mortal communities. A dhampir in such circumstances might serve as spy, steward, or herald to their master. Yet they remain acutely aware that they shall never attain positions of genuine authority within vampire hierarchy—forever caught between two worlds, belonging fully to neither.

#### Using Dhampirs in Your Campaign

Dhampirs embody the quintessential gothic horror theme of the monster within. Their dual nature creates perfect story vehicles for exploring inner darkness and societal rejection. On the other hand, dhampirs could be insurrectionists, looking to overthrow their vampire masters using the strength granted to them from the same bloodline.

Whispered rumors of a dhampir apothecary selling miracle cures might draw players to a reclusive figure whose medicines contain traces of their own blood—effective but potentially addictive. Those who consume these remedies develop subtle connections to the dhampir, experiencing glimpses of their memories or cravings.

Noble vampire families might secretly employ dhampir “cousins” to protect their interests during daylight hours, while vampire elders dispatch dhampir agents to investigate threats without risking themselves. These agents walk a dangerous line, neither trusted by their masters nor accepted by those they infiltrate.

### Disembodied

Suzanne Helmigh To pierce the veil between worlds remains the forbidden aspiration of countless arcane practitioners, and nowhere did that ambition come closer to fruition than within the vanished city of Ulmyr’s Gate. Established in the Bürach Empire by a cabal of audacious wizards who bristled against the constraints imposed by imperial authorities, Ulmyr’s Gate offered unfettered study to all mages who sought refuge behind its walls. The Great College dedicated to arcane pursuit quickly transformed into a sanctuary for practitioners of all disciplines from across Etharis, ushering in a brief, brilliant epoch of magical advancement within its boundaries.

This luminous age collapsed in a single night of hubris, when the founding mages of Ulmyr’s Gate attempted a ritual of unprecedented scope designed to cleave the veil and establish a permanent threshold to the Ethereal Plane. Instead, their spellwork rent an immense wound in the fabric of reality, and in one catastrophic moment, the entire city was cast into the void between worlds. Ulmyr’s Gate and all its inhabitants were presumed obliterated. The disaster precipitated another savage inquisition against arrogant arcanists who meddled in forces mortals were never meant to comprehend. Soon after, the empire’s attention turned elsewhere, and the tragedy faded from memory.

Years later, unsettling accounts began to circulate of spectral figures glimpsed in the region where Ulmyr’s Gate once stood. These haunting entities appeared strangely insubstantial or incomplete—witnesses reported that they would dissolve into nothingness as abruptly as they manifested. Gradually, the terrible truth emerged: these wretched souls were survivors of the Ethereal Rift, now trapped in the liminal space between dimensions, desperately striving to maintain their tenuous connection to the Material Plane.

Those former denizens of lost Ulmyr’s Gate who have summoned the will to return to the mortal realm have been designated “the disembodied” by the scholars who have examined their condition. These rare individuals have abandoned or escaped their former home, traversing the Ethereal Rift to wander the mortal world. Though they bear semblance to their former selves, they are simultaneously more and less than human—entities existing between realities, perpetually haunted by memories of their city’s annihilation, of which they seldom if ever speak. Their eyes reflect glimpses of the void between worlds, and in moments of distress, their forms may flicker or partially fade—reminders that their grasp on corporeal existence remains forever precarious.

#### Using Disembodied in Your Campaign

The haunting presence of disembodied beings offers unique horror elements perfect for Grim Hollow campaigns. Their fractured existence bridges physical and ethereal realities, creating encounters where the laws of nature bend unpredictably.

Small rural communities near the former site of Ulmyr’s Gate might experience periodic “bleeds” when the barrier between worlds thins. During these events, buildings partially transpose with ethereal counterparts, landmarks appear that aren’t there by daylight, and disembodied visitors manifest seeking objects or people they’ve lost. These incidents create perfect investigation scenarios where players must piece together fragmented realities.

Ambitious nobles might secretly capture disembodied to extract ethereal essence for arcane experiments. These nobles believe studying these beings could unlock the secret to transcending death itself, creating unsettling scenarios where seemingly benevolent patrons have sinister motivations.

The ruins of Ulmyr’s Gate itself exist simultaneously in multiple planes, with different areas accessible depending on atmospheric conditions, magical resonances, or specific rituals. Players venturing into these ruins might find themselves shifting between planes unexpectedly, encountering fragments of the city’s final moments frozen in time.

A disembodied arcanist might serve as a reluctant mentor to magic-wielding characters, offering knowledge of forbidden spells while exhibiting disturbing behaviors—disappearing mid-conversation, speaking to entities others cannot perceive, or unconsciously manipulating reality around them during emotional moments.

### Downcast

Suzanne Helmigh At the culmination of The Gods’ End, thousands of divine souls fell from the sky like burning stars. A host of celestial beings were thrown from the heavens, condemned to live in the mortal realms among the people of Etharis, bereft of divine powers they once held. Without their deities, each of the Arch Seraphs took their patron's mantle and attempted to impose their will upon the world cast into disarray. The Arch Seraphs were the most powerful divine beings that remained who still possessed a semblance of their divine power following Gods' End. But an angel is not a god. The Arch Seraphs cannot embody all aspects of the divine domains they once served, with some focusing on a narrow set of virtues. Others cast off their divine grace to instead become feared Arch Daemons who cultivated mortal vice. Those divine souls who fell from the heavens to endure mortal lives are called the downcast.

Many downcast are no more. For some, the sickness and despair they suffered after their fall from grace was too much to bear. Some of those who remain still serve the Arch Seraphs seeking to recover the divine order that was lost. Others have accepted their new mortal existance, ignoring the divine host to which they once belong. Still others have chosen to follow the Arch Daemons, embittered by their fall and gaining pleasure by spreading fear and destruction.

Those who were cast down from the heavens still retain a small portion of their previous angelic form. This might manifest in their eyes, which still faintly glow with divine light. For others, faded Celestial runes still mark their once perfect skin. Those who have embraced an Arch Daemon's corruption might present subtle fiendish traits such as cracked skin, devilish horns, or a faint scent of sulfur that follows in their wake.

#### Using Downcast in Your Campaign

Downcast rarely settle into communities with others of their kind. For some, seeing the constant reminder of who they once were in the mortal form of others is too painful. Some prefer the company of true mortal beings, soaking in everything that it means to live in the constant pain and doubt of mortality. Others still seek to dwell in the realm of sin and debauchery, and they don’t want the judging glares of their peers haunting their revelry.

Downcast characters are affected in some way by the loss of their immortality and divinity—one cannot simply forget when they were once immortal and imbued with divine power. That loss might be crushing, an excruciating loss that leaves an unfillable void. The loss might be a challenge, a call to prove one’s worth even though the tools to achieve glory are much duller than before. It might be a call to oblivion, a tacit permission to rush headlong toward a void that is waiting to be filled with your mortal essence and weakened, but potentially immortal, soul.

Other mortals may not notice a downcast's former divine presence unless they look closely. Once they recognize what they are—or more precisely what they were—others might look at the downcast with a variety of emotions and attitudes. Pity is common, as mortals try to grapple with the loss they can only imagine must have been endured. Hope can shine through others’ eyes towards a downcast's direction, as they are reminded that if gods once oversaw their lives, they might again someday, but fear is never far behind. Divine powers once wielded for good may still be adrift in the world, untethered from their once altruistic forms, and perhaps, now used for ill. There’s always something else, some emotion that is unknown and unnamable but that nevertheless reminds the downcast that they are different, and always will be.


### Wechselkind

Suzanne Helmigh A mother wakes to a faint sound in the midnight hours and anxiously inspects her slumbering infant. All appears undisturbed. Yet weeks later, an unseen enchantment dissolves and a nightmare is unveiled: her true child has been spirited away by the fey, a wechselkind abandoned in its place. For the faerie are ancient, callous entities, unchanging through centuries—and nothing ignites their terrible fascination and bitter envy more than the malleable, radiant essence of a young mortal child. A wechselkind is a construct meticulously fashioned from wood, clay, and porcelain fragments in the semblance of a small child, animated through inscrutable faerie enchantments and shrouded in illusion that renders them indistinguishable from the stolen mortal infant. Once that glamour dissipates and the cruel deception stands revealed, a wechselkind is typically banished by the abducted child’s family, if not destroyed outright in grief-stricken rage. On rare occasions, a family might show mercy to the forlorn creature and attempt to nurture them, only to discover that while their mind develops naturally, a wechselkind remains eternally imprisoned in the unchanging form of a childlike doll—a grotesque mockery of growth and maturation.

Whether grudgingly tolerated or violently rejected, most wechselkind eventually find themselves existing as societal outcasts, forced to survive through whatever means their artificial nature permits. The lingering echoes of their faerie glamour allow a wechselkind to disguise themselves for brief intervals, either as the child they replaced or as a diminutive adult such as a halfling, gnome, or other being of comparable stature. With minimal physical requirements for survival, a wechselkind can drift between settlements, observing the mortals they encounter with covetous eyes, eternally searching for acceptance that perpetually eludes them.

With the devastating spread of the Weeping Pox across Etharis, many wechselkind have emerged from self-imposed exile. Their immunity to disease enables them to assist healers in plague-ravaged communities, earning a measure of respect—or even begrudging admiration—from those capable of seeing beyond their unsettling origins. Nevertheless, even wechselkind who achieve conditional acceptance among mortals typically remain vigilant, haunted by the certainty that once their utility expires, they will inevitably face abandonment once more—eternal children with ancient eyes, never truly belonging to either the mortal world or the faerie realm that created and discarded them.

#### Using Wechselkind in Your Campaign

Wechselkind inject profound tragedy into Grim Hollow’s horror landscape. Their dual nature as both victims and unwitting agents of fey mischief creates morally complex scenarios that challenge player assumptions.

Rural villages might harbor old superstitions about wechselkind, leading to witch hunts when children fall mysteriously ill. Players must determine if an accused wechselkind is responsible or merely a convenient scapegoat for human cruelty. These investigations reveal how fear transforms communities into monsters more terrible than those they hunt.

The party may come across a dilapidated workshop at a village’s edge housing a wechselkind artisan whose exquisite, handcrafted toys display uncanny lifelikeness. Rejected decades ago, they create these playthings as gifts for children who can experience the childhood the toymaker will never know.

The wechselkind may seek help bringing their creations to market, unable to venture into town without facing persecution. Their toys—dolls with glass eyes that seem to follow movement, music boxes that play melodies no one taught them, and wooden animals that occasionally move when unobserved—sell remarkably well despite their unsettling quality.

Unknown to their creator, faint fey magic is woven into the toys, causing children who possess them to experience vivid dreams of the faerie realm. Some begin sleepwalking toward the woods, others develop sudden personality changes, and a few report “invisible friends” who whisper secrets.

### Wulven

Throughout Etharis, from wilderness edge to settled heartland, in woodland hovels and isolated homesteads, dread of lycanthropy burrows deep into mortal hearts. Yet few ever comprehend that the towering stranger they glimpsed on the fog-shrouded trade road at twilight, the forest recluse who materialized without warning to caution of lurking perils ahead, or the enigmatic druidic devotee guarding an ancient spring harbor no such terror. These beings have already been marked by the lycanthropic taint, and have embraced the primal gifts it bestows upon their blood. Wulven are the natural offspring or descendants of one cursed with lycanthropy, inheriting not the curse in its entirety but instead bearing its feral essence within their veins. Those born with this bloodline are most commonly associated with werewolves in the fearful minds of common folk, inspiring the name thrust upon them. Yet wulven are equally likely to descend from werebears, wererats, wereravens, or more obscure and terrible forms of lycanthropy that haunt the darkest corners of Etharis. Konstantin Porubov

The diluted strain of lycanthropy flowing through a wulven’s lineage means they cannot assume animal or hybrid forms like their accursed forebears. They remain as composed and sociable as any folk, suffering neither loss of control nor mindless bloodthirst beneath the full moon’s pale light. Nevertheless, the untamed nature of the beast they carry within their blood drives most wulven toward lives of isolation and the hushed solitude of wilderness realms.

Wulven embody the mysticism of the primal world rather than its brutality. Where they traverse forest and untamed scrubland, their wild countenance and solitary habits often lead them to be mistaken for fey spirits, druids, or true lycanthropes. Many forge existences as members of secretive druidic circles or as silent guardians of small, sequestered communities. Some serve as emissaries or champions of the fey courts, though the stain of cursed magic within wulven blood causes certain fey beings to regard them with suspicion. Ultimately, most wulven exist as perpetual outsiders, wary around strangers for fear their solitary disposition and subtly bestial features—elongated canines glimpsed during rare smiles, eyes that catch the light in unnerving ways, the occasional low growl that accompanies moments of stress—will be misinterpreted as signs of the very curse they have been spared, yet can never fully escape.

#### Using Wulven in Your Campaign

Wulven bridge the gap between civilization and wilderness in Etharis, creating perfect intermediaries for stories exploring humanity’s relationship with nature’s darker aspects.

Villages on the edge of forests in Ostoya, Valika, and Charneault Kingdom might rely on wulven guides who navigate territories where true lycanthropes hunt. These guides walk a precarious line—valued for their knowledge yet always suspected when villagers disappear. This suspicion intensifies during full moons, despite the wulven’s immunity to lunar influence, creating dramatic irony as players witness witch-hunt mentality forming against the very protectors keeping true monsters at bay.

Mountain passes guarded by solitary wulven sentinels offer safe passage in exchange for small offerings or news from distant lands. These gatekeepers maintain ancient traditions, marking territorial boundaries between human settlements and primal forces best left undisturbed. The wulven might warn travelers away from certain paths not because of physical danger, but because those routes lead to places where reality thins, allowing influence from Primordial Realms to seep through.

Druidic circles might include wulven members who serve as intermediaries between the circle and local communities, appearing during festivals to perform rituals ensuring continued harmony. Their presence creates tension—does their magic protect the village from supernatural threats, or are they subtly preparing the community for some darker purpose guided by ancient woodland powers?
