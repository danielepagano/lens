# Chapter 10: Folk of Etharis

The people of Etharis are hardy folk shaped by a world of hardship and monsters. Humans dominate the continent, while elder peoples and rarer heritages share these lands — often to suspicion, fear, and outright violence. Eldritch heritages are frequently found outside city walls, making lives in the dark.

## Common Heritages

### Dragonborn

Dragonborn are scaled, imposing figures whose draconic features — jagged scales, deadly talons, fearsome jaws — mark them as something ancient and dangerous. Their civilization fell to ruin before the Era of Expansion; when human missionaries offered answers for the silence of their gods, dragonborn converted to the Eternal Dogma with desperate fervor. Today they are among the most zealous adherents of the Arcanist Inquisition, making fearsome inquisitors and missionaries. NPCs will often be wary of dragonborn yet deferential if the character bears church authority.

### Dwarves

Carved from the stone they revere, dwarves are stoic, prosperous, and deeply scarred by generational trauma. Their mountain kingdoms were shattered during the Era of Expansion; survivors made pacts with the Bürach Empire and emerged as respected craftspeople and merchants. Beneath the outward affluence runs centuries of grudges. A dwarf NPC may smile at imperial officials by day while nursing private resentments about lost sovereignty.

### Elves

Ancient and hauntingly beautiful, elves trace their lineage to the nature spirits of primeval forests. Their great kingdoms were burned during the Era of Expansion, and they retreated into the deepest woods before eventually forming the Charneault Kingdom with humanity — a compromise born of necessity. Elves compose mournful art about their lost paradise, call themselves *ulufey* (mortal fey), and harbor secret hope that the faerie courts will restore what was taken. They carry an air of melancholy beauty with a dark undercurrent.

### Gnomes

Slight, relentlessly inventive, and prone to obsession, gnomes share distant ancestry with dwarves but channel it into creation rather than endurance. They gave the world explosives, clockwork automatons, and cannons — often without considering the consequences. Their settlements feel like laboratory-towns where the line between genius and madness has blurred. Gnome NPCs tend to be brilliant, distracted, and ethically careless about their work.

### Halflings

Halflings survived the Era of Expansion through calculated accommodation — paying tribute, infiltrating human society as advisors, merchants, and educators, and compiling centuries of forbidden knowledge in private libraries. Their outward docility conceals fierce resilience and deep resentment. When cornered, they are dangerous. Halfling NPCs often know far more than they reveal and pursue long private agendas beneath a pleasant facade.

### Humans

The dominant people of Etharis, humans spread across the continent through violence and settlement during the Era of Expansion. Their greatest strength is adaptability — their short lives prevent them from being trapped by tradition. Their greatest flaw is ambition, which turned inward after there were no more peoples to conquer. Human NPCs represent every shade of the moral spectrum and drive most of Etharis's political conflict.

## Rare Heritages

### Dreamers

Ancient mystics who sealed themselves in dreamscapes to survive a forgotten cataclysm, dreamers were unearthed by dwarven miners beneath Stehlenwald after millennia of slumber. The dream realm has permanently damaged their sense of reality — they cannot reliably distinguish memories from dreams. They fall into sudden sleep whenever unstimulated, feeling most authentic in the dream world. They are found primarily in the Rock-Teeth Mountains, where they coexist uneasily with their discoverers.

### Grudgels

Descendants of orcs — a people who departed Etharis en masse in a mysterious ancestral calling — grudgels bear their forebears' imposing physique but none of their mythologized savagery. Most common in Valika, they are artisans, wanderers, and guardians. They alone know the craft of forging stryllum, a material formed when starlight congeals into glass. Grudgels are conspicuously reluctant to discuss their orc ancestry, suggesting they hold a secret about why the orcs truly left.

### Laneshi

Pallid, hauntingly alien humanoids from the sunless Llana'Shi Empire beneath Etharis's eastern seas. Their society operates on rigid duality — all things are ally or adversary, life or death. The mystic caste houses two souls in each body: one living, one ritually sacrificed and bound within the survivor. They use reanimated corpses for labor. Laneshi are now directing their gaze toward the surface world, for reasons unclear — desperation, expansion, or dark compact. Surface encounters with laneshi carry an undercurrent of cosmic dread.

### Ogresh

Enormous, solitary figures of rare occurrence, ogresh spend decades wandering during their long adolescence before settling into an intensely sedentary second phase marked by voracious appetite. Many develop uncanny social insight — bordering on supernatural perception — to ensure their continued access to food. Folklore paints them as cryptic sages; in practice they are often manipulative community advisors whose counsel quietly serves their own comfort.

## Eldritch Heritages

### Accursed

The accursed are singular anomalies — each a unique being born of aberrant magic, planar rifts, or forgotten bloodlines. They represent no collective people and share no common culture. Each is perceived by Etharis society as a walking omen or living aberration. The heritage is a catch-all for characters whose origins defy categorization: they might appear as corvid-like beings, unfamiliar species, or creatures touched by something unknowable.

### Arisen

Constructed beings — assembled from organic and sometimes mechanical components, animated by arcane or scientific means, and granted consciousness by a creator's intent. Arisen are not undead and not born; they are *made*. Each grapples with questions of whether they possess a soul and whether they truly have a self. Most bear scars, mechanical limbs, or embedded gems that betray their origin. Found primarily in Ostoya and Stehlenwald, where dark experimentation is commonplace. NPCs react to arisen with fear, pity, or exploitation.

### Dhampir

Half-mortal, half-vampire — born from necromantic accident or forbidden alchemy rather than any natural union. Dhampirs carry a persistent hunger for blood, though most manage it through willpower. They are rejected by mortals as monsters and dismissed by vampires as diluted imitations. Their existence is defined by the search for belonging in a world that offers none. Some serve vampire masters as daylight agents; others rebel against the blood-drinking hierarchy.

### Disembodied

Survivors of the Ethereal Rift — the catastrophic magical accident that cast the mage-city of Ulmyr's Gate into the void between worlds. Those few who clawed their way back to the Material Plane exist permanently between realities. Their eyes hold glimpses of the void; their forms flicker in moments of distress. They seldom speak of the city's destruction and carry the weight of a tragedy too enormous to process.

### Downcast

Celestial beings thrown from the heavens at the Gods' End, stripped of divine power and condemned to mortal lives. Some downcast still serve the Arch Seraphs, seeking to restore divine order. Others have accepted mortality. Still others, embittered by their fall, have turned to Arch Daemons and spread darkness. Physical traces of their former nature linger: glowing eyes, faded celestial runes, or — for the corrupted — cracked skin and the faint scent of sulfur.

### Wechselkind

Fey constructs of wood, clay, and porcelain, animated by faerie enchantment and left in place of stolen mortal infants. When the glamour concealing them fades, they are usually cast out or destroyed. The few who find conditional acceptance remain trapped forever in the unchanging body of a child — an eternal doll with an aging mind. Their immunity to disease has given some wechselkind a foothold in plague-ravaged communities, where they serve healers in exchange for grudging tolerance.

### Wulven

Descendants of lycanthropes who inherit the feral essence without the full curse — they cannot transform, do not lose control under the moon, but carry unmistakable marks: elongated canines, eyes that catch light strangely, an instinct toward wilderness solitude. Most live as hermits, druidic circle members, or guardians of isolated communities. They are perpetually mistaken for true lycanthropes by frightened villagers, making their lives a constant negotiation between usefulness and suspicion.
