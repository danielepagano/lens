# Divinity in Etharis

The Gods' End

## Divine Magic in Etharis

Divine spellcasters believe their deity sanctions them to draw power from the heavens -- gifted magic in answer to prayers. Others believe faith is not transactional, that divinity itself chooses the virtuous. A darker theory suggests clerical prayers are no different from wizardly incantations -- divine spells shape otherworldly energy exactly as arcane magic weaves numen, and perhaps both sources of power are one and the same. Religious orders like the Hearthkeepers decry this as blasphemy.

The Gods' End undeniably diminished divine magic across Etharis, eradicating divine mages until only a scattered few remained. No living person witnessed the gods rise or fall. The cosmology presented here is what is preached in Etharis's holy places -- gospel in one area could be heresy elsewhere.

### The Aetheric War and Gods' End

The gods came into being at the start of the Era of Antiquity, shaping the firmament, seas, land, and all creatures. They named the land Etharis and created its peoples. Worship lifted the gods to greater heights, securing their power.

#### The Aetheric War

The gods were not alone. At the fringes of their ordered realm lurked the Aether Kindred -- incomprehensible beings of pure chaos predating the gods themselves. They gnawed at reality's edges: stars vanished, oceans dried, cities disappeared into nothingness. Even the gods could not fathom these discrepancies.

The Kindred had no intent or desires -- they were an existential threat by mere presence. Fearing all they had built would be stripped away, the gods attacked the Kindred while still at the height of their power. Scriptures differ on what happened: some say slaughter, others say the gods were consumed. What is certain is they were utterly defeated.

Only four survived -- Galt the Builder, Maligant Warclad, Ulmyr the Wise, and Aurelia of the Hearth. They had held back from the war, perhaps from caution or fear.

#### The Gods' End

The four surviving gods -- the Four Divines -- decided to create an ideal empire: the Burach Empire. They graced Emperor Indorius I with four holy artifacts to rule wisely. For a time, all was good.

The Church of the Hearthkeepers imposed the Edict of Eternal Blood, mandating only Indorius I's bloodline could ascend the throne, encouraging intermarriage that weakened their blood. The wretched outcome manifested in the Mad Emperor, Leopold I. When he touched the divine artifacts, his mind connected with the Four Divines -- driving them all to madness. They warred among themselves, each vying to reign as One True God, culminating in the Gods' End when these last deities destroyed one another.

Etharis carries on as a godless world. Divine magic remains but is a shadow of its former glory.

### Arch Seraphs & Arch Daemons

In the absence of gods, Arch Seraphs and Arch Daemons have taken their former deities' mantles. They are not gods -- they can only embody narrow aspects of their forebears' domains: truth as an aspect of knowledge, violence as an aspect of war. This makes their edicts extreme and unyielding, encouraging puritanical expressions of faith.

Who is Seraph and who is Daemon is culturally dependent -- a deity shunned in one region may be celebrated in another. The difference between fiend and celestial is a matter of morality with no gods left to point the way.

#### Empyreus

**Arch Seraph of Valor.** Patron of righteous warriors. Emboldens the strong to punish horrors preying upon the weak. His uncompromising nature drives followers toward zealotry bent on cleansing whatever they perceive as corrupt. Competes with his brother Tormach for the title of war god -- Empyreus favors strategy and heroism where Tormach favors slaughter. Depicted as a knight in heavy plate, wings made of sword blades, helmeted but nothing escaping his stern gaze.

**Followers**: Questing knights, war clerics, noble warriors. Prime deity in Castinella, where the Arcanist Inquisition wages holy crusades in his name. His followers wield terror as a weapon against heretics -- which delights his rival Beleth, who knows mortals create what they fear most.

#### Zabriel

**Arch Seraph of Truth.** The Watcher. Inherited her domain from Typharia, goddess of knowledge. Only truths are worth keeping -- lies, euphemisms, and folktales are weapons of tyrants. Her enemies are spies, assassins, and illusionists shrouded by Venin, the Arch Daemon of deceit. True form: a giant eye with a keyhole-shaped iris, wreathed by countless pairs of wings.

**Followers**: Churches of Zabriel are places of learning. Priests are studious and generous with knowledge. However, their predilection for truth can outweigh morality -- inquisitors hunt criminals with clinical tenacity regardless of reasons, and artificers plunge into science without regard for ethics.

#### Aphaeleon

**Arch Seraph of Temperance.** Most contemplative of the Seraphs. Patron of the strong-minded and spiritual. Nurtures contentment and spiritual freedom. Former servant of Myria, goddess of joy. Opposes Sitri, the Arch Daemon of hedonism -- Sitri gifts joy through corporeal delights that become addiction, while Aphaeleon offers a harder path to enduring fulfillment. Depicted as an old bearded elf floating above golden light, so still that sparrows land on his shoulders.

**Followers**: Austere monks and priests in secluded monasteries. Provide absolution to the wicked through donations to the poor, penance, or last rites. Monks trained in mastery of body are not to be underestimated in combat.

#### Solyma

**Arch Seraph of Justice.** The Great Judge, the Burning Scales, Lady Vengeance. Worshipped since antiquity -- her deity was destroyed during the Aetheric War, so long ago nobody remembers his name. Solyma has sworn silence until all cosmic horrors are defeated. She detests Gorodyn, Arch Daemon of avarice. A warrior deity wielding a flaming sword, dealing punishment until evildoers grasp the suffering they've inflicted. Depicted as a long-haired angel with countless wings, sometimes blindfolded, lips stitched closed.

**Followers**: In Ostoya, Solyma exceeds Empyreus as prime warrior deity. Invoked by paladins (especially the Order of Dawn), monster hunters, vigilantes, and judges. Her most devout tend toward zealotry -- her silence offers no guidance, leaving followers to stray toward extreme retribution. Her hatred of Kindred, daemons, and undead extends to anyone dabbling in the eldritch or occult.

#### Morael

**Arch Seraph of Sacrifice.** Patron of martyrs and unsung heroes. Sacrifice is the truest virtue -- the purest expression of love is to put others' needs first. Shaped by Vetara, goddess of love. Opposes Malikir, who whispers that the focus of love should be oneself. Depicted carrying a giant bladed wheel so burdensome it forces him to his knees, arms scored by lacerations.

**Followers**: Commoners invoke Morael when burdened by pain or misfortune. His most ardent followers, the Flagellants, teach congregations to tear down society's comforts -- dragging nobles from homes and burning taverns. In some regions, his reputation is more akin to an Arch Daemon of pain.

#### Miklas

**Arch Seraph of Mercy.** Former servant of Aurelia, goddess of the hearth. Simply lacks strength to fulfill Aurelia's domain, offering aid only to those already in pain. The sick, wounded, impoverished, and distressed all cry for her mercy. Opposing her are the Weeping Pox, Shadowsteel curses, and endless cycles of war. Struggles against Tormach on every battlefield -- unable to prevent carnage but foiling his bloodlust with every life rescued. Depicted as a tall, golden-haired figure in white robes bearing a staff of ash whose leaves continually wither and regrow.

**Followers**: Doctors, apothecaries, and herbalists pray to Miklas. She has many temples; a divine healer blessed by Miklas is truly rare and remarkable. Commoners trust those wearing her sigil. Her followers tend toward nonviolence but may confine those they perceive as cursed or afflicted. Paladins sworn to Miklas harshly rebuke any use of violence.

#### Beleth

**Arch Daemon of Fear.** The Trickster, the Laughing Man, Blackjester. Finds mortals atrociously funny. Delights in torturing them with their worst fears, knowing the terror is often worse than the actual threat. Endlessly amused that mortals create what they fear most: tyrants induce uprisings, witch hunters see mages everywhere. His favorite game is vexing Empyreus, whose followers' greatest fear is a world they cannot control. Depicted as a horned, goat-legged shadow with jet-black eyes, arms wrapped in spiked chains that stab mortal hearts whenever fear grips them.

**Followers**: Bards and assassins who wield fear as a weapon. A paranoid aristocrat may hire mercenaries against imagined threats, truly haunted by the Laughing Man. The Arcanist Inquisition hunts mages in Empyreus's name, but perhaps they have been blinded by Beleth's trickery and inflict tortures in the Trickster's honor instead.

#### Venin

**Arch Daemon of Deceit.** Mistress of lies, illusions, and insanity. Known as Dusktongue, the Hooded One, the Great Deceiver. Relishes the lies people tell themselves to justify depravity. Triumphs when objectivity dissolves and mortals scream over whose perceived reality is truth. Frequently disrupted by Zabriel's searing light. True form unknown -- appears as a towering figure wreathed in black furs, or a hooded figure whose face is a mirror reflecting a distorted version of the observer.

**Followers**: Rogues, thieves, and murderers. Hidden cults include assassin guilds, spy networks, and information brokers. Her priests are tricksters and orators skilled at convincing others -- eventually, even they lose track of what is real.

#### Sitri

**Arch Daemon of Hedonism.** The Tempter, the Bloodrose. Whispers into mortal hearts, turning wants into cravings -- lust, hunger, intoxication. Has never once forced a soul to give in, yet those who succumb lose themselves to addiction. Once desire becomes all-consuming, no evil is beyond what he asks of followers. His most prized possessions are the souls of Aphaeleon's followers who backslide. Appears as snaking tendrils of black mist forming the shape of the viewer's greatest desire, or as a beautiful person offering a luscious rose, thorns causing blood to trickle between fingers.

**Followers**: Countless in Altenheim and Nov Ostoya, where every indulgence is at hand. Cults form in profligate corners of cities -- folk may not realize they're being snared. Frequently glorified by Ostoyan vampires who view indulgence as their purpose.

#### Malikir

**Arch Daemon of Pride.** Prefers the title "God." Most powerful among the Arch Daemons. Embodiment of tyranny and the abuse of authority. Her influence seeps into bold sovereigns and mighty heroes -- knights become conquerors, kings become tyrants. Promises power and dominion, but all who rise in her favor inevitably come to terrible ends. Considers Morael a fool -- her vanity cannot conceive of love through sacrifice. Depicted as a towering figure of undulating flesh, flaming ram skull crowned with golden circlet, taloned hands.

**Followers**: Ambitious nobles, fame-seeking bards, glory-craving paladins. Cults center on singular powerful personalities -- rulers, merchant lords, military leaders -- whose followers perform the leader's bidding hoping to share in their glory. Malikir's blessings never come freely.

#### Tormach

**Arch Daemon of Wrath.** The Manslayer, the Marauder. Brother of Empyreus, former lieutenant of the dead god Maligant. Has forsaken familial ties to seize the war god mantle. Gives no praise for strategy or valor -- the only purpose of armed conflict is to kill. Every battlefield is his altar, though Miklas the Redeemer steals his tributes with every life rescued, infuriating him endlessly. Depicted as a flaming warrior with a bull's head, riding a chimera-pulled chariot, wielding the warhammer Wrath of Heaven.

**Followers**: Many soldiers invoke his name for strength. A growing faith in Unterland honors Tormach as an Arch Seraph. His truly devout twist into raging berserkers. Commoners are wary of anyone wearing his sigil.

#### Gorodyn

**Arch Daemon of Avarice.** The King of Red Coins. Proselytizes that greed is virtue -- a vital instinct for survival and prosperity. Luxuriating atop hoarded wealth is the greatest happiness, even if others go hungry. Seized his domain from Jezra, god of commerce, after the Aetheric War -- forgot fair trade, rewarding those strong or cunning enough to hoard by whatever means. Fears Lady Solyma's retribution: the rage of the impoverished can burn into revolution. Depicted as an enormous grotesque bag of rotting flesh, enveloped in flies and gaudy jewelry, body covered in hundreds of mouths speaking with one voice.

**Followers**: Greedy nobles, ambitious courtiers, even paupers all pray to Gorodyn. Hunger exists in all mortals, so he is worshipped throughout every level of society. Many followers in Morencia, Altenheim, and Graffendam.

### The Aether Kindred

After the Aetheric War, the Kindred seemed to disappear -- possibly sleeping deeply after destroying so many gods. Their presence has returned in more recent times: abominations spawn from their dreams, and it is feared they may wake to take revenge on the gods' remaining creations.

Warlocks and aberrant sorcerers are undeniably touched by the Kindred. One horrifying theory suggests Numena itself is an Aether Kindred. Worship is exceedingly rare -- they have no formal church or doctrine. Their desires are truly unknowable, their existence an existential horror.

#### Vraigoroth

**Kindred of Devouring.** Imagined as an enormous maw that sucks all light into itself, or an unfathomable growing mass of wriggling flesh with tendrils extending into reality. It gnaws at existence's edges, digesting in the dark. Polyps of Vraigoroth bubble into the mortal realm wherever it has chewed through reality -- amorphous horrors that dissolve organic matter and grow as they consume.

**Worshippers** believe everything was once part of Vraigoroth and shall be again. The devouring is inevitable. Warlocks who siphon its power become gorgers themselves, consuming everything within grasp until transformed into another polyp.

#### M'rorcameleth

**Kindred of Dreams.** The Mind Hunter. Slithers into mortal minds through dreams. Described as a landscape of flesh punctuated by bulbous eyes and worm-like trees bearing sleeping faces. Dreaming of M'rorcameleth is an omen -- the Kindred tortures visitors with visions of inevitable doom. The only escape is not to sleep.

**Worshippers**: Wizards who reach M'rorcameleth through oneiromancy appear to master psionic magic, but it is unclear if the same person who closed their eyes is the entity who awoke. Cults form around such individuals, with fanatics hoping to have their minds similarly "opened."

#### Pharazorthok

**Kindred of Knowing.** Described as an enormous tentacled tree with countless eyes on its branches, twisting beneath reality's surface. The mortal realm, Numena, the Netherworld -- all sit upon these branches. Pharazorthok sees and knows all. Its tendrils can reach into mortal minds to listen and speak, though its intentions are never clear.

**Worshippers** are patient and learned, not raving fanatics. The Kindred's branches manifest visions of cosmic understanding -- some break under this knowledge, others become sorcerous educators. Wherever its branches wriggle through the veil, aberrations of eyes and teeth spawn.

## Primal Magic in Etharis

Somewhere between arcane and divine magic is primal magic -- the power of the elements and of things that grow, live, and die outside the purview of god or mortal. Two main cultures practice it: the elves of Tol Leyemil (communing with Spirits of Nature) and the Valikan Clans (evoking magic from the Primordials). To divine spellcasters, Primordials and Spirits of Nature are no different from daemons.

### The Primordic Cycle

The first living thing was Gormadraug, the Great Prismatic Wyrm -- a creature that consumed all elements until its size encircled existence. From a mountain called Citrolach that awoke on the Wyrm's back, the first mortals emerged. Morakesh exploded from Gormadraug's breath as fire and lava, wielded as a weapon. Her flames melted the ice that formed the Endless Sea, releasing Alondo. Ilhara formed in the shadow and smoke, traveling unseen to learn of discontent with Gormadraug's tyranny.

### The Kentigern Saga

Each Primordial ached to be free of Gormadraug. Ilhara found a mortal hero named Kentigern and his six companions. The Primordials betrayed their sire: Morakesh and Citrolach forged weapons to pierce his flesh, Ilhara composed a sleeping song, Alondo taught enchantments and revealed where to strike the heart. Kentigern slew Gormadraug and used his body to form the mortal realm -- flesh became earth, spine became mountains, blood became frozen seas.

All four Primordials know their sire merely slumbers. Mortals still obtain elemental command from the Prismatic Wyrm. With the appearance of coldfire, Gormadraug's resurrection seems ever closer.

### Primordials

#### Lady Morakesh
**Primordial of Fire.** The Burning Lady, Red Queen, Emberheart. Rules Flamebourne, commanding azers, efreeti, and fire giants. Suffered worst under Gormadraug as his destructive weapon. Highly capricious -- rewards characters with riches as easily as turning them to cinders. Druidic blood-and-fire cults perform burning sacrifices to invoke her favor.

#### Alondo
**Primordial of Ice and Water.** Lord of the Deep, ruler of the Endless Sea. Worshipped by the laneshi. Has no love for mortals beyond Kentigern and wishes to be left alone. Most conscientious of the Primordials, working diligently to prevent Gormadraug's return. Rumors persist he has something to do with coldfire plaguing Etharis.

#### Citrolach
**Primordial of Earth and Stone.** The Living Mountain. Masters all who pledge to earth and stone. Progenitor of stoneborn trolls and earth elementals. Worshipped by dwarves and gnomes who delve for riches, and by druids of land and moon. Citrolach's worshippers bury gems and gold back into the earth. Legendarily stubborn -- "easier to move a mountain than convince Citrolach to change his mind."

#### Ilhara
**Primordial of Air and Shadow.** Queen of Air and Shadow. Most ambitious Primordial. Her spies hide in candle smoke and empty shadows. Ultimate aim: fill the void left by Gormadraug. Requires a dark secret whispered into the air before she can be summoned. Important to Clan Morgong's spies in Valika.

#### Gormadraug
**Primordial of All Elements.** The Great Prismatic Wyrm. A powerful source of primal magic in his slumbering state, though there is no known way to invoke him. Worship is forbidden throughout Valika. The Primordials warn that drawing power from Gormadraug may awaken him. Much blood is spilled in Thrull to keep him asleep -- raiders take prisoners specifically for tree-hung sacrifice.

### The Faerie Courts

Before the Era of Expansion, the Faerie Courts were worshipped like deities in Caer Neiada. The elves believed they descended from the archfey and that magic was possible only by listening to the Spirits of Nature. In modern Charneault, the Courts are still revered, though culture is now interlaced with Burach religions.

There is scholarly rumor that the archfey are in fact seraphs, downcast from the heavens during the Aetheric War, who cultivated a second paradise within the Realm of Faerie. Many folk consider the fey more devilish than divine. Some paladins swear oaths to an Arch Seraph but accept gifts from the Spirits of Nature; others bind exclusively to the Faerie Courts.

Similar beliefs exist in eastern Nordenland and parts of Valika, where human cultures train mages and make pacts to wield primal magic despite regarding the Archfey as capricious devils.
