# Divinity in Etharis

The Gods’ End

## Divine Magic in Etharis

Divine magic may seem contingent on the presence of gods, deities, Arch Seraphs, and Arch Daemons, but it is much more nuanced and complex. Divine spellcasters believe their deity sanctions them to draw from the power of the heavens—gifted magic in answer to prayers. Others believe a deity is not necessary to perform miracles, that faith is not so transactional, and that divinity itself chooses the virtuous in times when divine magic is in great need.

Less theological literature suggests a darker truth: a cleric’s prayers are no different from a wizard’s incantations. Divine spells commune with otherworldly energy and shape it to the caster’s will, exactly as arcane magic weaves numen—perhaps the two sources of power are one and the same. Religious orders like the Hearthkeepers decry such theory as blasphemy. Of course, if it were true, by what right would the church limit the magic of others whilst celebrating their own spellcasters as prophets?

Yet as obscure as theology can be, evidence of the divine in Etharis exists. The downcast still remember the heavens as a glorious dream. The Gods’ End itself undeniably diminished the light of divine magic across the continent, eradicating the population of divine mages until only a scattered few remained.

Of course, no one who records history witnessed the gods rise or fall—if there were ever gods at all. The cosmology and mythology presented here is just one that can be heard preached in the chapels, churches, and holy places of Etharis. What is seen as gospel in one area could be heretical if preached elsewhere.

But while mortal minds can accept their own lives are fleeting and small, the need to believe in a greater story that gives purpose to existence fills the gaps in knowledge with faith.

### The Aetheric War and Gods’ End

The gods came into being at the start of the Era of Antiquity. They opened their powerful eyes to behold a universe of wonder and chaos, and they were enamored. They wanted to shape it into something that resembled them. But to do that, they needed to impose order.

The gods banded together to form a pantheon. They shaped the firmament and the seas, then the land and all the creatures that followed. They named the land Etharis and created the people to populate it and beasts to roam it.

As these newborn mortals turned their minds to their creators, the gods found an endless font of believers. Their worship lifted the gods to greater heights, securing their ability to turn the wheel of the universe.

#### The Aetheric War

The gods were not alone. At the fringes of their ordered realm lurked the Aether Kindred—incomprehensible beings of pure chaos who predated the gods themselves. The Kindred gnawed at the edges of reality. Occasionally the gods would find a piece of creation in disarray—a star gone missing, an ocean dried up, an entire city disappearing into a void of nothingness. Even the gods, in all their power and insight, could not fathom these discrepancies.

That’s because The Aether Kindred had no intent. No desires. They were an active threat to the gods’ power by their mere presence, and the gods feared that one day all they had built would be stripped away. They had to strike now, while they still had the faith of their followers and stood at the height of their power. Thus, the gods banded together as a host and attacked the Kindred. This became known as the Aetheric War.

Scriptures differ on what happened to the gods who participated in the war. Some theologians say it was a slaughter. Others believe the gods were consumed by the eldritch horrors they tried to defeat. Others state that some gods retreated from this plane to someplace the Kindred could not reach them. While nothing is certain about what happened to the gods thereafter, it is clear they were utterly defeated.

Of the great pantheon, only the four youngest survived: Galt the Builder, Maligant Warclad, Ulmyr the Wise, and Aurelia of the Hearth. Perhaps out of caution or fear, they held back from joining the other gods in the Aetheric War. Thus, they were the only ones left to pick up the pieces, becoming responsible for the fostering of Etharis’s future.

#### The Gods’ End

As the surviving gods left to guide the mortals of Etharis, the Four Divines decided to band together. Their idea was to create an ideal empire, the “one above all” to act as a shining beacon for all mortals to emulate. This idea became the Bürach Empire.

Its first emperor, Indorius I, would be graced with the power to become a guiding light for all the nations of Etharis. To this end, the Four Divines gifted him with four holy artifacts that would enable him to rule wisely and protect his empire.

For a time, all was good. Unfortunately, this method of salvation carried the seeds of its own destruction. The Bürach Empire so loved their emperor that they decided to keep his bloodline eternally pure. The Church of the Hearthkeepers imposed the Edict of Eternal Blood, mandating that only those of the line of Indorius I may ascend to the throne. With that, they encouraged his descendants to intermarry, weakening their blood.

The wretched outcome of this practice finally manifested in the Mad Emperor, Leopold I. When he ascended to his legacy and touched the divine artifacts, his mind and soul were instantly connected with the Four Divines—and it drove them all to madness. Galt, Maligant, Ulmyr, and Aurelia warred among themselves, each vying to reign as the One True God of the universe. This culminated in the Gods’ End, when these last remaining deities destroyed one another.

Etharis carries on as a godless world. While divine magic remains, it is but a shadow of its former glory.

### Arch Seraphs & Arch Daemons

In the absence of the gods, the Arch Seraphs and Arch Daemons of Etharis have taken the mantles of their former deities. The machinations of these lesser divinities manifest in the deeds of their followers, providing a font of divine magic to the rare few of their most fervent worshippers. But they are not gods.

The Arch Seraphs and Arch Daemons can only embody aspects of their forebears’ domains. Truth as an aspect of knowledge. Violence as an aspect of war. For this reason, the edicts of the Arch Seraphs and Arch Daemons are extreme and unyielding, encouraging their followers towards puritanical expressions of faith.

The Arch Seraphs appear to nurture the light that flickers in the heart of every mortal, while the Arch Daemons are considered to embody people's more heinous aspects. In truth, who belongs to which moniker is culturally dependent. A deity shunned as an Arch Daemon in one region may be celebrated as an Arch Seraph in another. The difference between fiend and celestial is a matter of morality. There are no gods left to point the way.

Below are descriptions of Etharis’s most powerful Arch Seraphs and Arch Daemons, whose machinations frequently influence the lives of mortals.

#### Empyreus

Arch Seraph of Valor

The patron of righteous warriors, Empyreus emboldens the strong to punish the horrors of Etharis that prey upon the weak. In battle, Empyreus favors bravery and conviction. His uncompromising nature differs from that of Maligant, his predecessor. The Arch Seraph drives his followers to become zealots bent on cleansing the world of whatever they perceive as unworthy or corrupt. Suzanne Helmigh

Empyreus competes with his brother, Tormach, for the title of god of war. In this struggle, the Sword Saint encourages strategy and heroism among his knights and paladins, who shine as beacons of hope to the foot soldiers of Empyreus’s armies and the downtrodden of Etharis. Warfare without righteousness is merely butchery.

The enemies of Empyreus’s warriors have much to fear. Terror is a righteous weapon to turn against those who victimize the innocent, and one which Empyreus’ faithful wield through violence and torture. Beleth smiles to watch their moralizing belligerence. Nobody knows better than the Arch Daemon of fear that mortals frequently manifest their own greatest terror—as Empyreus’ zealots grow further inflamed, so do their deeds become more monstrous. For every charlatan, mage, and heretic Empyereus terrorizes, Beleth’s grin grows wider.

Empyreus is glorified as a knight clad in heavy plate armor. His wings are made from sword blades. Empyreus often wears a helmet, yet no heresy or wickedness escapes his stern gaze from within his visor.

Followers. Questing knights, war clerics, and warriors of noble houses are among those who wear Empyreus’ sigils on their armor and shields. Anyone who takes up arms might offer a prayer to the Sword Saint. Mercenary companies and even brigands might whisper a prayer before battle, asking for strength and courage.

As the prime deity among the Arch Seraphs in Castinella, countless inquisitions and holy crusades have been waged in Empyreus’ name. The Arcanist Inquisition frequently employs fear to discourage the use of magic. The Sword Saint’s most holy warriors are imbued with their own divine gifts, empowered to smite all that is filthy and blasphemous in the Arch Seraph’s sight.

#### Zabriel

Arch Seraph of Truth

All shadows and deceits are stripped bare by Zabriel’s all-seeing eye. The Watcher inherited her domain from the goddess of knowledge, Typharia, for whom Zabriel acted as librarian. Now, truths are the only knowledge the Arch Seraph considers worth keeping. Lies, euphemisms, allegories, and folktales are of no interest to Zabriel, who believes ignorance on any scale is a dangerous weapon in the hands of tyrants and deceivers. Suzanne Helmigh

Zabriel’s enemies are spies, assassins, and illusionists. These cunning operators, shrouded by Venin, the Arch Daemon of deceit, spread disruption and misinformation supports harmful conspiracies and oppressive regimes. Ignorance is a chain around society’s neck. Truth is the key to liberation.

It’s said that Zabriel disguises herself as a robed figure to treat with mortals. Her face glows so brightly from beneath her hood that her features cannot be seen, and she carries a tome with the names of every being in existence scribed inside. The Watcher’s true form is that of a giant eye, the iris shaped like a keyhole, wreathed by countless pairs of angel wings.

Followers. Churches devoted to Zabriel are places of learning. The Arch Seraph’s priests are studious and generous with their knowledge, wishing to spread the light of understanding among the folk of Etharis as widely as possible. If a priest does not know an answer, they usually know where to look.

However, among the Watcher’s adherents, their predilection for the truth can outweigh their sense of nuance or morality. Zabriel’s inquisitors hunt criminals and spies with clinical tenacity, never mind the reasons for the crime. Artificers pray to Zabriel for a flash of inspiration, hoping it will lead them to a new revelation. They plunge into the depths of science without regard for ethics or safety.

#### Aphaeleon

Arch Seraph of Temperance

The most contemplative and peaceful of the Arch Seraphs, Aphaeleon is a patron to the strong-minded and the spiritual. His presence is felt in the simple joy of contentment; whatever form it takes. The gentle warmth of a breeze. The satisfaction of a finished book. The quiet contemplation of meditation. Aphaeleon was an angel of Myria, goddess of joy. The Arch Seraph has witnessed little joy left in the dwindling light of Etharis and has chosen instead to nurture those who seek spiritual freedom and enlightenment. Indulgence invariably leads to craving more. Aphaeleon aims to liberate mortals from their addictions and worldly desires. Suzanne Helmigh

A rivalry over mortal souls exists between Aphaeleon and his seraphic sibling, Sitri. The Arch Daemon of hedonism gifts joy to mortals through all manner of corporeal delights, tipping the soul towards cravings that can turn into desperation. Aphaeleon offers a harder path, one that leads to a more enduring form of fulfillment.

Aphaeleon appears as an old, bearded elf floating above a sphere of golden light. Such is his stillness, sparrows land upon his shoulders to sing to him their whistling tunes.

Followers. Aphaeleon’s priests can be found leading austere lives in secluded monasteries and peaceful wildernesses. They’re pleased to share the teachings of their patron and the arts of spiritual healing with folk who come to them with peace and curiosity. On the other hand, monks in service to Aphaeleon are not to be underestimated if confronted with violence. Mastery of the body is part of mastery of the soul.

Worshippers of Aphaeleon also provide absolution to the wicked who truly desire spiritual freedom. Repentance might be achieved through a donation of worldly possessions to the poor, or by receiving last rites before burning upon the pyre. The form of atonement is dependent upon the nature of the sins.

#### Solyma

Arch Seraph of Justice

Solyma has been worshipped since antiquity. Statues and epithets glorifying the Great Judge are found in ancient tombs and ruins that long predate the Gods’ End. Her deity was destroyed during The Aetheric War, so long ago that no one today remembers his name, and Solyma has sworn a vow of silence until her former master is avenged. She will not speak until all cosmic horrors are defeated. Solyma is the Arch Seraph of justice. Her silence endures so long as evil persists. She detests all that is corrupt, abject, or villainous. In particular, she loathes the Arch Daemon of avarice, Gorodyn, who turns the hearts of mortals black over such a venal and petty thing as money. Suzanne Helmigh

In addition to her epithets of the Great Judge and the Burning Scales, Solyma has also earned the moniker of Lady Vengeance. She is a patron of the downtrodden and the persecuted. A warrior deity who wields a flaming sword, dealing punishment to evildoers until they grasp the full extent of the suffering they’ve inflicted upon others.

Lady Solyma appears as a long-haired angel with countless sets of wings, a sword glowing white-hot in her grasp. She is sometimes blindfolded. Her lips may be stitched closed, or otherwise her hair hangs in front of her face and down to her feet, which never touch the ground.

Followers. In Ostoya, Solyma exceeds Empyreus as the primary warrior deity. She is invoked by paladins, particularly in the Order of Dawn, and is also associated with monster hunters, vigilantes, judges, and those who root out evil. Prayers are offered to Solyma throughout Etharis, wherever oppression exists.

Lady Vengeance’s most devout tend towards zealotry. Her silence offers no guidance, leaving her followers to stray toward more extreme ideologies of retribution and intolerance, rather than justice. Her hatred towards Aether Kindred, daemons, and undead has led her warriors to pursue any who dabble in the eldritch or occult, including pact-making warlocks or blood-cursed dhampir.

Nevertheless, Solyma’s worshippers always rejoice and offer rewards when evil is destroyed. Priests of her temples may provide quests for hunting vampires, aberrations, and all manner of vile things.

A Fiendish Offer

#### Morael

Arch Seraph of Sacrifice

Morael is the patron of martyrs and heroes who seek no glory, sacrificing themselves to build hope for a future they’ll never see. The Arch Seraph teaches that sacrifice is the truest virtue. The purest expression of love. is to put others’ needs before oneself, or to suffer pain or anguish so that others may be spared. Morael offers strength in moments of desperate decision-making or surrender in the name of a great cause. Morael was shaped by Vetara, goddess of love, before her destruction in The Aetheric War. He continues her legacy of compassion and selfless devotion. His efforts are frequently undermined by the Arch Daemon Malikir, who whispers in mortals’ ears that the central focus of love should be themselves. Suzanne Helmigh

Morael is portrayed carrying a giant wheel upon his shoulders, which is so burdensome that it forces him to his knees. Blades extend from the spokes of the wheel. Morael’s arms and shoulders are scored by lacerations, blood dribbling down his back and reddening his beard. The wheel represents the unending nature of sacrifice. Without sacrifice, love cannot exist. Only pride.

Followers. Many commoners invoke Morael’s name when burdened by pain or misfortune, accepting their punishments as true and just.

Morael’s most ardent followers believe that virtue cannot exist without suffering. The Flagellants, priests of Morael, teach their congregations to forsake and tear down the comforts of society. Nobles are dragged from their homes. Taverns are burned down. In some regions, Morael’s frightening reputation and image is more akin to an Arch Daemon of pain.

A noble house might hire mercenaries to protect them from Morael’s worshippers, who see tyrants everywhere. Conversely, a priest might offer blessings to adventurers about to undertake a difficult quest. One they’re unlikely to escape from unscathed.


#### Miklas

Arch Seraph of Mercy

With the loss of Aurelia, mortals have been left without protection. Miklas, the goddess’s former servant, simply doesn’t have the strength to fulfil Aurelia’s domain, and can only offer aid to those already in pain. The sick, wounded, impoverished, and distressed all cry for Miklas’ mercy. Her work is endless. The extent of suffering is unfathomable in Etharis’s new dark age. Opposing Miklas are spreading plagues like the Weeping Pox, virulent curses like those wrought with Shadowsteel, and the endless cycles of violence and war inflamed by tyrants and battles for resources. Tormach laughs at Miklas’ wasted efforts. The two struggle over mortal lives on every battlefield, the Arch Seraph unable to prevent the carnage. Nevertheless, she foils Tormach’s bloodlust with every life rescued. Suzanne Helmigh

Miklas resembles a tall, golden-haired figure clothed in radiant white robes. She bears a staff hewn from an ash tree whose leaves continually wither and grow back.

Followers. Doctors, apothecaries, herbalists, and surgeons offer prayers to Miklas, that she might guide their hand in mending the sick and injured. Miklas has many temples throughout Etharis. Her priests can offer medicines and potions, though a divine healer is a truly rare and remarkable individual, blessed by the Arch Seraph. Her worship is popular, and commoners are more trusting of folk who wear Miklas’ sigil.

More than any other Arch Seraph, Miklas’ followers tend towards nonviolence. That doesn’t mean they’re not prone to zealotry. Folk who suffer from a curse, an eldritch heritage, a haunting, or a perceived mental anguish might be held against their will by worshippers of the Redeemer. Rescuing them from such confines can be tricky. Commoners who witness Miklas’ priests being attacked are quickly angered, and Paladins sworn to the Arch Seraph harshly rebuke any use of violence.

#### Beleth

Arch Daemon of Fear

Most mortals fear the Arch Daemons, but conversely, Beleth finds mortals atrociously funny. He delights in torturing them with their worst fears to witness their petrified antics, knowing that the terror induced is often far worse than the actual threat. The Arch Daemon of fear is endlessly amused that commoners most often create what they fear most. Tyrants induce uprisings. Sycophants fail to earn respect. Witch hunters see mages everywhere. Suzanne Helmigh

The Arch Daemon of fear goes by many names—the Trickster, the Laughing Man, and Blackjester. Beleth’s favorite game is to vex Empyreus, whose followers’ greatest fear is a world they cannot control. The warriors and priests who revere the Arch Seraph have turned to wielding terror as their weapon against heretics and mages, which makes Blackjester smile whether they intend it to or not.

Beleth’s likeness is that of a horned and goat-legged shadow. His eyes are jet-black, glinting with horrendous glee like dark pools of ink, and when his cruel smile curls the edges of his mouth, black ichor dribbles down his chin. Blackjester’s arms are wrapped with spiked chains. These shackles stab into mortal hearts whenever they feel gripped by fear.

Followers. The Trickster has many worshippers among bards and assassins who take inspiration from Beleth’s obscene antics. Anyone who wields fear as a weapon and can make the Arch Daemon laugh is worthy of his umbral blessings.

A paranoid aristocrat may hire mercenaries to protect against assassins, only for the adventurers to learn the threat is imagined and their patron is truly haunted by the Laughing Man. A vampire noble in Ostoya might wield fear to oppress their subjects, making dark sacraments to Beleth in return for heinous magics. In the Castinellan Provinces, the Arcanist Inquisition hunts for mages in the righteous name of Empyreus, but perhaps they have been blinded by Beleth’s trickery and inflict tortures in the Trickster’s honor, instead.

#### Venin

Arch Daemon of Deceit

Venin is the mistress of lies, illusions, and insanity. As an Arch Daemon of supernal deceit, she is known by many names and many faces—Dusktongue, the Hooded One, the Great Deceiver, and many others forgotten by most mortals. She relishes the lies people tell themselves to justify depravity and cruelty. Venin triumphs when objectivity dissolves and mortals scream at each other over whose perceived reality is truth. Suzanne Helmigh

The Great Deceiver’s schemes are frequently disrupted by Zabriel, whose searing light burns away the obscuring darkness. Venin rewards those who assassinate the Arch Seraph’s priests.

Venin’s true form is unknown. Often, she appears as a towering human wreathed in black furs and shadows. At other times, she’s depicted as a hooded figure whose face is a mirror, reflecting a subtly distorted version of the observer. Her voice seems to echo from deep within the listener’s head.

Followers. Rogues, thieves, and murderers whisper prayers to Venin so that her darkness may conceal their misdeeds. Her hidden cults include assassin guilds, spy networks, and information brokers. Venin’s priests are tricksters and orators. They’re skilled at convincing others of their versions of the truth, but eventually, even they lose track of what is real and what is not.

#### Sitri

Arch Daemon of Hedonism

Sitri. His name slips from the tongue like honey or blood. The Tempter. The Bloodrose. Sitri whispers into the hearts of mortals, turning their minds inexorably toward whatever they desire until a want becomes a craving. It’s said that Sitri has never once forced a soul to give in to hedonism. Yet inevitably, those who succumb to the Arch Daemon’s promises lose themselves to their addictions—lust, hunger, intoxication. Then, when desire becomes all-consuming, no evil is beyond Sitri’s purview to ask of his followers. Sitri’s most prized possessions are the souls of Aphaeleon’s followers. Both once servants to Myria, the dead goddess of joy, Sitri now derives his pleasure from seducing Aphaelon’s followers who backslide on their addictions. Suzanne Helmigh

Sitri has many depictions. Most often, he appears as snaking tendrils of black mist, which weave together into the shape of the viewer’s greatest desire. Other times, the Tempter resembles a beautiful person. He wears whichever mask suits his purposes, offering a luscious rose clutched in one hand, the thorns causing blood to trickle between the fingers.

Followers. Sitri has countless followers in Altenheim or Nov Ostoya, where every indulgence is readily at hand. Every bodily pleasure is seized upon. Music. Wine. Food. Sex. Drug-fueled parties can last for days on end.

Cults that glorify Sitri form in the most profligate corners of Etharis’s cities. Folk may not even realize they’re being snared by a cult when they’re first invited to an event, lured by the promise of whatever they desire. Inevitably, desire becomes addiction.

Sitri is also frequently glorified by the vampires of Ostoya, who view indulgence as the purpose of their very existence. A member of the Crimson Court may weave dark magics gifted to them by the Bloodrose. A creature trying to inflict the sanguine curse upon themselves might hope to do so through worshipping Sitri.

Sitri enjoys offering temptations to anyone, even those who spend their life adventuring. He might flicker into their thoughts in the guise of their greatest desire, leading them in service of Sitri’s purposes without the adventurer ever learning the Arch Daemon’s name. What depths would such a person sink to, to fulfil their deepest wishes?

#### Malikir

Arch Daemon of Pride

Malikir prefers the title “God.” Known to be the most powerful among the Arch Daemons, she’s the embodiment of pride, tyranny, and the abuse of authority. Malikir is obsessed with her power. Her influence seeps into bold sovereigns and mighty heroes, who might begin with honorable intentions but are overcome by ego and paranoia. Knights become conquerors, and kings become tyrants. Malikir promises power, fame, and dominion to those who worship her, yet all who rise in her favor will inevitably come to a terrible end. Suzanne Helmigh

Malikir considers Morael to be a fool. The Arch Daemon’s vanity cannot possibly conceive of how love is expressed through self-punishment or sacrifice. Nobody is more deserving of love than oneself.

The Subjugator is depicted as a towering figure of undulating flesh. Her flaming ram skull is crowned with a golden circlet, and her long arms end with talons.

Followers. Ambitious nobles, fame-seeking bards, and paladins who crave glory are among those most likely to believe themselves worthy of Malikir’s blessings. But Malikir is selective of her disciples. Her cults usually center upon a singular, powerful personality, such as a ruler, merchant lord, military leader, or priest. These exemplars of pride and greatness attract large followings of those folk willing to perform the cult leader’s bidding out of a sense of admiration, and the hope that the leader's glory will be shared among the group.

Of course, Malikir’s blessings do not come freely. For one who aspires to fame or influence, even for goodhearted reasons, there may be no limit to the sins they’re willing to commit in secret to gain the Subjugator’s aid.

A Zealous Gathering

#### Tormach

Arch Daemon of Wrath

The only truth of war is death. The only virtue is to kill. The only victory is to survive. Heroism, honor, and glory are illusions for moralizing cowards. These are the edicts of Tormach, Arch Daemon of wrath and war, who is known by such names as Manslayer and Marauder.

Once a lieutenant of the dead god Maligant, and brother to the Arch Seraph Empyreus, Tormach has since forsaken his familial ties to seize the mantle of war god for himself. Tormach gives no praise for strategy or valor. In the Arch Daemon’s mind, the only purpose of armed conflict is to kill. Victory itself is unimportant. All that matters is that as much blood is spilled and as much carnage is dealt as is possible, during and after the battle. Tormach is the element of war at its most violent. Every battlefield is an altar to the Arch Daemon of wrath, though his tributes are stolen time and again by the healing light of Miklas the Redeemer—a fact that infuriates him to no end. Suzanne Helmigh

Depictions of the Arch Daemon show a flaming warrior with a bull’s head. Sometimes it’s a decapitated head sitting upon a man’s shoulders, rivulets of blood running down his scarred chest. Other times, the bull is depicted as Tormach’s true face. He rides a chariot pulled by a pair of chimeras and wields his warhammer, the Wrath of Heaven.

Followers. Tormach is a source of strength for many soldiers and militaries. A growing faith among the armies of Unterland honor Tormach as an Arch Seraph, and even noble warriors may invoke his name to draw upon his strength as a war deity. Commoners are nevertheless wary of any who wear his sigil. Tormach’s unflinching demand for slaughter twists his truly devout into raging berserkers.

Adventures featuring the Arch Daemon are likely to include clashes with one of his cults. A cadre of soldiers may try to turn their entire legion to Tormach’s ideals, or a troupe of bandits might capture civilians and commit blood sacrifices in his name. Bloodthirsty fiends conjured from the Netherworld may join the ranks of a particularly powerful warband or even lead one. Valikan raiders who have forsaken their heritage might invoke the Marauder when taking heads from an unprotected settlement. A serial killer could be motivated by the Manslayer, intoxicated by the scent of their exsanguinated victims.

#### Gorodyn

Arch Daemon of Avarice

Gorodyn proselytizes that greed is a virtue. The Arch Daemon lovingly encourages mortals to voraciously hoard anything of value that can be bought with a coin. Food. Opulence. Secrets. Greed is sustaining. Greed is a vital instinct for survival and prosperity. To the Arch Daemon of avarice, luxuriating atop a wealth of resources is the greatest happiness one can achieve, even if that means the rest go hungry. Suzanne Helmigh

Gorodyn recognizes that the valuable resources of Etharis are finite. One mortal’s gain is always another’s loss. The King of Red Coins was once servile to Jezra, god of commerce, until The Aetheric War left the domain empty for Gorodyn to seize. Forgotten was the spirit of fair trade and mutual ascendancy. Instead, Gorodyn rewards mortals who are strong or cunning enough to hoard their wealth by whatever means necessary.

Though he considers himself loving and pragmatic, Gorodyn fears the retribution of Lady Solyma. The rage of folk impoverished by the King of Red Coins can burn with enough intensity that it sparks a revolution, causing a violent redistribution of wealth.

Gorodyn is often depicted by a red or filthy coin. His true appearance is whispered to be an enormous and grotesque bag of rotting flesh enveloped in both flies and gaudy jewelry. His body is also covered with hundreds of mouths that all speak with the same voice.

Followers. Greedy nobles, ambitious courtiers, and even paupers all secretly pray to Gorodyn to grow their meagre wealth or protect what they’ve already hoarded. Hunger exists in all mortals. So, the King of Red Coins is worshipped throughout every level of society.

In Morencia, Gorodyn finds many followers, as the wealth of the entire continent flows through those free markets and guilds. Cults praising the King of Red Coins also exist in the opulent cities of Altenheim and Gräffendam, where nobles jealously amass their wealth whilst draining the countryside of resources.

### The Aether Kindred

After the Aetheric War, the Kindred seemed to disappear. It’s believed these horrors slept deeply after destroying so many of the gods, possibly requiring rest to heal from their wounds. Or perhaps they slumbered in the aftermath of much devouring.

The presence of the Kindred has returned to Etharis in more recent times. Abominations spawn from their dreams, and it’s feared they may one day wake to take revenge on the rest of the gods’ creations.

Spellcasters who wield either arcane or divine magic may draw their power from the Aether Kindred. One horrifying theory suggests that Numena is an Aether Kindred, and thus why arcane magic is so dangerous. Whether the case or not, warlocks who make pacts with ancient gods and sorcerers whose minds are wracked with aberrant visions are undeniably touched by the Aether Kindred.

Worship of the Kindred is exceedingly rare, but not unheard of in Etharis. They have no formal church or doctrine. Indeed, few people even dare learn their names except the eldritch clerics who lead their cults. Their desires are truly unknowable. Their existence is an existential horror.

#### Vraigoroth

Kindred of Devouring

Vraigoroth is thought of as an enormous maw, ringed with fangs, that sucks all light and energy into itself, returning all existence to darkness. Others imagine it to be an unfathomable tumor, a growing mass of wriggling flesh with veinlike tendrils extending into reality, transmogrifying everything it touches into more of itself. Nobody truly knows its shape. It gnaws at the edges of existence, digesting in the dark.

Polyps of Vraigoroth bubble into the mortal realm wherever it has chewed a hole through reality. This can happen anywhere—deep within an unexplored cave, inside a dusty abandoned structure, or through the living flesh of a mortal host. These amorphous horrors twist and grow. They dissolve any organic matter they come into contact with, amalgamating it into their mass and growing in size as they consume. Unless destroyed, these nightmarish creatures can reach truly terrifying proportions.

Worshippers of Vraigoroth believe they’ve learned and accepted a horrifying truth—everything that exists was once part of this entity, and shall be again. The cycle has repeated endlessly. The devouring is inevitable. Therefore, Vraigoroth should be praised and its hunger fed. Once all of reality has been returned to its amalgamated mass, all suffering will end.

It’s not always clear whether followers of Vraigoroth are terrified or jubilant at their patron’s devouring. Warlocks who siphon some of the Aether Kindred’s power tend to become gorgers themselves, entire settlements going missing as the arcanist consumes everyone and everything that comes within its grasp. Inevitably, they’re transformed into another amorphous polyp of Vraigoroth itself.

#### M’rorcameleth

Kindred of Dreams

Some folk fear to dream. The fleeting realities that unfold behind closed eyes are more than merely the fancies of an unconscious mind. Many tragedies have been preceded by the occurrence of bad dreams. The great King Aymeric Noblecoeur was said to have witnessed his death in a nightmare. Days later, he was gone.

M’rorcameleth, known as the Mind Hunter, slithers into mortal minds through the realm of dreams. In their waking moments, those who’ve witnessed it describe a landscape made of flesh, punctuated by bulbous eyes and wriggling, worm-like trees. Each tree bears a face. Stretched. Sleeping. It’s unclear if M’rorcameleth is the master of this dreamscape realm or is the realm itself. Having visited this place, nobody wakes up the same as they were before.

Dreaming of M’rorcameleth is said to be an omen. Each night, the Aether Kindred tortures those it visits with horrifying visions of inevitable futures, causing them to wake knowing that they’re doomed. The only escape is not to sleep at all.

The purpose of these nightmares, or M’rorcameleth’s desires, is something mortals should not dare ask. Nevertheless, wizards and occultists have certainly tried. Oneiromancy is used to reach out to M’rorcameleth, with the few who succeed appearing to master psionic magics. Yet it isn’t clear if the same person who closed their eyes is the entity who awoke, or whether they’ve become a puppet of the Aether Kindred’s consciousness. Entire cults have formed around such individuals. Fanatics hope to dream of M’rorcameleth, so their mind too can be opened.

#### Pharazorthok

Kindred of Knowing

Followers of Pharazorthok claim that its branches hold reality in place. They describe the eldritch being as resembling an enormous tentacled tree, with countless eyes upon its branches, twisting and growing beneath the surface of reality like roots through the soil. The mortal realm sits upon these branches. As does Numena, the Netherworld, and all realms.

It’s whispered that Pharazorthok sees and knows all. Its branches pierce into reality. Its eyes watch from unseen corners to learn every secret and witness every deed. The tendrils of this Aether Kindred can also reach into mortal minds to listen and speak on the Material Plane, though its intentions for doing so are never clear.

Far from raving fanatics, Pharazorthok’s worshippers are patient and learned. The grasp of the Aether Kindred’s branches manifests visions of endless, frightening, cosmic understanding. Some break under such immense knowledge. Those who survive, and who aren’t reduced to living their days in terrified silence, manifest sorcerous power and become educators of Pharazorthok’s truth.

Cults devoted to Pharazorthok revolve around these enlightened spellcasters. Nobody can know for certain if what is being taught is true or the imaginings of a charlatan, but the cult will fervently protect their leader.

Wherever the branches of Pharazorthok wriggle through the veil and into the mortal realm, horrifying aberrations of eyes and teeth are spawned. Such abominations are pointed to as evidence of the claims made by Pharazorthok’s followers.

## Primal Magic in Etharis

Somewhere between arcane and divine magic is the magic of nature, called primal magic by those who differentiate between the three types. This force represents both the power of the elements and the power of things that grow, live, and die outside of the purview of either god or mortal.

Two main cultures in Etharis practice primal magic. The elves of Tol Leyemil believe the Spirits of Nature teach them how to cast spells by communing with the natural forces of the forests, mountains, and rivers. The Valikan Clans evoke their magic from the Primordials living within the Elemental Planes.

To divine spellcasters, the Primordials and Spirits of Nature are no different to the daemons and horrors from where arcanists draw their magic. Yet there is a deep spirituality to primal magic that is missing from the cold academia of arcane magic.

### The Primordic Cycle

Earth. Fire. Ice. Air. These are the primordial elements that roiled and thundered at the beginning of time. The first living thing sparked into existence amongst this primal chaos, and the elements were all it consumed to survive. It shattered rocks with its teeth, gulped lava down its gullet, and devoured storms of wind and hail. The creature grew and grew, until its size encircled all that was. Every element tempested inside of it. This creature was called Gormadraug, the Great Prismatic Wyrm.

For long millennia, Gormadraug was the conscious world. Then one day a mountain called Citrolach awoke on the Wyrm’s back. Citrolach’s heavy footsteps split the earth to create valleys, and here the ice seeped to feed the growing rainforests. From these trees emerged the first tribes of mortals. These tiny creatures were like gnats upon Gormadraug’s back, and the Prismatic Wyrm hated them for how they itched. Gormadraug lashed Citrolach for having wrought them. Thus began Citrolach’s hatred of the Wyrm.

Morakesh exploded from Gormadraug’s breath like lava and fire spewing forth from a volcano. The Lady Morakesh was wielded like a weapon, forced to battle to prove the extent of her fiery destructiveness. When the gods came to challenge Gormadraug’s strength, Morakesh burned them to husks—composing a symphony of agonized screams for the Wyrm's sick pleasure.

Gormadraug tested even his own strength against Morakesh, extinguishing her flames to prove she was no match for him. The Lady harbored a burning hatred. The cinders of betrayal flared inside her heart.

The fires of Morakesh melted the remaining ice that formed the Endless Sea, releasing Alondo, Lord of the Depths. Wishing to be left in solitude, Alondo tried to escape Gormadraug deep beneath the seas. The Prismatic Wyrm followed to show all Primordials that nowhere was beyond his reach.

Ilhara, Queen of Air and Shadow, formed in the shadow and smoke of Morakesh’s flames. Silent and unseen, she travelled throughout the realm learning of the discontent for Gormadraug’s tyrannical rule. Ambitious to replace him, Ilhara found a hero among mortals to kill Gormadraug.

### The Kentigern Saga

Enduring Gormadraug’s tyranny since time began, each of the Primordials ached to be free of their sire. They found their opportunity from an unexpected source: a man named Kentigern and his six companions.

Ilhara convinced her siblings to betray their sire and take the adventurers under their wing. Morakesh and Citrolach formed powerful magic weapons that could pierce Gormadraug’s flesh. Ilhara composed for them a song that would put the Prismatic Wyrm to sleep. Alondo taught them enchantments that would keep them alive, and he also told them where to strike Gormadraug’s heart.

Thus, Kentigern and his companions slew Gormadraug and used his massive body to form the mortal realm. His flesh became the earth, and his spine the mountains that divide the center of Etharis. His blood became the frozen seas and therein his heart was thrown to form an island. Fragments of Gormadraug’s scales can still be found today as precious stones. And thus the world was made.

Yet all four Primordials know that their sire was not truly destroyed, but merely slumbers in a state of hibernation. Even now, mortals obtain command over the elements from the Prismatic Wyrm. The day may come when Gormadraug awakens to wreak his vengeance across Etharis. The Primordials and mortals continually seek to prevent this from happening. Yet with the appearance of coldfire, it seems the Prismatic Wyrm’s resurrection is coming ever closer.



### Primordials

Each of Gormadraug’s children rule over their own primordial realms. The Valikans believe Etharis exists in the gulf between the four Elemental Planes, where air, water, earth, and fire converge and clash together. It’s said to be possible to enter the Elemental Planes where each element is strongest, and that druids draw their power from these realms like a wizard draws numen.

#### Lady Morakesh

Primordial of Fire

The Burning Lady, Red Queen, and Emberheart are among her many names. The Primordial of fire holds court in her realm of Flamebourne. She commands azers, efreeti, and fire giants in her army. Morakesh suffered the worst under Gormadraug, as the Wyrm saw her as his most destructive weapon.

As such, she is highly capricious and easy to anger; she rewards characters with great riches as easily as she can turn them to cinders. Great care must be taken in petitioning her. Druidic cults of blood and fire are among her worshipers, who perform burning sacrifices of sentient beings to invoke Morakesh’s favor.

#### Alondo

Primordial of Ice and Water

Lord of the Deep and ruler of the Endless Sea, Alondo controls the denizens of lakes, seas, and oceans. He is worshipped by the laneshi. Aside from Kentigern, Alondo has no love for mortals and wishes to be left alone. That said, he is the most conscientious of his ilk, working diligently to prevent the return of Gormadraug.

Still, rumors persist that he has something to do with the outbreak of coldfire plaguing Etharis. Blood druids who would invoke Alondo must first slay one of his hated enemies and hurl its heart into the sea.

#### Citrolach

Primordial of Earth and Stone

The Living Mountain masters over all those who pledge themselves to earth and stone. He is the progenitor of stoneborn trolls and earth elementals, who emerge from his underground realm of Stonereach. At times through history, Citrolach has been worshipped by dwarves and gnomes who delve for riches beneath the earth. He is also important to druids of the land and moon, who believe his power extends to plants and the denizens of the forests.

It is common for Citrolach's worshippers to bury their wealth in gems and gold back into the earth, returned to the Primordial’s realm from where it was taken. Dwarves are said to inherit their stereotypical stubbornness from Citrolach, who has held to a particular stand for centuries. The saying goes that it is easier to move a mountain than convince Citrolach to change his mind.

#### Ilhara

Primordial of Air and Shadow

The Queen of Air and Shadow is the most ambitious Primordial of all. Her spies hide in the smoke of candles and lurk in empty shadows to find secrets from among mortals and spirits alike. Her ultimate aim is to be powerful enough to fill the void left by Gormadraug. Ilhara requires that a dark secret be whispered into the air before she can be summoned.

Assassins and scouts who stalk the gloom or deal in poisons may petition Ilhara for aid. She is of particular importance to the spies of Clan Morgöng in Valika.

#### Gormadraug

Primordial of All Elements

The Great Prismatic Wyrm is also a powerful source of primal magic, though he doesn’t know this in his current state. There is no known way to invoke him. Moreover, worship of the Wyrm is forbidden throughout the Valikan Clans. The Primordials also warn that drawing power from Gormadraug may even awaken him, and they readily smite any druids or rangers who try.

Much blood is spilled in the land of Thrull to keep Gormadraug in slumber. Raiders from that region of Valika take prisoners from the settlements they attack, specifically to be hung from trees and sacrificed for the good of all the world. Smaller cults also perform this practice. They might be found in other regions of Valika or the northern Bürach Empire.

### The Faerie Courts

Before the Era of Expansion, the Faerie Courts were worshipped like deities in Caer Neiada—the realm now called the Charneault Kingdom. The elves of that region believed they were descendants of the archfey and that magic was only possible by listening to the Spirits of Nature. In ancient times, it’s said that many more elves were capable of magic than today.

In modern Charneault, the Faerie Courts are still revered. The culture is much more interlaced with the influence of Büracher religions, some settlements preferring to offer prayers to the Arch Seraphs while others make tributes to the archfey. The religions are not incompatible, however. There is rumor among scholars that the archfey are, in fact, seraphs themselves, downcast from the heavens to the mortal realm in one of Etharis’s ancient cataclysms—perhaps the Aetheric War. Here, the Spirits of Nature cultivated a second heavenly paradise, hidden within the folds of the Realm of Faerie.

Such beliefs are not commonly held. Many folk of Charneault consider the fey more devilish than divine. Some paladins might swear oaths to an Arch Seraph but are happy to receive gifts from the Spirits of Nature, while others bind their faith exclusively to the Faerie Courts.

Similar beliefs are found in eastern Nordenland and parts of Valika. Though these more human cultures feel less directly connected to the Faerie Courts, even regarding the Archfey like capricious devils, they still train mages and make pacts to wield primal magic.
