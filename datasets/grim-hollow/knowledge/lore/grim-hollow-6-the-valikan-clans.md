# Chapter 6: The Valikan Clans

The lands of Valika cover the very northernmost region of Etharis, crowning the continent with an icy splendor that few outside of the clans can appreciate. The six largest clans—Rune, Sýr, Völgr, Mithra, Morgöng, and Limgri—claim dominance over much of the land, though minor tribes can be found scattered throughout the harsh wilds.

Much like the clans themselves, the provinces of Valika each have distinctive traits that set them apart. Grarjord abuts the Bürach Empire along the southern border—with its vast pine forests. The barren lands of Thrull act as home to those clans that rely on raiding and primordial magic, where dark traditions are held in the highest esteem. The vast province of Kandar comprises the eastern half of Valika, meeting the Ostoyan Empire along the mountains of the Grey Spine—who strive to change the perception that the rest of Etharis has about the Valikan clans. Through Valika, the land is as rugged and enduring as its people.

While those outside Valika may only think of the raiders of Thrull, with images of bloodthirsty barbarians brought to mind, the perception has begun to change. This has led to population growth along the southernmost borders as an influx of southerners arrive for diplomacy and trade.

Ever adaptable, the folk of Valika shall persevere through whatever awaits them. It should be known though, that despite the ever-shifting winds of change, some things do remain constant, chief among them the tradition of passing down stories that relate the rich history and tales of the lands of Valikan ancestors. These tales shape each generation, ensuring the strength of the land for years to come.

## Geography

Matt DeMino Travelling across Valika requires one to be prepared for varied terrains and weather conditions. As the stories go, the region was once known as the Primordial Land, constantly in flux and uninhabitable by mere mortals.

Today, sudden elemental changes are infrequent, though not unheard of. Coldfire sweeps down from Volgen in the far northeastern reaches of Valika, consuming the land and transforming it into a harsh and unforgiving wasteland of ice. In the wake of the coldfire, an ice bridge remains connecting Volgen to the mainland. Many fear coldfire’s continued spread, envisioning the entire region as a massive barren tundra if the worst were to occur.

Presently, one can find mighty fjords along the coastlines, leading into steep, ice-covered peaks that descend into an open tundra that makes up most of the landmass of Grarjord and Thrull. Snowcapped forests dot the edges of the fjords south of the city of Halsfjord—the seat of Clan Rune. To the north of the Teeth, frozen rivers wind down from the perpetually frozen lake Frostmere, which shares its name with the ice fortress that stands atop its treacherous surface.

The Thockan Pass makes cuts through the Haeysor Mountains to Borgund, the seat of Clan Sýr. Northwest of these mountains lies the Holgar Strait, providing access to the island of Holgar and the city of Tyburn. This bustling port is the seat of Clan Völgr and the primary launch point for Valikan reaver fleets that sow terror along the coast.

East of Borgund, forests bleed into the open tundra before climbing the Grensfal Mountains and Foxwood Range, nominally delineating Kandar’s boundary. Hrist, Kandar’s capital, lies on the mountain’s southeastern edge along the valley formed by the Isbryteralva river, which flows south from Lake Jakkan towards the Skuggiskogr Forest. The cities of Summerhelm and Runeheim rest near the edges of the forest.

Continuing eastward, the Njucka Mountains complete the ring that protects Cold Iron Keep, with the Ormralva River flowing down from those peaks into Shadow Storm Gulf. The mountains continue eastward, meeting up with the coldfire bridge that spans the gap between the mainland and Volgen, meeting at Fort Kentigern. The isle of Cinderghast lies in a smoky haze off the northern coastline, in a stark counterpoint to the barren frozen wastelands of Volgen, host to the Snow Queen’s palace in the Astrik Mountains.

## History

The history of the Valikan Clans is mostly passed down via oral tradition, but the occasional legends have been written down by scholars throughout the ages. Most of these legends chronicle the major milestones that shaped the face of the lands and influenced the cultural standards that are still practiced by most of the clans to this day. The greatest legends were compiled into a manuscript known as the Lord Edda by the scholars at Hrist, and copies of various versions remain in safekeeping.

### The Great Prismatic Wyrm

Legends of the time before humans occupied the northern lands speak of the mighty primordials roaming this region. Their power was said to be so great as to rival that of the gods, drawing from each of the sacred elements—fire, water, air, and earth.

The mightiest among these elementals was a colossal serpent known as Gormadraug, and he was worshiped even by his peers, treated as a prince whom they bowed before. Stories speak of his enormous size, and the strength and powers he held that outshone any other primordials. Of these stories, none sow quite as much fear as the depictions of the utter destruction he left in his wake as he traversed the lands, with coldfire erupting at his every step, utterly consuming all living things and leaving behind a barren wasteland.

Of course, even such a fearsome beast as Gormadraug must have a weakness, and this was found to be true. The Seven Heroes—for whom the current ruling clans are named—sought out the Great Wyrm to end his reign: Kentigern of the Wastes, the sorceress Rune, Sýr the Seven-Bladed, Völgr Clad-in-Iron, Limgri Lightbringer, Morgöng Shadestrider, and Mithra of the Silver Harp.

These heroes hoped to vanquish Gormadraug so as to allow the northern lands a chance to settle and become suitable for habitation. They fought battle after battle against countless hordes of Gormadraug’s elemental forces, eventually being swallowed by the Great Wyrm himself. However, this was not to be the end, as they hatched a daring plan from within the belly of the beast to pierce the creature’s heart and defeat him once and for all…or so their story goes.

Many, however, believe that the Great Wyrm will one day rise again to consume all of Etharis. Many of the clans believe this tale—and the potentially apocalyptic ending—to be literally true. Woe to those who stand between these fundamentalist Valikans and their goals.

### Thorgard’s Saga

The Seven Heroes hold a special place in the legends of Valika, but other heroes have risen in the days since the land was claimed. Thousands of years after the fall of Gormadraug, the clans were divided over countless minor squabbles, and the need arose for a hero to unite the clans.

Thorgard, an orc of Clan Völgr, decided he would be the hero required by his people, and the mighty warrior sought a path to unite the clans. Aided by a hag who had visions of the future, Thorgard quested for the legendary weapon of Kentigern, the hero who assembled the Seven Heroes. With Bane-Slayer—a magical double-bladed sword wreathed in ice and flames—in hand, Thorgard began his campaign for unity, travelling from clan to clan gathering forces beneath his banner to defeat the common enemy of all clans: invading southerners.

By force or by diplomacy, one after another, each clan pledged their banners to Thorgard, sharpening their weapons and preparing their longships for battle. A cunning plan was hatched to draw the southern forces out into the open by undertaking a series of raids against the coastal settlements they had established. Once the southern troops had been driven inland to the tundra, they were met by fierce warriors from each of the tribes. Battles raged for some time, but the united strength of the clans proved to be enough to drive the invaders back south beyond the border of Valika.

With his victory in hand, Thorgard then set out with a mighty fleet to conquer more lands in the west for Valika, but alas, that was the last time anyone saw the ferocious conqueror. Tales of his courage and determination remain staples of Valikan storytelling, and the saga of Thorgard is forever in the minds of his people.

### The Prismatic Circle

The reputation of Valika as a land of brutal raiders has been propagated by the druids of the Prismatic Circle. Years ago, an elemental druid known as Kybard claimed to have experienced prophetic visions which proved that the Seven Heroes had not vanquished Gormadraug, but that he merely sleeps beneath the earth and sea. Kybard claimed that his visions showed a future where the Great Wyrm awakened to destroy the world. This future could only be prevented by sating the creature with magic and the blood of mortals.

The druids of the Prismatic Circle tirelessly travel the northern lands between the ritual sites known as Sentinel Woods, making great sacrifices of magical trinkets triumphantly taken from the southlands, stolen livestock barged north throughout the raiding season, and humanoid victims—both the condemned of the Thrull-based clans and thralls marked as offerings to slake the hunger of Gormadraug’s sleeping spirit.

The total number of Valikan warriors fallen over centuries of raiding, southlanders killed defending against the longboats’ attacks, and victims slain as part of the Prismatic Circle’s rituals might never be known and is certainly uncountable. With each new raiding season, the ground beneath the Sentinel Woods grows ever darker with blood.

The druids of the Prismatic Circle are led by the Grand Druid Hjadana, who steadfastly upholds the sanctity of Kybard’s visions as the driving force behind their purpose. Her fanaticism to the cause has led to the vicious cycles of raiding and death that make most in the southern lands tremble at the thought of Valika.

The brutality that Hjadana oversees has also allowed the Prismatic Circle to maintain a firm hold on the fealty of the clans of Thrull and Grarjord, especially that of Clan Völgr, whose might in battle serve them well as they functionally serve as the military might which enforces the Circle’s edicts.

### Raiding and Thralls

Valikan raids inspire a deep fear in the people of Etharis, all of whom have heard the tales of Clan Völgr’s berserker fury, the strength of Clan Sýr’s arms and armor, and the potency of Clan Rune’s destructive magic. When Valikan longships sail south early in spring, settlements along the coast from Kulen Sound to the Gulf of Lions look to the security of their food and trade goods, their livestock, and their valuables. While those items are precious, the most valuable treasure that the raiding parties return with is thralls.

Valika’s harsh winters take a toll on the population. The more desperate and immoral clans bolster their population through captives brought back from raids. Raiding for thralls is so old a tradition among the clans that many Valikans, mostly those under the banners of the Thrull-based clans, have no perspective to even question the practice. The north is a harsh land sparsely covered by small settlements, where folk understand that they are responsible for their own survival. But the number of people needed to work the rocky fields, to delve the mines, to crew fishing boats on the rough waters, and to hunt the fast-moving game herds of the north has never been matched by the available population of the clans. So, millennia ago, those clans looked southward for the folk they needed to keep their settlements and their culture alive. The Valikan clans raid each other endlessly, to be sure. Folk captured by another clan can be bonded into servitude as easily as the folk of foreign lands. But such local raids effectively see the clans only trading members back and forth, and so they focus more on the theft of resources, livestock, lore, and portable wealth—leaving thrall raids to the reaving fleets of the south. ### Politics

Grand Druid Hjadana and Chief Nolgr

Most of the clans inhabiting the western Valikan territory of Thrull, while not technically under the rule of the Prismatic Circle, follows the edicts and rules set forth by the Grand Druid. These edicts encourage the clans to participate in constant conflict, raiding enemies and neighbors alike in the hopes of appeasing Gormadraug the Great Wyrm and maintaining his slumbering vigil. The Grand Druid commands the circle from their base of operations on the island of Holgar. Technically, though, each clan has their own system of governance.

Clan Rune. Of the major Valikan clans, Clan Rune, enjoys one of the most representative political structures. An overseer controls each settlement. Larger towns typically have an overseer council of three elected officials, while the clan seat at Halsfjord is overseen by the Council of Rune—thirteen councilors elected by the folk of the city to govern the clan. The authority of local councilors is absolute in matters governed by clan laws, which have long been codified and recorded in written form. However, when clan members in dispute with each other disagree with a councilor’s decree, appeals can be made to the Council of Rune.

Clan Sýr. The politics of Clan Sýr are tied to the hierarchy of the Järnförning. At ten years of age, each member of Clan Sýr is bonded to the Järnförning, a hierarchy of labor, responsibility, and authority that shapes the lives of all clanfolk. The Järnförning is both guild and school, set up as twelve tiers—called “nivåer” by clan tradition—of knowledge and skill running the range from apprentice to clan master.

Each settlement, large or small, is led by three wardens, each of which must be a member of the highest nivå found in that settlement, and who are voted into service by other members of the same nivå.

Clan Völgr. The hierarchy within Clan Völgr is established by dominance, with the strongest and hardiest inevitably rising to positions of authority in their raiding parties and home villages. But strength is reckoned in many ways among the Völgr—the most powerful warriors often willingly pay fealty to figures with weaker physical and martial attributes who have proven leadership and tactical acumen.

Standing atop the clan power structure, Chief Nolgr Magnusson has ruled the Völgr for nearly thirty years, and as clan leader, Nolgr is also Keeper of the Three Tribes, a title bestowed by the Prismatic Circle, which effectively makes him master of all the Thrull-based clans.

Clan Mithra. Like Clan Rune in Thrull, Clan Mithra has a representative political structure. Villages hold things—also known as moots—in which they elect an overseer. All townsfolk over the age of fifteen can vote at the thing. Overseers appoint a Voice, usually from among the best musicians and skalds, to travel to the larger things of the jarldom in which they reside. While jarls are also elected, their positions tend to stay within their families, blurring the lines between democracy and a hereditary monarchy.

At the fall equinox althing, set at the end of the annual harvest celebration, the jarls elect a king or queen from among themselves. The current monarch is Queen Andrea Helsing, who rules from the capital city of Kandar, Hrist. The greatest foes to Clan Mithra are the clans of Thrull, whose incursions have forced the Mithrans to develop and hold their standing armies. Preferring diplomacy, Queen Andrea still hopes that there can be a solution to the conflict beyond the continued loss of life. Thus far, violence seems to be the only answer their foes will accept.

Clan Morgöng. Clan Morgöng lacks anything resembling a centralized government. Jarl Summerhelm holds a hereditary title and village leaders are typically the eldest villagers, those who have survived the most raids or weathered the most winters. As a matter of pride, Clan Morgöng has no written records. Laws are memorized by a lawspeaker, typically a skald, who then interprets disputes based on prior precedence. Typical punishments include fines, brief indenturement as a thrall, or an alternate form of action that rights the wrong. Petty thievery against other villagers may be accepted once, but repeat offenders risk being exiled from the village. The serious crimes of murder and sexual assault are not tolerated; killing is allowed on raids, but never at home, and sexual assault is forbidden even on raids. These, along with intentional damage to any of the village’s watercraft, are viewed as the most grievous crimes and are punishable by execution.

Clan Limgri. Like Clan Morgöng, there’s very little central government ruling Clan Limgri. Family units operate independently, although they do gather for celebrations four times a year, on the solstices and equinoxes. As far as others are concerned, Jarl Angrboða Grata of Solheim and Taala Brightstar speak for the clan. In most cases, this is true: while the jarl and the druid have little impact over how the families and communities are organized, they are the two most in touch with the rest of the elders and leaders among the clan. Among the small groups of traveling nomads, Limgri’s druids and warlocks are most likely to take on leadership positions.

There are few codified laws among Clan Limgri, although each community may have specific laws and penalties for transgressions as enacted by their own leaders. Across the clan, those who commit the gravest transgressions are abandoned in the wilds on their own. Without supplies or a community, abandonment in the wilds means almost certain death.

Hospitality is the one governing law of Clan Limgri. Any strangers found in the northern wilds are welcomed to stay as long as they need. Strangers are never denied food and drink; all that the Limgri have is shared with those they encounter in the wilderness. For this reason, the Limgri frequently find themselves victims of Völgr raids; their generosity is taken advantage of by scouts, who then report the nomads’ location to Völgr raiding parties. Unwilling to change their own cultural laws, the Limgri, through Jarl Grata and Taala Brightstar, have petitioned for the Clan Mithra armies to extend their outposts further north. Queen Andrea has provided those warriors in exchange for Clan Limgri taking control of Cold Iron Keep, which houses the most dangerous, non-Valikan criminals in Kandar.

## Provinces

Unlike the other kingdoms, borders in Valika are more of a nebulous concept. Each province flows into the next without much regard for boundaries, unless there is a physical feature, such as a river or mountain range, that easily delineates the divide. Which often means that clan members might cross into the territory of a rival clan without knowing—until they come across a rival clan’s hunting party.

### Thrull

The area known as Thrull stretches from the island of Holgar in the far west of the northlands—home to the city of Tyburn and the seat of the Prismatic Circle—to the foothills of the Grensfal Mountains that shield the city of Hrist against land raids from Clan Völgr. Thrull is the dominant culture in Valika, at least as far as the major clans are concerned. And because those clans define the raiding culture by which Valika is known, so too do folk across Etharis acknowledge the primacy of this province and its people.

#### Important Individuals

Grand Druid Hjadana. As the Grand Druid of the Prismatic Circle, Hjadana holds a large influence on the political and cultural habits of many of Valika’s clan and leaders. From her home in Tyburn on the island of Holgar, Hjadana presides over the ritualistic sacrifices that the circle claims are necessary to appease the sleeping Prismatic Wyrm, Gormadraug, and keep him in his resting state. These rituals originated from the visions of the druid Kybard, and her fanaticism for his views drive the clans of Thrull to bloodlust, which can only be sated by reaving. Whether the victims be Valikan or foreign is often of little concern to Hjadana, only that blood is spilled to appease Gormadraug. Hjadana’s closeness to Chief Nolgr Magnusson of Clan Völgr, and the Prismatic Circle’s presence in the Völgr clan seat of Tyburn, ensures that the bonds between the druids and that clan’s warriors and raiders remains strong.

Grenhildr. Once a protégé of Hjadana and thought to be her successor, a traumatic near-death experience caused Grenhildr to reject the traditional beliefs of the Prismatic Circle, in turn causing a schism of faith within the circle.

Grenhildr had been separated from her party after traveling to complete a ritual sacrifice and got lost in a blizzard, where she was assumed to be dead. Much to the surprise of the great druid, she returned to Tyburn, bringing with her a tale of having been saved and fed by prismatic drakes, and spending the winter in a sheltered cavern where Gormadraug the Great Prismatic Wyrm spoke to her. Grenhildr told any who would listen of how Gormadraug’s long sleep was causing the wyrm’s power to fade, and how the north would come to apocalyptic ruin if that power ever ended.

Her followers are known as the Cult of the Great Wyrm. Much to the horror of Hjadana, their beliefs have been spreading and catching on across the lands of Valika, even in the province of Thrull, where the Prismatic Circle holds the most sway.

Nolgr Magnusson. Chief Nolgr Magnusson of the Völgr is effectively the leader of all Thrull, though his control over the three primary clans is not as absolute as some would think. As the chief of the Völgr clan, Nolgr is the war leader of his people, personally leading raids against lesser clans, the lands of Kandar, and the peoples of the south for nearly three decades. As Keeper of the Three Tribes, he has been invested by the Prismatic Circle with an extra level of authority over Clan Rune and Clan Sýr, at least ceremonially. And Nolgr is a master of ceremony, and a leader whose charisma and personal magnetism have proved nearly as deadly as his axe hand. Nolgr proudly acknowledges that his relationship with the Grand Druid Hjadana is the foundation of his power in Thrull, and rumors fly that he may have fathered at least one of her children.

Ordun Doransson. The chief of Clan Rune, Ordun cuts an imposing figure, with a shadowed complexion and sun-bright hair, under-shaved and braided. Protected against the elements by his magic, he usually goes bare-chested even in the coldest weather, presenting an intimidating presence to all those who meet him. By presenting himself not just as a formidable rune mage but a potent war chief, Ordun has positioned himself in his people’s eyes as an equal to Nolgr Magnusson, chief of Clan Völgr and anointed as Keeper of the Three Tribes by the Prismatic Circle. And as Ordun has done so, the pride and independence of Clan Rune has grown.

### Kandar

The area known as Kandar reaches as far to the northeast as Volgen, which was once an island but is now connected to the mainland by a coldfire glacial bridge. The more populated regions are far to the west, near the center of the peninsula, at the Grensfal Mountains and the disputed lands adjacent to Thrull. There, the united troops of the Kandar clans of Mithra, Limgri, and Morgöng keep watch. The more temperate Summerhelm in the southwest provides a southern border for the major Kandar clans, though a city even further south, Runeheim, is nominally within Kandar’s borders.

Despite Kandar’s diplomatic efforts, the people of Etharis still first associate Valika with the greater violence of Thrull. Kandar’s desire for peace has allowed them to make inroads with the southern nations, but the wilds of the region, the facial tattoos worn by many of the northerners, and the reputation of their neighbors leave the people of Kandar fighting against stereotypes from any foreigners within their lands.

#### Important Individuals

Queen Andrea Helsing. Queen Helsing of Hrist is known to most of the people of Kandar. She is the leader of Clan Mithra, and the elected war chief of the united alliance of Kandar clans. Queen Andrea is a ruler who must balance the needs of the various clans with the threat of Thrull in the west. Her ambition and desire to drive change and progress in Kandar led her to become a good ruler, even though she may claim that she never desired the role.

Queen Andrea is dedicated to the survival of Kandar, free from the Prismatic Circle and the threat of Thrull. She has no philosophical or cultural problem with her fellow Valikans across the border, but she seeks to keep the clans within Kandar unconquered. This requires her to reach out to the southern lands. Fighting against the prejudice that all the people of the northlands are barbarians has been a struggle, and one of the ways Queen Andrea counters that image is by supporting Hrist’s cosmopolitan look and atmosphere.

She welcomes trade with other nations and pursues alliances with those who would offer military or magical aid. In addition to her political alliances, she is savvy when it comes to working outside the rules. She frequently has her agents hire adventurers for tasks related to the war effort, or in creating situations that would lead other cities and nations to ally with Kandar. If some of these missions seem to walk a thin line along the edge of honor, well, she’s not the one carrying them out—that ethical question rests solely on those she has hired.

Though much of her childhood was spent in Hrist, Andrea traveled throughout Kandar with a diplomatic mission from Clan Mithra to the other clans during her youth. She was part of the entourage, not as a diplomat herself, but as a bodyguard to Urda Longsight, a Clan Mithra seer and poet. The ancient woman took Andrea under her wing, and though the young warrior never took to the reading of runes or entrails, she learned a great deal about patience and listening—and how knowing what someone wants can be used as leverage when forming alliances or compromises.

Once known as a golden-haired shield maiden, Queen Andrea has grayed over the past several years, not only due to her growing age but also her worry over Kandar’s fate. Queen Andrea shows her battle scars with honor, frequently wearing sleeveless tunics under a fur cape, so that the old injuries are obviously displayed on her pale arms.

Taala Brightstar. Taala was raised among the wandering Limgri with their family and was marked as a seer at a very young age. Their talent for nature magic blossomed while Taala was still a child, and they plagued their family by running with the reindeer—sometimes in the shape of a reindeer—making it difficult to keep track of the youth. As the speaker of Clan Limgri, Taala devotes their time to manifesting the Lightbringer philosophy to the clans, making life as full of beauty and peace for their people as possible.

While they have seen many threats to the people of Valika during their long life, but none so frightening as the dual disaster of war with Thrull while coldfire expands its grasp of the north. The elderly druid fears for the future of their people, and does everything they can to ensure that Clan Limgri, along with the other clans of Kandar, will see the new world when it comes. Despite all the troubles plaguing the world, Taala has not lost hope. Taala views themself as a spiritual heir to Limgri Lightbringer and is dedicated to shining that light against any shadows that encroach on Valika.

As they aged, Taala’s temperament evened out, but they still prefer to wear a furred or feathered form to walking with humans. The latter has become a necessity, however, not only due to Taala’s aging body, but also due to their position as an advisor in Solheim and the central point for all of Clan Limgri’s complaints and concerns.

They seek to support the efforts of the Order of Kentigern in staving off coldfire and speak on behalf of Clan Limgri at althings to request protection for the northern clan from Völgr raids. Taala has also tried to reach out to the Redclaws, hoping that the secretive order will accept aid and support from the united Clans to face off against the Prismatic Circle, but the Redclaws remain elusive, perhaps because Taala is only skilled at shapeshifting and not a true shapeshifter.

Thrulldr. A prime example of the close ties between nature and humans in Valika, Thrulldr is an enormous werebear who spends his time split between his human and bear forms. He claims his hulking appearance is due more to the transformation he underwent as a boy than his Valikan heritage. Even in human form, Thrulldr’s body is covered with dark hair so thick it might as well be fur, and he proudly wears a braided beard extending down his chest.

Among his people, Thrulldr is known for his great rumbling laugh and his generous spirit. But until anyone enters the circle he considers his family, the huge man treats them with suspicion, or even derision. To Thrulldr, druids who have not undergone a final transformation to give their bodies a second, permanent shape, are not close enough to the wild to be trusted. For clanfolk who fear Thrull’s invasion, or coldfire’s looming presence, the Redclaws offer a kind of hope for another path, one that embraces their harsh natural world rather than fighting against it. Thrulldr views all recruits to the circle as potential allies, but he withholds judgment until an older member of the circle can speak for them.

The Redclaws are primarily druids, but other shapeshifters, both natural-born and transformed, are among their number, and the final test—the transformation into one of the werecreatures within the ranks of the circle—is not determined by skill with nature magic, but strength of heart and will. In this way, Thrulldr has built a widespread following, all beholden to him as their alpha, but few gathered in one place.

Njavesh Luunsdóttir. While Njavesh is a druid, she does not belong to any of the typical orders found across Valika, but instead was raised in the wilds. Born in a druidic circle known as the Fox Whisperers, she is a gifted caster and shapechanger who holds a deep connection with the many awakened foxes who roam the northern tundra and Grensfal hills.

As the leader of the Fox Whisperers, Njavesh has a wide network of information available to her, gathered from her decades of wandering and from the secrets that the frostbite foxes return to her. Any who become injured while traveling through the territory the foxes roam, or those who respectfully request an audience through the frostbite foxes to seek answers can gain an audience with Njavesh.

Sanrun. Abbot Sanrun is head of the formerly contemplative Order of Kentigern. A bald man with grudgel features, old enough to remember a time before the current coldfire crisis—and yet still appearing in his prime, the abbot has a mournful aura. Though he maintains a welcoming and friendly air, especially when greeting recruits to the order, there is always a sense of how much the world and its crises weigh on this man, who intended a life of quiet solitude, not war.

Originally a member of Clan Mithra, Sanrun is fully devoted to the Order of Kentigern, and is dismayed at the lack of funding the order has been receiving since the border war began. In fact, Sanrun believes that Kandar and Thrull must unite to face the coldfire crisis before it freezes the entire north, though his calls for unity are generally ignored in Hrist.

Despite this, Sanrun has expanded his work in recruiting those who would keep coldfire at bay, reaching out beyond the borders of Kandar and into the southern kingdoms. He is particularly interested in bringing evokers and alchemists into the order, as the first can combat coldfire directly with their spells, and the latter can create the tools with which the rest of the order fights. Alchemist’s fire, burning oils, and exploding casks are among the explosives collected by the Order of Kentigern, and Abbot Sanrun knows that they will never have enough.

Still, despite his sorrowful attitude, Abbot Sanrun runs a tight organization, giving rank and status to each member so they know whose commands to obey and where they fit in the hierarchy. The order’s training grounds within Fort Kentigern are extensive, and their work with ice drakes, polar bears, and giant lynxes requires many riders and handlers who are skilled with animals. Druids who are unable to take the final step to join the Redclaws frequently make their way, after their failed test, to the Order of Kentigern, where Abbot Sanrun welcomes them with open arms.

### Grarjord

Often, the entirety of the north is called Grarjord by southerners, and many from the southern lands still refer to the lands of the Valikan clans by this ancient name. While Grarjord still encompasses much of the land from the border of the Bürach Empire stretching up along the coastline, and outward across the open tundra, determining where the borders between it and Thrull or Kandar lie can be a tricky proposition.

The open tundra that comprises most of Grarjord is the home of many minor clans that enjoy the relative freedom they find here away from the influence of clans Rune, Sýr, and Völgr, and their constant clashes with the clans of Kandar. While technically, much of the tundra does fall under the authority of Thrull and its clans, there are very few resources worth squabbling over, and therefore, very little oversight of the people who settle there. The downside to this arrangement lies in the unforgiving nature of the land, the bitter cold, its barren soil, and the monstrous beasts that lurk outside the light of a warm campfire.

### Locations of Interest

The following locations are places of power, importance, and adventure within the lands of the Valikan clans.

#### Skarfanes

This town once served as a watch post and waystation where the reaving fleets could stop and resupply. However, its proximity to the Bürach Empire, and to their prized city of Erlefurt, led to an eventual capture by the Bürach’s fleet. Rather than using the town as a safehold to push further into Grarjord, the empire declared Skarfanes to be a free city and neutral trading post. Today, clan traders largely control Skarfanes, even as it’s protected by imperial forces set up as a satellite of the garrison in Erlefurt.

The town is a sprawling settlement of huts and homes, clustered around longhouses and warehouses and surrounded by a formidable stockade wall built of imported timbers. Four gates mark the entry points into the city, but just as many locals live outside Skarfanes as inside, with most of the minor clans of Grarjord maintaining permanent steadings within sight of the walls. Moreover, after three generations, intermarriage between the imperial occupiers and local clanfolk has created a mixed culture unique to Skarfanes.

#### Halsfjord

Situated along one of the many peninsulas that make up the Teeth— the series of long, fjord-cut peninsulas that give the northlands the appearance of a great beast set to consume the south—Halsfjord serves as the seat of Clan Rune. This position makes it one of the most easily protected cities in all of Valika, a fact which its residents surely appreciate with their intimate knowledge of raiding.

Halsfjord also boasts a store of magical knowledge to rival any colleges in the Bürach Empire. The residents understand all too well the value of their knowledge and craft. Stealthy raids by war bands are a constant threat, with many minor clans intent on bringing knowledgeable Clan Rune mages or artificers into their service as thralls.

Halsfjord stands as a beacon on rocky terrain, high above its namesake fjord, its walls fortified by years of enchantments adding to its layers of defense. Magical beacons blaze in the night, leading the way for ships and merchants alike. Magic is the leading commodity that Clan Rune trades for all the food and sundries required by residents.

Halsfjor’ds might is mostly attributed to it being the final home of Rune, the clan’s namesake sorcerer. She used powerful magic to imprison Cinderghast’s fire elementals before retiring to her village. In the years following, many of the world’s best sorcerers and artificers visited Halsfjord seeking her knowledge, which resulted in the formation of numerous magical schools. These schools enabled Halsfjord to become the vibrant city that is seen today.

#### Hrist

Hrist is the seat of the Kandar province and Clan Mithra. It is where Queen Andrea Helsing holds court for her jarls in the Great Hall. It is a bustling city, inviting people from across Etharis to live and work. Most foreigners who don’t have valuable skills to contribute to the clan—such as metalworking—find themselves relegated to the foreign quarter in the city’s outer circle beyond its walls. Soldier barracks and a large marketplace where less reputable merchants hawk their wares can also be found in the outer circle.

The inner circle houses the Great Hall and the residences of most of the oldest families of Clan Mithra. Many strive to move up in reputation enough to gain a coveted residence in the first circle, but most Valikans and skilled craftspeople reside in the middle circle, which also houses the Kaupang, Hrist’s largest market, and the Skald Library, which is renowned throughout Etharis for its extensive collection of relics and artifacts.

#### Tyburn

As the seat of Clan Völgr and the seat of the Thrull Province, this bustling port city is spoken of with equal parts fear and reverence. The fearsome fleets of longships that deposit raiders upon the shores of their southern neighbors depart from the deep harbor on the southern corner of the island of Holgar.

Further adding to the city’s reputation, the Prismatic Circle and its leader, Hjadana, established their base of operations in Tyburn. As a result, the circle has been able to heavily project their influence into the government, enough so that one could argue that Thrull is a theocracy dictated by the edicts of the Prismatic Circle.

#### Borgund

The city of Borgund in the Armarka Mountains is the largest settlement in Thrull’s north, and the seat of Clan Sýr. Said to have been first settled while Sýr the Seven-Bladed still lived, the upper levels of Borgund spread along a series of stepped mountainside terraces lined with stone-walled houses.

But the true size of the city becomes apparent only once visitors pass into Sýr’s Well, a set of slow-sloping ramps that descend from a broad central court atop the highest terrace, down into the stony depths of the mountain. Long tunnels radiate out from the foot of the well like spokes, with hundreds of chambers and halls sharing space with the great underground forges—the legacy of Clan Sýr and its people’s legendary skill with metalwork.

With much of Borgund set within the mountains of the far north, the half-year of night that descends across the region in winter is of little concern to those who dwell here. Borgund’s halls and tunnels are a warm warren of magical rune light, making this one of the most pleasant places to spend the long winters in Valika. The heat of the forges balances the chill of stone, with fresh air constantly cycled into the depths through elaborate ventilation tunnels.

Borgund is a hub of manufacturing for Clan Sýr’s hallmark weapons and armor, and the site of the classrooms and training forges where young clanfolk and thralls are trained. More so than in any other area of Thrull, many of Borgund’s thralls elect to join Clan Sýr once their term of service is up, dedicating themselves to the artistry of metal. As a result, Borgund boasts more dwarves, elves, and other folk of the southlands than any other settlement in Thrull.

#### Summerhelm

The second largest settlement in Kandar after Hrist, Summerhelm is the seat of Jarl Birger av Summerhelm. The city is ringed by a tall stone wall, which casts shadows over the city, making it seem as if caught in a perpetual twilight.

This shadowy atmosphere perfectly suits the needs of the many illicit merchants that make the city their home. Though many storefronts appear to be on the right side of Kandar’s cultural laws, the greater center of commerce is known as the Dream Market. As Jarl Summerhelm’s attention towards the city has dwindled, the Dream Market has become more open about its wares. Weaponry, frequently ill-gotten, goes for reasonable prices, and magic charms, both real and fake, fill the market tables.

Unlike most of Kandar, Summerhelm’s construction is more like what is found in the southern kingdoms, with the Summer Palace being the greatest example. The large fortress is built like an Ostoyan Castle and was likely held by the ancestors of that nation in the distant past. But deeper inside the building, it’s clear that humans were not the original builders of this monument to strength.

#### Solheim

As the name suggests, this small city was once home to a grand temple to the sun. However, that temple fell into disrepair after the gods fell, and now sits prominently in the center of the city as a decaying reminder of how faith alone cannot save you.

The smallest of Kandar’s three cities, Solheim sits on the Shadow Storm Gulf, bereft of faith, existing but no longer thriving. The shipyards are Solheim’s one remaining pride, along with the well-kept great hall, where Jarl Angrboða Grata stores her things. Still, an overarching atmosphere of doom pervades the city, even on the rare occasions when the sun does shine down on them.

#### Fort Kentigern

Originally built as a monastery for an order of contemplative monks, Fort Kentigern has become the last shield between the coldfire waves and the rest of the northlands. The high keep is built in a style similar to the fortresses of the south, with thick stone walls shaped by both craftspeople and mages. The fort’s curtain walls are 10 feet thick and sport defensive siege weaponry. The ramparts are lined with catapults, trebuchets, and ballista, all of which are designed to hurl flaming pitch into the cold blue flames. The ramparts also feature enclosures where evokers can hurl their own fireballs to the grounds below.

Here, the Order of Kentigern gathers, not for contemplation, but as defenders of the living—and, perhaps, the world. Unlike the rest of Valika, the clans hold no sway in Fort Kentigern. Once a person has joined the order, their past is forgotten; their life becomes devoted to one single cause. A majority of the order is drawn from Valikans, but people from all over Etharis count themselves among their number. While some members initially retain their suspicions concerning arcane magic or the faithful, they soon realize that any skills brought to bear against the coldfire have their value.

The Order of Kentigern’s accepting and inclusive outlook is not out of philosophy, but out of necessity: all are welcome in the fight, for as long as they can fight. And many, many members of the order die in the line of duty. And yet, despite all the efforts of the Order of Kentigern, the coldfire continues to freeze Valika and its people. Of the few settlements that once existed on Volgen, only a handful now remain, and even those have shrunk as their residents flee the danger—or die before they are able to run.

#### Cinderghast

As tales go, Cinderghast has always been a place better suited to fire elementals than humans, and the state of the volcanic islands now supports this theory. The Svalr, though, tell of a time when the settlement of Varra flourished there, its soil fertile, its crops rich, its people happy. When Rune of the Seven Heroes trapped the salamander Ixlalu there with a curse, the land erupted into the poisoned landscape that now exists. Though few dare visit these islands with their active volcanoes and treacherous waters, a few scholars have braved the journey to find that the Svalr tales have merit. Cinderghast has a vast ruin, covered in ash and buried in rock, where an ancient city once stood.

The islands are bound by a curse that makes studying the ruins difficult. Some areas are blocked to mortal travel; though visitors can see where they would like to go in the distance, when they walk toward their destination, they find themselves turned around and back at their point of origin. This is the nature of the spell binding Ixlalu and his army of salamanders and fire elementals to Cinderghast. However, the bonds of that spell are weakening, and the smallest of the fire elementals are beginning to escape.

A Sentinel Wood

#### The Foxwood Range

The Foxwood Range is a narrow vale in the Grensfal Mountain foothills, comprising a network of warren tunnels and dens providing safe haven for frostbite foxes to whelp and raise their young in spring. The unique geography ensures a large number of foxes disperse back into the wilds for autumn and winter trapping. The frostbite foxes of the north, hunted and trapped for their resilient, weatherproof fur, are an important part of Valikan clan culture.

The moon druids of an order known as the Fox Whisperers are instrumental to the long-term health of fox stocks, shepherding packs of these valuable creatures and ensuring that hunting and trapping don’t deplete their numbers. But one druid of that order, Njavesh, has taken her dedication to the foxes to another level.

Packs of frostbite foxes, led by elders awakened by Njavesh’s magic, range out from Foxwood to patrol the tundra of central Thrull, on the lookout for lost travelers. Their particular focus is seeking newly released thralls attempting the arduous journey south to their former homelands. These foxes lead their charges along safe trails, even hunting to help feed those unable to do so themselves, and as such, the foxes have gained an almost mythical status among thralls and travelers. It is said that those who sight the foxes have been graced by the gods with luck and are sure to survive whatever travails the harsh lands of Valika put before them.

#### The Sentinel Woods

These trees are the Prismatic Circle’s altars of sacrifice, so named for the druids’ belief that the great oaks stand vigil above the underworld where Gormadraug sleeps, their roots drawing the blood of sacrificed creatures downward. In every season except the darkest months of winter, the druids of the Prismatic Circle make regular pilgrimages to the dozens of widely scattered oak groves known collectively as the Sentinel Woods.

These great clusters of trees are known to be magical sites by anyone cognizant of the normal range of flora found in the north—oaks are typically unable to grow in such a landscape. But an even darker magic makes its presence known to any who step into the eternal shadow of the groves, steeped into soil turned blood-black by generations of murderous sacrifice.

Four druids attending each sacrifice first speak Gormadraug’s tale aloud. Then, working together, they flense each victim’s skin from their body with ritual chanting and razor-sharp knives. During the humanoid sacrifices, the druids also sacrifice magic trinkets stolen from the southlands, incorporating them into their murderous rites. When the last humanoid sacrifice has been conducted, the druids and Völgr warriors eagerly watching the rites immediately depart, for by tradition, no one can linger in any Sentinel Wood between times of sacrifice. Animals and people are left where they died to feed the crows and wolves over long months, with the next group of druids who come to each Sentinel Wood entering another part of the grove where previous victims have long rotted away to nothing but scattered bones.

When Clan Völgr patrols pass a Sentinel Wood, the warriors always draw near it seeking intruders, not entering the trees but circling around them in search of trespassers who may wish to revive those who have been sacrificed, or who wish to prevent further sacrifices.

## Quest Hooks

The following quest hooks provide ideas for adventures that might take place throughout the lands of the Valikan clans.

### Echoes of the Beast

Hunting parties returned to Borgund with whispers that a great skeletal stag head was sighted in the fog. Shortly thereafter, hunters started lugging home various mutilated bestial corpses found along the trails. A scout has now returned with warnings that a warband of gnolls is roaming the tundra and adjoining forests. With the hunting grounds of the city endangered, the wardens of Borgund have requested that all able-bodied folk establish a plan to track down and eliminate the gnoll incursions and, if possible, determine if the Beast remains in the region.

“Do not venture too far from the fire” is advice which is commonly shared with travelers to the north, and all too frequently ignored. In this case, as the characters seek out the gnoll warbands, they must deal with the many creatures that lurk in the darkness, shrouded in the safety of the cold and gloom. Carnivorous cows or other mutated beasts stand between the heroes and their destinations. Patrols from Clan Sýr also inhabit the area, giving possibilities for either combat, roleplaying, or exploration to find the means to survive interactions with the most reasonable of the Thrull-based clans.

The characters finally begin to see signs of the warband’s progress. Broken branches, mutilated carcasses, and jumbled tracks are among the signs to look for. For each character, there should be one Beast Gnoll and one Venomous Gnoll, along with one Brute Gnoll for every two characters. Due to the mutations afflicted upon the gnolls, they are intent on destruction and fight with a vicious ferocity.

Returning with a trophy of a gnoll’s hide or head will gain the characters a small favor with the wardens of Borgund. This could be helpful in gaining information or a respite at a later point.

### The Creeping Cold

Clan Limgri have been the caretakers of Cold Iron Keep for many years, without much call for assistance, so when Queen Andrea receives an urgent request for aid from the keep, she reaches out for someone who can quickly make the journey to provide help, and who is not beholden to clan law…just in case the issue lies with the inmates of the infamous prison keep. The only way to reach the keep is by crossing Lake Jakkan, a frigid body of water that is also home to a terrible beast. The sailors that bring the prisoners to Cold Iron Keep whisper about sightings of this monster, sure that its antlers will cause the lake to freeze over, killing them all instantly. Some of the sailors even believe that the flickering coldfire on the creature’s antlers are the souls of those it has dragged beneath the surface, down to a watery grave.

Whether the beast in the lake is real or not, the coldfire it is said to spread is certainly real, as the latest transport ship discovered. The guards awaiting the prisoner transfer began to worry when the ship had not arrived by daybreak on the second day after it was expected, but when it was finally spotted slowly sailing forward as the sun crested in the sky, their relief was short-lived. Upon boarding the small vessel, every soul on board was found to be frozen solid, with strange glowing puddles scattered across the deck. As the puddles coalesced into Coldfire Oozes, an alarm was sounded amid desperate orders to return the vessel to the lake.

With a half-dozen of their best guards claimed by the oozes, Clan Limgri is hesitant to deal with the oozes on their own, especially as it remains unknown how the oozes came to be aboard the transport ship. The transport ship currently rests several hundred feet from the shore, entirely too close for the warden’s comfort. The oozes aboard the ship were hacked into by the guards who initially boarded the vessel, unintentionally proliferating their number, and further aggravation of the vessel is likely to cause them to spill over into the lake. It is unknown if they have the ability to freeze the lake solid. If so, doom is almost guaranteed for the keep. This adventure seed is intentionally written to be open-ended. The characters should seek creative methods for moving the vessel further from the keep to ensure the safety of the Clan Limgri guards and the prisoners within the keep. While the oozes are vulnerable to fire, this may not immediately be obvious to the characters, and even if it is, lighting the wooden transport vessel on fire could potentially lead to the oozes still being dumped into the lake. The DM has discretion, based on party skill and level, to decide if the oozes are capable of using their coldfire to freeze sections of the lake. Teamwork and cleverness should be rewarded. And definitely don’t forget about the beast in the lake, which can be anything ranging from Keppmir for lower-level characters, to Gegazol for high-level characters.

### Sheltering Boughs

This encounter is meant to demonstrate that even the well-meaning inhabitants of Valika can turn out to be dangerous in the wrong circumstances.

To the west of Halsfjord, the overseer of a small village has requested aid in locating their wayward healer. Sarvenka, a druid who dabbled in alchemy, was last spotted along a trail near a Sentinel Wood foraging for mushrooms and herbs that she used to make various tinctures.

In her quest to gain standing within her clan, Sarvenka had gathered magical texts from various traveling merchants hoping to improve her magical abilities. Her latest scroll contained instructions on a method to gain a blessing from a holy tree, which she believed to be a straightforward enough task. However, once she had located a tree near a sacrificial grove, but still far enough away so as not to appear blasphemous to the Prismatic Circle, things began to go wrong for Sarvenka. The tree she chose was an Ordeal Tree and her intentions were somewhat noble.

Unfortunately for the party, the night they discover Sarvenka’s body shines brightly under the light of a full moon. The Redclaws that were patrolling the Sentinel Woods seeking to prevent ritual sacrifices by Clan Völgr have been drawn in by the scent of her blood and have fallen prey to their baser instincts. Four Werebear Ascetic and five Werewolf Ravagers creep upon the party as they examine Sarvenka.

### A Crucible of Fire

Always seeking a solution to the spread of coldfire that threatens all of Etharis, the Order of Kentigern has hired an expedition to travel to Cinderghast to research a resurgence of flame-bearing monsters from the Netherworld.

The expedition is destined a small warren of tunnels on the southern edge of the island alongside one of the larger volcanic peaks. In these tunnels, a group of four Malcinder and their Malcinder Alpha have made a cozy home, hunting and enjoying the relative solitude on Cinderghast. These creatures are content to be observed and will even allow small samples of their rock-like bodies to be harvested if approached with care.

Not quite as benign, the true cause of the recent uptick in volcanic activity has been caused by a pair of Mjorks and a Mjork Charger, none of whom are delighted to see researchers invading their home. These elementals, however, could be of value to the researchers at Fort Kentigern, so capturing one alive would be a real accomplishment if the characters are able.

### The Fancy of Foxes

Traveling the tundra alone is almost always a foolhardy mission, but especially above the circle of shadows in the time when the lands are blanketed in a perpetual darkness. Fortunately for one such traveler who recently lost their way in a blizzard, the characters were not far behind them, nor were the frostbite foxes that roam the tundra.

Angr knew better than to set out on a hunting trip when the wind promised a storm, but her brother had recently fallen ill and food supplies were running out, so she felt she had no choice but to brave the tundra. She felt lucky when she spotted the fresh tracks of an elk, but that feeling faded quickly as the wind picked up and sharp ice crystals began to tear at her skin. With no sun still to show for weeks, and the stars hidden by the storm, Angr soon became disoriented and resorted to digging out a crude shelter in the snow to protect herself, however she knows that she cannot survive through the night.

Two Frostbite Foxes have been tailing the characters for hours, and now that the party has stopped to seek shelter from the storm, the foxes circle at the edge of the firelight, seemingly beckoning the characters back out into the storm. If the characters follow the foxes into the storm, and manage to keep track of them (tracking with survival skills, following prints with perception, or any other creative means) , the foxes will lead them to Angr’s snow burrow where they will find her shivering from hypothermia, dehydrated, and ravenous.

Once Angr has been rescued, she could use assistance recovering her strength and health, but then she is more than happy to share tales of what brought her out in such a storm to begin with. She beseeches the party to help her hunt down the elk she was tracking to take back to her brother once the storm clears up, promising her gratitude and hospitality in return, along with whatever other meager payment she can scrounge up.

## Cold Iron Keep

Far to the north, in the frigid peaks of the Jakkan Mountains, rests Cold Iron Keep. Silent battlements loom two hundred feet above the glassy surface of Lake Jakkan. To reach the step of the gate, guests must cross the large body of water—but not all who enter will exit Cold Iron Keep again.

In forgotten centuries, the wicked mage Mordu used spells to call this stronghold up from the living rock, filled its halls with perilous magic, and secured it against the outside world. Then the fearful folk of Valika stormed and ransacked the Keep, leaving it empty to the ravages of the elements—and to the lurking hunger of monsters.

Today, Cold Iron Keep locks away dangerous enemies rather than magical wonders. When Clan Mithra took the apparently empty ruin, Queen Andrea entrusted it to Clan Limgri, who converted its vaults into prison cells.

The sitting warden of Cold Iron Keep is Commander Bodil Garðr. Her manner as a former skald and doomsayer sets the tone of life in the Keep. She invites scholars, druids, and skalds from all over Kandar to reside above the dungeons. Few here thrive without her favor, a fact known by guests and prisoners alike.

Garðr’s unorthodox leadership style includes an unexpected rapport with her prisoners. Though the uncooperative may find themselves subject to elaborate punishments (including experimentation by the warden’s guests), the inmates who cooperate with Garðr get relaxed treatment—and might even be sent out of the prison on secretive missions.

### Granted New Life

Isolation and a hostile environment make Cold Iron Keep difficult to reach. A single road, worn with use and disrepair, climbs the broad Jakkan slopes toward the stronghold. As the Keep’s squared ramparts come into view, they hardly stand out against the snow-laden spires of jagged rock surrounding them.

The forboding walls of this place use no mortar. They bear mineral veins, strata, and other evidence of the living rock from which they were magically lifted. By a trick of design, the walls confine sound and their windows hamper light; even a grand festival would only be detected as a dull echo from the outside.

#### Guests of the Warden

Despite its cheerless exterior, the Keep is home to a growing community who share an interest in the stronghold’s history and secrets. With Commander Garðr’s leave, these free residents maintain quarters throughout the elaborate Guest Halls of the ground floor, drawn to the surviving magic of Mordu.

Over time, Cold Iron Keep drew the attention of adventurers and opportunists who schemed their way into the warden’s favor. These daring sellswords spend most of their time exploring the Deepvault, the unmapped and half-flooded lowest levels of Mordu’s citadel. Artisans, hirelings, and traders in the Guest Halls serve the delvers’ occupational needs.

#### Locked Away

Below the Guest Halls lie the Cells. There, the clans of Kandar store prisoners who can’t be dealt with by clan law, including those deemed most dangerous and impervious to death. Commander Garðr, under the consultation of her wisest guests, adapts the Keep’s punishments and security measures to each inmate. The enemies languishing in its cells include cultists, political foes, giants, primordial druids, and creatures even more monstrous.

The Cells are only the uppermost threshold of the extensive vaults that once comprised Mordu’s stronghold. Some two hundred prison chambers are maintained here, alongside a dozen or more laboratories where the least compliant inmates undergo various experiments.

Probing below the Cells (as many adventurers do) brings one to the Creep, a series of frozen corridors and chambers that typically end in rubble, magical seals, or barriers of frozen floodwater. Many passages in the Creep are deserted or picked clean, but every so often, a monster or magical phenomenon emerges from the Deepvaults below.

#### The Rule of Law

Above all this chaos sit the Offices of the Warden, a grand series of halls, observatories, and defunct magical sanctums refitted into a sort of administrative court. Here, Commander Bodil Garðr and her officers run the Keep. Their work is part prison staff part town council.

Adjoining the Offices of the Warden are extensive battlements and fortifications overlooking Lake Jakkan, though these are seldom used or maintained. Impassable mountain crags enclose its north, east, and west sides, making entrance or escape from those directions a dismal prospect.

#### Vaults of Opportunity

The Deepvaults that run hundreds of feet below Cold Iron Keep have become a wild fascination for those who dwell here. To the warden’s guests, they represent plunder, power and potential discoveries; to the prisoners, they are a glimmer of hope—an avenue of escape lurking just out of reach.

At least five prisoners have ventured into the Deepvaults, but it was at Bodil Garðr’s behest. The party was assembled from among the most favored inmates, each subjected to a Geas spell that would keep them on task under pain of death. Their charge was to chisel an entrance into a particular frozen vault, clear any monsters from the place, and return a peculiar set of funerary urns to the warden.

Few officers heard this plan, which proved fortunate for Commander Garðr. Whether the prisoners died in a monster attack, were struck dead upon defying the Geas spell, or simply escaped into the Deepvaults, no one knows.

Further experimentation and refinement of the Geas has given Commander Garðr hope that the attempt can be repeated. Perhaps, in time, the spell may allow her to evict the rabble of adventurers and undertake the clearing of the dungeons using her own obedient puppets.

The timeworn vaults of Cold Iron Keep share at least one thing with the inmates they confine: both lie hidden in the frozen waste, objects of exploitation by the warden and her entourage. Whether a new life awaits them at the end of the long dark depends on their ability to become useful.

### A History of Reclamation

The earliest historical record of Cold Iron Keep is its despoiling. At that time, the Jakkan Mountains were already shrouded in dread tales—a lingering shadow from centuries of half-remembered evil.

Stories tell that the Keep’s builder, Mordu, sent vile servants into nearby villages to abduct innocents and bring them to his laboratories for blood sacrifice. The beings he directed these offerings to are not recorded, nor are the secrets he won with this butchery.

In time, heroes trapped and killed Mordu in recompense for his foul deeds. In the aftermath of that victory, folk from the lowland villages stormed Cold Iron Keep, ransacked what treasures they could find, and left it to the elements. Abandoned in the inhospitable mountains, it became a forgotten ruin.

Soon after, Clan Mithra discovered the empty hulk of Mordu’s citadel and garrisoned it. Many centuries later, it was entrusted to Clan Limgri. Queen Andrea Helsing, convinced Cold Iron Keep lacked strategic value, agreed to station her garrison further north if Limgri converted the Keep into a prison and staffed it.

#### New Management

Life at Cold Iron Keep took a strange turn when the Røkkva skald Bodil Garðr was made warden. As a laureate of Solheim, Garðr railed against the power structures that prolong the end of the world and the rebirth of the gods; to Garðr, this doom is to be embraced, not feared.

When her performances sparked unrest, Clan Limgri forced Garðr into exile as warden of Cold Iron Keep, which she transformed into a haven modeled after her own image. Her refurbished Guest Halls teem with skalds, scholars, and adventurers eager to garner favor and emerge from the Deepvaults with wondrous treasure.

Life in the Guest Halls is often wild and jovial; just as Garðr prefers it. Her taste for rare and irreverent entertainment has become a signature custom in Cold Iron Keep. Shocking tales, predictions of doom, and the occasional shower of treasure from the Deepvault compensate for the Keep’s makeshift habitations and unruly culture.

#### Keeping the Peace

The Commander oversees Cold Iron Keep’s three administrative boards. Correctional Affairs, headed by Constable Ulherik Vander, directs the security and treatment of prisoners. Hospitality Affairs, commanded by Constable Heiga Iðun, keeps the peace in the Guest Halls. Provisional Affairs, under the direction of Constable Agnes Harlech, imports supplies, collects and disperses pay, and manages the Keep’s other resources.

Instead of citizenship, Garðr offers her “hospitality” to new arrivals. When revoked, the guest’s property is confiscated and they’re cast from the Keep into the elements. This threat carries significant weight. Those who secure Garðr's favor with might be released from their cells on a limited basis. Disrespectful prisoners are allocated poor quarters and food, arduous duties, or even subject to experimentation. Garðr is a crafty and vindictive enemy.

### Notable Locations

The following places of interest are found within Cold Iron Keep.

#### Approach to the Keep

The solitude and despair of the arctic are enough to deter any but the most dogged traveler from reaching the slopes of the Jakkan Mountains. Just when any wise soul would turn back, Lake Jakkan, Ferryman’s Sty, and the face of Cold Iron Keep itself come into view through the mist and snow.

Lake Jakkan. Several miles wide before it reaches the gate of Cold Iron Keep, the road from Hrist plunges beneath the surface of Lake Jakkan, whose waves lap against the castle’s foundations. Once, perhaps, the road wound around the shore, but the bounds of the lake have grown over the centuries, filling in any clear path to the doors of the Keep. This natural moat serves only to enhance the Keep’s defense and seclusion.

By order of Commander Garðr, no boats are kept on the shore of Lake Jakkan, but a ferryman named Huskol dutifully watches for approved passengers to convey to the Keep. Crossing the lake takes hours and costs nothing, eliciting the question of why Huskol serves the warden and what he lives on.

A fearsome creature is said to lair within the caves beneath the lake, often appearing to inmates who first cross the waters toward their captivity. Reports describe a shapeshifting monstrosity combining the features of a caribou, a whale, and a dire wolf. It’s unclear whether these fears are stoked by illusion magic or formed from sightings of a real monster.

Ferryman’s Sty. Huskol, the ferryman of Lake Jakkan, lives on an island of detritus near the center of the lake, which the Keep’s guests call Ferryman’s Sty. The precise origin of this scrap heap isn’t known. Giant beams and plates of metal protrude from a mound of broken stone rubble whose merest crest is visible from above the water but whose bulk reaches far down to the bottom of Lake Jakkan.

Tales tell that before the lake was formed, a village slumbered here in the shadow of the mountains. In some versions of the legend, the displaced rock and runoff from Cold Iron Keep’s magical emergence first buried the village, then drowned the survivors. In other variations, Mordu sent mudslides and avalanches down upon a besieging army before he was at last defeated. Whatever the truth, Ferryman’s Sty is believed to hide any number of secrets from before the rise of Cold Iron Keep.

Though old and bent, Huskol the ferryman has strong arms and keen eyes. The explorers of Clan Mithra who first sighted the keep claimed to have found the old man already living here in his leaning hut on the sty. Some say he sleeps for days, waiting for passengers approved by the warden to hail him from either shore using an ivory horn. By all accounts, Huskol’s devotion seems to be to the Keep itself, not its warden.

#### Guest Halls

The ground floor and its adjoining chambers are collectively called the Guest Halls. They include Kettle Row, Maker’s Market, Chalkstick Hall, and nearby vaults converted into living quarters for the Keep’s guests.

Kettle Row. The rattle of pots and the din of dining fill Kettle Row, where drinking wells, food carts, miniature taverns, and apothecaries gather to provide what food and drink can be had at Cold Iron Keep. The selection isn’t bad, considering the castle’s remote surroundings. By the light of cookfires and hanging braziers, the warden’s guests cling to whatever cheer Kettle Row can offer.

Maker’s Market. The shouts of Maker’s Market echo throughout the most populated areas of Cold Iron Keep. In this intersecting passage, suppliers of every stripe set up shop to serve the needs of the daring adventurers heading into the Deepvaults. Hospitality Affairs keeps a strong presence in Maker’s Market, ensuring that any cheating or thieving is done with Commander Garðr’s approval.

Chalkstick Hall. This wide hall featuring six frozen fountains is where adventurers gather to descend into the dangerous levels of Cold Iron Keep. A rift in the floor, known as Chalkstick Chasm, is thickly overgrown with pale cold-weather succulents—the “chalksticks” that give the place its name. A dangerous climb down the chasm bypasses the magical seals that prevent ordinary entrance to the Deepvaults.

#### Offices of the Warden

Most of the visible portion of the castle belongs to the Offices of the Warden, where behind closed doors, Commander Bodil Garðr and her trusted circle hold the reins of the Keep.

Battlements. Towering over the natural moat of Lake Jakkan, the three battlements of Cold Iron Keep provide an impregnable defense against invasion. Parapets, towers, barbicans, arrow loops, and murder holes all form redundancies against assault from outside and inside the Keep. Commander Garðr has diverted some of the garrison from defense to peacekeeping, leaving the fortifications somewhat understaffed; the guests must be kept in order, after all.

Hall of Black Rot. Garðr’s highest reception hall in the Keep is reserved for the raucous nightly revels of her most favored guests. The chamber remains stained by its namesake: a hideous black mold removed during the Keep’s refurbishment. An invitation to the Hall of Black Rot marks one as being among the most respected guests (or prisoners) in Cold Iron Keep.

Seersalir. The highest tower of Cold Iron Keep is crowned by Seersalir, the hall of the seer, where Commander Garðr has spent years restoring a large scrying contraption made by Mordu. Under the guidance of her most discreet scholars, she believes she has unlocked a great key to understanding the magic of Mordu and the Deepvaults. With Seersalir under her control, she can keep a close eye on both the prison and the world outside.

#### The Deepvaults

Extensive, mysterious, and diverse, the vaults beneath Cold Iron Keep are layered one after another, plunging hundreds of feet into the stony roots of the mountains—perhaps even beyond. One could spend a lifetime exploring the wonders of the Deepvaults and barely scratch its surface—in fact, many plan to.

The Creep. The uppermost level of the Deepvaults is plagued by coldfire. Curtains of the magical scourge move slowly through the tunnels in irregular patterns, allowing for careful plundering by adventurers who can keep their wits. The Creep, as it’s called by the renegades who navigate it, is disproportionately armed with traps and defenses designed by Mordu to keep them out. Perhaps half of the Creep has been mapped and plundered, but somehow, new monstrous denizens appear with regularity.

Fountain of Twilight. Under the caverns on the south end of Lake Jakkan lie a collection of hidden vaults originally accessible only from the deepest and most dangerous levels of the Keep. In the centuries since Mordu first built them, these chambers have been worn away by dripping water until they opened into a cascading waterfall two hundred feet high. The waters feed bioluminescent fungal forests that somehow thrive in the cold twilight. Few explorers prove skilled enough to enter here, either from the lake above or the vaults below.

Hidden Lake. The labyrinthine chambers, passages, and stairs that make up the Hidden Lake are partially flooded by water flowing down through the Fountain of Twilight into the Deepvaults. The edge of this lake crashes in waves against the stairs, balconies, and sealed gates of the vaults. Unlike the Flooded Deepvaults beneath them, the Hidden Lake’s elevated balconies and balustrades form a sort of shoreline through the maze of chambers.

Flooded Deepvaults. Sealed by magic and deathly cold water, the Flooded Deepvaults are among the most mysterious and inhospitable features of Cold Iron Keep. The secrets of Mordu’s most obscure magic lie inaccessible here, disturbed only by the floods. What life, if any, survives within the mage’s lowest vaults is too terrible to imagine, but those who stumble upon the seals have heard bellowing calls from beyond to open the forbidden doors and let the flood rise into the upper levels. Bruhvelving. The most remote known chamber in the Deepvaults, Bruhvelving can be accessed via twelve magical seals scattered about the Deepvaults in the form of animated relief sculptures. Each seal is depicted as a different image matching its personality: the archfey, beast, butcher, fool, lamb, raven, ruler, scholar, troll, warrior, wyrm, and youth. Each is said to hold secrets about the Deepvaults, and can be beguiled or entreated to allow entrance into their hidden vault. Rebecca Holloway Commander Bodil Garðr

Inside, Bruhvelving is believed to conceal extensive passages to vaults even deeper and more secret beneath the flooded levels. Savvy adventurers have theorized that because it’s so deep and unflooded, Bruhvelving might have no physical connection to Cold Iron Keep other than the sealed portals, and might have been installed in a separate part of the mountains.


### Important Individuals

Notable people associated with Cold Iron Keep include those described below.

#### Astar, Queen of Midnight

Ancient as the Keep itself, Astar is a medusa known in the folk tales of Clan Mithra as the Queen of Midnight and the Barrow’s Eye. Nearly a hundred years ago, clan hunters entered a battle of wits with her and, after three days, succeeded in trapping Astar in an iron mask emblazoned with an image of a smiling child. Held as a mead-hall trophy, then nearly lost in the calamity of Cinnabar, the bound medusa was eventually brought to Cold Iron Keep.

Once inside, Astar was sealed in a cell walled by mirrors. Far from the murderous monstrosity of folk tales, she nurses her hatred for her captors with cold detachment. But the divinations and unsought counsels she whispers from behind her cherub’s mask are as dangerous as her glance was said to be.

#### Fregr Ulfborn

The retired adventurer Fregr Ulfborn (known as the Winter Wolf) holds court in a shabby drinking hole called the Torchbearer at the center of Kettle Row. His weathered face and silver hair belie the strength in his axe hand. Ulfborn’s personal adventuring guild, the Vaulters, boasts over fifty members, and now dares to openly disobey the warden without losing her hospitality. The streetwise guests say a turf war is brewing between Commander Garðr and the Winter Wolf that will finally clear some space in the Guest Halls.

#### Gorm, Heir of the Wyrm

Gorm is a red-haired madman who burns continuously with coldfire from head to foot. Whispers of him echo all through Cold Iron Keep, claiming he has seen the cataclysm of the Great Wyrm, that the coldfire obeys his commands, and even that he moves freely about the Deepvaults with the blessing of the warden.

Indeed, speaking with Gorm was one of the foremost reasons Bodil Garðr accepted the post in the first place. She wasted no time tattooing her magical controls on him and sending him into the Creep. After all, if his prophecy is true and the touch of his foot upon the snow of Valika heralds the end of all things, that would suit Garðr just fine.

#### Jormund the Dreamer

The quietest vault in the Keep’s prisons is occupied by a thirty-foot sarcophagus made of quartz. Within lies the sleeping bulk of Jormund, a frost giant vampire. Kept in a state of torpor by the enchanted sarcophagus, Jormund dreams so vividly that the images of his nightmares can be scried from the magical device in Seersalir. Commander Garðr fears Jormund too much to wake him, but there are others in Cold Iron Keep who can think of many ways to use such an ally, if he can be swayed to their cause.

#### Riven Kreig

David Alunan Riven Kreig Recently apprehended in the streets of Hrist, Riven Kreig is a fanatical devotee of the Prismatic Circle whose talent lies in absorbing a person’s life essence at the time of their death. Even stranger are the rituals Kreig employs to wear the face of an absorbed victim. Since his imprisonment, the assassin has refused to assume his true form, if it could even be identified. As another who invites the end of the world (albeit for different reasons), Commander Garðr hopes to turn Riven Kreig to her Røkkva point of view. Until then, he remains a delicate laboratory experiment.

#### Tove av Eikenbu

This Clan Mithra runesmith converted to the Røkkva religion four years ago when he first met Bodil Garðr. Tove av Eikenbu is a thin, wiry man in his fifties. Despite the Keep’s raucous culture, he maintains an austere, almost disquieting focus on his studies.

Eikenbu is perhaps the only person in the Keep that Garðr fully trusts. As the first of twelve prominent scholars who took up residence in Cold Iron Keep when Garðr became warden, his research and encouragement were instrumental in developing the runic Geas tattoo that controls certain prisoners.

### Quest Hooks

The following quest hooks can be used to flesh out adventures set in Cold Iron Keep.

#### Beguile the Seal of Bruhvelving

Since entering Cold Iron Keep, the player characters heard many descriptions of Bruhvelving, the lowest and most hidden vault in the complex. Many name it, but few claim to have reached it. Down Chalkstick Chasm, through the Creep, and into the unmapped portion of the Deepvaults, one can find Bruhvelving’s twelve sentient seals.

If the rumors can be believed, choosing a seal is as important a step as contriving a way to open it. Whether by guile, knowledge, or bitter sacrifice, it is believed the figures in the seals can be overcome. Once past the seals, Bruhvelving’s vault remains a largely untouched trove of magical wonders—but such things are not to be tampered with lightly. Beyond the magical wards and lurking monsters, Bruhvelving may hide truths about the beginning and the end of existence that challenge the integrity of reality itself.

#### Black Rot Revels

Days of petitioning and favors have landed the player characters a rare opportunity: a stolen invitation to the Hall of Black Rot, where Cold Iron Keep’s warden hosts her most favored guests. This is where the real power rests—where deals are brokered and trust gained. Siv Hrothigvar, purveyor of the vilest delights in Valika, will be in attendance and off her guard. What foul crimes will the characters commit to take a place beside her and avenge the innocent souls she preyed on?

#### Forage the Fountain of Twilight

Plague is spreading through the Cold Iron Keep's Guest Halls. Medicine is scarce until new shipments arrive. The situation worsens as the plague victims rise from their deathbeds with a taste for living flesh. Rare fungal growths in the Fountain of Twilight hold the secret to defeating the undead plague, if only a party of survivors skilled enough to navigate the vaults could bring them back before the Keep’s denizens tear one another limb from limb.

#### Interview With a Vampire

Through careful investigation, the player characters have learned that the prisoner Jormund the Dreamer appears in tales alongside the Great Prismatic Wyrm defeated by the hero Kentigern. In an age long past, his thirst for blood stained the Jakkan Mountains red and drained the blood of entire generations—until he was betrayed. If indeed the vampire frost giant is over a thousand years old and harbors hatred for the Great Wyrm, he would make an invaluable ally in the confrontation to come.

#### Lost in Ferryman’s Sty

Queen Drejv the Betrayer’s five deathless heirs were decapitated, and the heads scattered across Valika in a forgotten age. The player characters’ search for the heads—which still live—has led them to Lake Jakkan, where the head of Økerhardt was cast upon Ferryman Sty. To obtain it, the characters will need to research accounts of the lake’s mysterious shapeshifting denizen. They must avoid or defeat the creature to scour the scrap heap for what they seek. If Økerhardt can be coaxed to speak, his knowledge may well reach back to the days of Mordu and the mysterious creation of Cold Iron Keep itself.

#### Oysters of the Hidden Lake

Where the tides rise and fall along the grand staircases and ramped corridors of the Hidden Lake, beds of giant oysters congregate, visible only in rare instances. The pearls these oysters form are the most valuable in Etheris. Some are even said to deposit souls within their shells rather than pearls. Perhaps a dark patron sends the player characters to the Hidden Lake to retrieve a specific soul, or the Vaulters require it as a token to join their adventurer’s guild.

#### Revolt at the End of the World

The Winter Wolf and his adventurer’s guild, the Vaulters, have heard of Commander Garðr’s Røkkva devotions and her plan to hasten the world’s end. With frightening tales of her human experiments, decadent revelries, and favoritism toward notorious enemies of Kandar, the Vaulters have convinced the player characters that now is the time to rise up and end Garðr’s reign at Cold Iron Keep for good.

#### Rescue in the Creep

Dungeon delvers come and go in Cold Iron Keep. Sometimes, they strike a promising trail and disappear for days. Other times, they meet a quick end far from help. Averik Olherdottir and her companions descended into the Creep fifteen days ago. Today, she called for help with a Sending spell. It seems her companions were killed by a wraith, so Averik jumped blindly into the dark and broke her legs.

With no hope of climbing to safety, no food, and exhausted from crawling to avoid coldfire, Averik has only a few days left to be rescued. The player characters must pick out her trail amidst the treacherous passages of the Creep, avoid the specters of her former companions, and get her out from the ever-shifting clutches of the coldfire.

#### Uninvited Guests

Having failed to impress the warden, the characters are shut out from Cold Iron Keep and its opportunities. But the fortified gate is only one way into the castle’s complex.

Perhaps the characters will dive into the lair of Lake Jakkan’s mysterious creature and follow the narrow chute into the Fountain of Twilight. From there, passage to the ground level of the Keep is a matter of picking paths through the flooded portions of the Deepvaults up into the Creep and Chalkstick Hall. They might instead scale the formidable glacial cliffs that rise above the third battlement to enter the Offices of the Warden. Or, should their skillset lean more toward disguise and deception, their plan might be to impersonate prisoners and get locked into a cell with the hope of courting Commander Garðr’s favor.
