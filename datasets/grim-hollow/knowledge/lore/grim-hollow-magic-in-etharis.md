# Magic in Etharis

## Arcane Magic in Etharis

Arcane magic emanates from a dimension parallel to the Material Plane called Numena, according to the archmage Raakan el-Adakar, the greatest authority on the subject. The energy itself is called numen -- magic involves using numen to give physical shape to the magician's will. Mages communicate with Numena through combinations of verbal, somatic, and material components called spells. Each spell weakens the border between Numena and the Material Plane, drawing and shaping numen into a magical effect.

Wizards learn spells through rigorous study and practice. Sorcerers touch Numena instinctively, like flexing a muscle. Warlocks receive arcane knowledge whispered into their minds from entities beyond the Material Plane.

The true nature of Numena remains debated. Theories suggest it encompasses all non-material realms, that it is an invisible force like gravity, or that it is itself a living entity attempting to reshape our dimension -- which would explain why spell knowledge slips from the mind like oily aberrations.

## Societal Mistrust

Society has always viewed arcane magic with division and suspicion. Its power is unrivalled, but its mystery, unpredictability, and dangers make it feared and hated. To rulers, magic threatens authority. To commoners, mages are mutual enemies blamed for all unexplained troubles.

Raakan el-Adakar himself went mad after attempting to open a portal to Numena, spending his final days confined in Cold Iron Keep while his guards suffered bizarre visions. The mages of Ulmyr's Gate upended their entire city into the Ethereal Plane, killing thousands. Among arcane scholars, many fear that magic permanently thins the veil between planes -- each spell picking at threads of a tapestry until holes form. This anxiety fuels the theory that spellcasters caused the advent of the Great Beast, making mages even further detested.

Characters capable of wielding magic should be wary of when and where they demonstrate their talents. Even friendly acquaintances may turn hostile. Rumors of magic could reach agents of the Arcanist Inquisition.

## Regional Attitudes

### Burach

Magic is valued as a tool for projecting power and maintaining order, but mages are heavily regulated. Licenses govern which spells a mage may cast and under what circumstances. Sorcerers and warlocks operating outside official sanctions are outlaws. With the coming of the Great Beast, suspicion has intensified -- some suspect the entity resulted from arcane experimentation gone wrong.

### Ostoya

In Crimson Court provinces, the lower classes fear magic and avoid its practitioners. The Court claims all magic use as theirs by right -- unsanctioned mages must serve the Court or be destroyed. Ostoyan mages primarily serve in wars against Raevo and the Burach Empire.

In Raevo, magic is accepted as part of daily life. Castalore's workshops host wizards trading occult objects under the watchful eye of the Ravencourt Sanctuary. Magic is valued to maintain sovereignty against both the Crimson Court and Burach.

### Charneault

Elves treat magic as integral to their lives, holding skilled mages in high esteem while treading cautiously around its costs. In human-controlled lands, attitudes vary -- mages are respected in learned circles but met with suspicion among Knight Chapters, and outright hostility from the Severe Templars, who hold elven magic accountable for the suffering caused by the dark mist.

### Castinella

The theocracy is hostile to all arcane and primal magic. The Arcanist Inquisition has assembled knights and soldiers under clerical command, backed by divine magic to locate and neutralize mages. Magic-using arcanists entering the provinces risk arrest, imprisonment, and death. Common folk hold all magic as witchcraft and devilry.

The Castinellans have not yet moved against magic-employing nations, but should the theocracy grow more powerful, a "righteous war" would certainly follow.

## Colleges of Magic

### Erlefurt Colleges

The capital of Erlefurt in western Nordenland has the highest concentration of magical colleges in Etharis, with the Magister College being the oldest and most venerable. All students swear to the Magician's Codex: only graduates may carry the Erlefurt Mage title, colleges must be defended, all students and experiments must be registered and approved. Violators are hunted by the Enforcers, elite abjurers, and judged by the Grand Mage.

Erlefurt treats other arcane institutions with contempt. Valikan students from the Grarjord are sent here for tutelage, as no formal colleges exist in Valika.

### Ravencourt Sanctuary

Hidden on an enchanted isle east of Raevo, the Sanctuary believes magic grows through freedom. It has few laws beyond one: keep the Sanctuary's secrets safe. Graduates who break this vow die -- their blood-signed page in an enchanted book tears itself free and burns, killing them.

Erlefurt views Ravencourt mages as dangerous occultists. The two colleges are technically at war, with standing orders to annihilate each other on sight, though this is rarely practiced.

### Sarmar Academy

The elven college in Tol Leyemil, famous for bards, mystics, and druids. Sarmar teaches that magic is learned through careful observation of nature -- students spend months in meditation before casting a single cantrip. Education can last decades. Only elves may enroll, except at Aemanyir House, a branch outside the city teaching the elven way to non-elves.

Tension exists over the academy's loyalties -- humans are wary of how Sarmar's mages would be used if elves pushed for independence.

### Renegade Colleges

Unsanctioned colleges exist across Etharis. Erlefurt Mages capture "renegade wizards" for reeducation. In Castinella, underground centers secretly train arcane-sensitive individuals before the Inquisition notices them. In Valika, Clan Rune handles magical training through master-apprentice relationships, with young mages free to travel and practice after completing their apprenticeship.

## The Planes

Very little is understood of what lies beyond the Material Plane. Talented mages and victims of misfortune who witness other planes of existence find the experience generally horrifying and often mind-shattering.

## Realm of Faerie

The Feywild is stitched into the Material Plane's fabric. Gateways can be hidden in plain sight: a forest ravine, an elven ruin's archway, a circle of toadstools. The realm appears differently depending on which seasonal court holds sway:

- **Spring Court**: dazzling golden citadels amid blooming forests
- **Summer Court**: humid, sun never sets, Spirits of Nature demand tithes
- **Winter Court**: gloom and snow, war-hungry goblins plan invasions
- **Autumn Court**: rot, where cunning sprites play cruel and deadly tricks

Druids and rangers can sometimes navigate the Feywild safely as an expedient way to journey through Etharis, though offending the Spirits of Nature is as dire as angering an Arch Seraph. Elves believe the entire Material Plane was once a paradise crafted by the Archfey, who retreated into the Feywild to escape violence during the Era of Expansion.

## Ethereal Plane

The Ethereal Plane exists behind the veil of reality, encircling the Material Plane. It overlaps the mortal realm but cannot be perceived without magic. There are no natural portals. The Great College of Ulmyr's Gate tried to establish a permanent portal and the entire city was torn through a rift, obliterated along with thousands of inhabitants. The disembodied who emerged afterward are trapped between realities. Eldritch horrors and Aether Kindred prey upon travelers here.

## Primordial Realms

The Valikan Clans believe the Material Plane is cradled between four Primordial Realms where roiling elements crash together to form the mortal world. Portals may exist where each element is strongest -- volcanic depths, ocean trenches -- but access usually requires magic.

- **Flamebourne** (Fire): ashen waste veined by rivers of lava. Efreeti and fire elementals hunt uninvited mortals. Realm of Lady Morakesh.
- **The Endless Sea** (Water): frigid, silent darkness beneath all waters. Realm of Alondo, who prefers solitude.
- **Stonereach** (Earth): depthless caves rich with minerals. Stubborn denizens offer no aid to explorers.
- **The Whispering Void** (Air): vast darkness with biting cold. Old Valikan tales say murderers and dishonored warriors are banished here after death.

## Netherworld

The hellish dominions created by Arch Daemons from the wreckage of the Heavens after the Aetheric War. When legions of seraphs were cast down by the death of their gods, these celestial beings twisted into fiends. Each Arch Daemon lords over a fiefdom reflecting their domain -- Sitri's is a grand pleasure house overgrown with polyps of flesh, Malikir's a towering fortress citadel with dismembered souls hung from parapets.

This plane is for the dead -- souls claimed by an Arch Daemon spend eternity tortured within their domain. Travel between the Netherworld and the Material Plane is exceedingly difficult for the living. Cracks in reality sometimes open, releasing daemonic horrors unless closed.

## The Heavens

Once a paradise for blessed souls, the Heavens were ruptured during the Aetheric War. What remains has been rebuilt as a fortress rather than a garden. The Arch Seraphs and their legions guard the celestial gates with devotion and fury -- no portals miraculously open to the mortal realm. Any attempt by mortals to open a path to the Heavens has always been met by calamity.

## Numena

Mages and academics have argued for centuries about Numena's nature. It can be spoken to through spells, but its only responses are the manifestations created with magic. If Numena is the realm of arcane magic, many fear it is also the realm of the Aether Kindred: a place of pure, roiling chaos -- the Astral Plane, the reality between realities. Depictions of such a place are found in dragonborn carvings beneath Ember Cairn.

That scholars continue exploring Numena's secrets causes great discomfort. With every spell cast, the veil may be further tattered. Aberrant horrors increasingly crawl through into the mortal realm -- fears that justify the Arcanist Inquisition's existence.
