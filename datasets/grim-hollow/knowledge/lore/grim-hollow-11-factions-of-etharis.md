# Chapter 11: Factions of Etharis

Well-Earned Rest

People of similar beliefs and goals come together to increase their power. This is true in any world, and Etharis is no different. Many organizations and factions hold sway in Grim Hollow campaigns.

The major factions of Etharis have some striking similarities, regardless of their motivations and goals. They have come together after enduring tribulations or in response to threats, and overtly or covertly they permeate every stratum of society, whether they hold sway in one nation, multiple nations, or throughout all of Etharis.

All of the factions discussed here—and so many of the lesser factions in Etharis—are driven by one overarching goal: power. Sometimes this power comes from the collected magical abilities of its members. Other times it comes from the knowledge and influence that the group’s leaders and members can bring to bear. The power may even come from the fear that the faction inspires in those it controls. Regardless, it’s this power that makes Etharis’s factions so formidable.

With power, of course, comes the will to use that power. Many times, this power works on behalf of the characters, and for society as a whole. The characters may have a symbiotic relationship with these factions: the factions can offer the characters a small piece of that power, as well as a sense of direction and goals to achieve.

On the other hand, each of these factions may abuse that power, making them institutions that the characters must oppose. Just as the characters in a Grim Hollow campaign must constantly be on the guard against their own corruption as they gain power, these factions have the capability to perform and propagate great evil in the name of doing good.

## Factions in Brief

The following are quick descriptions of each of the factions illuminated in great detail later.

Augustine Trading Company. Amid the splendors of Morencia sits an organization that guards its treasure as jealously as a dragon defends its hoard. The Augustine Trading Company is an ancient and venerable institution that maintains a presence in every country, from the southern reaches of Castinella to the misty city of Castalore. The Trading Company puts profit above people, meaning that missions often end with the members having to decide if they are going to do the proper thing or the expedient, profitable thing. Those who choose the latter are rewarded. Those who chose the former find themselves named enemies of the Augustine hierarchy.

Company of Free Swords. An ancient and elite mercenary company, the Free Swords have tipped the scales of war for many nations and provinces. The company includes warriors, wizards, priests, druids, rogues, and spies among its ranks. It has a branch in nearly every corner of the continent. They have maintained a code of honor since their founding: stay loyal to the company, be honest in your dealings, and take vengeance on those who betray you or your comrades. They live simple lives free of excess, spending most of their time honing their fighting skills.

Ebon Syndicate. The Ebon Syndicate is an organization with a finger in every major criminal trade. Assassination, kidnapping, drug trafficking, theft, and the black market all fall within their purview. The Ebon Syndicate prefers to keep its actions and schemes in the shadows, especially in areas where they may not have the strongest foothold. In these cases, the Syndicate tends to hire mercenaries and freelance charlatans to do the most dangerous work.

Thaumaturge. The Grand Thaumatic Order of archmages is a myth. Its existence has been a rumor throughout history, yet most commonfolk regard it as a fairy tale intended to frighten the conspiratorial. The truly downtrodden look to the Thaumaturge as a symbol of rebellion against authoritarian and intolerant regimes.

Watchers of the Faithful. Composed of priests, paladins, chaplains, and other clergy, the Watchers of the Faithful have dedicated themselves to preserving the old faiths in a new form. They follow the Eternal Dogma: revere the Arch Seraphs, follow their laws as handed down by the High Priest, and destroy all that is unholy and evil in their sight—through any means necessary.

Arcanist Inquisition. The Arcanist Inquisition is a religious order that hunts down arcanists to prevent them from using their powers to threaten the established political and social structures they prefer. They patrol the provinces of Castinella and beyond, spreading their teachings about the dangers of magic, stirring mistrust of arcanists among the local populations.

Crimson Court. The sun-starved nation of Ostoya is ruled by a cabal of vampire nobles called the Crimson Court. This loose system of nobility and feudal servants oversees every aspect of the nation, from the highest noble decrees to the lowest elements of criminality. As the ruling body of a nation, the Crimson Court has a great deal of power, but also a great deal of responsibility.

Monster Hunter Guilds. Monster hunting is an ancient trade. What separated hunters from common warriors was curiosity—a genuine desire to understand one’s enemy. Knowledge rose as the monster hunter’s most lethal weapon. Recent times have seen an increase in organized hunting guilds. Killing monsters has become a corporatized trade as hunters band together and share knowledge, resources, and contracts. Some guilds have arranged themselves into a formalized structure like a mercenary company, with captains accepting quests and assigning them to teams of hunters.

Morbus Doctore. The Morbus Doctore was created to combat the Weeping Pox, The members are easy to spot: boots and gloves dried with blood, strange coats and satchels hung with vials, and frightful masks meant to protect them from the disease. Established in the city of Liesech, they now operate in other major metropolitan areas of Etharis—looking for newly infected people to treat.

Order of Dawn. The Order of Dawn formed when Ostoyans fled the rule of their vampire overlords and settled in the sympathetic province of Raevo. These dissidents occupied a fort outside of Castalore, where they became a militia fully committed to the destruction of the undead and the liberation of the rest of Ostoya. Members are trained and battle-ready, prepared to give their lives for their cause.

Prismatic Circle. The Prismatic Circle is an order of druids who believe that only war and bloodshed can keep the world safe from the reawakening of Gormadraug, the Prismatic Wyrm. Several of the Valikan Clans follow their teachings, turning their home region of Thrull into an area of constant warfare and occasional human sacrifice.


## Augustine Trading Company

Success in all ventures and circumstances requires the unhindered flow of goods and services.

Siraj Fakhri A bastion of free trade, champion of education, and at the forefront of innovation, the venerable Augustine Trading Company is everywhere in Etharis. Behind its shining image is a ruthless corporation with a draconic thirst for coin. From its headquarters in Morencia, the Company’s tendrils reach every corner of the known world, and even some corners long forgotten. Founded by the disgraced clergyman Manoch Augustus, the Company originated as a local weapon distributor and evolved to become a global powerbroker. Though nominally headquartered in Morencia, the Company is found anywhere shipping lanes, caravans, or black-market networks do business.

Administered by a council of five called the Montarri lead by the financier Kolyhs “The Blazing Brain” Papinian, the Company controls various shell companies and governments via proxy ownership, debt-leverage, or economic entrapment. It funds wars, builds cities, collapses markets, and breeds monsters for battlefield use. It even maintains elite universities to develop the next generation of talent. The Company always takes the long view.

To the public, the Augustine Trading Company is a necessary engine of civilization, but in truth, it is a gilded machine of exploitation creating an empire bound by ledgers and blood. Adventurers who serve its interests are well-paid but expendable, and those who cross it face economic ruin… or worse. To many, the Company isn’t just business—it’s destiny.

### History

Siraj Fakhri Kolyhs Papinian Manoch Augustus began life as a priest. While in the priesthood, he came to work in the Secretariat of Coin, managing the church’s funds. There he discovered his talent for growing funds through unscrupulous means. When his peers did not applaud his financial acumen, he left the church amid scandal. With few options left, he turned his talents to commerce. He tried to learn the error of his ways and started several honest businesses, but all failed. Turning to what he was good at, Augustus found a more profitable profession: the weapon trade. Using the profits from the arms trade, Augustus diversified into lending, encouraging the wealthy to offer land as their collateral, knowing most nobility did not comprehend the ruinous terms he offered. Foreclosing on these properties after his clients defaulted on his predatory loans, Augustus was able to enter into yet another lucrative business: real estate. Land acquired was not sold but rather rented to tenets who worked it in exchange for a share of their labors, which were in turn sold. Building a pyramid of wealth, where the fruits of one business created the next, the Augustine Trading Company became the wealthiest faction in Etharis.

But it was not until an orphan turned banker became Treasurer, that the Company grew more powerful than most governments. Kolyhs “The Blazing Brain” Papinian has grown the Company to become the dominate economic force in Etharis; so powerful that most governments no longer dare cross it. The company has expanded into every trade and industry including warfare, security, lending, food production, medicine, construction, labor, crafts, resource extraction, automatons, and education. It doesn’t matter if it is legal; the Company stocks it and sells it somewhere.

#### Secret Projects

Ever fearful of stagnation, Kolyhs urges a system where the Company trains academics at a host of corporate universities called Augustine Centers, and the most promising are then directed into secret research programs to develop the next profitable product or service. The most famous is Project Warbreed, which creates lycanthropic super soldiers and continues to revolutionize by crossing these thralls with other cursed creatures like vampires, creating further useful mutations. Of late, interest has grown in the arisen. If an employee dies and returns, do they still have rights, or is their now reanimated body owned by the Company?

Warbreed is not the only secret project. There are dozens. Project Fumarialis designs new magical and black powder weapons of war. Magical automatons that look more and more like humanoids or animals are being created to embed spies and assassins in a target’s city or home. Project Mandragora uses alchemy to create performance-enhancing potions and drugs. Not only can they endow the average guard with the strength of ten men and the senses of a bloodhound, the Company can also lace the supplement with addictive substances, so guards stay loyal to get their high. Mandragora also seeks to treat, not cure, the symptoms of many ailments, allowing the Company a steady stream of revenue from desperately ill patients.

#### Market Adaptation

The Augustine Trading Company tailor their business plan to make them indispensable, no matter the local laws or customs. If a trade is looked down upon, such as arcane items in Castinella, they emphasize other goods, or bribe local governments to allow their trade to go unimpeded. If one Valikan Clan raids them, they bring vital resources and wonders from the south to another, emphasizing that trade can only continue if their customer can keep the trade routes open and safe. There is always a market, and always someone desperate, to buy from the Company.

This leaves many of the other factions with a conundrum. The Arcanist Inquisition and the Watchers of the Faithful generally despise what the Company stands for and the methods it uses, but they also need the goods only the Company can provide. They generally ignore the Company when they can but come down heavily if they catch it violating any laws. The Ebon Syndicate sees the Company as a competitor, but also a kindred spirit that it can deal with. One season might see the Syndicate purchasing narcotics from the Company, and another might see it robbing the lab where the pharmaceuticals are made. The Thaumaturge finds the Company useful for acquiring arcane reagents, but new spells or apprentices might be stolen by the Company.

Perhaps only the Company of the Free Swords understands the Augustine Trading Company. The latter pays good coin, provides powerful weapons, and then expects the former to complete a contract. When the contract is closed, there is no further connection until the next contract is negotiated. Their relationship is coldly transactional, as it should be.


### Goals

The Augustine Trading Company focuses on expanding resources to maximize its profits. It pursues its goals by ruthlessly opening new markets, developing new technologies, and removing the competition. As a soulless organization, it has no moral direction but profit, and it has the resources to hide the lengths it will go to achieve its goals.

#### Profit by Any Means

The Company pursues profit by any means available. This includes opening trade in all major, and not-so-major, cities, or sending explorers to find new resources to exploit. The Company uses less savory methods as well.

Using economic manipulation, the Company buys up essential industries to fix prices; sabotages rival businesses through bribery, blackmail, or “accidents;” and uses predatory high-interest loans to seize assets when the borrower defaults. They have been known to trap whole towns or cities in generational debt, creating de facto serfdom.

The Company militarizes commerce with programs like Project Warbreed, creating enhanced monstrous troops to be sold or rented. They hire adventurers to remove indigenous populations unwilling to cooperate and supernatural protectors of desired resources. The Company “redeems wayward souls,” capturing and trafficking magical beings and cursed individuals for research projects.

#### Protect the Company

The members of the Augustine Trading Company value the Company over any individual. Those who put the company first are rewarded, until they are no longer useful. People are disposable. Institutions endure.

To protect the Company, it employs hordes of lawyers and bankers. It wears contracts like armor and wields its wealth like a bludgeon. It maintains dossiers on everyone who has ever worked with or crossed them, saving these secrets for the future leverage. Whistleblowers are permanently silenced, and problematic adventurers sent on “suicide missions.” Governments and churches are incentivized to aid the Company through bribes, favorable deals, and financial support. Laws are rewritten to criminalize competition or make it illegal to buy from them. Corrupt religious leaders issue holy edicts encouraging the faithful to buy from the Company. Those who refuse the Company’s gold can always be blackmailed or disappeared.

#### Secure the Future

The Augustine Trading Company strongly believes in education and growing talent. It funds colleges and granting scholarships to their Augustine Centers found throughout Etharis, training the next generation of innovators. Applicants are carefully screened for flexibility, prizing accomplishment over morality or dignity. The accepted are given lavish scholarships, contingent on service and success, creating indentured servants afraid to lose their position.

Students and their instructors delve into all sorts of research that might become profitable: new spells to increase production, magizoology to develop new sources of magic or super soldiers, or even black powder and steam engines for weapons or locomotion.

### Leadership

“Borrowing” from the Competition

Kolyhs Papinian was orphaned by the age of eight and grew up on the streets, where she learned that being unobtrusive has its virtues, but the right words can control any situation. The woman who would become “The Blazing Brain” grew up to understand three things she would use to gain the power and control that she needed.

Truth is a luxury. Control is a necessity. The world is a dangerous place. Without family or position, any random calamity could ruin or kill her. To control the world around her is to survive. Truth is too expensive to afford. Lies and proxies are safer, and secrets keep you untouchable. This became the foundation of her plans.

Gold buys silence faster than truth buys trust. Kolyhs schemed her way to enough money that she could afford an apprenticeship to the Banker’s Guild. Along the way, she learned that loyalty, justice, and friendship could be purchased. Coin is a weapon that she wields more shrewdly than any blade.

Every system has a door, and every door has a price. Any system—religious, economic, legal, or magical—always has hidden loopholes that can be exploited to gain leverage. To master a system is not to follow it, but to break and own it. Using the legal system, she could turn her position as Head-Executor of the Banker’s Guild into a spot on the Montarri of the Augustine Trading Company. Using the financial system, she could use the Company’s money to secure a position on the Supreme Council of Morencia. Using the political system, she could bind all three together to make her unassailable.

There may be no shrewder creator of contracts short of the Arch Daemons than Kolyhs Papinian (Archmage). The gnome who rose to be on the Bankers Guild, the Augustine Trading Company, and the Morencian Supreme Council is disarmingly charming. When first met, she seems little more than a minor functionary, but in front of a crowd, her charisma blazes to life, enthralling all who listen. Kolyhs understands what people want, and she is always ready to sell it to them.

#### The Montarri

As one of five members of the Montarri, Kolyhs is head of the Augustine Trading Company, holding the simple title of Treasurer. In addition to the Treasurer, the other directing councilors include:

- Senior Preceptor Melchora Rosalle, who manages the researchers of the Augustine Centers.
- Master of Ledgers Enzo Reiff, who is in charge of trade operations.
- Lord Barrister Anlon Katzenbach, who is in charge of contracts and legal agreements.
- Intendant of Compliance Odriana van de Nijveldt, who is the Company’s spymaster.

Each of the Montarri control any number of agents, which they use to scheme against the Company’s enemies, and sometimes each other. Accusations of corruption are common but rarely bear fruit. The Montarri are essentially unquestionable within their spheres of influence. Forcing one to sacrifice a handful of pawns or lose some profits is the best any of them can hope for against each other. Only Kolyhs might oust one of the other Montarri, and why would she do so when she is the one who chose them for their positions?

### Agents

The Augustine Trading Company has employees from middle managers and trade factors, to specialists like preceptors and containment agents who specialize in working with magical beasts. The most talented agents are usually headquartered in Morencia and dispatched as needed.

Local branch directors cultivate their own specialists, deploying them only for the most important missions where their talents are critical to success. Instead, mercenaries and adventurers make disposable employees when situations are dangerous or require deniability. Pay is quite generous, as are the perks that often accompany such missions, so being selected by the Augustine Trading Company is quite a triumph for an adventuring party. The tasks are usually quite clear and uncomplicated, as long as the party has few morals and even fewer scruples.

### Important Locations

Towering over the mist-choked canals of Morencia like a black and gilded fang is the August Spire, a fortress-like palazzo that is equal parts palace, bank, and office building. The innovation of the Company is on full display, with gas lamps and waterwheels churning beneath it, powering arcane engines. The headquarters of the Augustine Trading Company has the expected boardrooms, offices, and vaults, but that is only a small portion of the nexus of power of the Company. The Company’s most prized possession is a portal made of twisted flesh and enchanted iron that allows the Company to dispatch couriers and spies across Etharis if the need is great enough. Powering the portal is ruinously expensive and requires vampire blood, the flesh of fiends, metal tainted by the primordial elements, and essence drained from rare magical items whose creation has been lost to time. No one but the Augustine Trading Company could afford to activate it, and even they do so only under the most important circumstances.

#### Trading Hubs

Outside of Morencia, the Company establishes trading hubs that blend with the dominant aesthetic of the region. They are always well-staffed and well-defended public storefronts, selling luxury goods and necessary staples. They also include a backroom where mercenaries might be hired, gray market deals can be struck, or more “complicated” shipments are handled. Nearby is always one or more warehouses, strategically placed near roads and waterways. Each trading hub is overseen by a Trade Factor who reports to the August Spire in Morencia.

#### Research and Development

The Company maintains the Augustine Centers, where they fund research and develop their latest talent. These elite schools are extremely expensive and frequently require sponsorship or significant tuition to enter. However, actual aptitude always outcompetes wealth, as the Company cannot afford to allow talented agents to work for their competitors. Graduating students can expect to move into senior field roles or coveted regional trading hubs, giving them more chances to succeed and further indebting them to the Company.

Nearly every major city has at least one Center. The most famous include the Collegium of Progress and the Augustine Academy of Applied Arcana. The former is located in Morencia, near the Company’s headquarters, training the next generation of middle managers, teaching law, economics, accounting, contract negotiation, and ethics. The later is located in Castalore and specializes in surveillance arcana, information warfare, and marketing.

#### Secret Sites

The company maintains several secret facilities. Under the frozen lands of Grarjord is Facility Null-9, home to the cutting-edge research of Project Warbreed to design controllable super soldiers. Here, new warbreed are created, combining lycanthrope and vampiric curses to make new thralls. The thralls are made obedient via trauma-induced memory erasure that makes them lethal command-following automatons…usually. Some say the facility itself howls when the wind dies.

Hiding in the Castinella mountains of Corova is Ashhold Alpha, a war college run off the books, where mercenaries and monstrous clientele test prototypes designed by Project Fumarialis.

In the forests of the Charneault Kingdom is the Unlisted Garden, home to Project Mandragora, distilling strange fey-tainted plants to create arcano-reactive reagents for alchemical and transformational drugs. Poisonings are, unfortunately, very common among the staff, both unintentional and intentional.

### Quest Hooks

The Augustine Trading Company is always looking for specialists, especially disposable assets like mercenaries or adventurers. No matter the Company’s shiny propaganda and lucrative pay, their assignments are almost always morally gray. Those in service to the Company must often choose between serving the Company’s ruthless interests or preserving their own conscience.

Widow’s Curse. The Company needs an escort for a trading vessel or caravan. Hidden amidst harmless cargo is the Widow’s Veil, a cursed, tattered black veil that is always damp with cold, unseen tears. Once the property of a grieving noblewoman from the Bürach Empire, she cursed death itself when her love was taken by war. Now the veil attracts incorporeal undead. This highly dangerous artifact is destined for a Company black site where a new project intends to capture unquiet souls for experimentation. The characters will have to deal with frequent attacks and possession attempts until the source is uncovered. Destroying the cargo forfeits payment, but completing delivery may be morally problematic for some characters.

Ambushed Trader. The party witnesses a violent ambush against a trade factor named Herlinde. If they rescue her, they can begin an investigation uncovering a conspiracy implicating rivals in the Augustine Trading Company. If they fail to save her, the party is blamed for Herlinde’s murder, and agents of the Company are hired to eliminate the “murderers,” or potential witnesses.

Monster Salvage. The Company hires the characters to salvage parts from various monsters, not all of whom are evil. Once the hearts of their quarries are realized, the party must choose whether to harvest the monster anyway or stop the Company from murdering an innocent monster.

Abduction. The characters are hired to abduct Humusi, a teen the Company claims murdered one of their trade factors during a street robbery. In reality, some quirk of Humusi’s birth renders her particularly resilient to the ravages of Transformations. The Company plans to send her to Facility Null-9 and use her as a new vampiric werewolf hybrid Warbreed. If the characters hand her over, Humusi proves a natural host, but eventually breaks her programming and begins hunting the party out of vengeance.

Plant Management. A mutated sentient plant with mind-controlling spores has gotten loose inside the Unlisted Garden, and the characters must rescue the researchers. There is a significant bonus that dwarfs the rescue pay, if the characters can capture the creature alive.

Bandit Control. Gorlga Zverferrin, a bandit captain and self-styled merchant prince, has been harassing Company caravans and threatening a local trade hub. The characters are offered a reward to eliminate the threat, but double if they can convince her to become an agent of the Company and harass its competitors instead.

Mage Rescue. The Arcanist Inquisition is no friend of the Augustine Trading Company, and they are good at capturing mages. The characters are hired to rescue a preceptor from the Augustine Academy of Applied Arcana before he can be burned. There is an extra reward for determining what spell or magic item they used to contain the potent spellcaster.

Debt Collection. Lord Léo Vaillancourt owes a significant sum to the Company and is no longer able to make his payments, defaulting on his loan. The characters are sent to collect on the debt. While money is fine, the Company would much rather have his land and family heirlooms, which are said to include several unique magical items, as well as a vast library on monster hunting. Unfortunately, for the characters, the Vaillancourts are skilled monster hunters and include fey and hag Transformed among their number.

Tainted Pirates. The Galling Visage has been taken by pirates and the characters are hired to recover it. Most pirates, would never risk tangling with a Morencian ship, especially a Company floating fortress, but Captain Ix’ilettal and his crew are twisted aberrant horrors of the deep. Now crewing the Galling Visage and the Ix’ilettal’s Tyrant’s Mercy, the crew seeks to spread its taint to even more crews.

Bookeeper’s Secrets. Lonzol, a Company bookkeeper, turns up face-down in a Morencian canal, pockets empty. Worse, his ledger’s missing. The Augustine Trading Company wants it found before the wrong kind of curious mind cracks it open. The characters are told it contains only numbers, but nobody murders over simple math. The party must follow the trail through rain-slick alleys, bribed guards, and Ebon Syndicate criminals who’d rather see the truth of what’s in the ledger.

Burning Warehouses. A warehouse goes up in flames; the third one this month. Officially, it was an accident. Unofficially, it was sabotage, and the Company’s losing patience. They want the party to find out who’s torching their assets and make an example of them. The problem is, the more they dig, the more they realize these fires might be the only honest thing they’ve been told. Each warehouse contained off-books caged daemons, to be sent for experimentation by Project Warbreed. Exposing the truth risks alienating another department of the Company.


## Company of Free Swords

Blood and Honor, Strength in Steel

Anastasia Komissarova The Company of Free Swords is the pre-eminent mercenary organization on the continent, with branches in most major cities. The company takes all manner of contracts—from ridding a village of a monster, to protecting a town from bandit raids, to sending hundreds to fight a full-fledged war for a well-paying lord. The company has a code of honor and an elite reputation they make sure to spread. Despite the name, its services do not come for free. If you want the best loyalty money can buy, a company known for slaying daemon lords and sending Aether Kindred into the void, then you want the Company of Free Swords, and you better be ready to pay.

Despite their love for coin, the Free Swords are not unscrupulous. The company's honor, its soldiers' well-being, and the outright refusal to commit atrocities are core principles. While individual branches may at times be swayed by coin to stray from their code, the Free Swords are known to “discipline” their own, so none take that chance lightly.

Skill and honor are the main qualifications for joining the Free Swords, and they have attracted warriors, priests, druids, rogues, spies, and monster hunters into their ranks.

### History

The founding of the Free Swords dates back nearly two centuries, to a bloody conflict in the Falaris province of southeastern Bürach. Here, conscripted soldiers of the rebel tyrant King Vaclav II fought an increasingly brutal guerrilla campaign against the Bürach army.

Things came to a head when the soldiers were ordered to slaughter a rural village who had refused to join the tyrant’s army. Commander Darion Harkin not only refused the order but also led a mass desertion, with many soldiers choosing to follow his tough-but-fair leadership.

Vaclav II sent the remainder of his forces to punish the deserters, but Harkin anticipated the move. By the time the tyrant’s forces arrived at the deserter’s encampment, Harkin and a small group of his most loyal soldiers had snuck into the king’s own keep and assassinated him.

David Alunan Free Sword Captain Harkin then sued for peace with the Bürach, offering to “purchase” Vaclav’s keep with the very treasure that they’d looted from it. The Bürach were happy to have an end to the conflict and a controlled presence in the area. In what eventually became known as Harkin’s Hold, the Company of Free Swords was founded with Darion Harkin’s famous words: “If we must deprive this world of fathers, brothers, and sons, and be killed as we do so, let it be for the highest coin, that we may die content.” Despite the mercenary mindset, the company took much of its ethos from its founding conflict. Harkin instilled a code of honor comprised of three core principles: stay loyal to the company, be honest in your dealings, and take vengeance on those who betray you or your comrades. Having their own space to rest and train, and a location close to plenty of opportune conflicts, the Free Swords soon became respected and feared. As their reputation grew, so did their ranks, and it wasn’t long until new branches opened—across Bürach and beyond.

Over the years, the Free Swords have fought in many notable conflicts, large and small. Encouraged to learn and recount (and perhaps embellish) their history, some of the Free Swords have also gained a bard-like reputation.

#### The Battle of Kovich Crag

An early conflict that helped established the Free Swords occurred during the early days of the Bürach-Ostoya conflict. After a failed over-the-mountains push, a battered Bürach regiment retreated through the Kovich Crag—a low but narrow pass through the Grey Spine. Emboldened, the Ostoyans readied a counterattack and marched up the pass.

With a contingent of Free Swords nearby, the Bürach forces quickly contracted the Company to hold the pass. Further complicating matters, an Ostoyan spy among the Bürach warned the Ostoyan troops, allowing them to prepare.

Against the odds, the Swords emerged victorious. Stories are still told of their hit-and-run feints and a detachment that climbed rock walls to decimate the Ostoyan flank. In a brazen move, Harkin sent the defeated Ostoyan commander back with a scroll offering the company’s services in the future, planting the first seeds of their expansion into Ostoya.

#### The Daemon Lord of Kravburg

Few events in the company’s history have brought as much glory and scrutiny as the story of the Silent Seven. The company had received a lucrative commission from the Burgermeister of Kravburg—a small but affluent town of nearly a thousand souls. There were reports of ritualistic murders, foul magic, and whispers of a daemonic presence.

Two dozen well-trained mercenaries traveled to Kravburg. What happened next is subject to wild speculation and the stuff of legends.

After two weeks, the town was burned to the ground, with half its population dead, with the remaining having fled to the surrounding woods. In the middle of what was once the town square smoldered the charred remains of an enormous horned creature. Many experts claimed it was a daemon lord—a creature of mythical power, thought immortal. Seven members of the Free Swords survived, but aside from a well-rehearsed phrase of: “we killed the daemon, we saved the people,” none ever spoke of what took place.

The legend of this grew. Without exception, the surviving townsfolk spoke of the mercenaries giving their lives to get them to safety, but there were also tales of them killing possessed townsfolk, of readying for an unholy ritual, and of fighting each other with unnaturally glowing eyes.

None argued about the dead daemon lord, and the company continues to leverage the tale decades later. When things don't go as planned, the Company's more sardonic members still grimly say: “We killed the daemon. We saved the people.”

#### The Company Today

Today, Company branches have spread across Etharis, with the four most profitable interests found in Bürach, Ostoya, Castinella, and Charneault. Free Swords chapters maintain the same tenets but vary greatly by geography.

In Castinella, the Free Swords are often engaged by the Arcanist Inquisition, who have strict rules regarding mages in the chapter. In the knightly courts of Charneault, chapters are often contracted on a prolonged basis, requiring an oath of service for the duration. There is even a small chapter of the company that is spirit-sworn, who only take contracts in line with the spirit’s wishes—conveniently, this spirit is easily aggravated.

Despite their growth, the Free Swords maintain a stellar reputation for their skill, fearlessness, and honorable conduct. They stand ready to face any danger, provided there's enough coin. Their renown for collecting payment “one way or another” is also well known, making crossing them unadvisable.

### Goals

The Company enables its swords to live grand, action-filled lives, and it does so by embracing its core goals.

#### For the Highest Coin

Nothing’s free. The mercenaries are well aware that their exciting lives are often destined to end in painful and inglorious deaths far from home. They’re equally aware that the mitigating factor to this is coin.

Gold. Treasure. Payment. The Free Swords take their money seriously—nothing gets done without it, and woe be to any client that can’t pay up.

However, coin does not trump loyalty or integrity—at least not for the Free Swords. The Company firmly believes that trustworthiness, and knowing that your ally won’t turn on you mid-battle, is worth a premium. Honor is good for business.

With varied independent chapters, there have been instances of a unit not honoring a contract or switching sides. One of the most prominent of these took place during the Ostoyan civil war. A company of Free Swords was hired by Raevans to reinforce the Order of Dawn at a key skirmish. When the Somans brought out the Swords, several members refused to break their oath, resulting in a bloodbath that that saw the Free Swords slaughter each other.

The battle caused philosophical debate to ripple across the organization, with members torn between the ideals of duty to their brothers versus the need to honor contracts. There are rumors (which the company tries to suppress) of a secret group who call themselves “The Red Swords,” who take the highest coin regardless of moral implications.

#### Never Leave a Debt Unsettled

The Free Swords will take payment one way or another. They consider it acceptable to pillage lands if they feel their work hasn’t been justly compensated. They’re not brigands, however, and will first pursue the lord who disrespected them rather than the land’s peasants.

#### Life Well Fought; Life Well Lived

In addition to the code he set in place, Darion Harkin’s own work ethic and disciplined approach to the mercenary life became an example to the rest of the company. When on the job, Free Swords were known to live free of excess, stay loyal, and focus on the work. Only once the job was done, their coin collected, and the company’s reputation upheld did they celebrate. And celebrate they did! Harkin encouraged a gregarious nature in his people, telling them to spend widely, and pay generously for things, not only out of fairness, but because success begets success.

This mindset remains in place today. When people speak of the two faces of the Free Swords, they speak not of duplicity, but of the difference between the colorful, well-natured big spenders at the tavern and the stone-faced soldiers on the job.

### Leadership

The Company of Free Swords are organized into local chapters, which are typically run independently, with only nominal oversight and sharing of contracts. However, it is common for several chapters to be run out of the same headquarters, and in such cases, there’s a Council of Captains and a Chapter Head. Certain rules and obligations of the greater organization must be met by each chapter, but the company’s strong discipline means that these are most often enforced by exception.

The Company of Free Swords today is led by Yrsa Bitterwind, a towering, musclebound Valikan woman. Her tale of coming to run the largest mercenary organization in Etharis is one for the Sagas.

She was soon seen as a potential successor to Nölgr Magnusson, the Völgr clan chief and Keeper of the Three Tribes. She yearned for ascension and recognition, but clan politics would not allow her to challenge Nölgr. She grew increasingly brash and brutal, striving ever harder to prove herself in a situation where nothing would be enough. Pivotal Battle. Yrsa Bitterwind has brought the company greater recognition and prosperity, but not all her impacts have been positive or well received. Her temper in negotiations and her utter brutality on the battlefield, while welcomed by some, have made others apprehensive. Some say that her drive for personal accomplishments and “besting of worthy foes” puts them at risk. Others worry that everything she does is motivated by her eventual return to Valika, and not for the advancement of the Company.

#### Chandra Veln

Yrsa’s right-hand and second in command is Chandra Veln, a mysterious elf sorcerer who acts as the chief spymaster of the Free Swords. Also prominent among the Company’s other leaders is Hans Tova, an ancestral Büracher and the son of one of the infamous Silent Seven. Hans is known for being a shrewd negotiator, a keen tactician, and drawing upon powerful unnatural abilities in combat.

### Agents

Historically, new recruits into the Swords—regardless of their training or military service—spend at least a year in regimented training. The action they see is plenty dangerous, but only under strict veteran oversight. The training is as much to ensure their combat prowess as it is to instill the Company mindset and tenets.

Since Yrsa’s unorthodox yet well-documented ascension, there have been more exceptions made for those with proven records and skills. This has been met with decidedly mixed feelings among the veterans. Ranks and Pay The Free Swords embrace their “simple soldier” mindset and avoid a complex hierarchy. A recruit who’s completed their first-year training and initial engagements becomes a Free Sword—the typical foot soldier of the company. All Free Swords of the same rank are paid the same share. After at least five years and having proven themselves, one becomes either a Veteran or a Specialist, gaining a bigger share than a regular Free Sword. Specialists are Veterans with unique skills. These include sappers who use engineering, alchemy, or magic to affect the battlefield, battlemages who are field-level combatants that bring powerful spell support, and infiltrators who are skilled in stealth, poison, and neutralization of individual targets. Finally, those who lead are promoted to Captains. Captains get the highest share of the coin. David Alunan Augustine Preceptor

Although there is no combat rank higher than Captain, there is a leadership hierarchy among the different chapters, which determines rank among the Captains.

In the field, a Captain can lead as few as one other Sword and as many as several hundred. For large battles, temporary military-style designations are given to different Captains to create the necessary hierarchies. This allows the company to field units of dramatically different sizes.

#### Equipment and Visual Identity

Free Swords are always proficient with the sword and shield, and many choose to carry these tried-and-true implements into battle. The company itself does not mandate equipment and encourages its members to take pride in the procurement and maintenance of the best weapons of war for themselves.

In times of mass battle, the Free Swords don their distinctive red and gold “Double Lion” tabards or those provided by their employer. Otherwise, the mercenaries are allowed to wear what they want. All are issued a sturdy double lion brooch, which they wear with pride, except in cases of espionage or other subterfuge.

While the company rarely provides more than the very basics of equipment, they do have access to some of the best merchants and artisans across Etharis, including many who set up permanently on-site at company locations. After all, where better to get a good price for weapons of war than amongst well-paid mercenaries for whom such things could mean the difference between life and death?

### Important Locations

Free Swords Pikemen


For several years after their founding, the Free Swords operated out of Harkin’s Hold—the keep they’d won as part of their founding insurrection. Although the company soon outgrew its confines, the small keep remains a Free Swords outpost. Darion Harkin’s grave and many artifacts from the company’s founding are kept here, and company members often make a pilgrimage to see the original tenets—painted by Harkin himself upon simple planks of wood. Harkin’s Hands—a small chapter of the company, many of whose members trace their lineage back to the original members—operates out of the outpost.

#### Expansion

When the company ran out of space at Harkin’s Hold, they realized they needed an independent keep, with privacy for both clients and mobilization, as well as space for barracks, stables, and training areas. By this time, many members of the original company (those still alive) were ready to retire and invested their substantial fortunes into the building of the Löwengard Fortress. Located in Bürach, the impressive fortress stands in the shadow of the Grey Spine, north of the Grove Maze and south of Vollendorf. The strategic location allows the company to travel by road, river, or mountain pass to Bürach, Charneault, Ostoya, and beyond.

Löwengard can house over a thousand Free Swords, although typically only three hundred active and two hundred recruits are stationed here. The reinforced fortress boasts training facilities, armories, and over two dozen expert craftsmen, including an enchanter. In addition to soldier barracks, the Fortress has a reception hall, several private war rooms, and a council chamber where the largest Council of Captains met for nearly a century before the Lions Den in Voros became the more centralized location. It is often said that: “If you want to start a rumor, go to Voros; if you want to start a war, go to Löwengard.”

Farms and small holdings have sprung up in the fertile areas around the fortress and now provide it with most of its basic needs without having to source from nearby cities. The company pays well for such goods.

#### Lions Dens

Even as the Löwengard was built and populated, the company needed guild-style representation offices in larger cities. Thus, the Lions Dens were created to serve as the company’s urban face, to drive recruitment and drum up local business. Most often a Lions Den is located in an urban center near an associated rural fortress of the Free Swords—a smaller, local version of Löwengard.

Lions Dens vary greatly by city and location. In some towns, they’re but a single-room shack run by a representative from the local keep. In other cities, they are resplendent guild halls with meeting areas, services, and lodgings for visiting Free Swords.

The most impressive of these is located in the city of Voros in Castinella. This Lions Den was built as a purposefully impressive display, designed to establish the company’s presence in both the Provinces and nearby Charneault. Despite increasing business, securing lands for new keeps and dens in the new regions proved slower than expected, and the Voros den grew in size and stature.

This was further helped by Armand Savranti—Yrsa Bitterwind’s immediate predecessor—who hailed from Castinella and took to holding many Councils of Captains in Voros instead of Löwengard. Few objected to this, as the High Captains very much enjoy their urban comforts when not out in the field.

Yrsa, on the other hand, prefers the cold mountain air of the fortress, and holds more and more councils there once again. The Voros location remains geographically and politically vital for the Free Swords.

The characters’ interactions with the Free Swords can range from uncomfortable interactions while on competing missions to full membership. Unaffiliated characters can find themselves hired by the company to fix urgent issues or to assist in times when extra hands are needed. Those in service to political powers or small settlements may find that the Free Company has been hired as an opposing force.

Characters who gain full membership are only allowed to leave the company with permission, which typically requires them to be in good standing and to maintain discretion regarding past dealings.

A Captain’s Death. A brutal murder has been committed in one of the towers of the Löwengard Fortress. The victim was a fervently loyal captain, but widely disliked. Yrsa Bitterwind herself may hire respected outsiders to investigate. Plenty of swords disliked the gruff captain, but was his death a personal vendetta or had he uncovered evidence of a Red Swords cell within the fortress?

Local Reinforcement. The Blades of Bressil, a distant Free Swords branch, have taken a monster-hunting contract to track down a werewolf terrorizing the town of Claussein. When one werewolf turns out to be a pack, and one of the Free Swords is taken during an initial skirmish, the company needs immediate local help.

Unpaid Protection. Kutor Bracht, the Burgermeister of the small Bürach village of Narskenn, has failed to pay a group of Free Swords for protecting his village from bandits. The Burgermeister argues that the bandits never attacked while the Swords were under contract, suggesting the mercenaries colluded with the bandits and asks for help to resolve the situation.

Bandit Company. The Swords of Swamach, a small and distant branch of the company, have turned to banditry to survive, attacking nearby villages. The Free Swords sent another company to deal with them, but they were waylaid by monsters and decimated. The few who survived offer significant coin for aid in the assault on the renegade company’s fort.

They seek to hire others to begin the investigation on their behalf, intending to assault the monsters once their existing contract allows. They do not expect the monster attacks to escalate during the two weeks.

Sword Proxy. The Golden Glaives, a well-respected Free Swords branch in Castinella, were approached by the wealthy owner of a mine in the hills of Corova. The Golden Glaives do not wish to directly intervene because of political concerns with the Inquisition. ## Ebon Syndicate

The night is our kingdom.

If behind every unexplained success lies an undiscovered crime, then the Ebon Syndicate’s successes are as innumerable as their crimes. The syndicate’s tendrils stretch out from the seedy shadows into every aspect of urban life in Etharis, and often beyond. They are the beggar on the corner inconspicuously eavesdropping on nobles’ conversations. They are the thief cutting a purse string. They are the traceless spy planting evidence before the militia raid. They are the hooded assassin coming for you in the dark alley, and the smiling waiter serving you poisoned wine should you escape that alley. They have the thing you want, and the thing you need, and their eyes on the thing you hold most dear. The syndicate primarily operates in Etharis’s biggest and richest cities, where targets and opportunities are most plentiful. Lesser organizations and freelance operatives soon find themselves facing a deadly offer: stop, join, pay, or find yourselves bleeding in a gutter. The latter is not an idle threat, but a quickly fulfilled promise—those who step on the Syndicate’s toes or try to take their territory are mercilessly dispatched. Etharis’s other major factions may look down upon the syndicate’s activities, but they know enough to respect its capabilities. Even groups like the Augustine Company and the Watchers that publicly claim to oppose the syndicate have been known to take advantage of its unparallelled spy network. The syndicate pursues every kind of under-the-table opportunity. From petty crime to organized drug smuggling, extortion, and assassination—if it needs to be done outside the law, they get it done. Siraj Fakhri Josh Chia Natalia Koscheii

### History

“All roads lead to Altenheim, eh? So do you fellows want your roads to end here, or shall we make a deal?” These were supposedly the words of famed bandit Hiram Greyman as he hopelessly tried to bluff a group of seven city watch who’d cornered his band of three after a botched heist.

The watch did not in fact take his deal, and the roads of both of his companions ended in that alley. Gravely injured himself, and delirious from blood loss, Hiram desperately fled into the city’s hedge maze. It was there—as he told the story—that a hawk moth led him to safety.

Hiram saw the moth as a sign of divine favor, sent by the gods themselves. As he recovered and rebuilt his organization into what eventually became the Ebon Syndicate, he took the hawk moth as his personal symbol. When he died, the organization kept the symbol. The nocturnal moth flies swiftly through the darkness, drawn through its short life to dangerous and shiny things. Over the years, different leaders have focused on its different aspects as inspiration for the organization.

As the syndicate grew in prominence late in Hiram’s life, he became a more and more elusive figure by necessity. Few knew his true face, and getting an audience with “the Greyman” became a unique honor. This started the tradition of the Syndicate’s leaders taking the title of “Greyman.”


#### The Red Gondola

Once the Ebon Syndicate became a major power in Altenheim, having the connections and support there greased the wheels for expansion into other Bürach cities and beyond. While establishment of a major criminal organization rarely comes bloodlessly, it never came as hard as it did when the syndicate tried to get their foothold in the free city of Morencia.

One of the syndicate’s most common targets and foils throughout its existence has been the Augustine Trading Company. The Company carries expensive goods that are always a tempting target for the Syndicate’s thieves. Further, the Company practices what the Syndicate calls “above-table extortion,” thus exercising control in areas where the Syndicate would prefer to collect. The Syndicate’s spies, on the other hand, cause the Company no end of headaches by discovering its unsavoury secrets and selling them to the highest bidder.

Thus, when the Syndicate started to operate on the Augustine Trading Company’s home turf of Morencia, the canals ran red with blood. The times of open conflict in Morencia saw assassinations in broad daylight and open skirmishes between syndicate enforcers and Free Swords hired by the Company. The conspicuous conflict filled the streets with dread, regularly flaring up over many years. But it all ended, at least in public, after Castinella’s failed invasion.

Many rumors swirl around the political machinations that spurred the invasion. Among the more subtle is the story of the red gondola. In the aftermath of the failed Castinellan attack, an unmarked anchored ship was recovered. The mysterious dark ship held the bodies of more than a hundred well-armed soldiers with no insignias. They had been brutally slaughtered. Most had had their throats slashed, soaking the ship’s wood with blood. Rumors spoke of a large Morencian-style gondola seen departing and returning in the dark among the chaos of the invasion.

Such a gondola—unmarked and painted fully red with fresh blood—was left the next morning near the home of the leader of the Augustine Trading Company. A bouquet of inky-black roses in the shape of a hawk moth was in the gondola. The truth of what had transpired has never been revealed. Was it an Augustine ship carrying Castinellan invaders? Was it a Free Company ship, and those who had hired them? Had the Ebon Syndicate known of the invasion, only to leverage it to their own benefit? Did the Syndicate recover proof of the Company’s betrayal of the city? If they did, that truth has never seen the light of day.

Since that day, the Syndicate was more accepted in Morencia, and the war with the Augustine Trading Company grew cold, going off the streets and back into the shadows.

#### The Syndicate Today

With timely interventions (true or embellished) like the Red Gondola, the Syndicate is now well established continent wide.

Their headquarters are in Altenheim, and they are active across Bürach. The Erlefurt syndicate is known for the use of faerie magic, and for a pair of famous wechselkind thieves who can get to any treasure. In Rauland, Ebon Syndicate agents have been instrumental in stealing and disseminating the mechanized weapons of Stehlenwald. The city's fading fortunes have been the Syndicate’s greater success.

In Ostoya, the Ebon Syndicate is much more prominent in Raevo, as the Crimson Court has its own spies. In Castinella, mage smuggling provides a unique opportunity for the Syndicate. The latter work is hardly altruistic, as the Syndicate is just as happy to track down or assassinate mages for the Inquisition as they are to help them get away to safety.

Other than distant Valika, the Syndicate finds it most difficult to operate in Charneault. However, the rising chaos of the dark mist has dampened faith in the knights, creating opportunities for quicker and seedier solutions which the Syndicate is eager to provide. Fading relations with Tol Leyemil have also opened a door for the Syndicate, who are willing to trade with anyone, and more than keen to get their hands on rare elven poisons and other potent natural products. Lord Sorcerer Diyanthar was particularly eager to facilitate such trade in exchange for arcane knowledge procured world over, and his acolytes continue the practice.

### Goals

The greatest constant is change, and the Ebon Syndicate’s goals are not only ever-changing but vary drastically by location and time. On any given day, the Syndicate could have spies undermining a government, assassins killing the government’s rival, and thieves stealing from both victims.

If anything remains constant, it’s that at the highest level the Syndicate seeks money, power, and deniability. How these balance out in any specific time and place is the most tangled of webs.

#### Everybody has a Price

Coin is a great motivation for the Syndicate, but more so as a tool than it is for the other factions. While the Augustine Trading Company and the Free Swords may be primarily concerned with how much they can get, the Syndicate’s monetary motivations are much more practical: it’s about what—or who—coin can buy. The Syndicate has no political offices or aspirations, so what influence they have is influence they’ve bought with coin or leverage. While a petty thief goes into the life dreaming of riches, it is not long until that dream turns into the realization that what they really want is a way out, and the cost of that is often more than gold.

#### Control from the Shadows

The Syndicate’s lack of open political involvement should not be confused with a lack of influence.

While most obvious in the political spectrum, the Syndicate’s preference to pull strings from the shadows is evident across all their dealings. Unless they’re sending a message, the Ebon Syndicate remains unseen. They don’t hesitate to use proxies, patsies, and mercenaries in tasks that require deniability. It’s not uncommon for a freelancer to not realize who their true employer is. The lack of a public presence lets the Syndicate freely operate in more illicit industries than their reputable competitors, such as the Augustine Company.

While secrecy can be their greatest weapon, the Syndicate does not hesitate to play the boogieman when it suits their ends. The sheer size and scope of their operations is so great that it’s impossible for them to go unnoticed. An occasional display of their mark can act as much to cover their tracks as to reveal.

#### We Are the Hand Upon the Scales

The Ebon Syndicate ensures that no authority grows too large to threaten their operations. They expertly manipulate to keep allies in power and undermine enemies.

It’s not uncommon for the Syndicate’s operations to receive public approval. When the Syndicate saves the kidnapped prince, assassinates a stalking vampire, or publicizes a Burgermeister’s corruption, there are those who take them for heroes. In a way, the Syndicate’s lack of allegiance and willingness to take payment as a debt make them the most accessible force for righting a wrong. However, it pays to remember that just because the Syndicate’s goals aren’t visible doesn’t mean they aren’t being served. A hand that can easily right a wrong can also wrong a right.

### Leadership

#### Natalia Koscheii

The syndicate’s current Greyman is Natalia Koscheii, who has become as famous for her ability to escape assassination attempts as she is for her hands-on leadership skills. Stories of her unlikely escapes have been exaggerated to such an extent that some now insist that she is one of several identical children, several of whom are now dead. Others, citing her Ostoyan heritage, insist that she is a vampire who turns into a hawk moth to make her escapes.

Ancestry. Natalia is indeed of Ostoyan heritage, and of minor nobility at that. Her ancestry goes back to the Somans who defied the vampires and founded the Order of Dawn. As a teenager, Natalia’s mother grew tired of the bleak war, fleeing Castalore aboard a smuggler’s ship. She was taken to Castinella, only to find herself in danger from the Arcanist Inquisition. In a turn that perhaps the smuggler had been counting on, she chose to remain aboard the ship and put her arcane powers to use in a life of freedom. Some years later, Natalia was born to this Ostoyan refugee turned charming smuggler.

Development. Natalia was five years old when she participated in her first larceny. She inherited her mother’s magical gift and her father’s dexterity, and also their wandering spirit, so Natalia left the maritime life behind after falling in love with a wily Morencian thief during a routine port stop.

However, the life of young love and diversion was not her destiny. Things turned abruptly violent when Natalia’s lover, a Syndicate novice, found herself on the wrong side of an upstart gang and killed. Left on her own and out for revenge, Natailia turned to the Syndicate. She infiltrated the gang, and after barely a month, led them into an ambush that saw seventeen thieves pay with their lives.

The experience awakened something within Natalia. She found that emotional detachment and secretive machinations came easily. Her ability to disassociate and manipulate both tactically and emotionally, and her skills with both magical and mundane trickery, helped her to quickly advance through the Syndicate’s ranks.

Even in her early days, Natalia often changed locations. After her start in Morencia, she took over a mage-smuggling operation in Voros, oversaw a spy network out of Vollendorf, then finally seized leadership after bloody betrayal left the Syndicate’s Altenheim headquarters in disarray.

Leadership Style. Natalia has brought greater stability and reason to the Syndicate, focusing on exerting greater political influence to create opportunities. Less brutal than some of her predecessors, she values loyalty above all. Her uncanny ability to infiltrate local Syndicate arms to uncover the disloyal keeps other leaders on edge.

#### Larger Structure

The Ebon Syndicate’s network of agents, operators, and lieutenants is intentionally inscrutable, but this much is true: local gangs report to their individual bosses, while those bosses report to a Lieutenant—sometimes called a “Grayman” with a regional subtitle—who in turn reports to Natalia, the true Grayman.

For example, the Axehands weapon-smuggling gang in Stehlenwald reports to their boss, whom they’ve historically called “Picker.” The current Picker is Eetra Strothklar. Eetra reports to the Grayman of Stehlenwald, a mysterious dwarf called Spinnebleck. He then reports to the Grayman of Bürach—Ardekii “Blackflag” Berntsen, who answers to Natalia.

One of the Syndicate’s most famous lieutenants is Darion Aufir, the Greyman of Grabenstein. The debonair ogresh controls most of the criminal element in the city, and keeps a very public court within his lavish mansion.

Possibly the most brutal of the current leaders is Kallista de Locra, the young Grayman of Morencia, who has on multiple occasions proposed outright assassinating most of the Augustine leadership. There is suspicion that several recent attempts on Natalia’s life were ordered by Kallista.

### Agents

The unknowing agents of the Syndicate are many. A wash maid with access to everyone from soldiers to nobles is a perfect scout for a den of thieves. A duke’s steward with a gambling problem may divulge an important delegate's arrival, enabling a road ambush that can’t be traced. A gang of street thugs tipped off to a rich dandy’s passage won’t know that their attack forced the victim’s influential father prompt a military reinforcement, leaving a distant outpost ripe for the taking. Such are the webs the Syndicate spins.

#### The Shadow Academy

For the chosen few identified to join as full members of the Syndicate, there is the Shadow Academy. The academy is not a school or training ground; there are no stern masked instructors preaching rules, and there is no graduation to a fancy moth mask. The Shadow Academy is itself a tenet, a concept of how agents are trained.

The core philosophy of the academy is of hands-on training: “Every lesson is an action; every action is a test.” Passage through the Shadow Academy is typically overseen by the member who vouched for the recruit. Trust is paramount. If you’re not willing to vouch for those you bring in, then they do not deserve anyone else’s time.

Recruits are expected to bring some level of craft to the Syndicate, but more importantly, the right mindset. Once a recruit is brought in, they are mentored by different specialists, learning tasks on the job, before being passed on to the next. The recruit’s performance is discussed by the leadership when they change mentors.

Promising recruits who fail at certain tasks are given chances to excel in other areas. Recruits that fail repeatedly, or who embarrass the Syndicate, tend to disappear. Many are killed, but some—if the knowledge they have poses little danger—may be released and watched or relocated. While there are no “instructors” per se, the Syndicate encourages skilled members to pass their knowledge on. In exchange, they get a skilled new member for their own crew.

#### Specialists

The Syndicate is not a military organization and has no official ranks other than Grayman. Members are referred to by name or alias, and often by their specialties, often using local slang. For example, spies might be called “ears,” “listeners,” or “flies,” while assassins could be “blades,” “blackmoths,” or “fins.” Slang varies widely across the continent.

#### Local Enforcers

Street-level agents deliver overt official messages. Thugs and brutes with some manners and basic business sense can beat both into a target when the need arises. In addition to common weapons, Syndicate enforcers favor fighting chains or “chain-knuckles” that give them the choice to strangle or bind their opponents. These have the benefit of being less conspicuous than a blade.

### Important Locations

It has been lost to history whether the Ebon Halls—a tangled maze of ancient tunnels and natural caves that run beneath Altenheim—were named for the Syndicate or vice versa. What is known is that even before Hiram Greyman’s time, these passages were used by thieves, smugglers, and other never-do-wells. While Hiram may not have been the first to use them, he was the first to invest heavily in mapping and expanding them, laying the literal foundation for the Grey City.

The Grey City is an underground fortress that’s impenetrable as much due to the elusiveness of its location as its ever-changing set of deadly traps and hidden doors. More than one cautionary tale tells of a novice misreading a hidden sign before withering to death in a walled-off room full of poisonous fungus.

Established by Hiram and heavily upgraded since, the Grey City remains the Headquarters of the Syndicate. On those rare occasions when multiple Greymen choose to meet, it is most often here. What makes it a true wonder, however, is not the fortress at the center, but the vast network of tunnels around it. They lead to all areas of the city, connect to the sewers, and even have several lengthy connections to the river. Today’s Syndicate smugglers still utter thanks to the infamous laneshi Greyman Sorranna, who dedicated several years overseeing tunnel construction, nearly bankrupting the Syndicate in the process. The Grey City is unique to Altenheim. Other Syndicate headquarters vary greatly in design. The Syndicate has operated out of anchored ships, dockside warehouses, hillside caverns, secret mountain castles, and so many tavern basements that experienced Syndicate operatives still tap their foot to check the creaky floorboards in whatever drinking establishment they enter. ### Quest Hooks

Unless the characters have criminal tendencies, they are most likely to openly encounter the Syndicate as antagonists. However, the hidden hands of the Syndicate often secretly guide the actions of anyone accepting freelance work across Etharis.

Liberation. Already known for cruelty and paranoia, Baron Kronn Van Vosderg has begun hanging peasants from his castle walls, in what he calls “a search for traitors.” The characters are approached by the wife of man slated for execution with a desperate plea for help. They are subsequently approached by a Syndicate spy with an offer of cooperation. The spy’s partner, a skilled assassin sent to kill the Baron, has been detained by the paranoid guards, who have no idea of his true mission. If the characters can free the assassin to do his dark business, they’ll be free to rescue others in the castle.

Elderly Patron. The party are invited to a lavish ball in Altenheim. There, they meet a humble but wealthy older woman named Serafille du Molenn, a collector of antiques. She offers the characters several opportunities to recover historical artifacts and magic items. The first assignments are perfectly legal, involving dungeon delving and negotiation. As the characters continue their work, the requests become more questionable, leading to the eventual realization that they’re gathering objects of arcane power for the Ebon Syndicate.

Novice Thieves. The characters are engaged by Tobril Vortberg, a well-to-do merchant, to recover possessions stolen during a recent burglary. The thieves were a small gang of petty thieves on their first real job. Not knowing where to fence the goods, they talked about their loot all over town, leaving a trail to the Gandering Goose tavern, a secret Ebon Syndicate gathering spot. The gang went there, were scolded, and turned away. When the characters come looking, a “helpful” bystander gives them directions to the gang’s hideout, and a warning that they’re armed and violent. The Syndicate’s interest is to silence the nascent gang.

Sending a Message

Castinellan Extraction. The characters are approached by a Castinellan woman in Camont. She needs help getting her magically gifted brother out of Toletum before he’s captured by the Arcanist Inquisition. She mentions that she’d been given the name of a woman known to arrange such things, but she refused her due to increased Inquisition scrutiny. Her contact, an Ebon Syndicate smuggler named Rella, can be persuaded to provide intelligence and travel routes, but only if they agree to pick up a special package for her.

Past Ties. Caravis Voss, the Burgermeister of Tolensk, hires the characters to root out an Ebon Syndicate cell that’s trying to “muscle in” on his city. After some investigation, the characters discover that Caravis is a former member of the Syndicate himself and is trying to settle an old score rather than protect the town. To complicate things, the leader of the Syndicate cell sent to collect the debt is his former lover, Alexi.

Walk Away. The characters find themselves approached by several Syndicate enforcers, who tell them to walk away from whatever quest they are currently pursuing. If the characters refuse, the enforcers rough them up, attempting to teach them a lesson. If the enforcers win, they do not kill the characters; they just leave them beaten. The enforcers themselves do not know why they were sent, and the characters are left to wonder about the Syndicate’s goals, or to investigate further.


## Thaumaturge

We who shape magic, shape the world.

Suzanne Helmigh Siraj Fakhri Thaumaturge Archmage “Dear reader, it is only fair that you should be warned at this point, that if you’ve come to the pages of this fine tome for concrete information on the organization known as Grand Thaumatic Order of Archmages, you may find the words contained herein lacking. This is not for the lack of effort by the humble documenters of these things, but due to the simple truth that despite great effort, we have not been able to collect even a single piece of hard evidence of the organization’s existence…” So reads the introduction to The Powers of Etharis—one of the best researched (if somewhat out of date) historical tomes known. If the Grand Thaumatic Order of Archmages, or Thaumaturge in short, does exist, then they have gone to great lengths to conceal their presence. The existence of a secret society of archmages seems only logical. Between divination and arcane history, such individuals are likely to not only be aware of each other, but also to share many common interests and compete over high-magic knowledge and artifacts. The fact that there is no war amongst the world’s highest mages implies a certain level of cooperation or oversight. Whether the widespread theories of the Thaumaturge having unleashed the Weeping Pox or banished Ulmyr’s Gate to thwart a competing arcane organization have any grain of truth is unknown. Even if not directly involved, the members of the order should at least know about them. What is certain is that every great magical event, whether calamitous or benevolent, has been either blamed on or attributed to the order, and in many cases both.

### History

Tales of a secretive cabal of archmages date as far back as there is recorded history. Unexplained manipulations accomplished via great magical force have been recorded across every nation of Etharis.

One of the most infamous conspiracy theories is that the Thaumaturge unleashed the Weeping Pox, only to lose control of it. They then allegedly equipped Morbus Doctore with scientific and alchemical knowledge to combat the epidemic. Members of Morbus Doctore maintain that all their knowledge is the result of their own scientific research.

Not all tales of Thaumaturge are dark. The Order is widely assumed to oppose the Arcanist Inquisition, and many a novice mage has an unbelievable story describing their escape from Castinella. Of these, suddenly teleportation to a distant land is most common. Others tell of charmed guards suddenly undoing their bindings and ushering their captive out the door. The least told tale is of a roomful of guards and Inquisitors suddenly combusting or imploding into a bloody mess. Some mages claim these events were the sudden heightened manifestations of the victims’ own powers, but the truth points to them having magical help.

There are also plenty of tales describing a mysterious mage appearing at just the right time to help a village slay a monster or depose a tyrant. Conversely, other tales describe a dark-robed figure at the tyrant’s side. Such figures tend to appear from nowhere and disappear just as suddenly. If Thaumaturge exists, the one thing that can be certain is that they keep their motivations to themselves. If one came across a true recording of Thaumaturge history, they would likely find it full of tales too grand to be believed.


#### Thaumaturge Today

The Order is no less mysterious in the present day than they ever were. However, as is the case when the names of the powerful are well-known, the suspicion of membership is more concrete.

The Lady of Masks in Ostoya is suspected to be a member of the order, suggesting they actively oppose the Crimson Court.

In Bürach, the sudden magical appearance of Martorius’ castle has people speaking the name “Thaumaturge” out loud in suspicion. Many of these whispers are perhaps in the hope that “a greater good” has come to save the land from the Great Beast. Whatever Martorius’s purpose, he has not shared it, and the Hearthkeepers are as suspicious of him as they are of monsters.

At least a dozen prominent figures are rumored to be Thaumaturge members. In addition to these high-profile individuals, two characteristics have recently emerged to be associated with the Order.

The first is the renewed and very keen interest in the Sacred Artifacts. While these legendary items have long been sought, the mysterious source of present-day interest has stirred up rumors. These include that the Order has the artifacts and is seeking knowledge of how to use them, that they have some of the artifacts and seek the rest, or that they’ve lost the artifacts and are now trying to recover them from an immortal enemy.

The second rumour is of the Thaumaturge’s connection to the Aether Kindred. Strange arcane traces have been reported continent-wide by independent mages and the Arcanist Inquisition. Encounters with aberrations believed to be aetheric in nature have become more common. These events have led to suspicions that some members of Thaumaturge may be Aether Kindred, their puppets, or carelessly using connections to dangerous aether planes for travel and other unstable magic.

### Goals

Thaumaturge Council Meeting Space

To say that the Thaumaturge’s goals are obscure is an understatement. To say that any such goals are entirely united is ludicrous. Different powers within the organization pursue their own objectives, often competing and occasionally conflicting with one another. Despite the difference, they generally fall into one of three categories.

#### Power is Knowledge

If nothing else, the Thaumaturge believe that understanding the cosmos is key to progress and survival. To this end, members of the Order are collectors and procurers. They seek out knowledge in the form of tomes, scrolls, and objects and places of power. They create arcane laboratories and experiments—sometimes on a grand scale. The Order also seeks people who can further their ends. Talented mages, those born to strange powers, and those in touch with greater beings are of most use and recruited, knowingly or not, into the ranks. Because each member of the Thaumaturge often serves their own interests, their pursuits and methods vary. At the same time, such individuality may hint at the seeker’s areas of interest or expertise. A Hand Upon the Cosmic Wheel If the order had a gilded hall, etched into its entry gate would be the saying, “When the hands of fate align, the hand guiding them remain unseen.” The Thaumaturge understand that whatever the grand schemes of the powerful are, the common people need to believe their lives are their own. In order to guide things, events must be allowed to proceed. Lucy Lisett Mina Taliesin

The Thaumaturge believe in subtle and invisible manipulation. Their schemes and goals can span decades, centuries, and if one is to believe the transcendent nature of some of their members, millennia.

One thing of paramount importance to understanding the Thaumaturge is they believe non-action can be as powerful as interference. Sometimes, the greatest influence is in letting things proceed just as they are.

#### Every Life a Candle

The Thaumaturge tend to hold that every life is its own flickering candle, precious but ultimately doomed to be snuffed. All lives can be meaningful, yet none matter more than the tapestry of the cosmos. The Thaumaturge’s methods and conspiracies can appear heartlessly cruel in their treatment of human life, but they often serve to avoid an even greater calamity.

The Thaumaturge is believed to be the world’s best defense against the great cataclysms such as Gormadraug in Valika, the Great Beast in the Bürach Empire, and the vampires of Ostoya. Yet, it is never clear in the moment if they are trying to save lives, sacrifice lives, or both. How many must die before the way forward is found? The trouble with planning on a cosmic scale is that common human lives don’t last long enough to see the result.

### Leadership

There is no known central leader of the Thaumaturge, but conspiracies and suspicions abound. The most common are:

- Raakan el-Adakar. An archmage scholar whose research founded the basis of magical knowledge in Etharis. He’s said to have perished in Cold Iron Keep, but his body was never recovered, and several of his apprentices mysteriously disappeared after his fall. Rumors insist that he survived and is gathering allies.
- The Ravencourt Keeper. Not even his students know the true identity of the Master of the Ravencourt Sanctuary. While a common ghastly theory is that the Master is an inhuman entity that consumes some of his students as payment for power, a more likely theory is that the Sanctuary is a recruiting ground for the Thaumaturge, and that the most fitting student of every class is recruited to serve in a secret war.
- Pharazorthok. A terrifying Aether god said to resemble an enormous tree made of tentacles and countless eyes, which grows into the material plane and spies on everything that happens. A common suspicion is that this “God of Knowing” controls the Thaumaturge’s most senior members as minions.
- Martorius. The mysterious mage whose glorious castle mysteriously and quite suddenly appeared atop a mountain in the Bürach Empire. Powerful and secretive, he is certainly the most prolific suspect for the leadership position.

#### The Council Theory

There is no proof that the Order has a singular leader. It is entirely plausible that the magnitude of power and independence within the Thaumaturge ranks make singular rule impossible. The members of the Order may co-rule as a council, or even meet as a caucus, to consult and share rather than to command.

In addition to the aforementioned possible leaders, several other individuals are suspected to be involved. For many of these, the very idea that they even know each other is itself scandalous. While all these mages definitely exist in Etharis, their association with the Thaumaturge is only hinted at.

- Mina Taliesin, “Lady of Masks.” An archmage ruler of northern Ostoya and the enemy of the Crimson Court in the south. While her face is always mysteriously covered, her immense magical powers are no secret.
- Professor Franciszka Welgirson. Disembodied arcane tutor to the Bürach Emperor, conspiracy theories swirl around her and what she tells the Emperor in private. The source of her magic knowledge is also subject to juicy palace gossip.
- Odis. This elven mage from the Sarmar Academy now travels Valika, seeking out stories of Gormadraug and coldfire. While the elf seems entirely independent, his very specific area of inquiry—and his timing—suggest that he was pointed in this direction by a greater power.
- Volarius of Aloran. The “knight-sorcerer” emerged out of obscurity in Charneault to both popularity and suspicion. Claiming to act in an oath to a powerful spirit, Volarious secured a place on the Royal Council, and is unafraid to wield his influence. Jealous rumors suggest that he is a powerful foreigner who has in fact enslaved the spirit.


### Agents

Like the Ebon Syndicate, the Thaumaturge uses many pawns throughout the echelons of society to keep themselves hidden. True members of the order are immensely powerful Mages or Archmages of every stripe.

There is no strict hierarchy or list of titles in the Thaumaturge ranks. Even among its senior members, titles are chaotic, with individuals using whatever they fancy—Archmage, Thaumaturge, Grand Arcanist, Lord Sorcerer, and Witch Queen have all been used. Most commonly, however, individuals of such stature use their names rather than a title.

The most elite mages each have dramatic individual abilities and specialties. They can go beyond using the most powerful known spells, and some can control time and space themselves, and call upon cosmic powers.

#### Initiated Mages

Having been initiated into the Thaumaturge is in itself a badge of honour and recognition of magical power. Such initiates are not to be trifled with. Skilled in deception, illusions, and arcane trickery, initiates can create chaos in social and combat situations. These crafty mages can create patches of obscure areas, switch places with their enemies, and become invisible before unleashing a custom-crafted cantrip or powerful spell.

### Important Locations

There is no known central stronghold of the Thaumaturge, and it is likely that if the Order holds large meetings or councils, these take place in magically created or sealed locations.

In Etharis, there are several notable locations where mages dwell, and it is likely that at any given time, at least some Thaumaturge members are on premises.

#### The Ravencourt Sanctuary

The mysterious island off the shore of Ostoya is easy to suspect as the home of the Thaumaturge. Virtually nothing is known about it, other than it is protected by a supernatural Mistwall, and that it both produces and devours mages. If it is the home of the Thaumaturge or acts as a meeting place for them, those who visit there do so via a secret or magical means rather than by taking the ominous boat that comes every seven years.

#### Erlefurt’s Magister College

The grandest of the magical colleges of the Bürach Empire, the Magister College welcomes accomplished mages and arcane scholars from all over Etharis. More than any similar institute, the reports of secretive goings on in its ancient marble halls are legendary, leading to speculation that there is more to the school than meets the eye. Night-time access to several areas of the college grounds is forbidden, and powerful arcane protections are in place.

#### Sarmar Academy

The Charneault academy, founded as a place for elves and humans to share magical knowledge, has been a contentious venture from the start, much like the relations between the two peoples. Today—as much to protect the academy itself as anything else—membership is strictly limited, and it has gained a reputation as a place of reclusion. Despite this, it is still a notable arcane institution and a premier source of knowledge on ancient magic.

#### Castle Martorius

Like the Ravencourt Sanctuary, not much is known about the goings-on at the mysterious castle. It belongs to a mage, it appeared magically, and those who attempt to enter without permission never return. Those who have visited the castle speak of magical wonders, and that the dimensions of its interior do not match those of its exterior. If ever there was a perfect place for mages to meet in Etharis, this would be it. Whether that is actually the case depends on the whims of Martorius himself.

#### Cold Iron Keep

Nestled into the high mountains of Valika, the mage-created prison keep is not a comfortable place for a meeting. Yet, it has a certain appeal for a potential Thaumaturge council. It is distant and relatively unobserved. It has magical safeguards. Most importantly, its cells are home to several “notable individuals” whose knowledge or very nature could be of interest to the arcanely inclined.

### Quest Hooks

Characters run-ins with the Thaumaturge are certain to be mysterious, arcane in nature, and most definitely lack any confirmation that the group involved actually is the Thaumaturge.

After thwarting a mage of the order, the party is likely to run into a dead end or evidence of a vastly more powerful mage “cleaning things up.” After assisting a Thaumaturge member, these mysterious benefactors—even when they are named and known—tend to dramatically disappear, leaving a note, a reward, and few answers.

Artifact Retrieval. The characters are tasked by a Morencian noble with the retrieval of a powerful magical artifact. The object is located in a sealed ancient crypt on the grounds of an abandoned estate on a tiny island a short sail from the city. Upon arrival, the characters discover that someone else has recently arrived on the island. Is this mysterious mage a competitor for the prize or the noble’s previously sent minion who has succumbed to the crypt’s curse?

Sarmar Invitation. One or more of the spellcasters in the party receive a letter addressed to them. A mysterious patron has taken notice of their deeds and arcane skills, and inviting them to visit the famed but obscure Sarmar Academy. Travel to the distant location is complicated, requiring passage through spirit-controlled forests and knight-controlled lands. Upon arrival, the characters are offered an arcane reward in exchange for dealing with a corrupted spirit.

Ravencourt Summons. The name of one of the party members has appeared on the list of mages invited to the Ravencourt Sanctuary. Strangely, seven years have not yet passed since the last boat, nor have any of the previous mages yet returned. Among the other mages waiting at the docks is Arexa Fallora, a previous student of the Sanctuary. Arexa is sworn to secrecy about her time there, but she proclaims this summons is a malicious ruse, and that the boat is not from the sanctuary. Someone powerful is trying to abduct would-be mages, and the only way to find out who is to get on the boat.

Strange Body. General Kavalla der Voran, a famed Bürach leader, has been assassinated under the strangest circumstances. Her corpse was found in her castle, wearing a full dress uniform, but her body was entirely hollowed out from the inside, as evidenced by looking through her empty eye sockets. The murder is clearly magical in nature, but no motive or message is apparent. The characters are asked to find those responsible and stop them.

Castinellan Extraction. Grand Arcanist Valeria Vistra Ostavore’s brother, Contrall Ostavore, has been captured by the Arcanist Inquisition. During the capture, he killed four Inquisition guards, and is now slated for a very violent and public execution. Valeria offers the characters a choice of powerful magic items and access to a mysterious arcane secret in exchange for her brother’s rescue and delivery beyond Castinellan borders.

Vampiric Service. The characters are contracted by the Crimson Court. A powerful necromancer has “stolen” several notable vampires and is suspected to have fled to Castinella, intending to use them to wrack havoc. The Crimson Court is willing to help the characters track the rogue mage in exchange for returning the vampires safely to them. News of brutal murders in Castinella has already begun to spread.

Jailed Shaman. Ertakon Cad’erval, the leader of a tiny remote enclave of Valikan elves, has sought out the characters’ assistance. The enclave’s shaman, Alfreja, has been imprisoned at Cold Iron Keep. According to Erkaton, the shaman keeps shadowy monsters away from their enclave, and by extension nearby villages, by absorbing them into herself and slowly suffocating them. Occasionally, the monsters break loose, which is what led to the inciting incident of her capture. Erkaton begs the characters to treat with Clan Mithra and find a way to return the shaman.

Shadowsteel Experimentation. Jurnalha Stohl, a dwarven ironsmith from Stehlenwald, asks the characters to investigate a mine, as a shipment of ore she ordered hasn't arrived. On their way there, the characters meet a veiled figure who tells them to kill everyone they find in the mine—for they are contaminated with fiendish power from their work with the Shadowsteel that is secretly mined there.

When the characters arrive, everything seems normal. The miners show no sign of anything being wrong, even under magical scrutiny. The mining captain says the shipment is late because they struck a rich vein of ore, and they wanted to excavate it before sending the shipment.

In reality, these miners are impostors working for the Cult of Tormach. They recently invaded, killing or imprisoning the real mining crew, who were indeed suffering madness and mutation from working with the virulent ore. The characters can uncover the ruse, deal with the ore thieves, and find the captive miners who are being changed by the ore.


## Watchers of the Faithful

Though the gods no longer grace the heavens, the Arch Seraphs still guide the way. Born in the fires of the Gods’ End, the Watchers of the Faithful rose to prominence first in Castinella as part of its theocratic government. Priests, paladins, chaplains, and other clergy rallied to the remaining Arch Seraphs to hold the fabric of society together. Throwing their faith behind the Arch Seraphs, the major churches reformed into a large organization dedicated to preserving the old faiths in a new form. Suzanne Helmigh

This young faction is utterly devoted to Sagrado Valieda’s Eternal Dogma: revere the Arch Seraphs, follow their laws as handed down by the Divine Speaker, and destroy all that is unholy and evil in their sight through any means necessary. Where the Arcanist Inquisition is militant in rooting out corruption, the Watchers instead pursue Valieda’s mandate to fight for the Arch Seraphs with fiery words. Swords are well and good in defense of the faith, but the real battle is in the hearts and minds of the faithful.

Through preaching and persuasion, the Watchers seek to make the change in the world that they believe the Arch Seraphs desire. Edicts declaring heresy inflame the Inquisition to hunt for monsters or tainted spellcasters. A public proclamation that a government is straying from the true path causes citizens to rise up and force change. Decrees are issued creating new ecclesiastical rules that the faithful embrace over secular laws issued by less religious rulers. Slowly but surely, the Watchers guide the world to a better place.

### History

After Sagrado Valieda unified the Castinellan Provinces, he formed a military theocracy devoted to Empyreus, placing the High Priest of the Sword Saint in charge of the military and creating the Arcanist Inquisition to control and limit the use of magic in the provinces. The Unifier was a brilliant tactician and made the military strong, but it was clear that while the faithful of Empyreus had the fire and verve to rule a new nation, they were ill-suited to some portions of the ecclesiastical bureaucracy. Fortunately, the clergy of the other Arch Seraphs were ready to help.

The Unifier asked the other High Priests of each Arch Seraph to come together and form a covenant that would become the Watchers of the Faithful. From among the High Priests of each Arch Seraph, they would elect the Divine Speaker who would be first among equals. It was no surprise that Sagrado Valieda was chosen to lead them. Spreading the Message Thus, the Watchers of the Faithful began, integrated into the Castinella theocratic government almost by default, and driven to spread to other nations by the Gods’ End. The Gods’ End legitimized the Watchers’ control of the government and furthered their outreach to the faithful in other nations, particularly the Bürach Empire. The Watchers of the Faithful ordered its missionaries to preach the light of the Arch Seraphs directly to the people of all nations, denounce corrupt clergy, speak out against sacrilegious laws and the rulers that make them, and condemn the exploitation of the poor. To the shock of those in power, the medaling of the Watcher’s evangelists found support among their citizens. Thousands flocked to the Watchers, with many going on pilgrimages to far-flung areas to spread the word of righteousness through diplomacy or warfare. Money continues to flow from these pilgrims into the coffers of the Grand Temple. Gonzalo Vega Watchful High Priest

Quickly the Watchers of the Faithful have become known as champions of the people, and to foreign powers, they are far more dangerous than the Arcanist Inquisition. The Watchers’ evangelists use faith to motivate followers, making the faction more and more powerful as it gathers legions of people to its side. The Arcanist Inquisition might attack a certain settlement accused of heresy or dangerous arcana. The Watchers’ watchful priests can enrage an entire regional populace into acts of religious fervor and violence. This is the power that keeps the leaders of other countries awake at night.

#### External Relations

The Watchers of the Faithful are found in all the nations of Etharis. They have tentative alliances with the Hearthkeepers in the Bürach Empire, and the Chapter Knights of Charneault, but the Watchers’ unyielding doctrine makes such alliances terse at best. The Watchers send missionaries to Valika to convert those hardy folk away from the Primordials and into worship of the Arch Seraphs. The faction detests the undying Crimson Court in Ostoya, but their relationship with the Magocracy of Raevo is tense due to the province’s embrace of the arcane, so these natural allies are denied them.

The Watchers have complicated relations with other factions as well. The Watchers incense the Arcanist Inquisition, their only true allies, to undertake various holy missions across Etharis to stamp out all that is, in Cirdanal’s words, “corrupt, heretical, iniquitous, or displeasing to the eyes of Heaven.” The Watchers pursue the Augustine Trading Company for its efforts at weaponizing monsters, the Ebon Syndicate for its corruption, the Ostoyan nobility for falling to vampirism, and the Valikan tribes for raiding, worshipping the elements, and more raiding. They save a special hatred for the arcanists of the Thaumaturge. They claim it’s because their magic spawns abominations and tempts others to heresy, but for some sects, it is their competition for the knowledge and ability to guide the world that is the Thaumaturge’s true sin. Any information about the Thaumaturge is quickly passed on to the Inquisition with the Divine Speaker’s compliments.

### Goals

The six different religious sects publicly agree to the three unifying principles of Sagrado Valieda’s Eternal Dogma, and internally, to a fourth goal that they see as the eventual outgrowth of their successful guiding hand.

#### Revere the Arch Seraphs

Place your faith in the Arch Seraphs and they will hold you in their hands, protect you from harm, and raise you to the light.

The Watchers of the Faithful are first and foremost, a religious organization. The constant trials the people of Etharis face can only be overcome through the grace of the Arch Seraphs. The most important facet of the Watchers’ holy mission is the conversion of as many people as possible to the faith of the Arch Seraphs. The faithful can then be protected and guided to a new golden age.

#### Follow the Sacred Laws

Sin and corruption are tools of Arch Daemons and their heretics lead the faithful unto chaos.

The Arch Seraphs have a plan, a plan that is whispered to the High Priests of the Watchers of the Faithful.

Each High Priest imparts the sacred laws as received from their Arch Seraph to the rest of the conclave and from there to all the faithful. These laws tend to be broad but unyielding; honor the priests, uplift the downtrodden, make war on the Arch Seraphs’ enemies, avenge heresies against the Arch Seraphs, suffer not the warlock to live, find purity in being of service, or eat not when thy neighbor is hungry.

#### Destroy All that is Unholy

Suffer not the Unholy, for they hate the blessings of the Arch Seraphs and would cause you harm.

While the Watchers of the Faithful disdain those who stray, they seethe with hatred for true monsters. Their followers are commanded to hunt any creatures that challenge the power of the Arch Seraphs and the blessed order, such as aberrations, daemons, fey, primordials, or undead. Some sects interpret the command to hunt the unholy more liberally and include heretics, anyone whose form is being corrupted by a transformation, criminals, or even magical artifacts and books that the sect believes to be blasphemous.

The Watchers of the Faithful scorn Arcane and Primal spellcasters, but they rarely hunt spellcasters unless their actions are otherwise profane. The faction’s purpose is broader than the Arcanist Inquisition. Information about such casters is passed on to the Inquisition if they are deemed a threat.

#### Theocratic Supremacy

From the whispers of the Arch Seraphs to the High Priest’s words shall blossom a holy kingdom of the Light.

While not shared publicly, the Watchers’ leadership has a fourth goal: the subjugation of the world’s governments through organized faith. By spreading faith in the Arch Seraphs, the Watchers of the Faithful hope to unify the common folk, and when that unified people rise up, they can and will force political change with new governments taking direction from the Divine Speaker and the High Priests.


### Leadership

Matheus Graef Nola Cirdanal The Watchers of the Faithful are split into six sects, each following the teachings of a specific Arch Seraph. Each elects its own High Priest, who is said to commune directly with the Arch Seraph of their sect. Then they collectively elect a Divine Speaker from one sect to lead the faction. For such a sacred institution, the conclave to elect a Divine Speaker is a surprisingly political affair involving the trading of arguments, favors, and threats as each High Priest tries to prove they have the support of the faction’s members, and the Arch Seraphs themselves. While the Divine Speaker can theoretically come from any sect, in the faction’s history, there have only been two that were not followers of Empyreus. Once for Zabriel, and once for Solyma, and neither held the seat for more than a few years before it was reclaimed by an Empyreusian. Day to day decisions are made by the Divine Speaker with the advice and guidance of the Council of High Priests. When the Council must decide the direction of the faction or there is a disagreement over policy, the six High Priests vote after a period of negotiation. Ties always go to the side the Divine Speaker voted with.

#### Nola Cirdanal

The current Divine Speaker is Nola Cirdanal, who is of course a follower of Empyreus. She is a strong, middle-aged Castinellan, who prizes courage and persistence. She favors deploying large numbers of evangelists to spread the Light of the Arch Seraphs to all peoples of Etharis, regardless of the wishes of those people’s rulers. Those rulers that complain loudest tend to find their realm plagued by the loudest firebrands the Watchers can field, accompanied by the most heavily armed guards. Her uncompromising manner has put the Watchers on a violent path against those nations and factions she believes violate the Eternal Dogma.

#### Other Leaders

The other current High Priests are:

- Audres D’Xeo (Aphaelon). The High Priest of the Arch Seraph of Temperance supports a more tranquil approach than the faction currently employs, and she finds herself, and her sect, often at odds with the current Divine Speaker.
- Carma Aruejo (Morael). The High Priest of the Arch Seraph of Sacrifice worries about the collateral effects of their faction’s aggressive evangelism, while at the same time supporting the heroes who do it. When the Council votes, her vote is often unpredictable and is easily swayed.
- Lawirsen Kheshev (Zabriel). The High Priest of the Arch Seraph of Truth is quiet when it comes to political maneuvering, but controls a network of spies and librarians that gather information to aid the faction’s missionaries in their work. He is a tacit supporter of the current Divine Speaker.
- Srokkush (Solyma). The dragonborn High Priest of the Arch Seraph of Justice believes the faction should take an even more militant role in rousing the common folk to burn heretics, criminals, and those who truck with eldritch abominations. He is a strong supporter of the current Divine Speaker.
- Velten Giessers (Miklas). The High Priest of the Arch Seraph of Mercy sends missionaries to heal the sick and care for those in distress. For the common folk, priests of Miklas are a welcome sight, and win many converts, making Velten perhaps the second most powerful High Priest after the Divine Speaker, and a natural rival. Most disagreements on the Council start between the two of them.

#### The Arcanist Inquisition

The Watchers of the Faithful and the Arcanist Inquisition have a special relationship. The Inquisition views the Watchers as clergy worthy of respect, but not capable of doing the direct, violent work the Arch Seraphs require. The Inquisition can rely on divine spellcasting support and a steady flow of reports from the Watchers.

In turn, the Watchers see the Inquisition as a blunt instrument that can be turned against enemies of the faith; even easier to manipulate than the common folk. By passing along reports of “heretics” from their missionaries in the field, the Watchers regularly induce the Inquisition to eliminate those who stand in the Watcher’s way.

The two factions find their alliance is mutually beneficial, but rarely coordinate their actions; each trying to use the other when it suits their purposes.

### Agents

They spur faithful populations to turn against those they deem heretical. They are not single heroes, but rather the voice behind the zealous mob. The most common missionaries are Watchful Priests who combine divine spells with an almost magical command of oratory to enflame crowds.

### Important Locations

The Watchers of the Faithful maintain their seat of power in the Grand Temple of Toletum. This massive cathedral forms a starburst shape with the main nave and central chapel devoted to Empyreus and smaller chapels radiating off the center. The glories of the Arch Seraphs are depicted in statuary and murals, religious splendor rarely seen elsewhere in Castinella. Each chapel displays a sacred relic, though they are only replicas and the true relics are well-hidden below. Rumors say that behind the beautiful walls lies a secret passage. The rumors are wrong, there are several, and also many holes that Watchers use listen to the whispers of those within its walls.

High above the central chapel rises a six-sided tower topped by the Shining Chamber where the Divine Speaker and the Council of High Priests meet around a hexagonal table. The leader of each sect sits beneath a massive stained-glass window, depicting the Arch Seraph that they follow.

The grandeur above ground is but a small piece of the whole structure. The Watchers have constructed several levels beneath the Grand Temple in secret. These passages allow the Watchers’ agents to come and go without being observed, and access the secret passages and whisper holes within the Grand Temple itself.

In the levels below, the Watchers maintain a vast scriptorium and a library to collate all the information that flows from their agents. On the bottom level, the holy relics of each sect also rest in separate, private shrines in hidden chambers. Each sect claims that their relic possesses great power, and its touch bestows power upon the most deserving believers. Only Zabriel’s sect is telling the truth. The Tome of Saint Lysimachus grants knowledge to those who would use it in the fight against evil. Others granted the chance to lay hands upon a relic are always “found wanting” by the Arch Seraphs.

Elsewhere, the Watchers of the Faithful make their homes in churches, traveling shrines, and all places of faith. Stationary Watchers rarely make their allegiance known, reporting back to the Grand Temple while pretending to be simple clerics of their Arch Seraph. These churches usually have hidden spaces where friendly agents might hide or private conversations might occur.

To better perform their duties, wandering evangelists wear patches depicting the symbol of the Watchers and travel with wagons called capillarante, that unfold into shrines. These rolling chapels contain all manner of banners, rugs, candles, and relics designed to impress the common people. It is not uncommon for the wagons to also carry food, coin, and cudgels that might help to spark a riot, should one be needed.

### Quest Hooks

Statues in the Cathedral

Most quests involving the Watchers of the Faithful are in some way wrapped up with religion, though not always to the glory of the Arch Seraphs. All too often the mortal leaders of the faction make political decisions to undermine their rivals or enrage a mob against a secular ruler. Completing or foiling these missions are best suited for characters skilled in oratory or stealth.

Civil Unrest. The populace of Adleheim has been roused against a local lord. Lady Mina Oberlinder is accused of heresy and the worship of Malikir. The townsfolk burn effigies and block all goods from entering the keep where she hides. The characters are asked to uncover the source of the accusation and deescalate the situation…preferably without slaughtering citizens. The origin is a Watchful Priest named Alect Brightson sent by the Watchers after Lady Oberlinder expelled the local cleric of Empyreus for being a faction spy.

Northern Captive. Valikan Raiders following Unnal Kellsdottir have captured a Watchful Priest named Vasesclaav in a raid on a seaside village and are trying to ransom the priest back. After the raid, the village can no longer afford the ransom, and the characters are needed to rescue the priest. When the characters find the Valikan camp, it is larger than expected, and Vasesclaav refuses to leave. From his prison cage he has been trying to convert the Valikans away from their paganistic Primordial religion to worship of the Sword Saint. He has at least one convert, and he is betting his life that he will win over many more.

Relic Recovery. Trusted members of the faction are tasked with recovering a holy relic of Empyreus, a magic pauldron said to have been dropped by the Sword Saint himself that blesses the worthy to be almost invulnerable to daemonic attacks. The relic is held in a monastery of blasphemous vampire “priests” in Ostoya that have caged dozens of innocent mortals. The Divine Speaker is desperate to recover this relic so that her sect might have a true relic like the sect of Zabriel, and she is willing to overlook whatever the characters might have to do to recover the relic; even leaving innocents behind.

Recruit the Downcast. A downcast name Rezzaeil has been seen living hermitically in the foothills, and the Watchers would like the downcast to join their faction. They call on the characters to travel into the rough countryside in the Bürach Empire to find the downcast. Unfortunately for the characters, Rezzaeil once fought for the Arch Daemon Tormach, but an alliance has been made even more unlikely because the downcast has heard the call of the Great Beast. The creature has shattered his mind and twisted his body with strange mutations. Once the characters are in the hills, the thing he has become starts hunting them for sport.

Village Hysteria. A forest village called Clarnesse is in a religious uproar and witch hunt, searching for werewolves and fey warlocks living among them. A Watchful Priest named Jahnfelix Pröll has created an environment of mass hysteria where fear, false accusations, and personal grudges are destroying the village. While hunting for the heretical abominations, it quickly becomes clear that those sent to the flames by their neighbors were innocents. Indeed, Jahnfelix has been corrupted by the worship of Venin, the Great Deceiver, and the characters will have to turn the people of Clarnesse against the priest and his guards before they burn down an entire village in search of heretics.

Priestly Escort. A Watchful Priest named Elissan asks for a safe escort across a foreign land (such as Valika or Ostoya) so she can preach and convert the locals. The characters are asked to act as her bodyguards throughout the journey, both in settlements and through the wilderness. If successful, and they demonstrate their faith in the Arch Seraphs to Elissan, she will speak for them should they choose to join the Watchers of the Faithful.

Relic’s Origin. A fire beneath the Grand Temple of Toletum was so inexplicably hot that it cracked the stones of the altar that houses the relic of Solyma. High Priest Srokkush did not truly believe that his sect’s relic held any power, but the relic was completely untouched by the fire. Is it blessed by the Lady of Vengeance? Not yet willing to trust the characters in the underhalls, he sends them on a mission to uncover the true origin of the relic: a red beaded scarf called the Torremuria, said to have been worn by a simple Morencian midwife who slew an abomination in defense of an infant. The trail leads to a path of lies planted by the fiend Zuurag, the Oathbreaker, as part of a plan to corrupt the Grand Temple from within. Once the plot is uncovered, the High Priest further requests the characters sneak into the Grand Temple and steal the shawl so it can be destroyed with any shame falling upon the sect for harboring a corrupted artifact.


## Arcanist Inquisition

Arcane magic is a scourge on all of Etharis. No mortal can be trusted to practice it without becoming corrupted.

Klyde Sosa Anastasia Komissarova Arcanist Inquisitor The Arcanist Inquisition is more than a religious order, it represents the core philosophy at the heart of the Castinellan Provinces. The Inquisition was founded by “the Unifier” Montego Valieda, after bringing peace to the warring provinces. Valieda claimed to have communed with Empyreus, the Arch Seraph of Valour, who told Valieda that arcane magic was a curse endangering its wielders and the greater world. Immediately after unification, he established a new order that would limit and control the use of magic across Castinella. Upon establishing the Arcanist Inquisition, Montego took the name Sagrado, as a symbol of transformation and devotion to the seraph. He set upon fulfilling the seraph’s mandate of cleansing the curse of arcane magic from the provinces. To this end, he initiated his most fervent followers as Inquisitors. These “messengers of the seraph” travelled far and wide. They not only preached the dangers of magic but also identified and began to arrest those suspected of being magically gifted. The Inquisition was not initially violent, and the arrested were intended to live in humble conditions, avoid arcane practices, and dedicate themselves to the seraph’s service—whether they wanted to or not. The Inquisitors sought to identify the gift at the youngest possible age, claiming it would be easier to contain. The rise of dark magic cults and monster attacks throughout Etharis only empowered the Inquisition’s dogmatic teachings. It also helped that in all matters outside of the arcanely gifted, the Inquisitors sought peace, justice, and valour.

They sought to embody moral purity, and their clearcut mindset was easy to embrace for those unwilling to wrestle with more complex truths.

With the Inquisition set upon its path, Sagrado Valieda traveled to the Shattered Peaks to bring the message of the Inquisition to the dragonborn. Finding them amenable to the Arch Seraph’s teachings, Valieda sealed their support, and the Inquisition’s hold on power, by offering them an opportunity to return to Castinella and rebuild their ancestral city of Ember Cairn. Many dragonborn embraced the Inquisition’s cause and returned. This has proven to be a prosperous arrangement, and today, the dragonborn of Ember Cairn are some of the most zealous proponents of the Inquisition’s dogma.

Although it had been Valieda’s intention that future leaders would take his title when he passed on, out of respect for his legacy, no other leader has taken the name Sagrada. The tradition to take a transformative name remained, but more recently, the title of Lord Inquisitor has become more prominent.

Ember Cairn became the heart of the Inquisition, and the massive Ember Cathedral—which the dragonborn constructed at Valieda’s behest—its headquarters.

The cathedral is a wonder of architecture and engineering. Constructed in a massive hollowed-out rock formation, it combines an impressive visage with a vast underground complex that includes everything from Inquisitor quarters to prison cells. It is said that the underground complex is so massive and its paths so twisted that only the Inquisitors can find their way.

The ruling seat of the Inquisition is located at the cathedral, and currently belongs to Lord Inquisitor Crevax, an imposing seven-foot-tall dragonborn. Crevax is zealous, uncompromising, and entirely committed to the teachings of the Seraph.

Over the years, and more than ever under Crevax’s reign, the treatment of the Inquisition’s prisoners has shifted from monastic-like confinement to cruel imprisonment. There are those within the Inquisition who believe it is losing its way, but they often find themselves afraid to speak their minds, for fear of having the tables turned on them. After all, it takes merely a word or suspicion to get thrown into the Cathedral’s maze.


### Agents

The Inquisition works as much on propaganda as on actual deeds, and its agents are rarely hidden. The very mention of the Inquisition can strike fear into the populace, and they use this reaction to their benefit. Servants of the Inquisition proudly wear the Inquisition’s emblem, which they get inscribed onto as many items as possible. Unless they are part of an Inquisitor’s retinue, lowest rank fighters, who are barely more than thugs, typically don’t have uniforms.

Inquisitor-level agents, including priests, knights, warders, and judges, tend to have both symbols and a uniform. Uniforms are determined by the individual chapter of the faction.

Grim Hollow: The Monster Grimoire contains the statistics and tips on using several Arcanist Inquisitor agents. Alternatively, consider leveraging Cultist, Knight, or Priest statblocks.

### Quest Hooks

Even when it’s not the direct villain, the Arcanist Inquisition should feel like an oppressive presence for the player characters. In situations when they work for the Inquisition or collaborate with them, there should always be an air of doubt regarding whether they have done the right thing.

Hunting Mages. Shortly after the characters arrive in a smaller Castinellan settlement, they learn of the arrival of a group of Inquisitors, who are hunting mages. The Inquisitors immediately take notice of the party, whether or not there are mages among them. The characters must either escape or defeat this branch of the Inquisition, but many locals are on the Inquisitors’ side and ready to turn on them.

Catching Thieves. Agents of the church seek help tracking down a band of thieves who’ve made off with a priceless relic. The band was aided by a mage, who can be tracked to the city of Camont. However, the Inquisition has set their own trap, as they look to capture both the thieves and any mages in the group sent to recover the relic.

Ally’s Execution. While on an unrelated quest, the party is given assistance or shelter by the village’s journeyman mage. Later, they learn that the mage has been arrested by the Inquisition and is being taken to the nearest city for a public execution. Time is short and the characters wishing to save the mage must either ambush the Inquisitors en-route or attempt a daring rescue in the city.

Herbalist’s Arrest. While visiting the shop of a “magic herbalist,” the characters are interrupted by the Arcanist Inquisition, who have come to arrest the herbalist for taking part in a dark conspiracy to destroy the city. The herbalist proclaims his innocence and attempts to gain the party’s favor. In truth, the herbalist is one of four mages in the city who are readying a ritual to summon an Aether Kindred. The Inquisition has only heard rumors of the conspiracy and are unaware of the greater plan. The herbalist uses anti-Inquisition sentiment to fool the characters. The characters must either work with the Inquisition to uncover the sinister plot, or, if they side with the herbalist, find themselves unwitting enablers of dark magic and must reap the consequences.

Child Healer. Seeking healing or a curse remedy, the characters arrive at a small village which has recently become famous for a child who is said to have performed healing miracles in the name of the Arch Seraph. Upon arrival, they learn that the miracle child is missing, and her parents are desperate to find her. The arrival of an Inquisitor further complicates the matter. Newly-promoted and a true believer in the Seraph’s word, Ghodelia Vasca, has been sent by her corrupt master Kodolus to judge the girl for blasphemy and ensure she is silenced.


## Crimson Court

I have slept since the world was young. Yet now that I’m awake, all I feel is hunger.

The Crimson Court is the ruling body of Ostoya, consisting of the seven most powerful vampire nobles in Ostoya. The Court governs through fear and command of vast wealth and resources, demanding complete obedience of their servants in return for protection from the undead and the Bürach Empire. Formed after the Darkfall, the Crimson Court consisted of Ostoyan nobility transformed by the undead plague released from the City Below. The vampiric curse proved crucial in turning the tide in the Second Bürach-Ostoyan war, and as such, the citizenry remains submissive to their leaders. For the commonfolk, donation to the blood tax is a fate far preferred to death by banshee’s wail, or even worse—the attention of the Court itself. Currently, the Court is ruled by Grand Duke Drago Koshevek. The Grand Duke is capable of executing political schemes and battlefield tactics in equal measure, though fear is his greatest weapon of choice. As the most ancient and powerful among the Court, all bow to the Grand Duke’s whims, lest they feel his wrath. His obsession with defeating the Bürach Empire has made him unpopular with the other members of the Court, though none dare make their opinions known. The other six members each rule a province of Ostoya, given relative autonomy and control over their vassals and holdings as long as they serve Grand Duke’s schemes. Behind closed doors, all members and lesser nobles engage in subterfuge, manipulation, and assassination to wrest power from one another and become leader of the Crimson Court. Even the most politically savvy nobles discover they are both player and pawn. Klyde Sosa Daniel Alessi Vampire Noble

Some Court members gather for lavish masquerades or fight together in blood-soaked battles against invaders. Others may actively avoid fellow members for fear of treachery. The Court is equally likely to negotiate and bargain with each other as they are to conspire against each other for their own aims.

Each Court member resides in their own estate or keep within their province. The location of the residence is publicly known to the commonfolk, but only the foolish arrive uninvited, lest they become the target of traps or unwitting participants in a noble’s depraved interests. On rare instances, the Grand Duke summons all Court members to his fortress stronghold of Blackharbour in Nov Ostoya. Invitations to such meetings often herald a change of Court membership.


### Agents

Each member of the Crimson Court controls many lieutenants, including minor nobles spawned from the ruling vampire. Soman Vampire Berserkers, Nobles, and Sangromancers are detailed Grim Hollow: The Monster Grimoire, or use any standard Vampire stat blocks to represent these creatures.

Each vampire is responsible for some aspect of governance and is, in turn charged with leading servants further down the feudal pyramid. The Court employs many mortals, some who serve out of genuine loyalty, others out of fear, and still others in the hopes of being deemed worthy of becoming vampires themselves.

The Crimson Court happily uses lesser undead to inflict terror or as disposable soldiers when needed. Indeed, it has become a grotesque game (presumably started by Viscount Warlow) for each Court member to decorate their undead in garish armor and grafted limbs to see who can strike the most fear in an enemy. Such undead are usually captured by mercenaries or unlucky serfs.

Ostoyan Vampires despise other lords of undead, such as liches, who they view as abhorrent imitations of themselves. Though they may employ other entities from within the City Below, such undead are never raised to the level of the Crimson Court. Nevertheless, these monsters possess ancient power and knowledge, highly sought after among the Court, even if the vampires do not always grasp the implications of accepting such aid.

### Quest Hooks

The Court welcomes well-meaning adventurers into Ostoya, regarding them as easily manipulated bodies to unwittingly carry out schemes. Indeed, it’s a favored pastime of some Court members to ‘compete’ for the number of mercenaries in their employ to demonstrate their power and influence over their rivals. Especially successful agents may rise to become prominent players in Ostoyan politics, potentially influencing the outcome of plots.

Delving Below. Baron Mihael the Wolf sends the party deep into the City Below to retrieve an ancient artifact rumored to empower the undead. However, neither the Grand Duke, nor the other Court members, are to be aware of this mission. While underground, the party comes across rival mercenaries hired by Countess Katalina Velpescu to retrieve the same artifact. These mercenaries are willing to work with your party to increase the chances of finding the artifact, but the question of who will claim it remains unanswered.

Undead Turmoil. Viscount Langkirk Warlow sends the party to a village in Estpine that is under threat by an undead creature that’s a mystery even to the vampire nobles. This new undead is a creation by Sacha, a secluded necromancer hoping to create a weapon to protect his village without paying the Blood Tax to the Court. The creature shakes free of Augustin’s control and begins to wreak havoc on villagers and vampires alike.

On Behalf of the Court. The Grand Duke sends the party on a diplomatic mission to Liesech to establish communication with the city’s leader, the vampire Analita von Raiza. The Grand Duke hopes to convince von Raiza to sabotage Bürach supply lines in return for membership on the Court. If the other Court members learn of this agreement, they may not welcome the idea of another rival to compete against.

Masked Intent. Marchioness Iulia Cutov hires the party to attend an extravagant masquerade held by Countess Vasinesca with the intention of assassinating her. However, your patron’s involvement cannot be made known, or your party will incur the wrath of at least two Court members.

Thirst for Power. A failed assassination attempt on a member of the Crimson Court spurs the Court to hire the party to track down who is responsible and make them an example to any who would threaten the Court’s hold over Ostoya. But what does the party do when investigations point towards another member of the Court?

Desperate Measures. To retain her seat at the Court, the disgraced Viscountess Zeilgor employs the party to establish diplomatic relations with Raevo. She has discovered that one of Raevan wizards is conspiring with one of her vampire lieutenants, Lord Gabren Arlov. Lord Arlov has been sharing court secrets and knowledge from the City Below to fuel Raevo’s defenses in return for the province bending the knee to Lord Arlov once he becomes a member of the Crimson Court. The party needs to identify the traitor and convince Raevo of the deceit.

Expanded Domain. The Grand Duke instructs your party to escort an arcane weapon discovered in the City Below to the front lines of the war against the Bürach Empire. The party learns that the weapon consumes the souls of vampires and mortals alike within range, fueling the machine and expanding the domain of the Darkfall. If news of this weapon reaches the other members of the Court, the Grand Duke’s position will be threatened.


## Monster Hunter Guilds

Hire the knight if you want him to die bravely. Hire me if you want the job done.

Klyde Sosa Matt DeMino Monster Hunter Trapper Guilds of monster hunters first came to be when hunters with shared methods gathered for support and to share knowledge. It is from this tradition that hunters still use “guild” to refer to the organization they belong to and their hunting methodology. The Carver Guild was once an organization of only carvers. Today, with members spread far and wide, carvers are present in many guilds. For example, the Landhaus Hawks once had at least four carvers on their roster, but the guild was founded by Hendretta Voland, an occultist. What unifies all monster hunters is the practice of studying their quarry’s nature and approaching the hunt with knowledge, even when it is applied through force. The often undocumented history of monster hunting makes the origins of its varied disciplines hard to pinpoint. The oldest recorded tale of a carver defeating a monster by learning of its weaknesses comes from the Valikan Saga of Ostra Cragstalker. The trappers trace their beginnings to two separate accounts—one originates with the dwarves of Stehlenwald using an ingenious contraption to contain a massive troll, while the other tells of Charneault knights who were forced into trapping when their sworn spirit demanded that they forcefully relocate rather than kill their enemies. Mage-hunting occultists are often associated with Castinella given the region’s tradition of arcane persecution. However, due to their own arcane dabbling, the relationship with the Arcane Inquisition remains volatile and unpredictable. Despite the many horrific folk tales born of disgust and persecution, the devourers have no widely believed origin. While their teachings refer to the wisdom of past devourers, there isn’t one who is considered the first. Because of the thematic connection with vampirism, devourers are suspected to have come from Ostoya. However, they aren’t any more prominent there than in other areas of Etharis, and substantial evidence suggests they predate the Darkfall.

Today, smaller method-specific guilds still exist, and the methodologies remain distinct enough that carvers or trappers likely have more in common with each other than with other monster hunters of the area. This is partly because most hunters train through apprenticeship, with an apprentice learning from an experienced monster hunter.

It should be noted that because their members are often shunned by others, devourer guilds—when they exist—usually consist entirely of devourers. These guilds are very private.

Larger guilds now rival the smaller mercenary companies. However, unlike mercenary companies, there are very few monster hunter guild halls. Some of the bigger guilds do have their hall or lodge in a city, but most use a castle or other distant and well-secured location. Many monster hunters and smaller guilds travel extensively and have no permanent home. All monster hunters are trained to work individually, accepting some individual commissions, even when part of a guild. They often join adventuring groups or mercenary outfits to complete specific hunts.


### Agents

Most monster hunters are independent and rarely follow a rigid organization. Their numbers are also small, allowing the more accomplished hunters to be known by name. Many guilds have leaders chosen by a council, with infighting for leadership roles being extremely rare.

One notable leader is Weaver Faloenea, a Dreamer who leads The Nightshield, arguably Altenheim’s most prominent monster hunting guild. Faloenea is a powerful occultist whose divination powers are renowned. It is said that she can reach into a monster's dreams to learn their true nature. Many have beseeched Faloenea to use her powers to learn the true nature of the Great Beast, but she has refused each time.

### Quest Hooks

The characters are likely to run into Monster Hunters when seeking information, competing for or collaborating on a hunt, or rarely as a stalking enemy.

Apprentice Rescue. Attillah Kregg, a retired monster well hunter living in a small village, asks the characters to find Raffus Thorn his experienced apprentice. Raffus did not return from his most recent hunt. Given the monster he believes Raffus was hunting, Atillah is hopefull his apprentice is still alive, but likely not for long. The old man offers to reward the characters with a magic weapon from his hunting days if they return with Raffus.

Hunted. The party finds themselves targets of a monster hunting guild, because one of them has a transformation or was working with a monstrous ally, such as a Warlock patron or the Crimson Court. If it comes to violence, the monster hunters have done their homework and are able to neutralize some of the party’s greatest strengths.

Noble Commission. Wealthy Bürachar Duchess Katrina Vishovska offers a massive bounty to whoever brings her the heads of a monster pack terrorizing one of the villages in her domain. Among those who seek to claim the bounty are a monster hunting guild. The party can either try to beat the hunters to the prize or work together and split the reward.

Guild Misdirection. The characters are hired to investigate a dramatic assassination. The investigation leads to the local monster hunter guild, who claim that they are also seeking the murderer and offer to assist. In truth, the guild has begun taking humanoid contracts and was behind the murder. The guild points them to investigate a remote location, where an ambush awaits.



## Morbus Doctore

No knowledge should be feared, because knowledge is power.

Klyde Sosa Klaher Baklaher Master Plague Doctor The Morbus Doctore appeared shortly after the release of the Weeping Pox in Liesech, cataloguing its symptoms and trying to treat the infected with a combination of alchemy and medical science. Sadly, they were rarely successful, but rumors abound that sometimes, they might be the only chance the infirm have. The Morbus Doctore are most often found treating the infected of Liesech, though they do travel to other major cities. They are easy to spot with their boots and gloves stained with dried blood, strange coats and satchels hung with vials, and frightful masks meant to protect them from disease. The medical and scientific knowledge held by the Morbus Doctore is staggering. While caring for those afflicted with the Weeping Pox and other illnesses, its physicians use these opportunities to examine the living and the dead. Their blend of arcane magic and science has given rise to unusual, but sometimes successful, treatments of dubious morality. The arrival of a Morbus Doctore is both a blessing and a curse. Their presence means disease is present, with misery not far behind, whether from illness or treatment. No breakthrough comes without suffering, and a physician must become inured to patients’ anguish. Some who’ve seen the Doctore work claim that they revel in every death. They torture their “patients.” They perform cruel experiments with no regard for their charges: grafting skin, extracting marrow, and injecting tinctures of exotic origin. Then, they observe with fascination, scribbling notes in the name of science. The most insidious rumor suggests they were even responsible for Weeping Pox's initial spread. Members regularly share insights with each other, so no valuable knowledge is lost or hoarded, but they do not share their research outside of their cabal. Information flows along through the faction’s pyramid organizational structure, where each level of agents accepts reports from those below and forwards them to those above, preventing members from identifying any but those directly above or below them. At the top of the faction sit the Trustees.

The five Trustees of the Morbus Doctore are shrouded in mystery. Unknown to the public, no Doctore has seen beneath their plague masks or knows their identities. Stories say that when the Weeping Pox came to Liesech, the Trustees were the first to treat the sick. The horrors they witnessed and failed to prevent created a manic obsession to understand the disease that they have since spread to others.

In those rare moments when the Trustees must speak, it is the Attending who does so. From the emotionless, rasping voice that echoes within his mask, it is believed the Attending is male and probably elderly. The Attending delivers pronouncements with the cold logic expected of the rest of the faction. “This subject must die so others might live. No knowledge is evil. The experiments must continue.”


### Agents

The Morbus Doctore have an insular, hierarchical structure with its members knowing only one or two members above them and only a handful below them. Members use secret titles for their ranks that are not used with non-members.

At the base of the faction are Adjuncts and Orderlies. While most Morbus Doctore hide their identities behind plague masks, the faction relies on these less mysterious members to interact with the public. Internally called Adjuncts, local doctors and nurses interact with a skittish public, boosting trust in the faction. In return, local physicians get the benefit of the the Morbus Doctore's medical expertise. Hired muscle, apothecaries, chemists, or others who don’t work directly with the public are called Orderlies. These members complete the many necessary tasks that the doctors cannot be bothered with, or risk doing.

The average faction member is called a Resident. They are apprenticed to a more experienced Fellow, who cultivates a regional network of Adjuncts and Orderlies that interact with the public, gather information, run errands, and serve as muscle when needed. A typical Resident is Plague Doctor whose statblock can be found in the Monster Grimoire. Alternatively, a Noble augmented with healing potions or a Priest armed with arcane magic can be used.

Underneath the Trustees are the Fellows. Each Fellow generally has one or two apprentice Residents. Fellows pursue their own ends, nominally in the service of the faction, but with wide discretion as to their methods and goals. Their approaches frequently filter down through the organization they supervise, coloring the public’s opinion of the Morbus Doctore in a neighborhood, city, or region. A typical Fellow is a Master Plague Doctor (also found in The Monster Grimoire ), or a Mage who can cast some healing spells.

### Quest Hooks

Adventurers, whether Orderlies or not, are needed to take risks members are not equipped to handle. Characters might be tasked with defending doctors from angry mobs or acquiring rare ingredients for treatments from distant locales.

With so much grief surrounding them, accusations of evildoing are inevitable—and sometimes well-founded. Not every member is without a moral center, but individuals often overstep their moral and ethical bounds more frequently than they should. While it’s unlikely the Trustees would ever question the research practices of its members, the characters might be called upon by those outside the faction to investigate the demise of an otherwise healthy patient or the spread of disease wherever a Morbus Doctore goes.

Dangerous Patient. The Morbus Doctore seek to rescue an escaped patient named Yannick, who should be in quarantine. Yannick is hiding in the city. If found, he claims the Doctore infected him and resists efforts to return him. He is clearly still contagious, and his current hiding spot endangers infecting nearby innocents.

New Treatment. The Morbus Doctore offers an experimental treatment to an afflicted party member, such as a cure to the Weeping Pox. The treatment works, but also inflicts strange side effects such as mutations, a craving for sentient flesh, a drug dependency, or even an Aberrant Horror transformation.

Change of Heart. Teodo Harlindt is a halfling resident who has begun to question his role as a member of the Morbus Doctore. He sincerely wants to help others and knows that not all treatments are pleasant, but he questions the methods of his fellow Doctore. He wants out of the organization, hoping to take his library of medical knowledge with him, but someone has discovered his intentions. He needs help to make it out alive.

Medical Caravan. The Morbus Doctore seeks help transporting one of their caravans across the country. Strange illnesses occur in every village visited by the caravan. Carolina DiBello, a nurse in the caravan, is a survivor of several diseases but was supposedly cured. The cure is not entirely effective, and while she is no longer sick, she has become a carrier, spreading sickness everywhere she goes. Worse, the Doctore know this, and are using the opportunity to run tests on both Carolina and the ones she infects.

Village Outbreak. An entire village that was supposedly inoculated against disease has been infected by the Weeping Pox. Despite their best efforts, the Morbus Doctore seem unable to stop the outbreak. The physicians seek help quarantining the village while they work, but the horrifying truth soon becomes clear. A clandestine branch of the Morbus Doctore is spreading the disease to test the efficacy of their new inoculation versus a placebo.


## Order of Dawn

The scourge of undeath will disintegrate before the cleansing light of the dawn.

Ostoyans who refused the rule of the Crimson Court fled to the northern province of Raevo. There, they settled in a fort outside of Castalore and formed the Order of Dawn, a militia committed to the destruction of the undead and the liberation of Ostoya. The Order of Dawn adheres to Ostoyan traditions with roots long before the Darkfall, revering the Arch Seraph Solyma, Lady of Vengeance. As such, the Order’s hatred of the undead is fueled by pious anger, giving no quarter to their enemies. Members are trained from childhood to hunt and destroy the undead. Captured undead are studied and dissected so that their weaknesses can be committed to memory and whispered as prayers before and after rest. Every waking hour is devoted to the pursuit and end of the Crimson Court and all undead that roam Ostoya. The Order is led by General Artemios Dulka, a Soman noble who fled the vampire curse and is obsessed with destroying the Crimson Court. Dulka is driven by the dream of an Ostoya without the Darkfall, without undead, and without the Crimson Court. The mere sight of a vampire within his vicinity triggers this typically benevolent leader into a vengeance-filled fervor. Similar to their leader, the Order possesses admirable intentions but achieves them through methodical, merciless, and zealous means. Graves are unearthed and corpses burned to prevent undead rising. The Order has a high tolerance for collateral damage as long as the target is destroyed in the process. As such, Ostoyans outside of Raevo have mixed opinions on the group, regarding their presence as trouble for not just undead, but also for those serving them, no matter how begrudgingly. There is little room for compromise among the Order. Klyde Sosa Olha Nykytiuk Order of Dawn Sentinel

While primarily based in Raevo, the Order has repurposed a few abandoned forts and wrecked structures on the fringes of Ostoya into strongholds. The Order particularly favors the rehabilitation of ruined churches, regarding the restoration as symbolic to their mission. From these strongholds, the Order systematically cleanses the nearby area of undead influence, ensuring that every village is clear before pushing their boundaries further out. The process requires unending vigilance.

Safe houses deep within Crimson Court controlled provinces provide refuge for their spies and assassins while also providing a platform to recruit new members. These safehouses shift frequently as a form of pragmatism mixed with paranoia. As enemies of the Crimson Court, the Order of Dawn is no stranger to deceit and betrayal.

Despite their size, the Order of Dawn retains an air of superiority and self-righteousness when dealing with others. Even Raevan mages, who welcomed the first members of the Order into their lands, are considered faithless for turning to the arcane arts for protection rather than the traditional teachings of Solyma. However, once one joins the ranks of the Order, they can expect steadfast loyalty and comradery.


### Agents

The Order of Dawn maintains a militaristic structure where piety and obedience are ingrained into its members from an early age. After basic training, members are assigned to one of three divisions, each committed to a different approach to cleansing Ostoya of undead. Each division is represented by a symbol related to Solyma which is tattooed onto the skin of its members. The mark contains blessed inks that prevent the member from ever being resurrected.

Few in number, members are either organized into small strike teams to carry out precise mission objectives or embedded into Raevan battalions to bolster larger armies that anticipate confrontation with undead.

Sentinels are Knights trained for direct confrontation against undead forces. They have a vast knowledge of all weakness points of every type of undead and train in squadron formations and battlefield tactics to best counter their enemies. The symbol of the sentinels is a glowing greatsword.

Stalkers are Spies trained to seek out undead, using the unending darkness against their enemies. For clandestine operations involving high-profile targets, elite stalkers bestowed with the blessing of Solyma are sent to deal with the target immediately. Elite stalkers use the Assassin stat block, dealing radiant damage instead of poison damage on relevant attacks. Stalkers are marked with four rays of light in the shape of a dagger.

Cleansers are Priests trained to deal with the incorporeal, equipped with banishing and protection spells. Within their fortifications, Cleansers provide spiritual guidance to the Order. While on missions, Cleansers are responsible for purifying corruption. Cleansers are marked with a golden pattern around their lips, symbolizing the vow of silence of their Seraph.

### Quest Hooks

The Order is slow to accept help from those outside their organization. However, when their pragmatism outweighs their piety, they will hire the assistance of mercenaries or monster hunters. This occurs most often when dealing with particularly dangerous foes, when the Order is outnumbered, or when they wish to keep their involvement hidden. Even then, the Order is slow to divulge any information considered non-essential, concerned that if an outsider can be enticed with gold, their loyalties may also be bought by their enemies. Any reverence of Solyma. respect for the traditions of Ostoya, or proven deeds against the undead are sure to soften dealings with the Order.

No Mercy for Undeath. The Order of Dawn has been pursuing a minor vampire noble, driving the noble to seek protection in a small village and charming the inhabitants. Rather than risk the vampire escaping, General Dulka has instructed the Order to burn the entire village down without warning the locals. This is despite the vampire’s attempts at bargaining for her life.

To the East. An injured Raevan mage on the road comes to the party, begging for aid. A nearby Order of Dawn fortress that was once a mining outpost in the Grey Spine is under siege from a Soman army. The party must break the siege or those inside will perish. The mage suggests making use of abandoned tunnels in the mountains that lead to the fortress as a means of reaching the Order.

Cursed Existence. The Order have captured a dhampir progeny of Grand Duke Koshevek. Friends of the prisoner plea for General Dulka’s mercy, insisting that the dhampir is innocent of any wrongdoing. The Order is fervent in dealing with those marked by the sanguine curse, and Dulka will likely command the execution of the dhampir. That is unless he can be convinced otherwise, or the prisoner somehow escapes.

Dawn in Darkness. The party has been hired by the Order to assassinate one of the members of the Crimson Court. Upon reaching one of the Order’s safehouses in the province, the party realizes that their mission is simply a diversion for the Order to conduct their true assassination attempt.

Push back the Darkness. The party are hired by one of the strongholds to cleanse a village and nearby woods of undeath. However, the village is protected by a benevolent ghost claiming to be one of General Dulka’s slaughtered family members and possesses crucial information regarding Crimson Court activity.


## Prismatic Circle

Only blood and sacrifice can keep the Prismatic Wyrm slumbering, sparing the world from its apocalyptic wrath.

Matt DeMino Grenhildr Most folk of Etharis view Valika as a barbaric land built on blood and malice. A good deal of this reputation is owed to the Prismatic Circle, who stoke a hunger for blood and violence among the clans of Thrull. The Circle embraces this fear, ensuring the Valikan Clans over which they hold sway remain dedicated to violence, reaving, and sacrifice in the name of ancient prophecy. The Prismatic Circle is an ancient religious order that existed long before the founding of Etharis’s modern nations. It is not a faith devoted to a divine ideal or godly salvation, instead, it is driven by the fear of what will happen should they fail to appease a sleeping Primordial’s thirst for bloodshed. The Circle was founded by the elemental druid Kybard, whose long-ago visions decreed that the Great Prismatic Wyrm Gormadraug had not been slain by the Seven Heroes of legend. Instead, the Prismatic Serpent still slept beneath earth and sea, and its reawakening would herald the end of the world. Kybard preached that the north must embrace the old ways of battle, fury, and sacrifice lest Gormadraug be roused by the lack of blood. Since then, the Circle’s Great Druids have driven the clans of Thrull to pursue their traditions of raiding and violence. This is the core tenet of the Circle—only through the constant supply of bloody sacrifices can the end of the world be forestalled As part of this tradition, the druids make regular pilgrimages to the Sentinel Woods. Dozens of these widely scattered oak groves rise from the endless landscape of rock, grass, and lichen, where the Circle’s dark rites allow them to survive. Here, the Circle’s druids and squads of allied Völgr warriors drive captured livestock and hosts of people for sacrifice. The condemned consist of criminals, captives stolen from southern lands, and those who’ve crossed the Grand Druid. Sometimes, when the ranks are thin, unfortunate folk of lesser clans are added to this march to doom.

Over several excruciating days, the druids oversee the slaughter of animals and people. Many victims are painfully flensed, with the druids using their primal magic to enhance the suffering. Once the last victim falls, the druids and warriors quickly depart, for none may linger in any Sentinel Wood between times of sacrifice. The dead are left to rot or be scavenged by animals until nothing is left but scattered bones.

The frequency of these horrid rites has increased since the scourge of coldfire reappeared. The Circle saw its return as a sign that the Great Prismatic Wyrm was beginning to wake. Only a renewed focus on the rituals of violence and blood could stop the foretold return.

The clans of Kandar in Eastern Valika oppose the Circle. This has fostered conflict between the western and eastern clans, which has escalated from necessary and acceptable raiding between clans to outright warfare. Outright opposition to the Circle’s control of the Thrull clans is rare for fear of being doomed to die in the Sentinel Woods as retribution.

Nonetheless, some groups do openly oppose the order. The Cult of the Great Wyrm, led by the druid Grenhildr, who was once the protégé of the current Great Druid. A near-death experience on the winter tundra changed her views. Instead of fearing Gormadraug’s return, she seeks it, believing the primordial will prevent the destruction of clan culture across the North. Her heretical views have brought a death sentence, but thanks to strange magic, she escaped the Order and defeated many sent against her.

Guhjdäl the Revenant was a druid from the eastern tribes who was captured by Völgr raiders and marked for sacrifice. As a Lightbringer, she sought to preserve all life, and she believed that by doing so, they could save the world. Incensed by her beliefs, the Circle’s druids were exceptionally cruel when they killed her. Days later, Guhjdäl awoke, returned to life with renewed power strong enough to cast Resurrection spells. In the years since, she has raised many who were sacrificed by the Circle, clandestinely supported by small clans who supply the gems needed for her powerful magic. Many of those saved join the growing ranks of her Revenant followers, seeking to ultimately build an army strong enough to break the Prismatic Circle’s power in Thrull.


### Agents

The Prismatic Circle is led by the Grand Druid Hjadana. She is the spiritual leader of the three clans of Thrull in Western Valika, effectively making the region a theocracy under her control.

Though the Circle’s spiritual direction strongly influences Clan decisions, it has never held true political power. Their might lies in the fealty of the clans—and on the fervor Hjadana has stirred in the warriors of Clan Völgr, who serve as the Circle’s military might. The Raider’s Guide to Valika includes stat blocks for Raider Veteran, Raider Lieutenant, and Raider Captain; otherwise, Berserkers and Warriors are good representations.

Hjadana has fostered this relationship by growing close to Chief Nolgr Magnusson. The clan’s seat lies at Tyburn on the isle of Holgar. It is here where Kybard first declared that Gormadraug still slumbered, and it is here where the Prismatic Circle’s sanctum is built. A living fortress of dense trees rises defiantly from the rocky ground. It is within where the druids of the Circle engage in their secret rites.

Formally, the Prismatic Circle is a communal organization, where the Grand Druid would consult with members for guidance on doctrine and interpret signs of Gormadraug’s return. However, Hjadana has ruled without challenge since her ascendance two decades ago. No one dares to openly criticize her rule—and those who do so privately understand that they put their lives in peril.

The Raider’s Guide to Valika, offers stat blocks for the druids of the Circle, including the Prismatic Circle Druid, Prismatic Circle Shaman, and Prismatic Circle Spy. Alternatively, its members can be represented using standard Druids, Cultists, or Spies.

### Quest Hooks

The Prismatic Circle has a tight control over most of what occurs in Valika’s Thrull region. They have agents in most larger villages and a network of fervent druids across the north who monitor goings on. The Circle is interested in protecting Thrull from all threats and are happy to use others to complete dangerous tasks. They keep watch for anything that could challenge their control of the Clans.

Rescue Captives. Several villagers are captured by Valikan raiders and taken north. While searching for the villagers, the characters must contend with Prismatic Circle druids who are preparing to take the captives to one of the Sentinel Woods to be sacrificed.

Primordial Artifact. The characters are approached by Heimir, a druid from Valika. He tells the party the story of an ancient Primordial artifact hidden in the frozen wastes of Valika. If they return the artifact to him, he promises they’ll be rewarded by the Circle. Heimir is actually allied with the Circle of the Great Wyrm and intends to use the artifact to invoke a powerful primordial to devastate a small village that is loyal to the Prismatic Circle.

Village Defense. While visiting a Kandar village, the characters help defend against a Thrull raid, with druids of the Prismatic Circle among the attacking forces. Afterwards, the party learns that the druids were looking to assassinate an important villager who was secretly a warlock of Gormadraug. The warlock is no longer in the village, having fled into the wilderness during the battle.

Coldfire Threat. A remote village is threatened by coldfire. A nearby Prismatic Circle druid has a stash of ice-oil, a rare substance that is highly effective in combating coldfire. The druid is willing to part with his supply, but only if the characters provide him bring him a villager who spoke against the Circle.
