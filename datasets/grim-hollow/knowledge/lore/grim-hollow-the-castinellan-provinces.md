# The Castinellan Provinces

Castinella is a peninsula of harsh climates and even harsher laws. The provinces of Toletum, Faro, and Therpena have been scuffling with each other for centuries. But the power of the great houses has given way to a new political entity that has reshaped and unified the peninsula under one banner: the Arcanist Inquisition, a zealous theocratic military organization.

The Inquisition has made the law of the church the law of the land. Arcane magic has been made illegal, and its practitioners are subject to unpleasant fates, the least barbaric of which is death. There is less to fear from bandits and monsters in the well-patrolled peninsula. But there is far more to fear from the people in systems of power.

The state religion of Castinella serves the Seraphs, with a particular focus on the Arch Seraph Empyreus. Many dragonborn have left their nomadic voyages and returned to their ancestral home of Ember Cairn to take up worship of the Seraphs.

The face of Castinella presents order and safety, but this is a mask that hides untold horrors. From the Hidden Labyrinth under Ember Cairn to the black markets of Toletum to the House Wars raging among the nobility, there are as many secrets in this region as any other; they are simply better kept. Deep shadows are cast by the bright light of the Inquisition. Those who care to look see more danger than they could hope to find from the wildest forests and the deepest caves.

## Geography

Castinella is a huge peninsula with a mountain range running down its center. In the north, they are called the Corova Mountains and are famous for the wealth of resources mined there, which drive Toletum’s ever-working forges. In the south, particularly around Ember Cairn, the range is known as the Shattered Peaks, their irregular forms seemingly broken by some cataclysmic event. Historians credit some of Castinella’s particularly poignant fear of magic to an event thought to have ‘shattered’ the earth and forced it into the air. Magic is dangerous everywhere, but there is a special fear of it in Castinella—an old fear buried deep from the ancient families’ graves to their descendants of today.

The Corova River flows down from the north, parallel to the mountain range on its western side. In northern Castinella, closest to the continent, the wide plains give way to jutting hills, where the multileveled city of Toletum resides on the slope leading down to the river.

Halfway down the peninsula, the Corova River splits, with both diversions eventually joining the Gulf of Lions. The space between the river’s branches belongs to the city of Faro. The Farosi enjoy a bountiful climate, with fertile farmland and tall forests filled with pines and aspen groves.

In southern Castinella, with wide coastal beaches, plains, and forested mountains below the Shattered Peaks, lies the province of Therpena. This province is filled with sunny hills and thriving vineyards.

### Dry Climate

From high-altitude pine forests to the densely jungled seashores to the scorching hills, the Castinellan Provinces are home to many different biomes, all characterized by a scarcity of freshwater. The forests are littered with lichen-covered volcanic rock, but there is never any moss to be found growing on trees. Snow gathers on the tops of mountains and flows down in streams that rarely last through the summer.

With little rainfall, farming must be done more creatively in the Castinellan Provinces than in other areas. Fields require irrigation to produce a yield in the dry climate. Farms cluster around the few major rivers the provinces possess. Ditches zig-zag down mountains between farmsteads, delivering water runoff to every landowner within an area. Communities must work together to ensure that everyone gets enough water to grow their crops every season.

With the flatlands starting at sea level, none of the mountains reach high enough to breach the alpine line, where the climate becomes too cold for trees to grow. However, snow does accumulate on the mountaintops, and their frozen peaks can be seen from much of the scorching shoreline. Vineyards run from the green plains and climb over the tall hills below the mountains, and orchards grow on steeply sloping hills held in the peaks’ shadows.

#### Deserts

On the north side of the mountains are flat deserts of low-lying trees and brush. Rocky mesas with wind-hewn stone columns break through the desert floor. While sand is prevalent, there are few dunes. Though they don’t look like it at first, these deserts are teeming with life. Lizards, insects, and foxes make their homes in the desert. Deer and elk cross the desert on their way to greener forests.

The lack of water means that organic material does not decompose and rot like it would in wetter climates. Instead, they slowly desiccate from exposure to wind and sand. Branches litter the desert floor, pale and brittle from years of dessication. Skeletons stick around for vast stretches of time, picked clean by animals and bleached white by the sun.

#### Wildfires

Wildfires have been suppressed in the Castinellan Provinces for the last hundred years. Fuel, in the form of sticks and fallen trees, litter the ground. When a fire does occur, it often becomes a catastrophic crown fire, destroying every tree within an area. The trees that grow back all grow from the same age, clustering close together, forming overgrown forests with even higher competition for water, more prone to burning again.

Some scholars say the suppression of the wildfires mimic the mishandling of the arcane—wild magic catastrophes are more common because of the suppression of all magic. These scholars rarely speak for long. They often end up taking extended vacations outside of the provinces. Others have simply disappeared.

In more practical steps, ranchers have taken to moving cattle through the forest in order to eat the dangerous ground fuel in the woods. This practice has become more widespread in Faro, where concerns over deforestation have prompted many circles to inquire about the preservation of the natural world.

### Civilized and Wild

The steep slopes and harsh climate have left much of the peninsula uninhabited for many years. The kingdom tightened security on the roads to make them some of the safest in the world, but just beyond the heavily patrolled roads are untamed lands full of wonder and danger. Sleeping drakes and hiding ogres can lurk mere miles from the roadside—not to mention the arcanists, gone to ground, camping out in the dense evergreens.

#### Architecture

Appearances matter in the Castinellan Provinces. Each province has a distinct architectural style that has developed over centuries of diverging culture and infrastructure.

As religious fervor on the peninsula grew, so did the cathedrals. A competitive spirit, indistinguishable from spiritual insanity, drove the three provinces to build taller and more elaborate cathedrals, each trying to prove their own superior worship of the Arch Seraphs.

The Cathedral of Embers, where the Inquisition resides, stands above all. An awkward pairing of public community center and theocratic military headquarters, the cathedral is open to the public but guarded closely and severely. Visitors often feel as though they are being watched. The clergy credit this feeling to the Arch Seraphs themselves, when really inquisitors with crossbows stand guard, and secret watchers with hard eyes discretely spy on the crowds.

Statues of Seraphs can be found on every street corner in the major cities. It is said that in a time of great need, when magic shakes the foundation of the earth, every Seraph statue on the peninsula will awaken and leap to guard the true believers from harm. The status of that statement has gone back and forth between religious canon and abject blasphemy more than once.

#### Ancient Ruins

Ember Cairn is not the only ancient structure left standing in the Castinellan Provinces. Ruins litter the deserts and high mountains of the peninsula. Granite standing stones dot the paths of old established roads. Desert canyons hide tall stone towers tilted to one cliff edge. Old stone bridges, larger than anything that could be constructed now, connect otherwise uncrossable canyons. Pine forests on the ridges under the Shattered Peaks hide fragmented structures that dig miles into the stone. These colossal towers remain half buried within the mountain chain.

Contrasting with the elaborate buttresses and spires of the modern architectural aesthetic, the dragonborn constructions are simple and elegant. Built on mathematical principles of simple geometry, they can be as stately as they are grand.

Many of these old temples have enormous skylights and clerestories, perhaps large enough to have once bid the come and go of true dragons through the temples.

## History

The history of Castinella in some ways reflects the history of Etharis itself: struggle and triumph leading to what appears to be stability, with the true darkness lurking just beneath the surface—and far too often peeking through.

### Unification

The provinces have vacillated between ages of war and peace. The Arcanist Inquisision has forged a tenuous alliance—a devoutly theocratic military force dedicated to the eradication of magic from the peninsula and beyond.

The Inquisition formed around a conqueror-prophet named Valieda. He claimed a vision of Empyreus supplied him with the mandate to form and carry out the goals of the Inquisition. The so-called ‘band of heroes’ gained popularity by keeping towns safe, and they eventually amassed support and resources from the Toletum noble families and the common folk as well.

The growing movement now known as the Arcane Inquisition managed to convince each town and city, one by one, to swear their eternal fealty to the church, and under the watchful eye of the Inquisition. Most towns and cities accepted their terms readily, longing as they did for a reprieve from the constant danger of rampant magic—or fearing the supposed danger posed by the stories of what magic-wielding maniacs might do.

Those who resisted the Inquisition struggled to garner enough support from their own community to contest them. Though few common folks had been hurt by magic, they believed the stories of its powers—real or imagined. Even more were directly helped in their time of need by Valieda’s group before it became the Inquisition. And so the movement continued to grow.

The church accepted ties to the Inquisition and endorsed their cause, providing funds and political support. Since worship of the gods and their Arch Seraph messengers was already the most common religion in Castinella, the Inquisition was readily accepted by most.

Even now, many townsfolk have only known life under the watchful eyes of the Inquisition, and they claim they prefer life with the Inquisition in charge. The roads are safer, which means that merchants and entertainers come to town more often. The steady flow of soldiers brings constant demand for food and supplies to local markets. And, of course, there is always someone to call on when magic reaches your door and befouls your life.

What began as well-intentioned groups of holy warriors and superstitious zealots who traveled from town to town dealing with local issues has evolved into something systematic and deadly. Monstrosities are hunted down to protect local populus. Evil sorcerers were brought low by divine magic. Innocent mages who could not control their magic were captured dutifully, solemnly.

But when the Arcanist Inquisition unified the peninsula, Valieda decreed that all arcanists should be hunted down and put to death. Captured arcanists would no longer be safely contained. They would be executed instead, regardless of their crimes, and regardless of their ability to control their magic. These deaths were seen as sad, solemn, and ultimately necessary for the betterment of all.

#### Shadows Behind the Throne

The state religion stringently follows the Arch Seraph Empyreus, with whom the founder of the provinces claims to have communicated directly. A few who question the motives and tools of the Inquisition claim that his tales are simply false, but others accept darker interpretations of his stories.

Some believe that the Unifier did speak with an otherworldly being in the Shattered Peaks, but that it was not Empyreus. Dark whispers delivered with bated breath suggest that it was the Arch Daemon Beleth who sent the Unifier on his path.

There are no sure truths behind these rumors. But Beleth is believed to take great pleasure in toying with priests and holy workers. More than one account claims that within every monster-ridden mine, every entrance to the labyrinth, there is the faint sound of something less than human, something laughing.

The clergy renounce these rumors, of course, and even the utterance of Beleth’s name has been outlawed—a crime punishable by the slitting of the perpetrator’s diaphragm, so that they may never laugh again and draw the attention of the humor-loving Arch Daemon.

#### Torture

Belief that arcane magic can be hidden in plain sight until it’s too late has propelled the clergy to justify the use of torture as a means of revealing hidden magical talent. But what was once a last resort, occasionally excused in the name of the greater good, has become a common convenience, or even a revered ritual.

Torture has been used by the Inquisition for decades, but following the recently declared purge of all mages, it has taken on a stranger, more reverent form. Instead of dark dungeons far from prying eyes, many tortures are carried out in churches, with masses gathered around. Hymns are sung as the torment “cleanses” the captive miscreants of their sins.

Many tortures are still performed privately, and most of the more horrifying torture methods devised by the Inquisition are not widely known. One such device has recently been introduced to the public, however. The Penitent Mage, a hollow statue filled with nails, large enough to swing open and fit a person inside. Once shut, the metal mechanism is suspended over a fire, and screams are channeled through the statue’s open mouth along with the steam and smoke.

In Toletum, these devices are built within amphitheaters designed to cast sound all the way down the city streets. As workers go about their business, they can often hear the din of mages screaming. Many are cowed by fear of the disturbing ritual punishment, but not everyone. Rather, the fear of magic prevalent in Castinella, and the demonization of those who wield it, grant many workers the ability to bear the brutal sounds with a smile, knowing deep in their hearts that with every scream, their world is a little bit safer.


#### House Conflicts

The noble houses of Castinella constantly compete for power. Sometimes this economic and political competition turns bloody. The clergy suppresses some—while claiming to suppress all—of the secret wars between the houses. But a well-placed war between two powerful houses can end up diminishing both, and the clergy has an interest in keeping any one house from rivaling the supremacy of the Church.

Some house wars are allowed; some may have even been instigated by the clergy themselves. Discreet mercenaries are sometimes hired by great houses or the Church itself to deliberately take out chosen members of rival families. Nobles disappear from their beds, ships are mysteriously lost at sea, and the finely honed order imposed by the Inquisition makes every odd occurrence seem too small to bring up, too dangerous to discuss.

In Toletum, those wars are typically crushed by the Inquisition. Assassins and spies are weeded out, hopefully before they’ve done what they have been paid to do. But in Faro, the clergy often turns a blind eye to the practices of each small house, even stoking the fire if one house becomes too powerful. Faro’s resistance and continuing practice of magic has left a lasting bad taste in the mouth of the Inquisition.

In Therpena, the clergy allows the great house of Therentine to enjoy a greater influence than the other noble houses. Therentine was the first to submit fully to the Inquisition, and their strong ties to the Church mean their working partnership is generous and mostly devoid of suspicion.

### Life as an Arcanist

Some arcanists lead normal, if fearful and often secluded, lives. Being magically gifted is a secret one would struggle to reveal even to a close friend, family member, or partner. Families often disown children showing signs of magical talent. The Inquisition does not treat the families of arcanists well, especially if their magic may come from a specific bloodline. The Inquisition has license to capture individuals for carrying magical blood within them, even if they themselves are not gifted. Children are locked away by their parents, never allowed outside for fear that their magic is discovered. These situations often end with magical outbursts that leave the child and their family injured, or worse.

Families successful in hiding their children’s magical talents often do so by raising them as a normal child, while teaching them the importance of concealing their magic. However, these families are burdened with the fear of being caught, and panic may tear them apart before the Inquisition can do so. There do exist, of course, well-adjusted families who handle their secrets with grace, though these are rare. In some places, families secretly share tutors or networks that assist in teaching the arts of magical control. These networks thrive for a while, but too often the secret leaks, and the Inquisition is quick to strike.



#### Curses

People cursed by powerful magic are often shunned and treated with mistrust; nowhere but the Castinellan provinces, however, makes it illegal to exist in such a manner. People cursed by magic, especially Shadowsteel curses, cannot openly search for a cure. The only cure supported by the Inquisition is a swift death. While the execution of a cursed person is handled more solemnly and swiftly than for practitioners of the arcane, the ending fate is the same. Practitioners of magic understand that some curses can be managed or even eliminated through the use of magic. The leadership of the Inquisition, however, posits that the soul of a cursed person has already been tainted, and the use of more magic—even to eliminate a curse—only adds to the corruption of the victim’s soul. A black market has been established in dealing with cures, as well as more dangerous and questionable magic. Some cures are fakes. Others are sold at monstrous costs by greedy magic-merchants who know how high a price people will pay for a cure while hiding knowledge the curse. But some healers on the black market are honest do-gooders who want to see people get better, often performing treatments at little or no cost. #### Transformations

Torture that ends in death or banishment to the labyrinth are just two of the punishments for magic use. Another transgression, perhaps even worse than having magical talent or being unfortunately cursed, is undergoing a transformation. Transformations are incredibly rare, and very few people are mad or desperate enough to undergo a Transformation on purpose.

Any Transformation that results in a physical change—which is most of them—is punishable by immediate death: no evidence, testimony, trial, or ceremony needed. Vampires, lycanthropes, aberrant horrors, hags, fey, and the rest are shown even less empathy or mercy than spellcasters—and those folks get practically none.

A few individuals have been spared of the Inquisition's wrath and swift “justice.” They were lucky not just despite their Transformation, but because of it: the Seraphs. When an individual undergoes the Seraph Transformation in Castinella, its signs are taken as a gift rather than a curse. There is much debate about whether this Transformation is truly holy. A conclave of high-ranking Inquisition members convenes to study the individual to ascertain if the gifts are truly holy, or a sign of corruption by arcane magic.

Powerful mages sometimes attempt to exploit this one avenue of acceptance. Whether cynically or through self-delusion, these mages search for arcane-sourced paths to divinity. Generally, their ruses, or their true attempts to become one with the divine, are detected by the Inquisition. Death is the common end to those stories.

Arcanists seeking the divine, or hoping to fool their persecutors into thinking they are divine, dabble with the magic that leads to lichdom. Equally earnest, yet more gullible, arcanists have been lured in by Daemons disguised as Seraphs, tricked into accepting a fiendish contract, and transformed into fiends themselves.

#### Monster Hunters

In Castinella, monster hunters are relied upon, though with little enthusiasm, by the Castinellan bureaucracy. To regular citizens, monster hunters are blessed warriors, heroes who keep back the dark without the need for dangerous magic. To the clergy, however, they are a troublesome necessity—possibly even magicians masquerading as do-gooders. Rumors run rampant that some monster hunters are really sorcerers or druids who have managed to hide their powers.

Servants of the Arch Seraphs reluctantly supply monster hunters with coin and other resources, even as they disparage their pursuit of occult knowledge. Even learning about magic is frowned upon in the Castinellan Provinces, but understanding their prey is key to a monster hunter’s survival.

Some monster hunters are held directly within the Inquisition’s employment. These individuals are surveilled heavily, but they are some of the only tradesmen in Castinella permitted to study—but never practice—magic. This has led to the creation of a new sector of the Inquisition known as the Order of Templars. These Inquisition-backed monster hunters study the theory of magic in order to fight against it. Members of this sect are treated with both awe and suspicion by other members of the Inquisition, and with outright distrust from most regular members of the clergy.

Templars are mostly Occultist Guild monster hunters tasked with fighting non-humanoid monsters that wield arcane magic. The Inquisition is exploring the opportunity to make arcanists legally indistinguishable from wild beasts. The definition of a being without a will of its own could, in theory, be stretched to include the uncontrollable surges of wild magic sorcerers.

More than once the Church has exerted pressure for the Inquisition to drop these members of its rectory, or even imprison them for being tainted with the stain of arcane magic. The Inquisition, however, holds that the study of magic is necessary for some of their soldiers in order for it to be properly fought against. This is one of the few points of friction in the otherwise united leadership of the Inquisition and the Church.

As a compromise, the School of the Dread Templar, the only college where magical books are kept for study, is in close proximity to the Cathedral of Embers. Such heretical volumes are not allowed inside the cathedral, but the clergy want the school to be close at hand to make surveillance easier. Clergy members are appointed to positions within the school so that they can keep an eye on these Occultist Templars who risk each and every day to succumbing to the arcane corruption.

Members of the clergy in these positions are often treated differently by the church, even if it was the church who sent them there. There is a belief among the clergy that being around such blasphemous study could contaminate a priest’s faith. It is difficult to continue climbing the ranks after working for the school, so many of the clergy working there resent their positions.

#### Vilified Communion with Nature

The aspen groves speak languages that druids and rangers can understand. But speaking to plants or animals is a sign of arcane magic, and is absolutely outlawed by the Inquisition. A fear of the magic of nature, and in some ways of nature itself, has nestled deep within the public consciousness.

Nature itself is demonized. The beauty of it is called seductive, and the power of it is called unfathomable. The Church equates primal magic, the magic inherent in nature, with the corrupting arcane magic they are sworn to oppose.

The despoiling of the land, through enterprises like logging campaigns and earth-churning mining operations, are often categorized as divine interventions on the earth’s nature-infested surface. That these endeavors are extremely profitable to the noble families is certainly just a happy coincidence, and the Church gains massive donations from these efforts.

Most people do not ascribe to the belief though, and an outright hatred of nature belongs only to the most radicalized of the Seraph worshipers. Those who make their livings by performing tasks that bring them close to nature understand both its power and its beneficence, and some of the most fervent anti-Church activists are farmers, hunters, foresters, and those who rely on the gifts of nature.

#### Wine and Festivals

Castinellan people work hard and play harder. For all their preached formality and prudishness, the Church allows and even funds many traveling troupes. At least four times a year, festival days with plenty of wine, dancing, singing, and audacious performances are sponsored by the Church throughout the Castinellan Provinces.

The Church and their noble sponsors support so many roving troupes that even the small towns see a professional performance at least twice a year, and often more. These troupes wear the colors of their patron houses, and they spread the good word about both the Church and their noble patrons.

The performances of these troupes are approved by the Church, and new content is welcome only if it extols the many virtues and altruisms of the Church, the Inquisition, the noble families, and Castinella in general. Many troupes acquiesce to this, although sometimes unsanctioned material is performed—often to the public-facing outrage but the secret delight of the audience.

Bards themselves are tolerated as long as they show no signs of magical abilities—that is, they hide their arcane knowledge when performing, or disguise it so well as to be indistinguishable from an amazing performance. The Inquisition barely tolerates those who study magic to fight magic; they certainly do not accept magic used for entertainment.

### Faro

The House of Faro does not exist anymore. Founded ages ago by Farolithia, a merchant who settled the valuable land, Faro was a force to be reckoned with, a fertile land coupled with an incredibly defensible position, with the cliffs on one side and the sea on another. Founded by a woman with common blood, the family entered the nobility with fierce ambition and strong ties to the people.

When the Inquisition rose to power, Faro resisted, unwilling to part with its power. The Inquisition launched a siege on the city for twelve days. It seemed that Faro would prevail and that the Inquisition would break itself on the cliff city’s formidable walls and angry soldiers fighting for their home.

On the twelfth day of the siege, Faro soldiers began slaughtering each other atop the walls. Madness moved like a plague through the army, leaving the city panicked and defenseless. It is said that the Faro family retreated into a secure cavern in the cliff behind their palace, but they were never heard from or seen again. The heavy stone doors to the cavern have been closed since, and no one is allowed in or out.

Claiming that the heretical tendencies of the Faro house have left their residency tainted with dark magic, the Inquisition has kept the palace guarded and closed off since the siege. It has been said that soldiers guarding the stone door sometimes hear weeping from the other side. This is attributed to the stress of too much time alone in the empty palace.

#### A Dark Center

The outskirts of Faro thrive. A strong global market meets at its harbor, and its fields produce a bounty every year, but the state of things sours the closer you get to the city itself, especially to the palace. The Inquisition never forgave Faro for its resistance, claiming that Faro was the last official place of magical malfeasance to be snuffed out on the peninsula.

The Inquisition rules Faro with an iron hand and exerts painful control over its citizens. People turn their heads downward and avoid eye contact when walking the streets. Festivals are often somber, with heavy tones of religious indoctrination. Bards and minstrel troupes know better than the trifle with any content that could be construed as heretical when performing in Faro. High taxes offset the city’s bountiful trade and high-yield agriculture. More executions take place in Faro than in any other province. Whispers of rebellion have only increased their frequency.

#### The White Fleet

The mood changes as one walks down from the cliff city to the harbor docks. Faro is a massive center of trade, with one of the most powerful fleets in Etharis. A sea of pristine white sails greets a traveler’s eyes when they see the harbor span before them.

The White Fleet, as the navy of Castinella is known, ensures that maritime traffic along the trade lanes and coasts near Castinella are safe from pirates, Valikan longships on raiding expeditions, and predation of other nations or powers. The fleet has hundreds of ships that dock just long enough to take on new crews and get repairs, and then they are back out to the high seas.

The Inquisition is vigilant in the area of the docks, ensuring that no arcane threats enter the region through the port. Occasionally, a few members of the Inquisition sail with a crew heading toward a dangerous territory of potential arcane threats. However, the sailors of the White Fleet, while paying lip service to the tenets of the Church, are happy to be away from the prying eyes and accusing stares of the Inquisition.

#### Noble Houses

The collapse of the House of Faro left a power vacuum in the city, which has yet to be filled by any one noble house. Many small houses continue to vie for influence, but the most powerful force is the Inquisition, which maintains a tight grip on the core logistical and political facilities in the city.

The most wealthy and powerful houses since the fall of House Faro, have competed relentlessly to gain an upper hand in the Faro economy and become the one house that can offer enough to the Church to be given dominion.

Many have come close, only to be brought low. Far too often, when it became clear that a house was about to make the business moves necessary to dominate, the smaller houses would cooperate to bring them down. This cooperation showed itself in combining resources to prevent monopolies. Just as often, however, it was carried out through less peaceful means. Assassination of powerful house leaders in Faro might be considered natural causes when it comes to death.

The most powerful houses in Faro today are listed below.

Heverna

The Heverna family is a large clan of shipwrights. They engineer and construct some of the largest and most pristine ships in Faro. While there are many shipbuilding companies in the city, Heverna ships are known by all to be particularly reliable. The success of the White Fleet vessels, over many decades of service, can be traced back to the innovations and crafting expertise of the Heverna family.

Damiara

The Damiaras are weavers, known for the strength of their products. Their process is a closely guarded secret, but the fabrics they produce are amazingly light and strong. Years ago, they manufactured many different forms of textile products, but their attentions have turned to the production of sails in service of the White Fleet; as quotas rise, the value of their work rises to match.

Nalcia

Extensive logging in the Faro area has led to local deforestation. Many Faro citizens have spoken up about the issue, but military administrators in Toletum maintain that the fleet must keep expanding. This has strained the already high tensions between the local populace of Faro and the Inquisition’s governance of Castinella.

Controlling many Faro lumber mills, the Nalcia family is particularly influential in the logging sector. While some companies refuse to continue what they consider to be “unsustainable” logging practices, the Nalcians have only increased the scope of their operations. Many of their laborers have disappeared mysteriously, but they simply hire more. There is no shortage of young and eager workers in Faro.

### Toletum

When the winds turn, all of Toletum’s great city is cloaked in noxious smoke. Most of the ore mined in the Corova Mountains is sent to Toletum for processing and smithing. Small smithies call other cities and towns home, but only Toletum has smith work at a scale that could be used to supply an army. This dominance maintains control over the flow of weaponry, ensuring that any city trying to break out of the unification will be unable to adequately supply themselves.

Above the smog and smoke of the lower city and its industrious work, the upper city is home to wealthy bureaucrats. Toletum is more geographically segregated by wealth than the other Castinellan cities, and there is a stark difference between the white-marbled upper side and the downtown area.

The administrators and heads of businesses within Toletum have significant sway over the legislative overseers of the peninsula. The lucrative business of supplying weapons to the Inquisition and timber to the White Fleet has prompted lobbyists to give their support for warmongers—and war profiteers—from the government on its military use.

There is also pressure from within Toletum to expand the Inquisition far beyond the reaches of Castinella. The likelihood that this could instigate wars with other nations does not seem to bother the war enthusiasts and their well-built infrastructure for selling military supplies.

#### Toletum Crime

The great houses control the administrative work and business legalities from the top of Toletum, but from the bottom, a different kind of house controls the city. Organized crime has grown like mold on the bottom of Toletum’s shoe. They siphon weapons from the military and sell them on the black market. Better hidden are supplies of magical origin. Magical potions, spell scrolls, and arcane focuses cannot be procured legally, but they can be found if one knows exactly where to ask the right questions.

Since arcane magic is illegal, many arcanists in Castinella make a good living using their talents for unlawful means. And it’s the Castibona family they turn to. If any of these criminal groups can be said to have good intentions (a huge ‘if’ indeed), it would be the Castibonas. They also happen to be the most doggedly pursued by the city law keepers, and that’s for two simple reasons: more of their number are arcanists than any other, and they often do jobs that work to directly oppose the Inquisition’s war on magic. You’ll pay dearly to secure their help, but if you need a loved one with magic blood spirited away from the Inquisition’s clutches, or given a secret way to escape the peninsula, the Castibonas are the ones to turn to.

The Castibonas have spread rumors that the leaders of the Inquisition secretly serve Beleth, the Arch Daemon of Fear. This was in direct response to the Church issuing edicts that members of the Castibona families were worshippers of Arch Daemons and secret practitioners of arcane magic: a statement that is half-true. The connections and power of the Castibonas, in the face of the power wielded by the Inquisition, has led to a stalemate.

In its campaign to root out the Castibonas, the Inquisition has worked its own fingers into the black market trade. They contract sympathetic crime syndicates that do not tolerate magic users to oppose the activities of the Castibonas. As such, there are fences and dealers who can get you illicit substances or dangerous weapons—and maybe even a magical potion or scroll. However, these dealers are quick to sell you out to the Inquisition if and when it becomes beneficial for them to do so.

#### Notable Individuals

Arieda Starwin, Exiled Arcanist. Arieda Starwin is an elf with human blood who concealed her arcane talents for years. Convinced that she was born wrong in some way, she repressed her magical ability and worked in the Castinellan military. She worked her way up through the ranks to become a trusted soldier, loved by her superiors and subordinates alike.

One fateful day, she stopped a Toletum nobleman’s son from being ravaged by a werewolf. However, during the confrontation she accidentally resorted to using arcane magic. She was captured and brought before the High Inquisitor himself, and all expected that she would meet the same fate as anyone using arcane magic in Castinella.

In this one case, however, something remarkable happened. She was spared by Hedric, and no knew why. Some claimed that the nobleman whose son was saved intervened on her behalf in some grand gesture. Others claim the High Inquisitor fell in love with her. The truth is very different, however.

In that meeting, Hedric heard a voice saying that Arieda’s power was condoned by the Seraphs, and that she had a greater role to play in this world. Arieda was prepared to die, and even welcomed it in some ways. Hedric instead exiled her, telling her to spread the word of the Seraphs’ grace and power to other parts of Etharis.

Arieda did as she was told, and during her travels became more devout and more powerful. With each successful blow against evil, the Seraphs, it is said, graced her with boons that slowly turned her into a Seraph. As her arcane might grew to match her new Seraph-like features and abilities, she came to understand the evil of the Arcanist Inquisition.

She now prepares to return to Castinella and purge the land of the scourge of the Arcanist Inquisition, including the High Inquisitor who had spared her. As powerful as she is, Arieda cannot succeed alone. She seeks allies, including those she’s saved from Castinella and elsewhere, to join her fight.


### Therpena

Therpena enforces the same code of laws that the other provinces toil under, but the general mood in this province contains a modicum more joy. That doesn’t mean the Arcanist Inquisition is lax here; in truth, they are simply less likely to have to spy out perpetrators, as the citizens of Therpena are less adept at hiding their negative feelings when they experience them.

When the Inquisition came to power, they took Ember Cairn and the Shattered Peaks as their headquarters, which are technically in Therpenan territory. The sunny hills now live within the shadow of the Inquisition and their much stricter rule. Traveling through Therpena can cause a strange sort of emotional whiplash. Local guardsmen are often less harsh and more forgiving than you would find in other parts of the peninsula. In contrast, enforcers from the clergy are twice as diligent when the Inquisition’s watchful eye is so close at all times.

#### Center of Art and Culture

Therpena’s noble houses have long been the greatest patrons of the arts, and many of Castinella’s most prominent painters, sculptors, writers, and musicians were trained there. This focus on beauty and the natural senses leads to a freer and more joyful demeanor among the population, at least on the surface.

These days, most art revolves around the perfection of the Arch Seraphs, the celebration of goodness and beauty, and the fight against the evils of the arcane and the Daemons—as dictated by the Inquisition. Representatives of the Church censure any artwork that can be seen as heretical. Along with books of magic burned in piles by the Inquisition, many statues have been shattered, paintings torn down, and writing ripped up and scattered into compost in the name of Empyreus.

Some pre-Inquisition artwork has been saved in the streets of Therpena by nobles, who have stepped in and vouched for the artwork as worthy of preservation. Nobles with political stature can sometimes make these claims without being punished, though they are often watched closely by the Inquisition for years after.

Other statues and works of art have been secreted away in the private collections of local great houses. More genteel, yet just as cutthroat as the black market in Toletum, there is good money to be made in the discrete business of ferrying famous works of art, performers, or artists across the border.

One house with particularly dedicated connections bent on preserving the old arts is the House of Calcien, whose region extends from the sunny plains’ vineyards on to the coast, where smuggling vessels disguised as personal recreational sailboats pick up shipments of artistic works from the beach.

While artwork is limited to the depiction of Empyreus and the other Arch Seraphs and their servants, it is still remarkable in its quality and scale of production. Huge statues can be found on every street. Churches every couple of blocks teem with multi-story artworks. And though the scale is grandest in Totelum and Ember Cairn, it is said that Therpena boasts the most beautiful structures and artwork in Castinella.

#### Music

The music and drama in Therpena is without equal in Castinella. Musicians come from hundreds of miles around, often from other countries, to try their hand in the many taverns and theaters in Therpena. What they lack in hospitality toward the arcane, Castinella makes up for with a ready purse for talented musicians.

The competition for prestige is fierce in Therpena. In other regions, someone with an old fiddle and a bit of practice could get by for a night in front of a tipsy crowd. But not in Castinella, and certainly not in Therpena. Audiences are discerning, and the same skill that could get you a position at a wealthy tavern in other kingdoms may only earn you the most basic of performing houses here. And the steady gaze of Inquisition members could make even the most experienced minstrel or actor crack.

#### Noble Houses

The strongest great house in the sunny coastal plains of Therpena is the House of Theretine. Once a royal bloodline, all that is left of their once-staggering power is their holding of the largest vineyards in Castinella. Other houses exist, but all swear fealty to the Therentines.

House Calcien is one of those houses, and as powerful merchants and teamsters, there is a cordial relationship between these two powerful houses. The rumors that House Calcien caravans and marketplaces are fronts for black market goods and smuggling operations, particularly of illegal art and artists—as well as arcane items—has not yet caused a rift.

#### Notable Individuals

Nicholas Calcien. The head of the Calcien family is Nicholas, an unimposing figure who quietly oversees his family’s interests with a deft touch, expertly balancing fealty to the church with business acumen.

The Inquisition has never raided the Calcien residences with full gusto, or at least not yet. They have heard rumors of illegal and illicit activities, but the Calciens have always been incredible patrons of the arts relating to the Seraphs. So half-hearted sweeps through the Calcien Mansion, and occasionally their summer homes, have failed to uncover an ounce of evidence against them.

The unspoken and quite publicly known secret is that behind trick bookshelves and false walls, a treasure trove of art is hidden within the Calcien residences. This unspoken agreement between the Calciens and the clergy protects the Calciens on one hand and the generous donations the Church receives on the other.

The truce and understanding hide a much more dangerous smuggling business. Few are old enough to remember, but the Calcien family has a magical bloodline. Along with their hordes of artifacts and treasures, rival noble families have accused them of hiding arcanists as well. These accusations are never said louder than a whisper, as many of the Calciens are part of the clergy, and their roots within the church run deep.

## Other Locations of Interest

The following important locations can be found in the Castinellan Provinces.

### Ember Cairn

Destroyed long ago in the deep history of Etharis, the granite city of Ember Cairn has been rebuilt and repopulated by the once nomadic dragonborn. Their city was built atop the Shattered Peaks before an unknown cataclysm shook the mountains, reducing the granite structures to rubble. Today, Ember Cairn is a sacred city: a bridge to the lost heritage of the dragonborn, and a reminder of the Arch Seraphs’ glory.

Ember Cairn is the strictest and most heavily regulated of all the Castinellan cities. However, even in Ember Cairn, there is no completely rooting out troublesome dealings in the dark. A black market is just as lucrative, if much smaller, in Ember Cairn than elsewhere.

Additionally, there is a growing interest in the history of dragonborn and dragons in the area before the city’s destruction. The Inquisition discourages this interest by every means it can. There is no wish within its ranks to revive a dead culture that did not revere the Arch Seraphs, and instead worshipped dragons and magic.

Siraj Fakhri Hedric, the High Redeemer Notable Individuals Hedric, the High Redeemer. The leader of the Arcanist Inquisition is an aging black dragonborn with a lower jaw misshapen by a scar. A personal hatred of arcane magic has transformed into an unrivaled zealotry that matches the goals and methods of the Inquisition. The High Inquisitor is keenly aware of corruption within the Inquisition. His paranoia toward even his closest staff spawns a nagging fear that mages have slipped into the ranks, or that the power of the Inquisition will be used for personal gain rather than the pursuit of safety. Years of taking responsibility for the safety of the nation has worn on him. He sees danger everywhere, even where it doesn’t exist. The more cunning and less scrupulous members of his advising council play on his fears, pulling his strings by manipulating his concerns, both valid and imagined.

Hedric was born in the Shattered Peaks, not far from the site of the rebuilt Ember Cairn. He wandered the kingdoms for a while, looking for purpose in his life. Having been raised worshipping the Seraphs, he visited many towns and learned about the divine beings at the center of his world. He provided support to those who led the worship of the Seraphs—both medical and violent in nature.

He found he was adept at cutting through the beasts that harassed the villages he found, as well as treating maladies with his growing affinity for divine magic. With each passing quest, Hedric’s path and personal ethos became clearer.

During his journeys, Hedric fell in love with a dragonborn named Serace. Serace clung to the old ways of worshiping the primordials. With no answers to prayers, Serace found that druidic magic was his best way of communing with the lost gods. He taught Hedric that learning about the world was the closest thing mortals could do to learning about the old gods. Hedric believed this—for a time.

Hedric traveled with Serace for years, though he held his own fear of his lover’s magical talents. If one asks Hedric what happened to his lover, the old dragonborn’s eyes go cold. Only those closest to him know what truly happened, but the result is clear. Serace no longer walks the face of Etharis, and Hedric despises all magic not derived from the Seraphs.


Headmistress Zafoa. The Headmistress of the School of the Dread Templar in Ember Cairn is an elven mage-slayer who has bought the trust of the church with the bodies of dozens of mages over the course of her long career. Even in her growing age, she is sharp as a razor and quicker than flint striking steel. It is said that she can tell if a person has cast a spell in the past 24 hours by scent alone.

It is also said that Zafoa knows enough magical theory to rival the most powerful wizards in the realm, in knowledge if not in practice. She started learning the theory of magic from a wizard who traveled in her mercenary party before she was introduced to the Arcanist Inquisition.

### Cathedral of Embers

The Arcanist Inquisition’s headquarters is the Cathedral of Embers. Appearing to be a celestial palace on a mountaintop above Ember Cairn, the truth is closer to a dread fort. The Inquisition can house an army inside. Beneath the white domed towers is a depthless catacomb, filled with jail cells and echoing with the tortured screams of the accused.

### Cathedral of Music

Once a summer palace for the Calcien family, the Cathedral of Music is a famous venue for entertainment in Therpena. Nobles from all over the peninsula make trips to catch the world-class shows frequently on display. Freshly remodeled and adorned with divine iconography, the Cathedral of Music is one of the many clergy-approved music venues in the peninsula. Any musician aiming to become a household name in Therpena must first book a show at the House of Music, and therefore must ingratiate themselves with the Church.

The Cathedral of Music also draws people from other regions, some of whom would fear to tread in other parts of Castinella for fear of crossing paths with members of the Inquisition. The Calcien family tells acquaintances from other areas that they are safe in the Cathedral of Music, as long as they don’t perform overt acts of arcane magic in full view. This makes the Cathedral a valuable meeting place for all types, including spies and diplomats and members of less savory organizations that need a neutral ground to conduct business.


### The Empyrean Pit

Fighting pits, forever popular in other areas of Etharis, are beginning to catch the fancy of the citizenry of Castinella. Fighters face each other in hand-to-hand combat in a pit filled with shallow water. Onlookers watch as the water turns red as the duel wears on. While these pits can be found in the larger cities, and even some smaller ones, Ember Cairn boasts the newest and most elaborate fighting arena in Castinella. The arena, called the Empyrean Pit, contains sculptures and artwork from the most talented and respected artisans of the day. Several times a month, the spectacle of pit fights is presented. One might think the clergy would denounce the violent sport, but nothing could be further from the truth. The church has made these battles expressions of faith, using them to proclaim the truth of the Eternal Dogma. The violence of the sport prepares citizens for the violence of arcanist executions. Fighters of extreme skill and brutality can be recruited. And occasionally, a fighter accidentally reveals arcane powers in the heat of battle, and they are quickly arrested. ## Quest Hooks

The following quest hooks are appropriate for adventures in the Castinellan Provinces.

### The Kribblen Gang

A well-known traveling minstrel band, the Kribblen Gang is comprised of four of the best musicians in Castinella, and a retinue of tumblers, jugglers, artisans, and other hangers-on. The leaders’ voices sound like honey, and their fingers are swift against their lutes, fiddles, and drums. They sometimes lure arcanists in by asking them in low, conspiratorial tones if they are indeed practitioners of magic. They make promises of camaraderie; after all, they’re “always happy to help out a fellow artist.” As quickly as they draw the gullible mage in, they sell them out to the Inquisition.

The characters may need to infiltrate or ingratiate themselves with the Kribblens to find out what happened to an individual who was close to them and suddenly disappeared. Such an investigation would likely reveal the duplicity of the group and its followers.

### The Death Mage of the Labyrinth

Long ago, a young dragonborn of thirteen years was thrown into the labyrinth for the illegal use of magic. In the thick darkness, the child taught himself simple spells in order to survive: spells to give light, then spells to kill and cook rats, then spells to deal with larger problems.

He wandered the labyrinth for years, searching for a way out. The one goal of reaching the exit consumed his young mind. Through the twists and turns of the labyrinth, his magic became twisted as well. It curdled and became a deadly, awful thing. The child did not notice his magic turning vile, because he was just looking for a way out.

Sandra Grygierzec He barely noticed when he stopped eating rats. He simply started plucking their souls out and consuming them. When faces stared back at him from the dark, he could no longer remember if they were the faces of mortals or monsters, and he took their souls as well, and they followed him wordlessly. Somewhere in the back of his mind, he knew this was wrong. But he pushed his worries away, and decided that his new face must have been the one he wore all along. He resolved to be frightened of the new creatures that fell into the labyrinth, for they did not look like him, and therefore must be monsters. He took their lives as well.

The boy, now a Lich Troll, toils away in a secret fortress of old towers buried deep within the labyrinth. If you speak with him, he may hear you out, though he will become very frightened if you show your living face. He will want you to die and be silent like the small army he has created.

### Trouble in the Mines

The mines of the Castinellan Provinces can be dangerous places, and clearing out strange beasts from the mines is lucrative for adventurers and monster hunters.

The town of Salume in Therpena once dug a well in the middle of town. A shout was heard coming from the excavators, followed by the sound of a clattering shovel and a pained groan. The excavators disappeared into a vast cavern with unknown depths. When ropes were lowered and the men dragged up and out, they kept whispering about a beast that spoke to them in the dark. They claimed it had been days they waited for rescue, though it had scarcely been a few minutes.

The townsfolk cannot sleep at night until they find out what delves in the tunnels at the bottom of the mines. If adventurers wait too long before investigating, the excavators begin disappearing, one by one, without a trace.

### Whispers in the Woods

Farosi lumber companies have continued expanding what some have called “unsustainable” logging practices, claiming more and more of the woods as material for the White Fleet. As the expansion has continued, keen-eyed logisticians have observed a significant increase in accidents, injuries, and untimely deaths in logging operations. Many lumberjacks have quit abruptly, citing a perceived ceaseless, babbling whisper that permeates the woods.

The companies—and the clergy—have denounced these rumors as naught but a hallucination brought about by isolation and overexertion. To the rumor’s credit, though, many loggers have been forcibly retired after going mad overnight.

A coven of hags has recently moved into the area, and they delight at the veritable smorgasbord of innocent souls and fragile minds right at their doorstep. Worse yet, the madness they inflict upon the loggers is spread through contact, so it’s only a matter of time before the hometowns of the loggers become infected with the hag-madness.


### The Little Witches of the Mountain

Hunting Little Witches

A community of magically gifted folk is said to hide away in the Shattered Peaks, right under the nose of the Inquisition. Little is agreed upon regarding what these people actually are. Some say they are giant hags, and that their name is an ironic misnomer. Others say they are a group of halfling scholars, hiding away until magic is allowed again in Castinella.

They say the little witches can give answers to any question, and there are many stories of people venturing into the Shattered Peaks to learn something of use. This has led to the phrase ‘little witching’ being used to denote someone looking for answers in foolish places. One might say a man has gone ‘little witching’ when he seeks an answer to an unsolvable problem.

The phrase can also be used for someone who was taken prisoner by the Inquisition without a stated reason or a lack of compelling evidence. You could say that your brother was taken in for ‘little witching’ if you wanted to slyly imply that there was no good reason for it.

Adventurers are asked by members of the Inquisition to investigate this rumored witch community—find it and bring the inhabitants to justice if they are indeed practitioners of arcane magic. What the adventurers find are people living in close commune with members of the Summer Court. The fey feel protective of these people, not allowing any harm to befall them.

### The Thaumaturge

Members of the Castinellan clergy are spreading the word that a group of foreign spies and infiltrators are attempting to topple the Castinellan government and the Castinellan way of life. The have given a name to this group: the Thaumaturge.

These folks are demonized as boogeymen—strange kidnappers lurking in the dark, hoping to corrupt the local populace and spread their teachings of dark magic.

It is rumored that the Thaumaturge makes expeditions into the labyrinth, hoping to free evil wizards and recruit them to their plans. Anyone who can survive a number of days within the twisted passages is deemed worthy of climbing the supposed ranks of the Thaumaturge.

The Inquisition uses the idea of the Thaumaturge as an excuse to make more raids and arrests than normal in an area. The Thaumaturge’s secretive nature enables the Inquisition to accuse almost anyone of having ties to the group.

It is thought that members of the Thaumaturge council were close counselors of several noble families. To the magic users trying to survive in Castinella, the Thaumaturge is a spare hope in a country that seeks to destroy them.

Characters may be called upon to find and infiltrate the Thaumaturge, find out who their leaders are, and bring the whole organization tumbling down. The real question is, though, do they even exist?

## Ember Cairn

The holy city of Ember Cairn is the religious center of the Castinellan Provinces. Home to the Cathedral of Embers, headquarters of the Arcanist Inquisition, there are few cities of such sacred or spiritual importance as Ember Cairn.

Built high in the Shattered Peaks, Ember Cairn was the ancestral home of the dragonborn since long before the Era of Expansion. Though the city was shattered by some unknown calamity deep in antiquity, since the rise of the Unifier, the dragonborn have come to resettle these ancient lands.

Pilgrims flock to Ember Cairn and the Cathedral of Embers. People arrive simply to pray on the steps beneath the Cathedral, in a place where they feel the hand of the Arch Seraphs upon their shoulder. Priests stroll the plazas doling out blessings and sanctifying the air with blessed censers. Many who live in Ember Cairn believe it to be the safest place in Etharis, with no taint of magic able to pierce the veil of reality and threaten the good folk within.

Yet the dangers of Ember Cairn are on full exhibit. Corpses rot in gibbets above market squares, and the skulls of the punished are piled into ashen cairns, displaying the fate of the wicked. The faithful and the blessed have nothing to fear. Everyone else must tread carefully, every deed directly beneath the censorious gaze of the Arcanist Inquisition.

### Geography

In lands far beyond Castinella, the Shattered Peaks invoke an image of dark jagged spires—a mountain range devoid of life. But the cataclysm that created the Shattered Peaks happened long ago.

Many pilgrims are surprised to discover that Castinella is not simply hot desert brush and low-lying vineyards. Upon the mountains, where the elevation allows for a drop in temperature, grand forests thrive. Water is still scarce. Wildfires remain a natural part of the forest’s life cycle. Antiques from ancient times suggest the dragonborn

predicted the movement of wildfires using druidic magics that are now forbidden in the era of the Arcanist Inquisition. Instead, fire towers are spaced throughout the mountain range. With well-tended roads and a network of runners, the threat of fire is kept at a manageable distance.

Ember Cairn is cradled between three stone monoliths that rise on each side of the city like colossal titans. The forest thins close to the city’s edge. The three peaks are each bare rock, like broken teeth. Legends claim they were once a single summit upon which the original Ember Cairn was built, fragments of that ancient home of the dragonborn still visible beneath the stonework of the modern holy city.

#### Inquisitorial Stronghold

The western peak is the seat of the Cathedral of Embers. Its polished marble and white domes, held aloft among the clouds, make the cathedral appear like a palace for angels. A switchback staircase ascends the mountain toward the celestial structure. Pilgrims crowd the ascent. They fall to their knees and pray at the gates, begging the Inquisition for protection or leniency as if they spoke to Empyreus himself. Yet despite appearances, the Cathedral of Embers is not a church. It’s a fortress.

The rock formation beneath the cathedral has been extensively excavated into a labyrinthine dungeon. This vast underground complex contains countless prison cells and interrogation rooms, used to hold and question the accused. The public have no concept of just how massive the cathedral’s prison is, nor how many heretics fill the cells, or the extent of tortures inflicted upon them to elicit their confessions.

#### City of Granite and Wood

The populated districts of Ember Cairn rest in the canyon between the three peaks. At night, when braziers and torches reflect off the white granite Ember Cairn is built from, the city itself appears to burn. Some claim the red glow of Ember Cairn can be seen through the evening gloom from the sunny hills of Therpena: a speck of light in the distant horizon, like a glowering eye, and a constant reminder of the zealotry which burns at the heart of Castinella’s governance.

Heavy construction is frequently taking place throughout the city. Though the plans for Ember Cairn are grand, with broad streets and a skyline of countless domes, there are currently wooden scaffolds and temporary structures built over the ancient stones placed by the original dragonborn.

The heart of the city is crowded between the existing buildings and new constructions. Narrow lanes choked with pilgrims and laborers spill out into bustling markets or quiet plazas where a congregation kneels in prayer. These worshipful places can be tranquil. Equally, they can be filled with rage and fanaticism whenever the Inquisition decides an apostate deserves public execution.

The markets are always loud. Merchants bring provisions from all corners of Castinella to supply the residents of Ember Cairn, alongside the constant flow of visitors to the holy city. Smuggling rings and purveyors of counterfeit sacred trinkets operate within the din of the market stalls. Though such activity is illegal, the city watch is responsible for dealing with it. The Inquisition involves itself only if the rancid smell of magic wafts up from the hidden corners of the markets.

#### Sacred Roads

Whatever cataclysm shattered the mountains left great cracks and treacherous broken cliffs on the roads to Ember Cairn. Some ravines are hundreds of feet wide. The work is constant to construct bridges and stairs that traverse these divides. Some structures are elegant works of granite, with freshly carved statues of the Arch Seraphs placed to safeguard the road. Others are temporary, and slightly precarious, timber builds.

These structures extend far beyond Ember Cairn itself and trace a navigable path through the Shattered Peaks, frequently used by pilgrims and supply merchants. Without such infrastructure, the mountains would be extremely difficult to cross.

Taverns and other businesses have popped up along the road to cater to the needs of travelers. Heavy patrols keep the roads safe, even in the high mountains, from both monsters, bandits, and apostates who dare to sneak beneath the notice of the Inquisition.

These long roads wind through mountain forests, across exposed grasslands, and along rocky outcroppings. There are many mountains within the range that have not been settled and remain wild, where bandit camps are easily concealed. Other places hide remote communities. These could be monasteries whose elite warriors or priests are trained in quiet seclusion. Or they might be folk who wish to live outside the gaze of the Arch Seraphs, for one reason or another.

#### Granite Quarries

The sides of the mountains are stripped of granite, leaving sheer cliffs with boxy indentations. These quarries are a large industry centered upon Ember Cairn. The massive blocks of granite are labor-intensive to mine, immensely difficult to transport, and sold to construction, or artisan guilds, or else transported to other parts of Castinella or beyond.

Ember Cairn is famous for its white, freshly carved stones that the city is built from, making it easy to spot from a distance. Sculpture is a practice of intense cultural importance to the dragonborn—a rare craft that connects them to both their modern religiosity and their more ancient heritage. Old statues of forgotten deities are buried beneath the modern Ember Cairn. Young dragonborn demonstrate their devotion by turning this ancestral art into a form of modern worship, sculpting stunning granite reliefs and edifices of the Arch Seraphs in all their glory.


### History

In the fabled Era of Antiquity, the dragonborn had settled the lands of the Castinellan peninsula all the way to the Grove Maze, which borders Charneault. Almost nothing is known about this neolithic culture. All that remains of it are cave reliefs, broken statues, and oral stories passed through generations of dragonborn. Nobody is certain if the dragonborn can trace their lineage back to true dragons. They share more in common with elves than wyverns.

Whether the dragonborn never wrote their histories, or the texts were lost in the calamity that destroyed their home, modern generations know almost nothing of their past. Half-remembered mythologies of long-silent gods. Ancient structures crumbled to their foundations. Granite carvings of a flame-breathing dragon, or perhaps a deity, lording over dragonborn, either bent in terror or worship. This is all that remains. Much of it was rediscovered in the old ruins of Ember Cairn.

The dragonborn became nomadic after the cataclysm. Their people spread to every reach within Etharis, even forming small communities in other folks’ empires. But nowhere could they call home until Ember Cairn summoned them. The cultural hole left by their misplaced heritage has since been filled by the reconstruction of their broken city and worship of the Arch Seraphs.

Dragonborn are not the only people who dwell upon the Shattered Peaks. As with most places after the Era of Expansion, humans are populace within the holy city. Dwarves have also settled within Ember Cairn and formed small villages near the quarries. Dwarven and gnomish ingenuity is relied upon often in the city’s reconstruction, establishing new granite mines, and the careful excavation of the cathedral’s catacombs.

Of course, faith draws pilgrims of all cultures and species who wish to revere the Arch Seraphs and purify themselves from the taint of magic.

#### Reconstruction

After Sagrado Valieda formed the Arcanist Inquisition, he chose Ember Cairn as the order’s stronghold—at the time, a secluded place to sequester dangerous arcanists. Valieda’s broader reasons also included extending an invitation for the dragonborn to rebuild their ancestral home. But rumors persist that his true motives were less than generous.

Archeologists of Therpena were already digging through the ruins of the ancient city, unearthing the history of Castinella’s draconic ancestors. Some accounts suggest Valieda feared what might be discovered. Rather than permit a culture of arcanists or dragon worshippers to be revived, the Unifier erected his fortress above those ancient stones so they would never be disturbed. Other rumors suggest the catacombs beneath the Cathedral of Embers already existed: an umbral pit for the Inquisition to lock their enemies away, their pleas for mercy never to be heard again.

In the many years since, the Castinellan theocracy has spent an abundance of time, wealth, and labor reconstructing Ember Cairn. The church claims this was always Valieda’s desire. Though the progress is ongoing, the benefits this effort has yielded to the provinces are countless.

The dragonborn have been loyal and fervent servants of the Arch Seraphs. Ember Cairn is a reward for their piety and has become a rallying place to recall their diaspora back to the ancestral lands from across Etharis. Often, dragonborn who return home likewise devote themselves to the Eternal Dogma.

Ember Cairn has become a powerful symbol in the minds of all Castinellans. It’s both a projection of the nation’s strength and righteousness, and a dire warning to all who would break the laws of the Eternal Dogma. Many aspire to visit the holy city and behold its splendor for themselves. None wish to be dragged there in inquisitorial chains.

The prosperity of Ember Cairn also spreads wealth to the rest of the Castinellan Provinces. Granite and other building stone are a lucrative export. So are divine statues, necklaces, clasps, and other holy trinkets crafted by the hands of priests in the most sacred city on the continent. These holy symbols reach as far as the Bürach Empire or Charneault Kingdom, where Chapter Knights sworn to the Arch Seraphs wear them. Donations also flow into church coffers at Ember Cairn, before being funneled to the rest of Castinella for the construction of more extravagant temples, finery for the ecclesiastic class, and weapons for the Arcanist Inquisition.

#### Wildfires

Wildfires are a reality for Ember Cairn, as they’ve been for all the Castinellan Provinces. Casualties occur on the roads into the city when a blaze spreads without warning, and refugees from threatened or burnt-out villages come to the holy city to beg for alms.

Many advocate for better fire suppression. Shepherds who cross the mountains with goats or cattle provide a surprising mitigation to the danger of wildfires, as their animals eat some of the ground fuel, such as fallen sticks and dry plants. The shepherds of the Shattered Peaks have become glorified by the faithful. They’re associated with Aphaeleon, whose image is sometimes that of a penniless herder protecting his flock from the dangers of temptation.

### Notable Locations

The reconstruction of Ember Cairn is progressing and reshaping the city. Less developed than other major cities, there are nonetheless many significant locations that have made Ember Cairn revered.

#### Cathedral of Embers

The Cathedral of Embers is perched atop a narrow summit. Encircled by a high curtain wall that’s almost flush with the cliffs beneath it, the Cathedral gates open only for the Inquisition and the poor souls condemned to imprisonment. Countless pilgrims crowd the steps outside those gates. Their constant prayers are a mix of pleas and adulation for Empyreus’s divine protection. The Cathedral itself is a sizeable keep encircled within those walls. Its white towers are crowned with majestic domes, and countless arched windows keep vigilance over Ember Cairn. Despite projecting the image of a sacred temple, the Cathedral of Embers is a fortress capable of housing an army. Inquisitor knights are summoned here from across Castinella to receive blessings, purifications, and sacred orders before being dispatched on crusades. A legion of soldiers guards the cathedral at all times. Marco Gonzalez Sebastián

The Inquisition also houses judges, jailers, and interrogators within their stronghold. The domed towers encircled within their curtain wall are not all the Cathedral of Embers is, as there are tunnels delving into the rock beneath the cathedral and deep into the roots of the surrounding mountains. The upper levels contain quarters, armories, and offices for those whose duty is dealing with imprisoned apostates. None know the depth of the lower levels.

#### Cathedral Catacombs

The catacombs are a vast network of ancient tunnels that spread from beneath the Cathedral of Embers. The Arcanist Inquisition claims to have built these tunnels themselves, though most scholars presume this is a lie. The catacombs are simply too vast for that to be possible.

The darkness hold countless cells and crevices for incarcerated apostates to be thrown. Rumors suggest the Inquisition isn’t even aware of how many mages they’ve locked away, condemned to rot within those tunnels and never be spoken of again. For some, the catacombs are a test. Not every room is locked, and the accused are permitted to wander the labyrinth freely to search for a way out. Those who find their way back through the Cathedral of Embers, their path said to be lit by Zabriel, are offered a chance at redemption through confession or joining the Inquisition. Accused who discover another way to escape the catacombs are swiftly recaptured and condemned to death. None have accomplished such a feat without the aid of magic.

The jailers of the Inquisition are intimately familiar with the upper catacombs, aware of the many paths an apostate might take in their attempt to escape the labyrinth. Traps and torture chambers await those who try to wield their magic. Black runes inscribed in the stonework make it impossible to cast most types of spells, making it impossible to teleport or discorporate. Most apostate mages are cornered and punished before they’ve breathed a gasp of fresh air, never to see the sky again.

Deeper in the catacombs, the stonework becomes more ornate and vast, crumbling railings lining the edge of steps that descend into cavernous chambers. Great statues of draconic warriors hold aloft vaulted ceilings. Giant bas-reliefs illustrate intricate scenes of dragons being fought or worshipped, and astrological charts depicting images of the night sky.

An accused person who flees this deeply into the catacombs may lose the pursuit of their inquisitorial jailers, yet they are no more likely to ever experience freedom. Even worse monsters are said to lurk within that hidden labyrinth.

#### Hidden Labyrinth

An ancient labyrinth exists beneath Ember Cairn. It contains the history of the dragonborn, still largely buried under the holy city, hiding the true story of what happened to that ancient civilization. Stone slabs and fragments of dragon bone are occasionally discovered in the Shattered Peaks surrounding Ember Cairn. While the only well-known entrance into this labyrinth is through the catacombs beneath the Cathedral of Embers, other openings do exist in the crags and fissures of the mountains. Archaeologists explore these caves in secret.

The Inquisition takes a dim view of anybody trying to access this hidden labyrinth. Officially, their stance reflects a concern for public safety. Yet tension does exist between the Castinellan theocracy and the dragonborn who aren’t swept up by faith, who believe that the church is intentionally trying to keep them separated from their heritage. These voices are small in Ember Cairn. It’s not difficult for the Inquisition to draw a link between a person’s interest in the hidden labyrinth and a probable curiosity in the occult.

The Inquisition’s vigilance against harm from the hidden labyrinth isn’t without merit, either. Deadly monsters, arcanists gone mad, and artifacts beyond modern understanding are sequestered in the dark, awaiting an explorer unlucky enough to find themselves within those depths.

Who knows what greater horrors could be slumbering in the dark?

It’s rumored that Ember Cairn’s black market knows of multiple secret entrances into the labyrinth, and can provide access for a price. Their clients include treasure hunters and historians. The hidden labyrinth is also sold as a path to sneak into the Cathedral catacombs and rescue a condemned mage.

#### Marketplace

Among the dense cluster of buildings near the heart of the city is where the bustling marketplace is loudest, constantly filled with merchants and customers. The stalls range from food vendors, fabric sellers, stonework artisans, bead makers, jewelers, and, unavoidably, counterfeiters. Both local and travelling merchants hawk their wares. The marketplace is always a mix of locals purchasing imported produce and pilgrims searching for mementos of their visit to the holy city.

Preachers stand on street corners screaming the will of the Arch Seraphs to whomever will listen. Divine healers make a modest living mending ailments. Acting as an unsanctioned prophet is a dangerous vocation in Ember Cairn, as the Inquisition is intolerant toward folks whose religious values may contravene the Eternal Dogma, or whose miraculous healing may unknowingly draw from the power of an Arch Daemon. Even the devout are not free from the Inquisition’s suspicion.

Surprisingly, the smuggling trade is less scrutinized, provided the wares being peddled are not magical in nature. Souvenirs from the heritage of the dragonborn, counterfeit sacred baubles, stolen granite carvings, and rare contraband from other regions of Etharis can all be found in the narrowest alleys of the marketplace. However, the most lucrative illicit trade is in people and information.

The Inquisition can be difficult to extract information from, considering their members are fanatics and idealists. Nevertheless, the enemies of the ecclesiarch pay great sums for whatever secrets can be extracted from Ember Cairn, including the movements of Inquisitorial legions, the locations of high-ranking judges are ripe for assassination, and the identities of those interred in the Cathedral of Embers.

Folk who wish to visit the holy city unnoticed rely on the same smuggling rings. They might arrive with a desire to rescue a friend or loved one from the fires of the Inquisition, possibly even desperate enough to try entering the catacombs. Or they’ve come to verify information that could help save arcane lives in the broader Castinellan Provinces. In any case, far more people are smuggled into the city than ever survive to make the return journey.

#### Empyrean Pit

The Empyrean Pit is an elaborate fighting arena where warriors worship through combat. The walls of the indoor arena are carved with ornate artworks, featuring winged seraphs falling upon hordes of writhing daemons. The overhead dome is painted with an image of the heavens. Violent spectacles are presented in the arena throughout the year, under the Inquisition’s gaze, who ensures every fight is in accordance with the Eternal Dogma.

The Empyrean Pit is, in fact, an opportunity for the Inquisition to build the reputation of its knights. Tortured arcanists are sometimes herded into the arena. Their enfeebled magics are no match for an armored inquisitor knight, who doles out violent executions in the name of Empyreus to be witnessed by an audience of the Arch Seraph’s faithful.

Monsters are also caged and brought in for sanctioned warriors to fight against. Such entertainment is far too enticing for large crowds to pass up, and so audiences flock to see these battles.

These violent showcases are quite different from the tournaments held in the Charneault Kingdom, where death is rarely the aim and knights are admired for virtues such as grace and humility. In Ember Cairn, the goal is to eliminate creatures who offend the Arch Seraphs. Every battle begins with a prayer. Rather than a cheering crowd drunk on wine and merriment, the executions are accompanied by an audience chanting solemn hymns and righteous adulations. Every match is overseen by an Inquisitor Priest, ensuring it’s performed with sanctity. Detractors ponder if Tormach derives an equal measure of satisfaction from the Empyrean Pit as its namesake does—a blasphemous thought.

Officially, gambling on the results of battles is illegal. This doesn’t mean it isn’t possible through bookies in Ember Cairn’s black market.

### Important Individuals

Notable people associated with Ember Cairn include those described below.

#### Lord Inquisitor Crevax

The commonfolk of Castinella have come to revere or dread Heretic’s End, the flaming longsword of Lord Inquisitor Crevax. One of the deadliest weapons in the provinces, according to body count, the Lord Inquisitor has swung it during enough executions of blasphemers, heretics, and mages to make the weapon famous. Crowds cheer at the scent of burnt blood. Never mind that Crevax has also wielded Heretic’s End to slay daemons, aberrations, and all manner of monstrous horrors conjured to the mortal realm but reckless arcanists.

Quintin Gleim Lord Inquisitor Crevax The golden dragonborn herself is the image of a warrior angel. Her scales reflect the gods’ given sun. Her armor, the same she’s worn since taking office, shows every sign of repair and rehammering, like scars of battle proudly worn. Crevax is fearless in battle—even careless in zealotry. Despite her seraphic reputation, Lord Inquisitor Crevax has aged. Still formidable, few would wish to face her in battle; yet Crevax no longer strikes out on crusades. Instead, she remains in Ember Cairn to guard the city itself. Warden of the Cathedral of Embers, the most dangerous apostates are brought before her to receive judgment. When she descends into Ember Cairn to perform executions, crowds gather in their hundreds to witness her deeds and hear her words. From the Cathedral of Embers, the Lord Inquisitor also continues to make plans that would see the armies of the Arcanist Inquisition crusading across Etharis. She desires desperately to spread the Eternal Dogma into every realm.

#### Hedric, the High Redeemer

Hedric, a formidable inquisitor in his own right, commands the legions of the Arcanist Inquisition whilst crusading outside of Ember Cairn. Though not much younger than Lord Inquisitor Crevax, Hedric’s frightening legend is beginning to eclipse his superior’s own.

Hedric does not evoke the image of a seraph. His grizzled maw and curved horns make the dragonborn appear more like a daemon, which has aided in eliciting confessions from terrified heretics. Few apostates placed in Hedric’s care go into the afterlife with the stench of sin still upon them, the cleansing of confession allowing each to die redeemed in the eyes of the Arch Seraphs.

Despite his infamy causing most enemies to flee and hide from the High Redeemer, Hedric sees danger everywhere. He is relentless in his pursuit of the eldritch and arcane. He would rather order his troops to raze an entire settlement than allow a single mage to escape his wrath—a matter of tension between the Inquisition and Castinella’s great houses. The provinces are most at ease when Hedric’s pursuits take him far from home, seeking evils in other realms.

#### The Bleeding Man

All cities have their share of urban myths and ghost stories. Not even sacred Ember Cairn is exempt. Over many years, a tale has emerged of the Bleeding Man, an arcanist who has sworn to topple the stones of the Cathedral of Embers and slaughter any who have sworn the oaths and joined the Inquisition. Many inquisitors have died already. Bodies of soldiers or priests have been displayed with the sigil of an Arch Seraph or Arch Daemon carved into the flesh of their skin. The victim is bloodless. A wall beneath their corpse is scrawled with a crimson manifesto and a promise that more inquisitors will meet their end.

The Inquisition is loath to acknowledge the Bleeding Man’s existence. Twice they’ve claimed to have caught him and conducted public executions, yet both times his blood-written threats reappeared within days.

Some folk suspect the Bleeding Man is a sangromancer who escaped the cathedral’s catacombs to terrorize Ember Cairn. Others believe he was tortured to death, and his crimson wraith now haunts the city. In truth, nothing is known about the Bleeding Man. His heritage. His gender. Whether he’s a mortal or a monster.

An obvious theory is that he’s more than one person. Some suspect an enclave of mages operates within the city to spy on the Arcanist Inquisition. If that’s true, they’d be taking a tremendous risk.

#### Sebastián, the Goat Shepherd

Sebastián brings his grazing animals across the mountains periodically, like many shepherds. Unlike many shepherds, he leads his flock to the driest areas of the Shattered Peaks, most at risk of wildfire, and lets them eat up all the ground fuel to limit the intensity of the inevitable burn. Despite the ever-present danger, Sebastián has never been caught in a blaze.

Good-natured and maybe a little reckless, the goat herder often seems to appear at homesteads or villages in the aftermath of a blaze, offering what little comfort and aid he can. Most folk feel lucky to encounter his strange but calming hospitality. Sebastián reminds his countryfolk that suffering is temporary, but a necessary part of life for growth and humility. He does not consider the fires to be a reminder of the world’s chaos or evils, but a message from the Arch Seraphs that life is to be cherished because everything is fleeting.

A rumor’s begun that the humble shepherd is the Arch Seraph Morael in disguise. That he’s come to bless those who’ve suffered and spurn those who haven’t. Indeed, Sebastián does not speak highly of a church that gilds itself in glory while commoners burn and starve. Mortal or seraph, the Arcanist Inquisition has taken a keen interest in meeting this kindly goat herder.


### Quest Hooks

The following quest hooks can be used to flesh out adventures in Ember Cairn.

#### Danger Beneath the Quarry

Miners at a granite quarry have reported mysterious sounds coming from the lower levels of the mine. The rock has also felt unstable. Recently, a huge chunk of stone fell at the bottom of a mineshaft, almost causing a collapse and injuring several miners.

Furthermore, deep in the mines, the workers have discovered carvings of dragonborn antiquity. Many depict stars arranged into runic patterns. Other valuables, including ancient jewelry and precious stones, have also been found.

Alberto Yogren, foreman of the mine, would rather keep the Inquisition uninformed of these troubles. The gnome wishes to unearth whatever treasures might still be hiding in the ancient caves, despite the risks involved in concealing discoveries of possible arcane providence. However, his workers are uncooperative. The miners are almost as frightened of the grinding noises coming from deep inside the mountain as they are the Inquisition’s wrath if Alberto’s excavation is discovered.

Through Ember Cairn’s marketplace, Alberto looks to hire mercenaries to brave the unstable mines. Where do the shafts lead? What lies at the bottom? How long can Alberto continue digging before the Arcanist Inquisition is alerted?

#### Julianna’s Rescue

Julianna of Aurelia was downcast during the Gods’ End. She was once a prominent member of the Watchers of the Faithful but was horrified to witness the violence mortals inflict on each other in the name of faith. Rather than strictness, Julianna taught the love of the Arch Seraphs through kindness.

For a time, she was a healer living in the Shattered Peaks, giving clemency to the sick and homeless. Then the Inquisition came. They accused Julianna of contravening the Eternal Dogma and performing sangromancy with her divine blood.

Now Julianna is housed within the Cathedral catacombs. Her congregation has, in secret, raised funds to hire mercenaries to rescue the downcast from torture and death.

Entering the Cathedral of Ember’s will be its own undertaking, before even thinking of escape. Sneaking in would be nigh impossible. Voluntary capture would be the most expedient and most painful method, assuming the Inquisition did not decide upon execution over incarceration. Sneaking in through the hidden labyrinth would be no safer, unless a guide could be found to help light the way through the darkness and monsters.


#### Whispers in the Black Market

A new commodity has begun circulating in Ember Cairn’s marketplace—tomes and scrolls penned in blood: texts written by the hand of the Bleeding Man. Many of these writings include actual sangromantic spells inscribed on their pages, which could be learned and cast by any mage able to read them. These texts are incredibly difficult to find. Obviously illegal, anyone found peddling them would be dragged immediately to the Cathedral of Embers. There is a doctrine of dissent and anarchy in the Bleeding Man’s words.

Rumors in the market suggest the books and scrolls are being spread by a cult, disciples of the Bleeding Man, who call themselves the Whisperers. Their voices must be small to avoid being heard by an inquisitor’s ear. Finding them through the black market requires patience and guile; many an inquisitor who has searched for them has been found hanging above the market square. Paranoia helps keep the Whisperers safe. They’re suspicious of even other spellcasters, who could be used by the Inquisition to root out their members.

Once contact is made with the Whisperers, they offer many services in exchange for favors. They know of secret entrances into the hidden labyrinth and will help mages get inside. They’ll sell scrolls, potions, and spell components otherwise illegal in Ember Cairn. Meeting the Bleeding Man, if he exists, is only possible to the innermost circle of the Whisperers.

Of course, the Inquisition would also pay in gold or fire for any information they could learn about the Whisperers. The reward received would depend on whether that information was given willingly or yielded under duress in the depths of the Cathedral of Embers.

#### The Empyrean Pit Murders

A series of tournaments in the Empyrean Pit has taken place, each crowning a new champion over the course of several weeks. Each night for the last four, one of those fighting champions has been murdered in a ritualistic manner. The Ember Cairn authorities have not been able to make any sense of the murders, nor can they find any leads.

A new tournament is set for this evening, and the characters are asked to have one of their group enter the tournament. The fights will be fixed in such a way that the character representative will win. Then the characters can set a trap for the murder, who will ostensibly come after the new champion.

A couple of nights after the tournament, a group of Tormach cultists, led by a Champion of Tormach (or some other level-appropriate servant of Tormach) appears out of thin air right in front of the character who won the tournament. They attack as a genuine test of slaughter skills on behalf of Tormach, who sees the fighting pit as a mockery of true battle.
