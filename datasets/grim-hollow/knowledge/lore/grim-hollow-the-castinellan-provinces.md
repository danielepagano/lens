# The Castinellan Provinces

Castinella is a peninsula of harsh climates and harsher laws. Three provinces--Toletum, Faro, and Therpena--scuffled for centuries until unified under the Arcanist Inquisition, a zealous theocratic military organization. Arcane magic is illegal; practitioners face torture and death. The state religion serves the Seraphs, with particular focus on Arch Seraph Empyreus. Many dragonborn have returned to their ancestral home of Ember Cairn to take up Seraph worship.

The face of Castinella presents order and safety, but this masks untold horrors: the Hidden Labyrinth under Ember Cairn, black markets of Toletum, House Wars among nobility. Deep shadows are cast by the bright light of the Inquisition.

## Geography

A huge peninsula with a central mountain range. In the north, the Corova Mountains yield resources for Toletum's ever-working forges. In the south, the Shattered Peaks around Ember Cairn bear irregular formations seemingly broken by some ancient cataclysm. Historians credit Castinella's deep fear of magic to this event.

The Corova River flows from the north, splitting halfway to join the Gulf of Lions. Toletum sits on the northern slope above the river. Faro occupies the fertile land between river branches, enjoying bountiful climate with tall forests of pines and aspens. Southern Therpena has wide coastal beaches, forested mountains below the Shattered Peaks, sunny hills and thriving vineyards.

### Dry Climate

Freshwater scarce throughout. Farming requires creative irrigation clustered around major rivers. Ditches zig-zag down mountains between farmsteads. Communities must cooperate to ensure water distribution. Snow accumulates on mountaintops but streams rarely last through summer. Vineyards climb from green plains over tall hills; orchards grow in peak shadows.

North of the mountains: flat deserts of low-lying trees and brush. Rocky mesas with wind-hewn stone columns. Teeming with life despite appearances--lizards, insects, foxes, deer, elk. Organic material desiccates rather than rots. Branches pale and brittle from years of exposure. Bleached skeletons persist for vast stretches.

### Wildfires

Wildfires suppressed for a hundred years, causing catastrophic fuel buildup. When fires occur, they become crown fires destroying every tree in an area, creating overgrown same-age forests even more prone to burning. Some scholars note this mirrors mishandling of the arcane--wild magic catastrophes more common because of suppression. Those scholars rarely speak for long; they often end up on extended vacations or simply disappear.

Ranchers move cattle through forests to eat dangerous ground fuel. This practice has grown widespread in Faro, where deforestation concerns prompt inquiry into natural preservation.

### Civilized and Wild

Roads are some of the safest in the world, but just beyond the heavily patrolled routes: sleeping drakes, hiding ogres, arcanists gone to ground in dense evergreens. Steep slopes and harsh climate left much of the peninsula uninhabited.

#### Architecture

Appearances matter. Each province has a distinct style developed over centuries. As religious fervor grew, so did the cathedrals. A competitive spirit, indistinguishable from spiritual insanity, drove the three provinces to build taller and more elaborate structures.

The Cathedral of Embers stands above all. An awkward pairing of public community center and theocratic military headquarters--open to the public but closely and severely guarded. Visitors feel watched. Clergy credit this to the Arch Seraphs; really, inquisitors with crossbows and hard-eyed watchers spy on crowds.

Statues of Seraphs on every street corner in major cities. Legend says that in a time of great need, every statue will awaken to guard true believers. The status of this claim has vacillated between religious canon and abject blasphemy more than once.

#### Ancient Ruins

Dragonborn ruins litter the deserts and high mountains. Granite standing stones along old roads. Desert canyons hide tilted stone towers. Old stone bridges, larger than anything modern, connect uncrossable canyons. Pine forests on ridges hide fragmented structures that dig miles into stone, half-buried within the mountain chain.

The dragonborn constructions contrast with elaborate modern buttresses and spires: simple, elegant, built on mathematical principles of simple geometry. Enormous skylights and clerestories perhaps once accommodating the passage of true dragons.

## History

### Unification

The Arcanist Inquisition formed around conqueror-prophet Sagrado Valieda, who claimed a vision of Empyreus supplied his mandate. His band of heroes gained popularity keeping towns safe, then amassed support from Toletum noble families and common folk. The growing movement convinced each town and city, one by one, to swear fealty to the church under the Inquisition's watchful eye.

Those who resisted struggled to garner community support. Few common folk had been directly hurt by magic, but they believed the stories--real or imagined. Many were directly helped by Valieda's group. The church endorsed and funded them. Most townsfolk have only known life under the Inquisition and prefer it: safer roads, steady commerce, someone to call when magic threatens.

What began as well-intentioned holy warriors evolved into something systematic and deadly. Monstrosities hunted, evil sorcerers brought low, innocent mages captured dutifully. But when the Inquisition unified the peninsula, Valieda decreed all arcanists hunted and executed regardless of crimes or control. These deaths seen as sad, solemn, and ultimately necessary.

#### Shadows Behind the Throne

The state religion follows Arch Seraph Empyreus, with whom Valieda claims to have communicated directly. Some believe he spoke not with Empyreus but with Arch Daemon Beleth, who takes great pleasure in toying with priests and holy workers. Within every monster-ridden mine, every labyrinth entrance, there is the faint sound of something less than human, laughing.

The clergy renounce these rumors. Even uttering Beleth's name is outlawed--punishment: slitting the diaphragm so the victim may never laugh again and draw the humor-loving Arch Daemon's attention.

#### Torture

Once a last resort, torture has become revered ritual. Performed in churches with masses gathered around and hymns sung as torment "cleanses" captives. Many tortures still performed privately.

The Penitent Mage: a hollow nail-filled statue large enough to fit a person inside. Once shut, the mechanism is suspended over fire; screams channel through the statue's open mouth along with steam and smoke. In Toletum, these are built within amphitheaters that cast sound down city streets. Workers hear the din of screaming mages while going about their business. Many bear the sounds with a smile, believing each scream makes the world safer.

#### House Conflicts

Noble houses constantly compete for power, sometimes turning bloody. The clergy suppresses some wars while claiming to suppress all--but a well-placed war between houses can diminish both, keeping any one from rivaling the Church.

Some house wars are allowed; some may have been instigated by the clergy. Discreet mercenaries hired to eliminate chosen family members. Nobles disappear from beds, ships are lost at sea. In Toletum, wars are typically crushed. In Faro, the clergy often turns a blind eye, stoking fire if one house grows too powerful. In Therpena, House Therentine enjoys favored influence for early submission to the Inquisition.

### Life as an Arcanist

Being magically gifted is a secret one struggles to reveal even to close friends or family. Families often disown children showing magical signs. The Inquisition captures individuals for carrying magical blood, even if ungifted themselves. Children locked away by parents; magical outbursts often leave families injured or worse.

Successful hiding requires raising gifted children normally while teaching concealment. Families burdened with fear of discovery may be torn apart before the Inquisition can do it. Secret tutoring networks exist briefly but too often leak, and the Inquisition strikes quickly.

#### Curses

People cursed by magic face illegal existence. Only Inquisition-approved cure: swift death. The Inquisition posits that a cursed soul is already tainted, and more magic--even to cure--adds corruption. A black market exists for cures: fakes, sold at monstrous cost by greedy merchants, and honest healers who treat at little or no cost. Strangely, the honest healers get caught more frequently despite existing in fewer numbers.

#### Transformations

Physical transformations punishable by immediate death without evidence, testimony, trial, or ceremony. Vampires, lycanthropes, aberrant horrors, hags, fey shown less mercy than spellcasters.

Exception: the Seraph Transformation. When an individual undergoes it, the signs are taken as a gift. A conclave convenes to verify authenticity. Powerful mages sometimes attempt to exploit this avenue--whether through self-delusion or cynicism, searching for arcane paths to divinity. Generally detected and killed. Some seeking divine paths stumble into lichdom; others are tricked by Daemons disguised as Seraphs and transformed into fiends.

#### Monster Hunters

Relied upon but distrusted. To citizens, they're blessed warriors. To clergy, troublesome necessities--possibly mages masquerading as do-gooders. Learning about magic is frowned upon, but understanding prey is key to survival.

The Order of Templars: Inquisition-backed monster hunters permitted to study (never practice) magic. This has created friction--the Church has exerted pressure to drop or imprison these members. The Inquisition holds that study is necessary to properly fight magic.

The School of the Dread Templar, the only college with legal magical texts, sits near the Cathedral of Embers. Clergy appointed to surveil the school resent their positions--proximity to blasphemous study contaminates their careers, making it difficult to climb the ranks afterward.

#### Vilified Communion with Nature

Speaking to plants or animals is absolutely outlawed. Nature itself demonized: its beauty called seductive, its power unfathomable. The Church equates primal magic with corrupting arcane magic. Logging and earth-churning mining are categorized as divine interventions. That these are extremely profitable is certainly just a happy coincidence.

Most people don't subscribe to outright nature-hatred. The most fervent anti-Church activists are farmers, hunters, foresters--those who rely on nature's gifts.

#### Wine and Festivals

Castinellan people work hard and play harder. At least four times yearly, festival days with wine, dancing, singing, and audacious performances are sponsored by the Church. Traveling troupes wear patron house colors and spread the Church's good word. Content must extol Church virtues, though unsanctioned material is sometimes performed to public-facing outrage and secret delight.

Bards tolerated as long as they show no magical abilities--that is, they hide their arcane knowledge so well as to be indistinguishable from an amazing performance.

## Provinces

### Faro

Founded by Farolithia, a common-blooded merchant. Fertile land with an incredibly defensible position: cliffs on one side, sea on another. The family entered nobility with fierce ambition and strong ties to the people.

When the Inquisition rose, Faro resisted. The Inquisition launched a twelve-day siege. Faro seemed certain to prevail--until the twelfth day, when soldiers began slaughtering each other atop the walls. Madness moved like a plague through the army. The Faro family retreated into a cavern behind their palace, never seen again. The heavy stone doors have been closed since. Soldiers guarding the door sometimes hear weeping from the other side.

The Inquisition claims the residence is tainted with dark magic and keeps the palace guarded and closed. Outskirts thrive with strong global markets and bountiful fields, but things sour closer to the city. The Inquisition rules with an iron hand. Festivals somber with heavy indoctrination. More executions than any other province. Whispers of rebellion increasing.

#### The White Fleet

Castinella's powerful navy. Pristine white sails fill the harbor. Hundreds of ships ensure safe maritime traffic along trade lanes and coasts. The fleet guards against pirates, Valikan longships, and foreign predation. The Inquisition is vigilant at docks, ensuring no arcane threats enter through the port. Sailors pay lip service to Church tenets but are happy away from the Inquisition's gaze.

#### Noble Houses

The collapse of House Faro left a power vacuum. Many small houses vie for influence. The most wealthy compete relentlessly, but when one house nears domination, smaller houses cooperate to bring them down--combining resources or through assassination. The most powerful houses:

**Heverna.** Large clan of shipwrights. Engineer and construct the largest and most reliable ships. White Fleet success traced to Heverna innovations.

**Damiara.** Weavers known for amazingly light and strong fabrics. Process closely guarded. Attentions turned to sails for the White Fleet; quotas and value rising.

**Nalcia.** Control many lumber mills. Influential in logging despite deforestation concerns. Laborers disappear mysteriously but new workers are plentiful.

#### Notable Individuals

**The Nameless Queen of Faro.** A woman sentenced to execution claimed Faro blood and vowed to see the downfall of Church, Inquisition, and any supporting noble family. When the pyre was lit, a massive explosion killed everyone within fifty paces. No sign of her body found. Rumors persist that she gathers resources and allies for war against Faro's authorities.

### Toletum

Industrial center where most Corova Mountain ore is processed. Small smithies exist elsewhere but only Toletum has army-scale production, maintaining control over weaponry flow.

Above the smog of the lower city, the white-marbled upper city houses wealthy bureaucrats. Stark geographic segregation by wealth. Administrators and business leaders hold significant legislative sway. Lucrative military supply business prompts lobbying for warmongers and expansion of the Inquisition beyond Castinella--the likelihood of instigating wars doesn't bother the war enthusiasts.

#### Toletum Crime

Organized crime has grown like mold. They siphon weapons for the black market. Better hidden: magical potions, scrolls, and arcane focuses--illegal but findable if you know where to ask.

The Castibona family: if any criminal group can be said to have good intentions, it's them--and they happen to be the most doggedly pursued. More of their number are arcanists than any other group. They do jobs opposing the Inquisition's war on magic. You'll pay dearly, but if you need someone with magical blood spirited away from the Inquisition or given a secret escape from the peninsula, the Castibonas are the ones.

The Castibonas spread rumors that Inquisition leaders secretly serve Beleth. The Church issued edicts that Castibonas worship Arch Daemons and practice arcane magic (half-true). The resulting stalemate persists.

### Therpena

Sunny hills, thriving vineyards, coastal estates. House Therentine dominates through early submission to the Inquisition, enjoying greater influence than other noble houses. Strong ties to the Church mean their partnership is generous and mostly devoid of suspicion.

#### Cathedral of Music

Once a summer palace for the Calcien family. Famous entertainment venue with world-class shows, remodeled with divine iconography. Any musician aiming for prominence must first book here, thus ingratiating themselves with the Church.

Draws people from other regions who would fear to tread elsewhere in Castinella. The Calcien family assures acquaintances they're safe inside--no overt arcane magic in full view. This makes it a valuable neutral meeting ground for all types: spies, diplomats, and less savory organizations.

#### The Empyrean Pit

Fighting pits catching Castinellan fancy. Fighters face each other in hand-to-hand combat in a shallow water pit. The Church has made these battles expressions of faith, using them to proclaim the Eternal Dogma. Violence prepares citizens for arcanist executions. Fighters of extreme skill can be recruited. Occasionally, a fighter reveals arcane powers in the heat of battle and is quickly arrested.

## Quest Hooks

### The Kribblen Gang
Well-known traveling minstrel band of four excellent musicians with a retinue of tumblers and artisans. They lure arcanists by asking in low, conspiratorial tones if they're practitioners, promising camaraderie. Then they sell them to the Inquisition. Investigation needed when someone close disappears after contact.

### The Death Mage of the Labyrinth
A dragonborn child of thirteen thrown into the labyrinth for illegal magic use. Taught himself spells to survive: light, then killing and cooking rats, then larger problems. His magic curdled and became a deadly, awful thing. He stopped eating rats, began plucking souls instead. Faces staring from the dark became indistinguishable as mortal or monster; he took their lives as well. Now a Lich Troll in a hidden fortress of buried towers. Speaks if approached but becomes frightened by living faces. Wants you to die and be silent like the small army he's created.

### Trouble in the Mines
The town of Salume in Therpena dug a well. Excavators disappeared into a vast cavern. When dragged up, they kept whispering about a beast that spoke in the dark, claiming days had passed though only minutes had elapsed. If adventurers wait too long, excavators begin disappearing one by one.

### Whispers in the Woods
Farosi lumber expansion causes increasing accidents, injuries, and deaths. Lumberjacks quit, citing ceaseless babbling whispers. Companies and clergy denounce this as hallucination, but loggers are being forcibly retired after going mad overnight. A coven of hags has moved in, delighting in the smorgasbord of innocent souls. The madness they inflict spreads through contact.

### The Little Witches of the Mountain
Community of magically gifted folk said to hide in the Shattered Peaks under the Inquisition's nose. "Little witching" has become slang: seeking answers in foolish places, or being arrested without good reason. Inquisition sends adventurers to investigate. What they find: people living in close commune with Summer Court fey who feel protective and allow no harm.

### The Thaumaturge
The clergy spreads word of foreign spies called the Thaumaturge, demonized as boogeymen hoping to corrupt the populace and spread dark magic. Rumored to make expeditions into the labyrinth to free and recruit evil wizards. The Inquisition uses the idea as pretext for raids and arrests--the group's secretive nature lets them accuse almost anyone. Rumored council members were once close counselors of noble families. To surviving magic users, the Thaumaturge is a spare hope. Real question: do they even exist?

## Ember Cairn

Holy city in the Shattered Peaks. Home to the Cathedral of Embers, headquarters of the Arcanist Inquisition. Built high in the mountains on the ancestral home of the dragonborn, shattered by an unknown ancient cataclysm. Since the Unifier, dragonborn have resettled to rebuild.

Pilgrims flock to Ember Cairn simply to pray on the Cathedral steps. Priests stroll plazas doling out blessings. Many believe it the safest place in Etharis with no taint of magic able to pierce the veil. Yet the dangers are on full exhibit: corpses rot in gibbets, skulls of the punished piled into ashen cairns. The faithful have nothing to fear. Everyone else treads carefully.

### Geography

Cradled between three stone monoliths. Western peak: Cathedral of Embers with polished marble and white domes among the clouds. A switchback staircase ascends where pilgrims crowd and pray at the gates. Despite appearances, the Cathedral is a fortress housing an army.

Rock beneath the Cathedral extensively excavated into labyrinthine dungeons with countless cells and interrogation rooms. The public has no concept of its massiveness.

Populated districts rest in the canyon between the three peaks. At night, brazier light on white granite makes the city appear to burn--a red glow visible from Therpena's hills like a glowering eye, constant reminder of zealotry.

Heavy construction throughout: wooden scaffolds over ancient dragonborn stones. Narrow lanes choked with pilgrims and laborers spill into markets or quiet prayer plazas. Markets always loud with merchants, smugglers, and counterfeiters.

Sacred roads wind through the Shattered Peaks with bridges and stairs spanning great ravines. Some are elegant granite with Arch Seraph statues; others precarious timber. Taverns and businesses along the roads cater to travelers. Heavy patrols keep routes safe from monsters, bandits, and apostates.

Granite quarries strip mountainsides, leaving sheer cliffs. White carved stone makes Ember Cairn easy to spot from distance. Sculpture connects dragonborn to both modern religiosity and ancient heritage--young dragonborn demonstrate devotion through stunning granite reliefs of Arch Seraphs.

### History

In the Era of Antiquity, dragonborn settled the peninsula to the Grove Maze. Almost nothing is known of this neolithic culture--cave reliefs, broken statues, oral stories. Whether they descend from true dragons is uncertain (they share more with elves than wyverns). The cataclysm made them nomadic; Ember Cairn's reconstruction filled the cultural hole.

Valieda chose Ember Cairn as Inquisition stronghold--originally a secluded prison for arcanists. Broader reasons included inviting dragonborn to rebuild. Rumors persist: he feared archaeological discoveries might revive arcanist culture or dragon worship, so he erected his fortress above those ancient stones. Others suggest the catacombs already existed as a ready-made pit.

The church has spent vast wealth and labor reconstructing the city. Dragonborn have been loyal servants of the Arch Seraphs. Ember Cairn rallies the diaspora home. Dragonborn are not the only residents--humans are populous, and dwarven and gnomish ingenuity relied upon for construction and excavation.

### Cathedral of Embers

Perched on a narrow summit behind high curtain walls. Gates open only for the Inquisition and condemned souls. Pilgrims crowd the steps outside. A sizeable keep encircled within walls; white towers crowned with domes, arched windows keeping vigilance. Inquisitor knights summoned for blessings and sacred orders. Judges, jailers, interrogators housed within.

#### Cathedral Catacombs

Vast tunnel network. The Inquisition claims to have built them, but scholars presume this a lie--they're too vast. Countless cells and crevices for apostates thrown in and forgotten. The Inquisition isn't even aware how many mages they've locked away.

Some rooms are unlocked. The accused wander freely searching for a way out. Those finding their way back through the Cathedral (path said lit by Zabriel) are offered redemption through confession or joining the Inquisition. Escaping another way means recapture and death; none have accomplished it without magic. Black runes inscribed in stonework prevent most spells, making teleportation impossible.

Deeper in, stonework becomes ornate: crumbling railings, steps descending into caverns, great dragon warrior statues holding vaulted ceilings, dragon bas-reliefs, astrological charts of the night sky. Accused who flee this deep may lose pursuit but face even worse monsters in the hidden labyrinth.

#### Hidden Labyrinth

Ancient labyrinth beneath Ember Cairn containing dragonborn history, still largely buried. Stone slabs and dragon bone fragments discovered in surrounding peaks. Only well-known entrance through catacombs, but other openings exist in mountain fissures. Archaeologists explore these caves in secret.

The Inquisition suppresses access--officially for safety, but tension exists with dragonborn who believe the Church separates them from their heritage. These voices are small; the Inquisition easily draws links between labyrinth interest and occult curiosity. The Inquisition's vigilance isn't without merit: deadly monsters, mad arcanists, and incomprehensible artifacts lurk in the dark.

Ember Cairn's black market knows multiple secret entrances and provides access for a price. Clients include treasure hunters, historians, and those desperate enough to rescue condemned mages through the catacombs.

### Notable Locations

#### Marketplace

Dense market near the heart of the city. Food vendors, fabric sellers, stonework artisans, bead makers, jewelers, counterfeiters. Preachers scream the Arch Seraphs' will on corners. Divine healers mend ailments. Acting as an unsanctioned prophet is dangerous--the Inquisition is intolerant of religious values contravening the Eternal Dogma.

Smuggling less scrutinized if non-magical. Dragonborn heritage souvenirs, counterfeit sacred baubles, stolen granite carvings, rare contraband. Most lucrative illicit trade: people and information. Enemies of the ecclesiarch pay great sums for Inquisition secrets--legion movements, locations of assassination-ripe judges, identities of the interred. Folk smuggled in to rescue loved ones rarely survive the return journey.

#### Empyrean Pit

Elaborate indoor fighting arena. Walls carved with winged seraphs falling upon writhing daemons; overhead dome painted with the heavens. Tortured arcanists sometimes herded in--enfeebled magic no match for armored inquisitor knights who dole out violent executions. Monsters also caged and brought in. Audiences don't cheer drunk--they chant solemn hymns and righteous adulations. Every match overseen by an Inquisitor Priest.

Different from Charneault tournaments where death is rarely the aim. Here the goal is eliminating creatures who offend the Arch Seraphs. Detractors ponder if Tormach derives equal satisfaction from the Pit as its namesake. Gambling officially illegal but available through black market.

### Important Individuals

#### Lord Inquisitor Crevax

Golden dragonborn, image of a warrior angel. Scales reflect the gods' given sun. Armor shows every scar of repair. Wields the flaming longsword Heretic's End--famous for its body count. Aging but still formidable. Warden of the Cathedral of Embers. The most dangerous apostates are brought before her for judgment. When she descends into Ember Cairn for executions, crowds gather in hundreds. Plans crusades across Etharis to spread the Eternal Dogma.

#### Hedric, the High Redeemer

Commands Inquisition legions outside Ember Cairn. Grizzled maw and curved horns make him appear more daemon than seraph--which aids confessions from terrified heretics. Few apostates in his care go to the afterlife with sin's stench still upon them. Relentless in pursuit of the arcane. Would order troops to raze an entire settlement rather than let a single mage escape--a matter of tension between Inquisition and great houses. The provinces are most at ease when Hedric's pursuits take him far from home.

#### The Bleeding Man

Urban myth turned real threat. An arcanist sworn to topple the Cathedral of Embers and slaughter any who've joined the Inquisition. Bodies of soldiers or priests displayed with Arch Seraph or Daemon sigils carved in skin, bloodless. Walls beneath scrawled with crimson manifesto. The Inquisition twice claimed to have caught him; threats reappeared within days.

Theories: sangromancer who escaped the catacombs. Tortured-to-death wraith. More than one person. An enclave of mages operating within the city. His cult, the Whisperers, spread sangromantic texts through the black market. They know secret labyrinth entrances and sell otherwise-illegal scrolls and potions. Meeting the Bleeding Man requires reaching the innermost circle. Paranoia keeps them safe--they're suspicious of even other spellcasters.

#### Sebastián, the Goat Shepherd

Leads grazing animals across the Shattered Peaks' driest areas, letting them eat ground fuel to limit wildfire intensity. Despite ever-present danger, never caught in a blaze. Appears at homesteads after fires offering comfort. Teaches that suffering is temporary but necessary for growth. Does not consider fires chaos but messages from Arch Seraphs that life is fleeting.

Does not speak highly of a church that gilds itself while commoners burn. A rumor has begun that he is Arch Seraph Morael in disguise, come to bless the suffering and spurn those who haven't. The Inquisition has taken keen interest in meeting this kindly goat herder.

### Ember Cairn Quest Hooks

#### Danger Beneath the Quarry
Miners report mysterious sounds and unstable rock. A huge stone fell, nearly causing collapse. Deep in the mines: dragonborn carvings depicting stars in runic patterns, ancient jewelry, precious stones. Foreman Alberto Yogren (gnome) wants to excavate secretly before the Inquisition learns. Workers are uncooperative, frightened of both the grinding noises from deep inside and the Inquisition's wrath.

#### Julianna's Rescue
Julianna of Aurelia, once a prominent Watcher of the Faithful, was a healer in the Shattered Peaks giving clemency to sick and homeless. Accused of contravening the Eternal Dogma and performing sangromancy with divine blood. Now in the Cathedral catacombs. Her congregation secretly hires mercenaries. Entry options: nigh-impossible sneaking, voluntary capture (expedient but painful), or through the hidden labyrinth with a guide.

#### Whispers in the Black Market
Tomes and scrolls penned in blood--the Bleeding Man's texts--circulating in the marketplace. Actual sangromantic spells anyone can learn. Incredibly difficult to find. The Whisperers (his cult) offer services in exchange for favors. Many an inquisitor who searched for them has been found hanging above the market square. The Inquisition would pay in gold or fire for any information.

#### The Empyrean Pit Murders
Series of tournaments crowning new champions over several weeks. Each night for the last four, a champion murdered ritualistically. Authorities have no leads. Party asked to enter the tournament (fights fixed for them to win) and bait the murderer. A group of Tormach cultists appears to attack--a genuine test of slaughter skills on behalf of Tormach, who sees the fighting pit as a mockery of true battle.
