# Chapter 2: The Bürach Empire

Languishing in the withered shadow of its former glory, the Bürach Empire is a land of relentless greed and infamous struggles for power and control. With its faith fractured and its people divided, the Hearthkeepers of Bürach manage the remains of this once-great nation. They siphon the flow of information to better control where it leads and upon which ears it lands, hoping to slow the dissolution of the empire’s power and its people’s hopes and dreams.

The empire still has an ambition that its might can no longer support, which only serves to fuel the flames of grim, and ultimately futile, determination. The Bürach people feel the growing urge to burst into a blaze of glory rather than allow their legacy to be left like an untended pyre of embers in winter, waiting to succumb to the cold.

Having once ruled over the greatest stretch of territory in Etharis, the Bürach Empire’s influence exceeds its current borders. From north to south, the great expansion once saw the golden age of the empire’s most daring dreams made reality, as nearly the entire continent flew the flag of the Bürach Empire.

However, with such a vast continent and foreboding barriers like the mountainous Grey Spines, the empire’s conquests and desire for greener pastures ended. Soon after, the military resources necessary to maintain the ludicrous amount of territory grew too great. From this recession new nations were born, which drove the borders of the once-unstoppable superpower back to the heart of the continent.

From the ashes of what remains of the empire’s most glorious days, echoes of their past victories ring out as its end draws near. In the face of its impending fate, this plagued lion is determined not to fade silently into obscurity.

## Geography

The Bürach Empire’s landscape has changed greatly over many years. Covered in a vast and deep forests, the tree line recedes ever so slowly to quench the empire’s constant demand for lumber. While efforts to maintain the forests have proved effective, there is little that can prevent the empire from one day taking more from the forest than they plan to return.

### Western Bürach

The density of trees in the west ranges from open and scenic to thick canopies that sunlight couldn’t hope to pierce even on the brightest of days. Much is concealed inside of the woods that serve as the withering heart of the empire. The forest at the empire’s center is home to a wide variety of tree types, tending toward oak, pine, and birch. However, the more westward one travels, isolated pockets of the forest occasionally receive bursts of humid air. This anomaly has transformed the local flora there into tangled jungles of unfamiliar plants.

While rain is plentiful in the forests of the empire, small valleys hidden within the deeper sections of the trees prove fatal as sudden flooding can catch unsuspecting travelers in a deluge that ends in unceremonious watery graves for the unwary.

The Empire’s westernmost land comprises a rocky coast that hugs the Emerald Sea. The terrifying waters beyond the shore hold monstrosities and dangers far beyond what the imagination can conjure. The dual threat of sea monsters and Valikan raiders has made the coastal regions of Abendland as treacherous for settlers as the deep forests of the heartland.

However, bold and brazen mercenaries and monster hunters of every type patrol the Bürach forests looking to make coin from commoners’ troubles. The city of Walstein, now nothing more than an old ruin of ash and soot, has become a camping ground for adventurers seeking a challenge in the surrounding wilderness. It’s said that even the Empire’s soldiers are sent to Walstein to train and hone their combat prowess, as the area is riddled with monsters of all kinds.

For those who travel by the roads, the forests prove treacherous but remain traversable. Nevertheless, wise travelers choose the widest roads and the shortest routes between settlements wherever possible. Over the years, the roads have become broader as a result of lumberjacks shaving the forests’ edges back from the established trade routes. Regardless of their size, in the Bürach Empire, all roads lead to Altenheim.

The capital city overlooks the miracle that is Lake Osea, crowning the great lake’s southern shore with its high walls and great towers. The great City of Joy flourishes despite the hardships of the Empire’s smaller settlements. Like a leech syphoning the nutrients from a healthy body, Altenheim draws all the wealth and produce from the surrounding provinces into its own coffers.

### Southern Bürach

The southernmost part of the Bürach Empire is occupied by the Rock-Teeth Mountains. Narrow and dangerous roads wind through valleys and along cliffs to ancient mines and forgotten settlements. But the Rock-Teeth Mountains are also home to one of the greatest wonders the Bürach Empire has ever produced: the tiered city known as Stehlenwald.

Stehlenwald resembles a celestial stairway carved from granite and marble and adorned with great statues—a feat of architecture and engineering unrivalled throughout Etharis. The forges of Stehlenwald spew fire and ash endlessly. The city’s main trade is in metalworks: jewelry, tools, machinery, and above all, weaponry.

Stehlenwald does not sit at the peak of the Rock-Teeth Mountains. The summit belongs to an enormous palace of opalescent towers and silken banners. Living inside this collection of spires is an archmage by the name of Martorius. Swearing no allegiance to any nation, he claims to predate the Bürach Empire and is invested in how it shall progress in the years to come.

### Eastern Bürach

Following the eastern roads along the foot of the Grey Spine mountain range is Unterland, a marshland comprised of weathered buildings and battle-hardened soldiers. Great care has gone into maintaining the roads that pass through the swamps as it attempts to swallow any structures built there.

Reinforced and regularly repaired, the roads stand out among the old buildings that form the city of Olstenburg and the town of Vollendorf. Both locations rely heavily on the trade routes that pass through them, with the surrounding land yielding few resources beyond rich hunting grounds.

The fortress city Grabenstein blocks the mountain pass through the Grey Spine, guarding against the great shadow of Ostoya. The city’s forges bellow. Its towers are guarded by hardened soldiers. Yet its ancient walls crumble, the city having been conquered and rebuilt by Bürach several times.

### Northern Bürach

Nordenland stands between the icy chill of the north and the warm winds of the south. The people who call this place home are accustomed to constant rain and storms, and the greatest hazard by far is the regular river flooding. The endless turbulence makes traversing Nordenland arduous.

Settling the area is nearly impossible for outsiders. Many homes in this region are left abandoned to floodwater or ruinous winds, just as new homes are constructed in a safer location. Ultimately, most who can’t tolerate this seminomadic lifestyle have since fled to the city of Landhaus or made the journey to reach Runeheim.

Mt. Vengeance lords over the surrounding mountains with a regal, volcanic fury. Its active eruptions disrupt travelers and worsen the looming storms covering the area. This once-dormant volcano awakened during Gods’ End and has grown more restless ever since.

In the wake of divine devastation, the volcano has become a place of intrigue for adventurers and scholars alike. While few visited the volcano before the death of the gods, since its awakening, many have traveled there to investigate why its activity has become so hostile and so constant after centuries of silence. Among the ash and waves of heat are intense pulses of arcane power.

The ever-superstitious Norden fear the mountain must be casting judgment down upon the Empire’s sorrowful state. Others believe it’s a portal erupting up from the Netherworld in the aftermath of the Gods’ deaths, spewing out all manner of hellish monsters into the mortal realm. Of the mercenaries, mages, and monster hunters who’ve dared to investigate the volcano, only scorched survivors emerge with frightful tales of the monsters lurking within.

Researchers are growing ever more concerned that Mt. Vengeance’s eruptions and tremors are growing more frequent for seemingly no reason. The mountain spreads its ashes over the Empire as far as Duesenberg.

### Varied Landscapes

The variety of land types that comprises the Bürach Empire has made its people diverse, adaptable, and innovative when it comes to surviving.

Between the marshlands, the forests, the mountain valleys, and the coastlines, Bürach has amassed a wealth of natural resources. What one region lacks due to constant storms or infertile land, another can provide through trade. Communal prosperity through commerce greatly contributed to the Empire’s growth, and these mercantile practices leak into other nations.

Provided the plants continue to grow, the rains bring fresh water, and the mines yield enough minerals, the Empire has always been thought able to survive any calamity it may have to endure. A lack of trade, due to the hazards and monsters spreading through the empire, adds to the darkening mood of Bürach’s citizenry.


## History

A nation born in the Era of Expansion with the crowning of the first Emperor Indorius I, the Bürach Empire came into the world of Etharis as a child of necessity and emerged as a titan of prosperity. Their might was renowned. Their luxuries were plentiful. Standing at the pinnacle of what a civilization can achieve, progress became inertia propelled by their success. Not without its hardships or sacrifices, the Empire’s determination to touch the heights of the sky began by claiming all that the soil had to offer.

The smaller kingdoms and tribes of central Etharis first became united by their faith in the Four Divines: Aurelia, Galt, Ulmyr, and Maligant. This pantheon chose Indorius I as the one to begin forging their new empire. As a boy named Thancred, the future emperor was discovered by an order of priests called the Hearthkeepers.

Acting on visions from their gods, the priests witnessed Thancred return a lamb to life with his bare hands, demonstrating his divine providence. As one, the priests fell to their knees and named him Emperor Indorius I, first of his line. To aid in his task of unifying the realms, Emperor Indorius I received four gifts filled with the divine power of each of the gods.

From the pure wish of healing and protection, Aurelia brought forth a breastplate like no other. Gifted to him, she hoped it would forevermore shield his bloodline from harm.

From the beacon of his everlasting authority, Galt forged a crowned circlet crafted from golden sovereignty. Bestowing it to Indorius I, Galt demanded that the Emperor’s lineage know not only how a nation is valued, but how much the ruler serves a nation by the laws they enact.

From a longing to see and understand, Ulmyr removed their eye and placed it directly into the Emperor’s hand. Blessing them with wisdom and the power of foresight, they imparted words of warning: “Consistency brings comfort while complacency can only bear the fruit of calamity.”

From his side, Maligant unsheathed a longsword and presented it to the Emperor. In doing this, Maligant cut the emperor’s finger and revealed the blade’s sacred name as soon as his blood touched its edge: Dämglesvir, the “Might of Heaven.” Maligant praised Indorius I on the victories yet to come and told him that a sword wielded by a coward can cut nothing. To be mighty is to bow only to the dawn of what cannot be changed.

With indomitable blessings laid down by the heavens upon the Bürach Empire’s gleaming fate, there was none that could oppose them. One region after another fell into line, as the empire marched, slaughtered, negotiated, and conquered their neighboring territories. With their bold momentum, they saw fit to unite the world and stamp out their foes. For the glory of the Four Divines the provinces put their faith in the emperor’s leadership and confidence.


### Gods’ End

The four Divine Gifts were far more valuable than anyone could have known. Within each of the Sacred Artifacts, all the gods had stowed a small part of themselves. More than just a fraction of their powers or will, inside these items held the manifestations of the very essence of each god.

None could have predicted the disaster that would come from the trust the gods put into Emperor Indorius I’s descendants. Out of love for the first emperor’s reign, the Hearthkeepers enacted the Edict of Eternal Blood, which decreed only the emperor’s direct descendants could be raised to the throne. Purity was maintained within the coming imperial generations. Decade after decade, the emperors resembled their glorious ancestor less and less.

After the birth of Leopold I, the Bürach aristocracy feared the day he would be crowned. Leopold I’s troubles revealed themselves in his youngest years. He was an impatient and insensitive child, very often cruel. He would imprison or torture servants and subjects for even minor infractions. He insisted on executions at noble feasts, like the executioners were minstrels performing for his guests. His early life was spent terrorizing those under his protection and betraying the people who wanted to believe in him the most.

But of all the mindlessly cruel acts he became the source of, the worst was how Leopold I tainted the Sacred Artifacts and by consequence, the very gods themselves.

In an apparent religious fervor—though it’s debated whether his faith was genuine or another cruel manipulation—Leopold I declared an imperial order that the Bürach Empire would now only worship the One True Deity, branding the remaining three as usurper deities. He never named which god would serve as the Empire’s sole patron.

Like wildfire, feuds sparked into the unforgivable inferno of war. Each region insisted their favored god was the One True Deity that would lead the Empire into its next glorious age of victory. The Four Divines themselves began to quarrel. The minds of the faithful were set aflame as the gods’ once beautiful ideals tarnished into obsessive and unyielding rhetoric. The Four Divines no longer saw the potential in the Bürach Empire’s capacity for greatness in unity, but now were bitter rivals that demanded absolute devotion to only themselves.

To end the violence, individuals from the four provinces conspired against the emperor. Their assassins swept into the imperial palace and killed Leopold I, and with his death his influence on the gods through the Sacred Artifacts diminished.

Aurelia was the first to awaken from her futile vanity and realize the gods’ battles would soon destroy Etharis. Using all her divine strength, she shielded the mortal realm right at the moment of utter calamity.

From the sky, like comets or shooting stars, fell countless sparks of glittering light to the earth. Each one had been an angel in service to the gods, and their downfall was the devastating portent that the gods were no more.

### Aftermath

In the wake of the Gods’ End, every region of the Bürach Empire faces hardships threatening to crumble their greatest achievements and cripple their hopes of better days to come.

Though the Hearthkeepers ordained a new emperor with forged Sacred Artifacts, and the bonds of shared history kept Bürach from disbanding entirely, the civil war left deep divisions. As a result, border disputes have become more frequent, folk have grown paranoid, and travelers are often greeted with hostility.

Furthermore, the true Sacred Artifacts that had protected the realm and been a symbol of the Emperors’ divine right of rule went missing. After absconding with them from the imperial palace, Leopold I’s assassins intended to bring the Artifacts to Nordenland. They hoped the wizards could reverse whatever eldritch corruption had overtaken them. They never reached their intended destination.

To this day, the fate of the Sacred Artifacts remains a mystery. Most believe the assassins, each loyal to different provinces, quarreled among themselves for possession of the holy treasures. The treasures were separated to stop any single individual from controlling the fate of the Bürach Empire, and they have since been lost to history.

A more theological tale says the Artifacts blazed with holy light and vanished before the eyes of their possessors. Only one who can bring all four back together is worthy to wield them and claim Indorius I’s legacy.

### Rising Shadows

With the absence of the gods, a new force of dread now stalks the lands of the Bürach Empire, a powerful and corrupting presence that spreads through the realm like rot within an apple. Where it wanders, death follows. Hearts are poisoned and minds are turned to wickedness. No commoner dares even whisper its name; such is the fear of this rising shadow.

Aberrations have appeared in its wake. The forests of Abendland are filled with many-limbed mutants, their flesh twisted into tortured shapes. They attack wayfarers and set upon villages. Nordenland is plagued by corrupted flora, including trees with human limbs and faces instead of knot holes and branches. Wolf-headed monsters prowl the hills of Rauland, leaving settlements abandoned wherever they roam. Unterland has reported sightings of corrupted warriors. Their armor is melted into their flesh. Their weapons are extensions of their limbs, and they hunt from place to place, killing indiscriminately.

The sigil of this horror has been found scrawled on church doors and in basements—a deer skull crowned with many antlers. There are no survivor accounts—at least none that can be trusted. It’s described as a behemoth wreathed in shadows, whose body is that of a rotting corpse and whose bony crown rakes through the clouds. Its trumpeting shriek transforms people into monsters. None know what it is.

It has no other name than “the Great Beast.”

## Provinces

Bürach War Table

The mighty Bürach Empire is comprised of four provinces: Abendland as its heart, Nordenland as its mind, Rauland holds a pickaxe, and Unterland carries a sword.

Each province began as its own unique kingdom or culture. The natural resources of Abendland’s fertile woods made them a mercantile kingdom of great wealth and influence. Magic and philosophy defined the complex cultures of Nordenland. Rauland was a land of builders who resisted conquest through technology. Meanwhile, the proud Unterfolk keep a long military tradition stretching deep into their history as warring tribes.

At the Empire’s height, the provinces worked in harmony for the sake of Bürach’s bright future. Yet the Empire has never been homogenous. In the wake of the Gods’ End, the cultural divides between the provinces have only grown deeper, and mutual prosperity has given way to fear and self-interest.

### Abendland

Abendland is the region where Aurelia, goddess of protection, healing, and the hearth found worship. It is also where the holy church of the Hearthkeepers began. According to scripture, Aurelia instructed her first priestess, Amara, to strike the ground with a spear, from which a wellspring of water emerged. This spring swelled into the blessed Lake Osea, which fed the surrounding land until it grew into a verdant forest. The city of Altenheim was built upon Lake Osea’s southern bank, and from its cathedral the Hearthkeepers spread their holy message.

Abendland is rich in farmland and timber, but its most valuable commodity has always been religion. Scripture marks the province as the birthplace of Indorius I. With such a blessed history and divine authority, the Hearthkeepers controlled the fates of many who sought the favor of the gods.

To maintain their influence in the aftermath of the Gods’ End, the Hearthkeepers were swift to crown an imperial successor, a distant relation of Leopold I. However, in most recent times the church’s grip on power may finally be loosening, for good or ill.

After Emperor Aratron II was tragically lost at sea along with his imperial ship, the pregnant Empress Valeria was sheltered and protected by the Hearthkeepers at all times. Her only public appearances were alongside Pope Roland Murrich, head of the church. After Empress Valeria gave birth, the Hearthkeepers were quick to coronate the newborn infant and shield him from public view or influence.

Yet the citizenry of the Bürach Empire cries out for governance. Faith in the Hearthkeepers has waned with the absence of divine magic. The realm teems with monsters, internal strife, and foreign threats—compounded by an empty throne. In answer, the nobility have established their own authority: the Säullan Council.

Self-appointed and defiant of church authority, the Säullan Council comprises powerful leaders from each of the Empire’s provinces. Though far from politically united, the Council negotiates trade and resources between the provinces to deal with the Empire’s many troubles. Once the young Emperor Odalric I grew into his station, the Säullan Council began serving as close advisors and soon superseded the Hearthkeepers as the Emperor’s most trusted aids.

Over the years, Pope Murrich has grown weary and ineffectual. With his protégé, Aratron II, dead, and Emperor Odalric I distrustful of the church, many under Murrich have questioned how many years of service he has left.

Of all the choices that Emperor Odalric I has made during his ascension to the throne, the most controversial has been the permanent installation of the Säullan Council as an advisory body. While it was believed that the council would be disbanded when the emperor came of age, Odalric I insisted they remain to offer their knowledge and perspective.

At the time, the Emperor cited his inexperience as the primary reason for this choice. Others wonder if the seeds of mistrust had been planted in the young ruler’s mind, and the Emperor clearly seeks the advice of people not directly tied to the Church of the Hearthkeepers.

Meanwhile, tensions within the Hearthkeepers rise. Many feel Pope Murrich no longer commands the influence over the throne he once did. For this reason, the church quietly searches for Murrich’s successor while it waits for his death—timely or otherwise.

Meanwhile, a bishop in Altenheim has set the hearts of the faithful ablaze with his impassioned and prophetic sermons. His surging popularity among the crowds has led to an eventual summons to speak with Odalric I at the Imperial Palace. Little is known of Karlos Reinach’s life before joining the Hearthkeepers, yet the church has seen mounting support for him to succeed Murrich, if indeed Karlos has captured the Emperor’s ear.

#### Notable Individuals

Emperor Odalric Pongor Aurelius I. Practically coronated in the womb, no other child’s birth was more anticipated than Odalric I. The babe knew nothing of his father, Aratron II, and was raised between his mother Valeria and Pope Murrich. Throughout his life, the empress and the pope would hover over him in desperate attempts to mold his mind.

However, once introduced to the Säullan Council, Odalric I became fascinated in learning about the world outside Altenheim. He desires to understand the threats facing his empire. Odalric I spends long hours questioning members of the Säullan Council, learning economics from Lady Büchandt, strategy from Lord General Roemer, production from Master Lorand, and philosophy from Professor Welgirson. The council members share their vast expertise openly, fostering close relations with the young Emperor.

It isn’t certain whether Odalric I has matured enough to understand the difference between honest guidance and political coercion from his councilors.

Bishop Karlos Reinach. A quiet man of the cloth, Bishop Reinach’s contemplative manner disguises the fiery passion of his faith, which explodes forth during his sermons. Reinach preaches that the Gods’ End was only the first omen of the apocalypse. The second is the Great Beast.

The Hearthkeepers have, until now, decried any public mention of the Great Beast as heresy. This makes Reinach a controversial figure within the church. Yet the Bishop’s unorthodox preaching has captured the attention of the Emperor, creating a pathway to bring Odalric I back into the Hearthkeepers’ sphere of influence.

Nevertheless, members of the church remain suspicious of Bishop Reinach, whose history before the Hearthkeepers is mysterious. Rumors designed to discredit him link Reinach to the Celestial Gate—a doomsday cult that praises the Great Beast and claims the world’s end is inescapable.

Gatekeeper Chantal Sommerkin. Gatekeeper Sommerkin is dead. Or that is what the rulers of Abendland wish to believe. Sommerkin led a dangerous cult called the Celestial Gate, preaching that the Great Beast was divine retribution for all humanity’s corruption. The Hearthkeepers had her assassinated to prevent such heretical lies.

Despite this, Sommerkin’s blasphemous writings continue to circulate. Though the Celestial Gate was disbanded, her disciples quietly infiltrate each tier of society. Their goal has remained the same—turn Abendland into a theocracy in preparation for the end of the world. Previously, they aimed to depose the emperor for this cause. Now subtle influence is their weapon of choice. This change in direction has led to the frightening suspicion that the Gatekeeper herself is still alive, scribbling her heresy in some dark place.

### Nordenland

Inside Mount Vengeance

Nordenland is old. Its living cultures predate recorded history. The land is rich with the spark of magic, entwining Norden traditions with arcane practices and spiritual communing. The people of Nordenland have less fear of magic than other Bürach citizens. Their leaders have weaved the power of the arcane since the god Ulmyr first taught them how to grasp it.

Diversity runs through the cultures of Nordenland. While folk who reside in the west hew to the Bürach Empire and its traditions, the further north and east one travels, the closer the Norden begin to resemble their Valikan neighbors.

When Emperor Indorius I was crowned, Ulmyr became part of a united pantheon. Many Norden followed the wisdom of their deity, and Nordenland became a province of the growing Bürach Empire. Yet the eastern settlements preferred their own ancient ways to the laws of a foreign power, resisting the absorption of their territory.

The Raulfolk came to harvest timber and metal for their workshops, logging the forests and cutting open the earth. The Hearthkeepers came to spread their religion, chasing away the primal spirits the Norden communed with. Soldiers from Unterland arrived to enforce the empire’s laws and tithes. Norden fought for a time, but as they were outnumbered, they eventually had to accept the empire’s embrace. Their simmering anger never waned.

In the aftermath of the Gods’ End, the Norden has abandoned their oaths to the Bürach Empire and reverted to their old traditions. They live closer to nature, refusing to fell living trees or lift stones without first beseeching the primal spirits to which their ancestors spoke. While some villages make sacrifices to the Primordials, other settlements whisper to the faerie folk who dwell in the deep wildernesses of the north.

Nordenland has long been the focal point of magical studies in the Bürach Empire, both to their acclaim and their great folly. The city called Ulmyr’s Gate is no longer found on any map. The ambitious culture of the Bürach Empire emboldened the northern mages to believe they could look upon the faces of the gods.

Arcanists and occultists from across the empire found sanctuary within the city walls, toiling in unrestrained magical research. They built great citadels aimed toward the heavens. When they performed a ritual intending to part the veil between Etharis and their gods, the entire city was lost, ripped into the maelstrom of the Ethereal Plane.

Ulmyr’s Gate became a cautionary tale: all mages are children playing with fire. At any moment they could burn the world to a lifeless cinder with their ignorance and ambition. After Ulmyr’s Gate, magic became heavily sanctioned, and the arcane practices of the Norden divided along cultural lines.

The regional capital of Erlefurt began as a mage college. The wizards and sorcerers trained and educated through its long history have advised emperors and reshaped battlefields. Erlefurt has since grown into a city. Its reputation as a place of enlightenment and astounding knowledge is also tempered by a cautious approach to the dangers of magic. The professors and graduates of Erlefurt’s Magister College are considered among the most meticulous and powerful mages Etharis has known.

By contrast, the druids of Nordenland are viewed with contempt by the empire. The Order of the Golden Bough is a druidic circle who practice the old ways of their ancient peoples—rumored to be pact makers and heretics. The Golden Bough are scattered across small settlements deep in the wilderness of east Nordenland, teaching their apprentices to wield magic in harmony with nature.

The east Norden defend their boundaries with primal wards crafted from the magic of the forest. Yet these protections are failing. Nature has begun to foul in the presence of the Great Beast. Verdant forests are transmogrified into hellscapes, where blood and flesh polyps rain from the sky. Terrifying monstrosities ingest every living thing to grow their cancerous forms.

Indeed, the only thing keeping the Norden from all-out civil war is the ongoing threat of the Beast. Despite their division and the Hearthkeepers’ decree, all the great minds of Nordenland are bent towards the Beast’s destruction. To this end, they offer riches and magical secrets to any who can help recover the Sacred Artifacts that appear linked to the birth of this monstrosity.

Another plague upon Nordenland is the spreading ash from Mount Vengeance. The Norden have made their lives alongside the extreme whims of nature, but the horrors that spew from the mountain are more than fire and smoke. Adepts from the Magister College have begun to study Mount Vengeance from its slopes. Though many theories abound, none can be proven without folk daring enough to adventure within.

#### Notable Individuals

Archduchess Maelfa Erlekind. The last heir to the founding house of Erlefurt, Archduchess Maelfa is an assertive leader who has never once bowed to an emperor. Nevertheless, the Bürach Empire accepts her as ruler of Nordenland. The duchess achieves her autonomy through diplomacy, understanding that trusted mages are too valuable for the empire to risk alienating Nordenland.

Maelfa has the love of her people and the respect of the mage colleges, enabling her bold leadership. Rumors circulate that Maelfa may also have the support of the Thaumaturge—a secretive cabal of powerful beings said to control global politics from the shadows.

Alfric, the “Red Hart.” Archdruid to the Order of the Golden Bough, Alfric is suspicious of all who trespass upon the lands he calls home. It’s said that Alfric patrols the borders of Nordenland disguised as a giant red stag. The animal is an omen to folk wayfaring through his realm.

Alfric detests the children of civilization. His body is scarred by Imperial assassins. Therefore, every outsider entering Nordenland’s primordial forests is treated with suspicion until they can prove themselves harmless or peaceful. Despite this, the Archdruid’s goal is the same as any ruler’s—protect his people from the Great Beast. To this end, he secretly trades knowledge with the few Erlefurt mages he trusts.


Professor Franciszka Welgirson. A powerful archmage and expert on the nature of the planes, Professor Welgirson is the court mage and arcane tutor to Emperor Odalric I. The young ruler values Franciszka’s lessons with breathless attention. The power of the Säullan Council means the professor can advise the emperor in private, without oversight from the Hearthkeepers—a dangerous proposition to some, considering the unpredictable nature of magic and those who wield it. The professor is despised at court as the “Wraith of Welgirson Hall.” She was present during the destruction of Ulmyr’s Gate. Courtly gossip ponders whether Franciszka’s mind is as corrupted as her spectral body. What eldritch schemes could she be whispering into the mind of an impressionable emperor? ### Rauland

Rauland is a place said to have been crafted by the hands of Galt himself. As such, those born to the province are taught from a young age the nobility of labor and self-reliance. This is the core of Rauland’s culture.

Rauland began as a collection of dwarven and gnomish fiefdoms in the southernmost reach of central Etharis. Their territories spread from the Rock-Teeth Mountains to the north and east as far as the Grey Spine.

For generations the Raulfolk tinkered in their mountain halls. They forged weapons and jewelry of breathtaking beauty. They built the mountain cities of Stehlenwald and Grabenstein—feats of architecture and engineering unrivalled throughout Etharis. Carved from granite and marble, Stehlenwald resembles a celestial stairway ascending the mountainside toward the heavens. The Quartz Palace that crowns the city remains one of the wonders of the Bürach Empire.

Witnessing the skills of their artisans and the bountiful resources of the mountains, Rauland’s neighbors coveted such wealth. The Raulfolk resisted annexation within their fortress cities. Though Grabenstein fell to the fires of war, Stehlenwald endured for centuries. When finally the Bürach Empire began to take shape, Stehlenwald yielded its sovereignty in exchange for open trade and provincial self-governance.

Bürach came to rely on the craftsmanship of the Raulfolk. Thousands came to Raulish guilds to learn carpentry, smithing, architecture, and construction. The forge fires of Stehlenwald burned ceaselessly to fill the need for the weapons and tools required by a growing empire. In every realm the Bürach Empire has ruled, the touchmarks of Raulish artisans are found.

The demand for instruments of warfare only increased during the Gods’ End. Some say when the madness fell upon Galt, he began controlling the minds of his followers directly. Stehlenwald placed itself under a strict curfew. The city watch patrolled the paved streets to ensure artisans moved quickly between their homes and workshops without deviation. Farmlands were taxed relentlessly to feed the city. New factories were opened. New inventions were discovered.

The Raulfolk were worked ceaselessly to outpace the production of the other provinces, their rulers believing that Raulish weapons and technology were the key to winning Galt’s dominance.

After the events of Gods’ End, Rauland was a husk. Its people were exhausted. Its resources were spent. Though it remains the industrial epicenter of the Bürach Empire, it relies more on metals from Unterland and timber from Nordenland for its production. This has weakened Rauland’s borders and reduced the quality of their craftsmanship to an echo of its former glory.

Then the Great Beast came.

There have always been monsters in the darkness of the mines that honeycomb the Rock-Teeth Mountains. Now the cacophonous shriek of the Great Beast summons them to the surface. Horrors of flesh and stone emerge to attack settlements and leave behind dismembered remains. Entire villages are silenced in the wake of hyena-like monstrosities. In Stehlenwald itself, feverish artisans build golems from muscles and sutures. The Great Beast’s influence is said to have poisoned their minds.

Rauland has prided itself on its fortifications. No foe has ever breeched Stehlenwald’s walls. Now, every entrance, every battlement, every corner that can be reached has been reinforced for fear of the Great Beast’s coming. Those who cannot build pray to the Arch Seraphs instead. As many have pointed out, what use is a city wall when a creature’s mere presence turns commoners into monsters?

#### Notable Individuals

Archduke Ulrich Eizenherz. The dwarven lord of Rauland, Archduke Eizenherz, was fair minded, organized, and well loved in his younger years. Then age and strife tainted his mind with fear and paranoia.

The Archduke has since become reclusive. Despite being a picture of quiet dignity while in public, a crippling terror of the dark fills him privately. He goes nowhere without a light, even when he sleeps. Rumors swirl that Eizenherz sees the eyes of the Great Beast in his dreams. The abhorrent horror whispers secrets to him, though what they contain he shares with no one.

Another longstanding rumor is that Eizenherz holds a Sacred Artifact deep in the vaults of the Quartz Palace. Which among them has never been revealed, and the Archduke does not travel into such gloomy places anymore. Though perhaps it’s his connection to such an Artifact that enables the Beast to touch his mind.

Master Izsak Lorand. A peculiar member of the Säullan Council with a disinterest in politics, Izsak seems carefree and distracted. Very little concerns him, and he commonly abstains from discussions that fall outside his areas of expertise. As an artisan, he’s not always treated with the same level of respect by the other councilors—a fact that doesn’t seem to bother him. He advocates well for the resource needs of the empire’s skilled and dedicated craftsmen, but is otherwise scribbling designs when talk turns to other matters.

If there’s one quality about Master Lorand that no one can argue, it’s his diligence. He may have no patience for politics, but he’s extremely patient with “projects.” His gnomish tinkering and experiments have made Izsak highly favored by the young Emperor Odalric I, who asks for frequent demonstrations of Master Lorand’s creations.

Izsak has taught Odalric I that failure is inevitable, but to carry on in the face of uncertainty is the spirit of invention. As he once told the young emperor, “To be brave is to be scared but kind. To be fearless is to be foolish and dead.”


### Unterland

Unterland’s history dates back to warring tribes of hunters who lived in the marshlands and mountains of the eastern Bürach Empire. For generations they fought among themselves. Minor kings quibbled for territory, whilst their lands were being conquered by warlords and monsters. Some tribes collapsed; others united. Eventually, it was the Unterfolk who rose from the lion’s den as the strongest among them.

Worshipping the war god Maligant, the Unters chose generals for their strength and cunning. They forged strong alliances but also knew when to sacrifice allies for victory. They learned to wage war with surgical precision instead of relying on brute force—to break their enemies’ spirit as well as their armies.

The Unterfolk defeated the Vorkan tribe by sabotaging a dam, sweeping away an army camped beside a river. They won the war in a single night without losing any soldiers on their side, though many innocents perished along with the Vorkan army. When Falaris warriors marched north, the Unters harried them for days through thick marshlands. The invaders realized they were trapped only after the ambush was sprung.

Favored by Maligant, the Unters even managed to capture Grabenstein—a feat many tribes failed to achieve during the Era of Expansion. The dwarven fortress fell to siege engines designed by Maligant’s priests, and the crumbling remnants of the walls are still visible in the city to this day.

Using Maligant’s teachings, and with access to Grabenstein’s forges, the most formidable military Etharis has known rose from Unterland. They became the sword of the Bürach Empire when Indorius I came to power, defending its borders and expanding its reach. Rival nations feared the legions of Unterland. Yet its reputation has tarnished in recent times.

Unterland suffered greatly during Gods’ End. Its armies were expected to protect the Empire from strife, yet its soldiers’ loyalties were split between their nation and their god. Thousands died in pointless battles. In the chaos that followed the death of the Four Divines, those same soldiers were needed more than ever to defend the Empire from both internal and external threats. Unterland’s legions were scattered. Countless soldiers never returned home, left on warfronts to fight for the future a splintering empire.

In the regional capital of Olstenburg, Unterland tries to rebuild its strength. Here, the Academy of Swords dates back to Maligant’s earliest teachings in warfare. Every able-bodied youth is sent to the Academy for two years of training and military service, helping Unterland maintain its standing army. Yet soldiers are needed in every province. The most talented recruits are sent into battle as quickly as new ones can be trained.

The Academy of Swords produces infantry composed of pikemen, swordsmen, and archers. The strongest youths or noble heirs may be chosen to become heavy cavalry. The Academy also trains scouts and rangers, sometimes in forts hidden deep in the wilderness of Unterland, where their skills are best sharpened.

With shadows rising inside the Bürach Empire, another type of military school has also emerged. Those that train monster hunters. The old strategies used to repel armies are not always effective against swarming monstrosities—particularly those spread by the wandering of the Great Beast. Though monster hunter guilds are not always sanctioned in Unterland due to their mercenary reputations, the teachings of Maligant certainly encourage cunning methods of warfare like those employed in the hunting trade.

The need for monstrous knowledge also extends beyond the borders of Bürach. The Unters still remember the horrors of the last war with Ostoya, when the sun melted from the sky and death crawled from the earth. Though both nations reserve strength to deal with internal strife, the garrison at Grabenstein fears that one day Ostoya’s bloody grasp may reach across the Grey Spine.

#### Notable Individuals

Lord General Vassily Roemer. A beloved figure of both martial prowess and diplomacy, Lord General Roemer is widely recognized for his strategic mind and honorable bearing. He was appointed to the Säullan Council for his loyalty to the Empire. His dedication to protecting its realms is unquestioned.

Though one wary eye watches the shadow of Ostoya, Roemer is forced to divert troops elsewhere to deal with threats across the provinces. With never enough troops or resources, the Lord General relies on mercenaries more than he’d like.

Meanwhile, the military leadership in Unterland worries Roemer is too distracted by the politics of Altenheim to recognize the true threat rising in his own province: the cult of Tormach.

Lady Yule Büchandt. Trained to have a strategic mind at the Academy of Swords, Lady Büchandt left military service. Instead, she traveled. Her journeys took her to every province and beyond the borders of the Bürach Empire, and she learned much about the empire’s neighboring nations.

A master of commerce, Büchandt could put a price on anything. She puts little stock in loyalty or faith, and instead puts all her trust in coin. Her rivals at court say she isn’t above accepting bribes. Regardless, she has become vital to the function of the empire. Though her tithes and taxes are not always popular with the people, she’s an expert at ensuring her fellow councilors have the financial resources they need to hold the nation together.

Captain Damon Andval. When Maligant fell, his two lieutenants competed for the title of god of war. The Arch Seraph Empyreus embodied righteousness and honor; the Arch Daemon Tormach applauded strength and violence.

Tormach believes violence is the truth of war. Killing is the only virtue. Survival is the only victory. The Arch Daemon is a blunt hammer compared to Maligant’s keen blade, and his perpetual demand for bloodshed drives warriors into a battle frenzy.

With countless threats looming over Unterland, a growing movement believes Empyreus has failed as a deity. Captain Andval leads these voices, though he must bide his time to convert more soldiers to the cult of Tormach. Their belief is that with the Arch Daemon’s strength, the shadow of Ostoya can be cleansed, the Valikans subdued, and the Great Beast destroyed. Many fear Andval will try to overthrow Lord General Roemer’s command.

### Other Locations of Interest

#### Walstein

Walstein, once a large city within the Bürach Empire’s northwest, was razed during the Gods’ End. Norden mages burned it to ashes in Ulmyr’s name. The event left a permanent scar on the landscape and a deep cultural wound for which the folk of Abendland have never forgiven their northern kin. The city’s fate helps justify the strict regulation of magic.

Today, Walstein is a giant cemetery, a silent labyrinth of fire-blackened buildings and empty windows, grown with moss and haunted by ghost stories. Monstrous horrors lurk within the ashen ruins. These twisted aberrations skulk into the surrounding countryside, leading to the terrible rumor that Walstein’s destruction was the first visitation of the Great Beast.

The monsters that emerge from Walstein make the region a gruesome but lucrative hunting ground for mercenaries and monster hunters. Such guilds occasionally use the old buildings as bastions. Walstein also attracts treasure hunters looking to plunder its forgotten secrets.

Vagrants and criminals with nowhere else to go camp inside the city to shelter from the wilderness and from the soldiers stationed outside the ruins. Even a makeshift market has been established by travelling merchants and swindlers, who trade in rumors, equipment, and treasures plundered from Walstein’s dark corners.

Soldiers of the Imperial army are also garrisoned at Walstein. Recruits are sent to perform training exercises and help slay the monsters that emerge from the ruins. Though the soldiers’ presence deters the city from festering into a haven for brigands, their numbers are too few to enforce the Emperor’s laws effectively.

Academics of the prestigious Erlefurt colleges are occasionally seen practicing their mage-craft in Walstein’s ruins— the very city their forebears destroyed. The mages’ interest in the city is known only to themselves. Yet they appear to be searching.

#### Gräffendam

Gentle snowfall frequently drifts over the Gräffendam, a towering architectural marvel of Bürach. Built from obsidian-black granite and spanning a plunging valley, it was once thought built by the dwarves of Grabenstein. However, the time of its construction is a mystery even to them.

The water contained within the reservoir is a further enigma. It forever remains at a temperature just above freezing and can only be cooled or heated by arcane means. Crops watered from the reservoir produce greater yields. Potions brewed with the water are more potent. These properties have made the aristocrats and landowners of Gräffendam among the wealthiest in the Empire, who bottle and sell the waters of the reservoir as a product called Aquanos.

At the top of the dam is the wintery township of Gildreath, a gorgeous wonderland of wealth and luxury. Their trees are evergreen, despite the climate. Their gardens provide perfect crops. The privileged few who make their homes in Gildreath spend their lives indulging in excess and frivolity, though the same cannot be said for the desperate masses living in squalor at the base of the dam.

Those residing beneath Gildreath live in a frigid hive of crime and poverty. Stacked buildings climb the outer wall of the dam, built one upon the other like barnacles climbing a ship’s hull. The limited hours of daylight caused by Gräffendam’s long shadow provided a nickname: The Shade. But its true name is Ünterdam.

The wealthy elite of Gildreath tightly control the profits from Aquanos. The Shade’s citizens receive a pittance despite Ünterdam being responsible for transporting Aquanos from the freight elevators that descend from Gildreath to the river docks that ferry it across the Bürach Empire.

Secretly, the flow of Aquanos is handled by the Ebon Syndicate. The Syndicate receives a cut of the shipping revenue and control over The Shade in exchange for helping Gildreath avoid Imperial taxes and quashing civil unrest in Ünterdam. With unchecked authority over The Shade, the Syndicate is free to conduct whatever additional business they wish.

#### Castle Martorius

Few can say more of the wizard Martorius than that he is at once brilliant, eccentric, and cruel. The archmage presents himself with a scholar’s composure and a devil’s charm, yet his true nature or intentions remain secret. He is unquestionably dangerous. Those who visit him without invitation are sent back in pieces, if they’re ever seen again.

Martorius’ magnificent castle appeared over Rauland after the Gods’ End. Its glittering spires are a wonder to behold. Dignitaries visiting it report a structure of indeterminable dimensions, the rooms splitting apart like pieces of a puzzle before rearranging themselves into new shapes and sizes. Automatons of a design alien to even Stehlenwald patrol its halls. Illusionary people cater to Martorius’ guests, whom the wizard is pleased to entertain with parlor magic and his menagerie of bizarre creatures.

Given Martorius’ stature, both the Hearthkeepers and the Säullan Council have chosen to accommodate him. In return, the archmage offers his counsel on matters plaguing the realm. For all this, no one in the imperial court believes Martorius is truly benevolent. Some wonder in secret if he’s even of this world. Throughout the Empire, the archmage’s agents have made inquiries, searching high and low for the Sacred Artifacts.


## Quest Hooks

In the Beast's Wake

The following quests hooks provide ideas for adventures that might take place throughout the Bürach Empire.

### Aberrations

An urchin living in an isolated village in the Bürach wilderness is selling the eye of some strange creature. He claims it has brought him horrible nightmares. But it’s also given him tremendous luck. He seems at once frightened of the object and reluctant to part with it.

During the evening, the village is assailed by a horde of aberrant beasts: two-headed wolves, swarms of grotesque giant rats, a bear with its bones growing through its skin. Stories of these minions of the Great Beast are whispered about throughout the taverns of the Bürach Empire, yet they are far more horrifying in person.

If the urchin survives the night, he asks for help fleeing the village. The Beast’s minions seem drawn to the urchin’s monstrous eye, which inflicts ghastly visions and flashes of possible futures upon whomever possesses it. Additionally, if the eye is carried from the village, bestial abominations seem to follow wherever it travels and attack each night.

To what manner of aberrant horror did this eye belong, and is it possible to attune to it for greater magical abilities? What connection does it have to the Great Beast? Could it in fact be Ulmyr’s Eye itself, one of the Sacred Artifacts that disappeared during the Gods’ End?

The answer to any of these questions is a mystery to be solved. In the meantime, the possessor of the eye must seek safety. Perhaps they could shelter in Altenheim with the Hearthkeepers or bring the eye before Emperor Odalric I himself. Alternatively, the wizard colleges of Erlefurt might provide answers. The archmage Martorius also seems to take an interest in such arcane objects.

Whichever path is chosen, the discovery of a Sacred Artifact could shift the balance of power in the Bürach Empire. All will seek to control it, or its owner, for their own political machinations.

### Usurper of Olstenburg

A warlord calling himself Indorius VII of Unterland claims to wield Dämglesvir, the Sacred Artifact and Sword of Emperors. The Hearthkeepers have been swift to brand him a heretic and traitor. Yet soldiers of the Imperial army are defecting to join Indorius VII’s growing legion at a fortress near Olstenburg. The warlord appears to be planning to besiege the Unterland capital: a first step toward usurping the Imperial throne.

Captain Andval has declared for Indorius VII and musters a second legion near Olstenburg. His Tormach-devoted warriors sharpen their blades in hungry anticipation for war. Andval feverishly preaches that whoever wields Dämglesvir is aligned with both Empyreus and Tormach—the surviving lieutenants of the war god Maligant.

Whether Andval truly supports Indorius VII’s claim to the throne and believes he carries a real Sacred Artifact is unknown. It’s equally likely the Captain is using any pretense to sow division in Unterland, which can be used to sway more soldiers into joining the cult of Tormach.

Lord General Roemer marches the army of Altenheim to bolster the garrison at Olstenburg. Meanwhile the Säullan Council consider alternatives to open conflict. Weakening the Bürach army through civil war is perilous, especially should Ostoya choose this moment to march west through the Grey Spine.

Professor Welgirson supports a diplomatic approach. She wishes to learn whether Indorius VII’s weapon could actually be Dämglesvir. Lady Büchandt, meanwhile, allocates gold toward hiring spies and assassins to silence this rebellion with a single death in place of thousands. Whoever is chosen for such a task must also be trusted to bring the Sacred Artifact in question back to Altenheim for inspection.

Emperor Odalric I has been sequestered by the Hearthkeepers for his protection. It’s uncertain what Nordenland or Rauland will choose to do, and the killing of the current emperor could curry favor with a warlord usurper.

Of Indorius VII himself, very little is known. Whether he has a true claim to the throne, believes he wields the weapon of a god, or is simply an opportunist with an Arch Daemon’s blessing, nothing is certain. The Bürach Empire teeters once more upon the edge of oblivion. Few will escape the maelstrom without first choosing a side.

### The Sacred Grove

Deep inside the Nordenland woods there is a sacred grove where villagers gather to pray. They observe rituals whispered to them by a fey creature called a puck. This ritual includes leaving behind a human sacrifice.

The sacrifice is not killed, however. They’re simply left in the grove. In return, the villagers may pick fruit, hunt wild game, and cut down timber from within the forest. Yet the fruit regrows, the animals remain plentiful, and the stumps return to towering trees within days. With endless resources, the villagers deify the fey who tends the grove. However, the sacrifices given to the puck are not always willing. Some are abducted from neighboring settlements. Others are captured in battle, or are criminals brought to the grove as punishment for their transgressions. In any case, the slew of disappearances in the region haven’t gone unnoticed. Nearby towns are filled with missing posters and contracts for adventurers to bring back loved ones. A competent group of investigators arrive in the village and learn of the sacred grove. The villagers beg outsiders not to interfere with their ancient ways, yet are themselves powerless to defend the grove. Rebeca Ungurean Victim of the Sacred Groves

Slaying the puck ends the cycle of sacrifice for resources. However, doing so is not an easy task. The trees and shrubs animate to defend their fey keeper, which reveals the fate of those given to the grove. Their bodies have become twisted into unnatural shapes and their skin has hardened into bark. Their horrified faces are still visible among the foliage that grows from their bodies, having been transformed into plantlike horrors.

Whatever culpability the villagers have in the horrors of the grove, their community does not survive if their fey patron is destroyed.

### Field Experience

Erlefurt mages are among the most well educated in Etharis. Yet no amount of learning within the safe walls of a college can prepare a student for the true dangers magic presents in the wider world. They must experience it, firsthand.

Pupils are sometimes sent to the ruins of Walstein to gain the kind of field experience only possible outside of Erlefurt. It’s also considered a pilgrimage for newly trained mages to witness the destruction their ancestors wrought. This warning that a great responsibility burdens those with the ability to weave magic is a lesson hard learned.

Vulf Avorrs is one such student about to journey to Walstein. He wishes to hire an escort to ensure a safe pilgrimage. Vulf admires and seeks council from any spellcasters in his escort group. He asks many questions during rest times. Vulf’s particular areas of interest include the outer planes and the unknowable creatures that live therein, such as the ancient Aether Kindred.

When exploring the ruins of Walstein, the young mage ventures deep into difficult to reach places within the city. He seeks protection from bandits who scour Walstein for treasures, preying upon others seeking fortune in the ruins. Monsters of all sorts prowl the back alleys and collapsed buildings as well. Warbands of fiendish gnolls emerge from the most isolated parts of Walstein, though their source of origin is unknown.

Whatever dangers are encountered, Vulf never loses determination for his quest. He always probes deeper toward Walstein’s ruined center. If it isn’t revealed already, Vulf’s escort may eventually learn that at least part of the apprentice mage’s magic is drawn from an otherworldly being. That voice in Vulf’s head is driving him to discover some secret that rests among Walstein’s rubble, though what that secret is the young pupil doesn’t yet know.

That secret could be a Sacred Artifact yet to be recovered, or another arcane object of great power and peril. Or it could be Vulf’s imprisoned patron. The Daemon psychically calls out for aid, compelling young warlocks like Vulf to release it. In such circumstances, will Vulf’s loyalties remain with his escort? Or will the apprentice arcanist be corrupted by the voice whispering promises in his ear?

### The Heart of Vengeance

### Mark of the Beast

The sigil of the Great Beast is found scribbled in the journals of missing people, graffitied onto basement walls below taverns, and scarred into the skin of disgraced nobles. Its appearance signifies the presence of the Celestial Gate. This doomsday cult tries to spread its influence quietly through society, like the tendrils of a mind-corrupting parasite.

As the Hearthkeepers continue to deny the existence of the Great Beast, the Celestial Gate is of great concern to the church. Fearful gossip sweeps through the empire: a colossal monster roams the land. The Hearthkeepers instead blame such troubles on the actions of an eldritch and violent cult.

Afraid that members of the Celestial Gate may have infiltrated even the Hearthkeepers, Pope Murrich hires hidden agents to investigate the cult. Such a quest is likely to lead across the Bürach Empire, following leads and whispers of cult activity that could implicate powerful folk from each province.

Minions of the Great Beast seem to pursue Pope Murrich’s agents. The cult attempts to assassinate meddlers in their affairs. Rumors imply that the Celestial Gate’s leader, the dead prophet Sommerkin, is still alive and has been raised as a monster to be the immortal voice of the Great Beast. If true, only through her permanent demise can the cult’s ideology be halted.

## The City of Stehlenwald

The profile of the ash-covered city of Stehlenwald leaves many travelers fearful of their next breath. Thick clouds of smoke rise from ever-lit furnaces. Falling white flakes of soot cover the streets as footsteps reveal quality cobblestone roads crafted long ago. The heat vented into the granite and marble walls makes the city cozy in the winter but stifling in the summer.

The grey ash builds and builds into clouds that gather to block sunlight. They dissolve, however, before any rain can fall to clean the streets of the soot. It’s like the sky itself is determined to rob the people below of any comfort. Intention is inside every brick and carved slab, illustrating the industry of the people who call Stehlenwald home.

The city is old. Iron and steel cores are revealed as statues crumble, and the subjects of the carved murals and reliefs are now unrecognizable. The tapered shape of the city forms a grand staircase shaped from the stripped remains of the mountain that watches over the city from above the smoke and fog. Inside the city walls, reverberations reveal the constant motion and noise of the machines that overwhelm the senses.

Amazing yet unstable clockwork creations roam the roads, ostensibly performing tasks that range from the vital to the mundane. Iron and brass horses march down the stone roads pulling wagons and carriages. Lanterns remain lit with thin pipes leading to the glass panes as the air fills with the smell of burning chemicals and ignited gas. Even among the ruined remains of its new form, the city’s architectural majesty may never be entirely eclipsed.

### Geography

The city of Stehlenwald takes the shape of the mountains that it has been carved into. The city is littered with a hodgepodge array of steps and four main tiers made from cobblestones atop carefully shaped granite and brick. The city is like a staircase leading upward toward the celestial world, if heaven was made of metal, granite, quartz, and clockwork.

Many who call Stehlenwald home speak of how the city ushered in a new age of technological enlightenment, and how the mages of the world quiver at the power the city’s denizens have learned to harness. Others care little for the politics of the world and believe that science has a price that politicians cannot fathom. The technology, of course, can be just as untrustworthy and prone to abuse as the politicians who wield power and the wizards who wield magic.

Stehlenwald is really two cities: one open to the air and the other below the surface city. Even the above-ground city has a closed-in feel, with high walls and close stonework, giving the impression of a labyrinth or dungeon. To those not used to dwarven living, it can feel claustrophobic. To the dwarves and gnomes, the overcity is as spacious as a Charneault meadow.

#### The Four Tiers

The overcity of Stehlenwald is built on four tiers. The lowest tier, just inside the city’s thick and massive walls, houses the markets, businesses, shops, and guard stations. This tier is often the only one that visitors to Stehlenwald see. The rulers of the city do their best to make this area presentable, keeping it clean through the use of clockwork cleaners that scrub away ash and soot after the markets close and the inhabitants and visitors retire to homes, inns, and hostels.

The second tier is the business and work district. Smithies, masons, tradespeople, and other labor-intensive trades are practiced here. The place rings and thuds with the sound of hammers and chisels—and the occasional scream of an industrial accident. This tier is also where a bevy of gnome inventors ply their trade, turning the metal masterpieces of the dwarven smiths into functioning (and too often malfunctioning) machines.

The third tier contains the homes of the citizens and residents of Stehlenwald who do not wish to live their lives underground. The homes here are mostly built of stone or built into the mountain itself. A couple of wooden structures stand out among the stone and metal, which is an eyesore to the dwarves but a comfort for the elves and other cultures that prefer the wood of their childhoods.

The fourth tier is the administrative district, where decisions are made and power wielded. The Quartz Palace dominates this tier, along with the guildhalls, administrative offices, military headquarters, and other official buildings that need access to leadership. A few of the nicest homes in Stehlenwald can also be found here. The statuary here is of the highest quality, and the waterfalls that pour down into the depths of the city make for a majestic backdrop.

#### Waterworks

Though the wind grows cold in the winter, the constant radiating heat from the steam pipes keeps the citizens warm year-round. The smoke and heat rising from the large pipes being vented directly into the air melts much of the snow that crowns the nearby mountains’ peaks.

As water runs down the mountains, the city collects it. People can partake of the exposed aqueducts throughout the city, but once the routes go underground, the water serves as the city’s water-and steam-powered lungs, keeping factories working and homes warm.

Since the Bürach Empire began imposing a large fine for businesses, establishments, or homes that burn wood, the city has turned to burning coal and gas to maintain the fires required for steam production. Experiments with lightning promise a great source of power—assuming those who build with it can control its immense and deadly capabilities.

#### Lightningworks

A very specific home feature can be found in a number of buildings throughout the city. Large glass tubes run through the walls of various establishments. These tubes are empty most of the time. Other times, however, long arcs of lightning can be seen racing through the tubing in streaks of blue light. The lightning’s speed increases as morning approaches midday.

The lightning moves so quickly that it begins to pulse and makes reverberating noises, and at exactly midday each day, an explosion can be heard echoing from the Den of Disasters in the second tier of the city. You can hear the explosion even underground—after the explosion is heard, the lightning stops racing through the glass tubes. This project is responsible for powering many of the clockwork machines and other experiments throughout the city.

This project is led by one of the city’s provisional council advisors, Professor Leguntor, a leading expert in lightning manifestation. This regular phenomenon has affected the city in strange ways. Static electricity is rampant throughout the afternoon and late into the evening, and odd blue hazes can be seen around objects and people. Right before the explosion occurs each day, many people have reported seeing the clouds overhead darken and a strange smell enter the air for only a moment before both return to normal after the explosion.


### History

The current city of Stehlenwald was built atop a series of valuable veins of precious metals that ancient groups of dwarves had previously mined. As the new families found these mines and new veins of ore there, they began constructing small homes in larger cavities within the network of mining tunnels.

As time passed, more people came to call the settlement home, and gnomes joined the dwarves in their mining and smithing ventures. Together, they constructed the first wall of Stehlenwald to protect themselves from monsters and wild animals on the surface.

As the need for more materials became paramount, they unearthed even more caves and excavations. These old tunnels began the very first roads of the undercity, and at the same time, gnomes and dwarves went to work making a new portion of the city that was open to the sky, carved out of mountain ridges. Thus was the overcity and undercity distinction made manifest.

#### Era of Expansion

The relative peace and prosperity of Stehlenwald was shattered by the encroachment of the humans during the Era of Expansion. The appearance of the human tribes caused the citizens of Stehlenwald to reinforce their walls, add tiers to the overcity, and keep a wary eye pointed toward the north.

The citizenry reinforced the city with every available resource they had. After having built more walls and fortifications, they felt as though they were impenetrable, as the city of Stehlenwald had become a refined fortress of only the highest quality craftsmanship.

The separate battles between the human invaders and the citizens of Stehlenwald raged for decades, with human tenacity and determination clashing with the stubbornness of the dwarves and the craftiness of the gnomes.

Often, the humans would try to use old mine shafts to access the city’s underbelly, but the dwarves were too skilled as excavators to allow such tactics to be effective. The dwarven and gnomish discovery of adamantine and black powder meant the human armies were decimated. Stehlenwald could no longer be touched by torch or blade, as humans were slaughtered by the roar of canon fire and soldiers clad in impenetrable armor.

Stehlenwald remained independent until eventually hunger brought them down from the mountain. Conflict finally came to an end when the dwarves and humans agreed to a form of joint rulership over the area. The city of Stehlenwald, along with the parts of the Rock-Teeth Mountains where it was located, were officially claimed as part of the Bürach Empire’s territory.

The emergence of the Great Beast was a ruinous day for the people of Stehlenwald, for it wasn’t just the surface lands that suffered the plague of mutated creatures. The underworld also now sees its share of horror. Rumors are rampant, and the church disavows any discussion or acknowledgment of the creature as nothing more than a mass hallucination. With the people consumed by panic, such emotions always find their way to the surface—or to the depths.

### Notable Locations

Several notable locations are present within and around Stehlenwald. Various stairs and passages run between the four tiers of Stehlenwald and into the undercity. Some of these stairs are purely functional, while others are crafted from beautiful marble with adornments and reliefs worthy of the greatest dwarven sculptors.

#### Gateside Plateau

The area outside of the main walls of Stehlenwald is considered part of neither the overcity nor the undercity of Stehlenwald. It does play, however, a role in the life of the citizens there. For outside the main gates of Stehlenwald, near the base of the mountains, are haphazardly arranged farms and hamlets that provide most of the sustenance for the city’s occupants. Trade also brings in a good deal of food, but the staples come from the farms and orchards of the Gateside Plateau.

No single location there is noteworthy. The farmers fend for themselves and keep their own peace, save for when a larger threat presents itself. When that happens, the Stehlenwald City Guard emerges to protect the vital fields of the plateau.

A growing population of unemployed or unemployable folks have made their wretched homes on the plateau, because those who are injured or driven mad by the goings-on within the city are not kept within the walls unless they have kith or kin to tend to them. Those who have caretakers are kept in cells in the undercity, but the rest are deposited outside the walls and left to their own devices.

The homes of Gateside Plateau are mostly reinforced tents and shacks spread out among the flatter rocks as close to the city’s wall as they can be. Some shambled structures are built leaning on the wall for support, to the point where many of them look to only be half built. The further away from the walls of the city one goes, the larger the farms and wealthier the farmers, with equally ornate and refined architecture.

#### Gate Tier (First Tier)

Within the outer walls of the overcity, there are four tiers. The lowest tier is called the Gate Tier, and it houses marketplaces and other businesses that cater to or deal with visitors, merchants, and traveling tradespeople.

The Hübsches Gelding. This Shopping District is comprised of a warmly lit network of streets lined with storefronts. Artisans like tailors, clockmakers, jewelers, and toolsmiths all gather on this stretch as it runs right down the winding central road. The gas lanterns hang from the sides of the stores along with moving signs comprised of turning gears, making the signs move in eerie and distracting ways.

The Brass Abby. The Materials District is mostly comprised of warehouses and storage facilities. While sales don’t normally take place at the warehouses, it has become the perfect place for illegal backroom deals. Often, materials can be exchanged and traded in bulk in this way. Security is often supplied by the warehouses themselves, as they employ mercenaries to guard shipments and merchandise, both while it remains in storage and while it’s being delivered to its destination.

The Stehlenwald Stalls. The open-air market is the bustling heart of the Gate Tier. Citizens and visitors with special permits can set up tents and other temporary shelters to hawk their wares. Everything you can imagine might be found here, from bread to arcane mechanical objects of questionable make and utility.

#### Artisan Tier

The second tier is walled off from and raised above the Gate Tier. Although many smaller stairs can access it, this section of the city is walled off both to keep intruders out and to keep the dangerous work done here from affecting those in other areas.

This tier is home to most of the workshops, smiths, and other places where goods get made and dangerous services get performed. Visitors to Stehlenwald might be found here if they have business to perform or relationships with one of the resident artisans.

Den of Disasters. Among many notable workshops and smithies is this stone building that was, until recently, abandoned. Now, every day at high sun, a singular explosion reverberates from the building. No guards or other officials investigate or question the activities there, so the local population assumes that what is happening is not dangerous and questionable. Glass tubes carrying lightning emerge from the stone building and run through the ground and walls, offering a fascinating view for those who’ve never seen it before. Some have come to use the regular booming as a way to tell time.

#### Home Tier

The third tier is mainly the residential area, with a few small businesses that cater to the immediate domestic needs of the people who live and work in the overcity. The Stehlenwald City Guard is stationed here, ensuring peace for the residents and having easy access to the tiers below and the fourth tier of the overcity above.

#### Lord’s Tier

The fourth and final tier of the overcity is made of tall, towered structures of the finest artistry. The streets are illuminated by the light from windows along with glass tubing that occasionally fill with arcs of lightning, covering the street in blue light.

Guards patrol the streets regularly, with their weapons on full display. While firearms are rare, some officers carry blackpowder weapons.

Governance Grave. The Office District is filled with bureaucratic buildings, a mess of offices that truck in certifications, forms, licenses, and legal documentation. Many of the warehouse managers, guildhalls, merchants’ consortiums, and legislative offices are also located here.

Quartz Palace. The main administrative building of Stehlenwald is a dazzling structure of polished marble with carved statues of two dwarves standing guard at the entrance of the palace. One grasps a pickaxe along with gems in his other hand, while the other holds a hammer aloft with a bottle of alcohol raised in celebration. The building is covered in gold trim and embossed with copper and brass, which emphasizes the white marble. Inside, you’ll find the headquarters of Stehlenwald’s provisional council advisors.

#### Underways and Undercity

From points throughout the overcity, entrances allow access to the underground network of tunnels that comprise the undercity. These old tunnels have been filled with ductwork, cables, and other arcane trappings, and some pathways have been left to crumble or are so reinforced they may one day outlast the city.

Although the Era of Expansion brought such chaos to attack the city, few signs of battle are found above ground, since much of the damage has been repaired; however, the tunnels leading to the undercity showcase stories of failed sieges. Walking through the maze of tunnels, it becomes almost impossible to tell what part of the city is above without experience or the keenest sense of direction. They wind down deeper and deeper, even as the new tunnels are being formed as necessary.

As people pass through the tunnels, they might see many sights: crates of strange machinery waiting for assembly, a team of dwarves and gnomes desperately cranking and pushing a large drill bit toward a strange type of rock blocking their way, or merchants haggling over some goods coming from or heading toward the overcity.

It’s clear that many people working in the dwarven tunnels have some part to play in securing and enhancing the city’s water and energy supply. Additionally, keen-eyed travelers would notice there are few torches in the tunnels. Instead, the pathways are illuminated by glass tubes of green chemicals that glow brightly when shaken. The soldiers carry lanterns with this mysterious concoction inside to assist the workers.

Everyone is well aware that no open flames can be carried into the tunnels, as the air is thin and the gas that supplies the street lanterns above is also routed through the tunnels. While travelers are allowed to travel through some tunnels, the soldiers are quick to deny entry to anyone who is not there to work, and navigating them can become tedious to the point of exhaustion.

A smaller city supports these pursuits, offering homes, shops, and businesses to those who prefer underground living. Many dwarves proudly inhabit this city, looking with derision on those dwarves who deign to travel aboveground and interact with the other cultures that seek trade.

### Important Individuals

The following individuals play key roles in stories and adventures set in the city of Stehlenwald.

#### Lord Reggard Laurendor

The dwarven Clan Laurendor has inhabited the city since the first ore was mined from its mountain, or so they like to say. The current head of the clan, and administrator for Stehlenwald itself, is Lord Reggard Laurendor. This old and revered dwarf carries himself with both grace and homespun wit.

Lord Reggard has gone to great pains bolstering the standing of his eldest daughter, Janka Laurendor. He hopes to marry her to the Archduke’s oldest son, Adalbronz. When Archduke Ulrich Eizenherz finally dies, and Adalbronz takes his place, Reggard wants his daughter at the heir’s side. His true hope is for Janka to meet the emperor, or even become a member of the Säullan Council in her husband’s stead.

Tasked with administering Stehlenwald on the Archduke’s behalf, Lord Reggard’s attention has strayed from the city’s daily needs as he’s become obsessed with long-term ambitions. Convinced that Stehlenwald’s strength will collapse long before the empire falls into ruin, he hopes that Janka can one day gracefully undermine the Bürach Empire’s relentless consumption of the city’s resources. As such, he’s taken to frequently traveling and forging alliances with other political leaders throughout the imperial realms, leaving the care of Stehlenwald in the hands of his aides.

As young men, Lord Reggard and Archduke Ulrich were friends, but now, Reggard believes Ulrich has passed his prime and quietly waits for the Archduke’s death. At this stage, it would be a mercy. The Archduke’s paranoia and reclusion have made him an untenable leader, and Lord Reggard seeks to verify the rumors whether a Sacred Artifact is indeed locked beneath the Quartz Palace.

#### Professor Alarica Deiderich

Alarica Deiderich specializes in the study and use of lightning manifestation. She and her assistants research the creation and harnessing of lightning as a potential source of power that can revolutionize how the city operates.

The slight and stooped figure is widely considered standoffish and unsocial, which is the reason given for her inability to retain laboratory assistants. As soon as one role is filled, the assistant promptly goes missing, leaving the job open again.

Klaher Baklaher Canary's Lament Worker As the architect of the Lightning Speed Chamber, the professor is always present to observe her daily experiments and personally see to its maintenance and repairs. She has always informed the city’s officials that only she knows how the machine truly works, and thus it shall remain her personal property that she loans to the city for the experiments. When not serving the city as a respectable aide, she is busy in her laboratories, perfecting the recipe for blasphemy. As the city’s only resident dhampir, she often hides her features behind machine goggles with tinted lenses and covers her exposed skin with cloth. She has taken to devouring her assistants, but as she is not a true vampire, much of the bodies would go to waste. As such, she puts their remains to good use. Combining a vast knowledge of magic and scientific application, she joins them together to give life to a new people: the arisen. Her creations are of great interest to her, and she often fawns over them as they learn of the greater world around them. Some of the laboratory staff are familiar with her work in giving life to assembled corpses, and she encourages her true students to experiment with this new life.

#### Laboratory Assistant, Detlef Hagen

Detlef is a young human who traveled to the city in hopes of learning under Professor Alarica Deiderich. Upon arriving, he was treated poorly by the locals and had few coins to even rent a room.

In order to endear himself to the professor, Detlef took meticulous notes on her research. As his ambitions grow, his caution wavers. With something to prove and burning thirst for understanding, the young assistant began assembling composite bodies. He was meticulous in collecting materials and following Professor Deiderich’s grisly research. Nevertheless, Detlef’s creations have been less than successful.

Detlef’s ambition outmatches his patience or skill. Other assistants ponder the ethics of letting him practice biological engineering, actively fearing the constructed horrors he’s managed to build. Unless he improves, he may be but one more of Professor Deiderich’s missing students.

#### The Head Alchemist, Lady Gouden Burkta

When the dreamers were found deep in the Rock-Teeth Mountains, many wandered into the wider world while a few remained. Gouden Burkta, a student of alchemy, remained in Stehlenwald with visions of a brighter world. Literally.

Gouden is the mastermind behind the chemical lanterns used in the tunnels, which generate light without magic or other energy sources. Instead, these lanterns use bioluminescent algae found in deep cave lakes within the mountains. She has a large pool of the algae growing in her private laboratory and encourages people to use the algae lanterns instead of gas or oil lamps to help conserve resources.

#### Master Craftsmen, Nitter Soyar

This gnome is in charge of the city’s production of blackpowder. He is obsessed with weaponry and has been working with the Ebon Syndicate to smuggle weapons and substances in and out of the city without the empire’s or the City Official’s knowledge. Master Soyar is also the head of the Scrap Guild, comprised of the city’s orphaned children, who collect all the discarded scraps of tools, parts, and materials throughout the city. His love for machines sees him tending to many of the clockwork creations that perform tasks inside the city walls.

#### Dr Reisenheim

Doctor Veirgeld Reisenheim is a dwarven surgeon who has been placed in charge of the medical needs of the citizens of Stehlenwald. He accepted the post reluctantly, working out an agreement to undertake the responsibilities of the position if the city would fund his true passion: prosthetics. The violence and warfare of Etharis has led to a growing need for amputations, and Reisenheim is fascinated with helping those in need. When treating more mundane patients, a practiced medic would note that the dwarf seems more fascinated with the size and functionality of limbs than the actual symptoms of his current patients. Some of the physicians who work under Reisenheim’s tutelage grimly joke that one of his patients best not have a particular fetching arm, hand, leg, or foot—for they may awaken to him sawing it off to use as a model for a newly crafted prosthetic. ### Quest Hooks

The following quest hooks are appropriate for adventures inside the city of Stehlenwald.

#### The Diligence of Believers

Lady Gouden Burkta is seeking assistance with a rather urgent task. A group of people have gathered and are starting to dig the entrance to an unauthorized tunnel. She’s concerned because the group has been digging without properly mapping out the structure of the rock, meaning the tunnel could be extremely unstable. With blackpowder easily obtainable on the black market, if a member of the group brings anything explosive inside and follows by lighting a torch, they could trigger a devastating rockslide or sinkhole inside Stehlenwald.

Investigators discover most of the folks digging these new tunnels are newly created arisen. A man named Zayga introduces himself as the leader of a group calling themselves Canary’s Lament. He says that they're spreading the word that new life must be constructed if the people of the world are to survive what is to come.

The arisen of Canary’s Lament insist the characters not traverse their new tunnels, but anyone who sneaks or fights their way in sees that the group has been smuggling parts of machinery from Stehlenwald into the tunnel to construct a massive furnace. The fiery engine pulses and roars. Its purpose isn’t entirely evident, yet there are vats of bubbling blood and writhing muscle as part of its construction. Arisen appear to be grafted to this machine. The furnace resembles a giant beating heart, its architects worshiping before it as if their liturgies assist its function.

The group’s true intentions are soon revealed: they sees all of humanity as canaries trapped in a coal mine. The cult seeks to save the world and bring forth a new age of godliness, which will deliver them from the old ways of starvation and illness into a new era of progress and expansion. The Four Divines were distant and intangible. They seek to build a new god here in the mortal realm—one that doesn’t require faith to feel their presence.

The Canary’s Lament whisper their creation’s name: the Dread Machine. It speaks the secrets of its construction into Zayga’s dreams. All who gaze upon it shall fear and revere it, powered by the very life force that powers each of the arisen. Constructed deep beneath the mountain, the engine is too large to move through the tunnels.

Battling the cultists of the Canary’s Lament reveals that with each death, the Dread Machine pulses faster and the heat of its furnace turns blue. After the cult is slain, the heart begins to beat on its own.

If instead allowed to continue constructing their machine, the cult grows in power and influence across Stehlenwald. The people are hungry for something to believe in and eager to witness the birth of a new god.

Stehlenwald holds but the heart of this machine. Other sects of the cult could exist across Etharis who must also be hunted, in cities like Morencia or Malkovia, where other dreadful constructs are created.

#### A Student’s Greatest Failure

A young human frantically looks through the side streets as though he’s lost something. He begs for aid from anyone who takes interest, introducing himself as Detlef Hagen, a lab assistant who’s lost track of a rather dangerous experiment. He explains how his mentor is Professor Alarica Deiderich, one of the provisional aides of the city.


He further describes how the creature tried to attack him, and now he can’t locate it. He believes it to be extremely violent and dangerous, so he’s seeking mercenaries to kill it. Tracking this wayward creation through the city reveals clues that the arisen has entered the upper tiers of Stehlenwald.

Following the escaped arisen’s trail leads to an abandoned building. The arisen is large, muscular, and poorly built. Its ropey sutures strain to hold its misshapen flesh together, and indeed its mismatched eyes are filled with blood and a piercing stare. The man has long black hair. He can be seen leafing through a stolen book, slowly following each line with his finger. Despite his grotesque appearance, the arisen is no mere golem and clearly intelligent.

If approached calmly, the towering arisen speaks guardedly. His language is formal and strangely polite, well articulated for a creature born in the previous few hours. However, he is clearly afraid and confused. Paranoia makes him prone to violence without much provocation, using Suture Golem statistics during a fight.

If asked what happened, the arisen recounts the moments since his creation. He recounts the pain of waking with lightning surging through his muscles, the scent of blood filling his nostrils, and striking out at his creator. Detlef promptly screamed and fled the room. Confused and unrestrained, the arisen went searching to make sense of his existence.

The arisen gives the impression he has the mind of an intelligent child. It’s clear he has no understanding of who or what he is and no concept of the world around him. But he has a hunger and capacity for rapid learning, despite a roiling inner rage and a clear lack of empathy. If confronted by his creator, the arisen wants Detlef killed. He will become an ally of anyone who helps seek revenge for the crime of his creation.

Detlef meanwhile is grateful and pays anyone who kills the arisen and brings the remains back to his laboratory. He may even facilitate an introduction to his boss, Professor Deiderich, as well as provide use of his laboratory.

If the arisen is permitted to live, Detlef is furious and refuses to offer them any reward. He insists that the creature is dangerous and malformed, and he claims the parts the arisen is built from are far too valuable to waste on a failed experiment.
