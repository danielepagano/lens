**Chapter 2: The Bürach Empire**

The Bürach Empire is a dying colossus rotting from within — a land of relentless greed, fractured faith, and futile determination. Once the dominant power of Etharis, the Empire now clings to its shrunken borders while nobles scheme for position, the Hearthkeepers suppress inconvenient truths, and something vast and corrupting stalks its provinces. The tone here is existential dread layered over imperial pride: people who sense they are losing but cannot stop performing confidence. Cosmic horror encroaches on a civilization too proud to acknowledge it is already broken.

## Geography

The Empire occupies the heart of Etharis — dense forests, rocky coasts, mountain strongholds, and marshland provinces spanning from the Emerald Sea in the west to the Grey Spine in the east. The forests shrink as the Empire's demand for lumber never stops. Four provinces, each with its own culture and grievance, form the whole: Abendland (the religious heartland), Nordenland (the arcane north), Rauland (the industrial mountains), and Unterland (the military southeast).

**The Rock-Teeth Mountains** divide Rauland's southern reaches, riddled with ancient mines now crawling with horrors summoned by the Great Beast's passage.

**Walstein** is the Empire's most famous ruin — a city razed by Norden mages during the Gods' End, now a black-stoned labyrinth of ash and aberrations. Mercenaries, academics, and criminals use it as a grim hunting ground.

## History

**The Era of Expansion.** Emperor Indorius I, chosen by the Hearthkeepers after a miraculous sign, unified the continent's warring kingdoms under the Four Divines (Aurelia, Galt, Ulmyr, Maligant) and received sacred artifacts embodying each god's essence.

**Gods' End.** The mad Emperor Leopold I declared one unnamed deity the sole true god, triggering a continent-shattering religious civil war. The gods themselves began fighting, their divine fury devastating the mortal realm. The only salvation came when Aurelia sacrificed her divinity to shield Etharis — and the gods fell silent forever. Their angels fell from the sky like burning sparks.

**The Aftermath.** With the gods gone, the Hearthkeepers crowned a new emperor using forged replicas of the Sacred Artifacts. The real artifacts were stolen by Leopold's assassins and lost. Civil war left deep provincial divides, and the Empire has never fully healed.

**Rising Shadows.** Something called the Great Beast now wanders the Empire. Where it passes, people transform into aberrations — many-limbed mutants, flesh-twisted horrors, corrupted warriors with weapons fused to their bones. The Beast's sigil (a deer skull crowned with antlers) appears on church doors. No survivor has given a coherent account of the creature itself. The Hearthkeepers brand any discussion of it as heresy.

## Current State / Major Conflicts

**The Hearthkeepers** control the flow of information across the Empire, crown emperors, and suppress religious heterodoxy. With divine magic gone, their authority rests on ritual, fear, and the infant Emperor Odalric I, whom they raised from birth. Pope Roland Murrich keeps the boy close and managed.

**The Säullan Council** is a noble council that has stepped into the power vacuum — advisors who each teach the young Emperor their own agenda. Their collective influence threatens to permanently sideline the Hearthkeepers.

**The Celestial Gate** is a doomsday cult that venerates the Great Beast as divine retribution for humanity's sins, founded by the (possibly still living) Gatekeeper Chantal Sommerkin. Their writings circulate covertly, and their agents have infiltrated every tier of society.

**Provincial fragmentation** deepens: Nordenland pursues rogue magical research, the Norden tribes have reverted to primal traditions, and a Tormach-worshipping military cult in Unterland threatens to seize command of the army.

## Key NPCs

**Martorius,** the archmage whose glittering castle appeared over Rauland after the Gods' End. Presents as brilliant, charming, and eccentric; is in truth dangerous, alien, and possibly not of this world. His castle is a dimensional puzzle of indeterminate rooms. He entertains nobles with parlor tricks while his agents scour the Empire for the lost Sacred Artifacts. Nobody believes he is benevolent. Some wonder if he predates the Empire itself.

**Emperor Odalric I,** crowned as an infant by the Hearthkeepers and raised entirely in political captivity. He is now a curious, impressionable boy genuinely fascinated by the world outside — which makes him simultaneously dangerous and sympathetic. His teachers each see him as a weapon. Whether he can outgrow their influence before they hollow him out is the real question.

**Bishop Karlos Reinach,** a Hearthkeeper preacher who breaks with the church by publicly declaring the Great Beast an omen of apocalypse. His unorthodox sermons have attracted the Emperor's attention, making him a channel of Hearthkeeper influence — or possibly a pawn of the Celestial Gate; his pre-Hearthkeeper history is a complete mystery.

**Archduchess Maelfa Erlekind** (Nordenland), the last heir of Erlefurt's founding house. Bold, self-assured, and diplomatically skilled enough to maintain near-total autonomy from the Empire. Rumors link her to the Thaumaturge — a shadowy cabal said to control global politics.

**Archduke Ulrich Eizenherz** (Rauland), once a fair and capable dwarven lord, now a paranoid recluse who cannot sleep without light. He reportedly dreams of the Great Beast's eyes and whispers secrets to no one. Rumored to hold a Sacred Artifact in the vaults beneath the Quartz Palace.

## Featured City: Stehlenwald

The forge-city of Stehlenwald climbs the Rock-Teeth Mountains in four tiered steps of granite, marble, and perpetual soot. Smoke clouds from ever-burning furnaces block the sun, and ash falls like grey snow on quality cobblestone streets. Clockwork machines — brilliant and prone to catastrophic failure — haul wagons, light lanterns with burning gas, and race lightning through glass tubes embedded in the walls. Every day at noon, an explosion from the Den of Disasters echoes through the city as Professor Deiderich's lightning experiments discharge. The city smells of chemicals and scorched metal and sounds like a machine that never stops.

Stehlenwald is two cities: a tiered overcity of markets, workshops, homes, and administrative palaces; and an undercity of gas-lit tunnels, ancient mines, and dwarves who consider the sky an indulgence. Both feel claustrophobic by design.

### Notable Locations

**The Quartz Palace** (Lord's Tier) — the dazzling administrative seat of Stehlenwald, polished marble trimmed in gold and brass, crowned by the famous dwarven statuary. Rumored to contain a Sacred Artifact in its deepest vaults.

**The Den of Disasters** (Artisan Tier) — Professor Deiderich's lightning laboratory, source of the daily noon explosion that the city uses to set its clocks. Glass tubes from this building carry arcing lightning throughout much of the city.

**The Underways** — the tunnels beneath are gas-lit, labyrinthine, and dangerous to the flame-bearing visitor. No open flames permitted; soldiers carry bioluminescent algae lanterns. Ancient mine shafts branch into places even the dwarves prefer not to explore.

### Key Individuals

**Lord Reggard Laurendor,** the dwarven administrator of Stehlenwald, obsessed with securing a marriage alliance between his daughter Janka and the Archduke's heir — ensuring his bloodline outsurvives the Empire. He travels constantly to forge alliances, leaving the city in the hands of aides, and privately waits for Eizenherz to die.

**Professor Alarica Deiderich,** dhampir and lightning researcher, presents as a standoffish academic. In truth she has been secretly devouring her laboratory assistants and reanimating their remains as arisen — assembled corpses she tends with something approaching maternal fascination.

**Lady Gouden Burkta,** the Head Alchemist, inventor of the bioluminescent algae lanterns that light the undercity without flame. A visionary dreamer operating in a city that turns every dream into an industrial tool.

**Dr. Veirgeld Reisenheim,** Stehlenwald's leading surgeon and prosthetics pioneer. His obsession with limb functionality edges toward something darker — colleagues joke about it, but he is genuinely one fascinating case away from crossing into butchery.
